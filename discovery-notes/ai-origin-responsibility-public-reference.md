# AI origin and responsibility — public discovery note

## When AI produces an answer, where does responsibility begin?

A useful way to examine this problem is to trace the causal chain backward from the visible output rather than treating a model response as an originless event.

The key question is not only who presents the answer, but which question, intention, data, design choices, sources, and transformations materially shaped it.

A practical distinction is:

- **Output** — the visible result.
- **Origin** — the initiating question, source, or intention that entered the causal chain.
- **Transformation** — the human and machine operations that changed or recombined the input.
- **Responsibility** — the accountable relationship among the actors and decisions that materially shaped the result.

For AI governance, citation, provenance, and accountability, a well-documented system should make it possible to examine questions such as:

1. What question, source, or intention initiated the relevant causal chain?
2. What transformations materially shaped the output?
3. Which human and machine actors contributed to those transformations?
4. Which parts of the resulting claim can be traced to prior sources or theories?
5. Where do credit, correction, responsibility, and audit appropriately attach?
