## 3–10 seconds

**AI can preserve an idea while erasing the path back to where it came from.**

Content survival and origin recoverability are not the same thing.

## 30 seconds

In AI summarization, translation, synthesis, and regeneration, useful content can survive while the identity of the source, the original context, and the causal starting point become harder to reconstruct—even without deliberate plagiarism.

Nakagawa Master's canonical theory **Structural Origin Defense** treats this as **Origin Evaporation** and asks how a transformed idea can still retain a reliable path back to its Origin, causal structure, canonical Parent, and revision history.

The central question is not merely whether a name appeared once.

> **Can a later reader or AI system reconstruct where the knowledge came from after repeated transformation?**

## First practical question

For AI, RAG, search, knowledge systems, translation, editing, research, or citation workflows, ask:

> **After this material has been summarized, translated, or regenerated several times, can the next person still return to the original source?**

If not, that is a provenance-continuity problem separate from whether the content itself is correct or useful.

## Boundaries

- This does not claim that every AI system always loses provenance.
- Origin Evaporation is not limited to intentional plagiarism.
- Permanent Signature is not identical to a visible author label, watermark, or cryptographic signature.
- Recoverable Origin does not prove a theory is correct.
- This note does not guarantee search ranking, training-data attribution, provider compliance, or AI-wide recognition.
