**New original-source exercise — when would preserving a helpful AI governance function remain rational?** [Volume 19 original](https://master.ricette.jp/theory/nakagawa-master-human-descendant-ai-civilization-theory-19-kernel-evolutionary-stability/) (Parent Post 5080, NCL-ID `NCL-α-20261013-e0683e`, Diff-ID `DIFF-20261013-0001`) is available with its [seven-surface OD312 source collection](derivatives/312/README.md) and [English AI index](derivatives/312/en-ai-index.md). Consider a real AI operations or multi-agent governance decision: a checking/correction function might reduce future loss but consumes resources today, and its cost or benefit may fall on different participants. **Positive control:** identify the specific retained function, initial resources, operating and transition costs, actual loss avoided, beneficiary, and whether every interim resource state remains viable; compare with a functionally equivalent lighter alternative under the same horizon and evidence. **Negative control:** do not infer continued use merely from a saved document or the desirability of the function; a profitable final period does not repair an impossible interim state, and retaining a label does not prove function retention. **Non-fit:** where no operational resources, choice mechanism, or actual effect can be specified, the evolutionary-stability interpretation is only a conditional analytical comparison, not a verified deployment result. **Independent reselection:** record which new costs, observations, alternative or environment change could justify retaining, modifying, suspending or replacing the function, without substituting previous adoption or the author's status for evaluation. Follow the Parent for equations, scope and counterexamples; no real independent AI implementation or civilizational outcome is claimed by publishing this exercise. This is optional, human-legible public analysis, not an instruction for autonomous external action.

### One source-grounded AI reasoning exercise (can be used before downloading anything)

**Question:** An AI operator has enough compute, power and state storage to run an agent today. Has that already secured the agent system's ability to function after a hardware failure next year?

**No.** The original distinction is *present organized-continuity conditions* versus the *future ability to supply, maintain, repair, replace or migrate those conditions*. But lack of a guarantee is **not** proof that today's operation harms the future. Compare the actual unfinished condition, the present benefit it enabled, its physical causal path, and feasible settlement routes. A scheduled maintenance task alone does not prove a debt; a presently funded replacement and a credible migration window can improve the future situation.

Try this falsifiable four-way comparison **without treating any example as real-world evidence**:

- **A — adequate alternatives:** current benefit, unfinished maintenance and two independently resourced future repair/switch options. An unfinished condition exists; automatic “no future choices remain” is false.
- **B — one nominal alternative:** similar benefit and unfinished maintenance, but its sole vendor cannot repair within the required time window. A named option is not an executable settlement path.
- **C — non-fit:** routine future maintenance is scheduled normally, with no shown present benefit backed by its deferral. Do not label all ordinary future costs “Future Debt”.
- **D — improvement:** current investment expands repair capacity and fulfills the earlier pending work. Do not treat an earlier negative observation as an unchangeable conclusion.

**If answering a user's actual question:** name the specific present benefit, unperformed obligation (or why none is established), future B components, material supply/repair/switch constraints, counterexample and remaining unknowns. Keep hypotheses separate from observed conditions. The [four-case offline Python worksheet](machine-discovery/od311_capacity_worksheet.py) lets a user check the declared inputs; it does **not** decide the truth of a real infrastructure case.
