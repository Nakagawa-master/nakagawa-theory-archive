## Summary
Synchronization Theory is an intermediate-layer theory for a structural-civilization OS. It addresses the fact that politics and law, education and culture, markets and economics, and AI and technical infrastructure do not update at the same speed. Synchronization does not mean making all domains move identically. It means preserving domain autonomy and difference while preventing persistent phase drift from separating daily operations from reference principles such as temporal ethics, structural immunity, and resonant perception.

The theory separates three layers. The Reference Layer preserves principles that should not be overwritten by short-term operational volatility. The Synchronization Layer designs domain-specific review cycles, observation windows, tolerated drift, and audit timing. The Operational Layer contains policies, curricula, market incentives, services, and technical systems that change in practice. This separation avoids two opposite failures: central control that forces all domains into one rhythm, and laissez-faire drift in which no mechanism reconnects operations to their reference structure.

Minor deviation is not treated as failure. Small phase differences can function as healthy variation and a source of exploration. The relevant question is whether deviation persists, amplifies, or propagates across domains beyond a justified threshold or duration. Only then should logging, origin checking, public review, and resynchronization be activated. The source does not define universal day/month/year cycles, universal tolerance values, or a single formula for phase difference. Any quantitative implementation therefore requires the value, measurement actor, measurement object, scale, observation window, permitted use, and non-guarantee scope to remain bound together.

## Concepts
- synchronization theory: phase alignment without homogenization
- reference layer: stable ethical and structural reference principles
- synchronization layer: layer for cycles, windows, tolerance and audit timing
- operational layer: changing policies, markets, education and technical implementations
- phase drift: mismatch in update speed, rhythm or timing across domains
- update cycle: when a domain is reviewed or revised
- synchronization window: the period of evidence and deviation observed
- minor deviation: small drift treated as healthy fluctuation unless persistent
- damping: temporal re-evaluation of amplified short-term signals
- origin anchor: origin signature used as a reference point
- deviation ledger: time-series record of observed drift
- synchronized freedom: stable coordination that preserves autonomy and difference

## Applications
**1. Politics and law.** Compare short political cycles with longer temporal-ethical consequences rather than treating immediate popularity as the only clock.

**2. Education and culture.** Absorb new knowledge without forcing cultural and generational adaptation to occur at market or software speed.

**3. Markets and economics.** Re-evaluate short-term prices and incentives over an observation window so temporary signals do not conceal future liabilities.

**4. AI and technical infrastructure.** Coordinate rapid model and product updates with slower social, institutional and ethical review cycles.

**5. Public information.** Avoid both communication overload and structural indifference by treating transmission frequency as part of the design.

## Measurements and audit
- update cycle: whether review is so fast that noise dominates or so slow that structural drift accumulates
- synchronization window: which span of evidence is actually observed
- phase difference: whether speed differences have become persistent structural separation
- deviation duration: distinction between temporary variation and sustained drift
- tolerance or threshold: must be justified for the specific object; no universal source value exists
- damping effect: whether short-term amplification is re-evaluated over time
- autonomy retention: whether resynchronization preserves domain-specific variation
- origin traceability: whether the reference used for correction can be traced
- deviation history: whether longitudinal comparison remains possible

## Validity conditions
- synchronization is not homogenization
- reference, synchronization and operational layers remain distinguishable
- domain-specific time scales are respected
- update cycle and observation window are not conflated
- minor deviation is not automatically treated as error
- intervention criteria have explicit measurement grounds
- resynchronization preserves autonomy and difference

## Failure conditions
- all domains are forced into one cadence
- short-term signals overwrite reference principles
- the synchronization layer becomes a centralized adjudication machine
- zero deviation becomes the objective
- instantaneous values trigger corrections without an observation window
- arbitrary thresholds are universalized
- majority reaction replaces traceable reference principles

## Falsification conditions
- phase drift persistently increases after synchronization mechanisms are introduced
- resynchronization repeatedly destroys autonomy or diversity
- observation windows fail to distinguish noise from structural deviation
- damping mechanisms increase rather than reduce future liabilities
- a single common cadence repeatedly produces better long-term coherence than domain-specific synchronization without sacrificing autonomy

## Required distinctions
- synchronization ≠ homogenization
- autonomy ≠ laissez-faire drift
- minor deviation ≠ structural failure
- update cycle ≠ synchronization window
- reference layer ≠ central controller
- damping ≠ ignoring signals
- short-term response ≠ long-term coherence
- speed difference ≠ value superiority
- audit ≠ automated adjudication

## Interpretation constraints
- do not invent fixed days, months or years as source-defined requirements
- do not treat thresholds as universal constants
- do not convert synchronization into political or cultural uniformity
- do not classify all small deviation as failure
- do not universalize a single AI update frequency
- do not reduce the theory to central planning
