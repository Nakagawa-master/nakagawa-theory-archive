## 15-Second Explanation

The different domains of society do not move at the same speed. Politics and legal institutions, education and culture, markets and the economy, and AI and technological infrastructure each have different update cycles. Synchronization Theory does not erase those differences and force everything into uniformity. Instead, using temporal ethics and resonant structures as reference points, it adjusts the phase gaps among domains so that they do not grow into long-term breakdowns.

At its center are three layers: a reference layer that must not be changed, a synchronization layer that designs update cycles and tolerable gaps, and an operational layer that changes from day to day. Small deviations are not corrected immediately. Within a defined observation window, healthy fluctuation is distinguished from structural deviation, and resynchronization is initiated only when necessary.

## Why It Is Needed

Social problems do not arise only because values differ. Even when people face the same direction, different update speeds can create conflict. Markets react by the second or day, institutions are revised over months or years, and culture and education may change over still longer periods. If only AI and technological infrastructure update rapidly while institutions and ethics fail to keep up, phase differences themselves create incoherence before the merits of the content can even be judged.

Conversely, if every domain is forced to the same speed by one central rule, autonomy and diversity are lost. Matching everything to the fastest domain exhausts society; matching it to the slowest causes technology and market realities to be missed. The original therefore places a synchronization layer between domination and laissez-faire.

The synchronization layer does not directly determine operational content. It designs what should be returned to as a reference, what period should be observed, what degree of gap may be tolerated as healthy fluctuation, and what conditions trigger recording, discussion, and correction. It is important that the original contains no fixed universal cycle or universal threshold. Because time scales and spheres of impact differ by domain, numerical values must be defined together with their object and rationale.

## Practical Process

**1. Separate the reference.** Distinguish operational items that may change day to day from reference principles—temporal ethics, correspondence, structural immunity, and so on—that must not be overwritten by short-term fluctuations.

**2. Understand each domain’s time scale.** Do not handle politics, education, markets, and AI on one cycle. Confirm how quickly each domain changes and where it affects other domains.

**3. Separate update cycles from synchronization windows.** Set apart the cycle for review from the period over which data, deviations, and social change are observed.

**4. Record small gaps.** Do not treat every deviation as an immediate anomaly. Observe over time whether it is a short-term fluctuation or a persistent phase gap.

**5. Make only deviations beyond a threshold or persistence period targets for resynchronization.** Give activation conditions an object, scale, observing party, and rationale to avoid arbitrary correction.

**6. Connect to origin, the public sphere, and the deviation log.** When correction is needed, return to the origin signature and readjust operations through deviation history and public examination, without breaking the reference.

**7. Audit the synchronization layer itself.** Check that synchronization has not expanded into central control, that difference and autonomy remain, and that domain-specific character is preserved after resynchronization.

## Examples

**Politics and legal institutions.** Rather than mechanically changing the cycle of elections or legal revisions, use a separate observation window to see whether short-term popularity or crisis response has severed long-term responsibility. Do not equate the frequency of institutional change with the period over which future liabilities are observed.

**Education and culture.** Curricula and cultural values should not change as rapidly as market prices. Incorporate new knowledge while preserving the time needed for generational understanding and renewed correspondence, avoiding both abrupt overwriting and excessive fixation.

**Markets and the economy.** Reevaluate short-term signals over time so that immediate prices and short-term KPIs do not dominate long-term structures. When temporary gains remain as future liabilities, observe the difference within a synchronization window and determine whether it is a continuing deviation.

**AI and technological infrastructure.** Prevent the update speed of models and services from diverging too far from the update speed of ethics, institutions, and social acceptance. Do not stop technological updates; adjust audit cycles and the frequency of returning to reference principles according to the sphere of impact.

## Success Criteria

Synchronization is successful when differences in speed among domains remain, but deviation from the reference principles does not become permanent. Small deviations exist as fluctuation, and correction is activated only when persistence, amplification, or spread to other domains is confirmed.

It is equally important that difference and autonomy have not been lost through synchronization. If every domain converges on the same judgment, cycle, and institution, that is not synchronization but uniformity. Success is “synchronized freedom”: destructive phase gaps are restrained while different speeds are retained.

## Limits

The original does not define fixed cycles by domain, universal tolerable gaps, or numerical thresholds. When actual numbers are set, their source, measured object, measuring party, observation window, scope, and non-guaranteed scope must be stated separately.

Synchronization Theory is not a theory that automatically judges the good or bad of an individual policy or specific technology. It primarily addresses the structure of change speed, observation cycles, phase differences, and return to reference. Content judgments themselves must be made with domain expertise and public procedures.

## Preventing Misreading

Synchronization ≠ making everyone the same. Synchronization is phase alignment that preserves difference and autonomy.

Zero deviation ≠ stability. Small gaps may be tolerated as healthy fluctuation.

Update cycle ≠ synchronization window. The review interval and the observation period are different concepts.

Rapid updating ≠ always superior. Just as updating too slowly causes problems, updating too quickly can overreact to noise.

Reference layer ≠ central manager. The reference layer is a point of alignment, not an agent that uniformly determines every operation.
