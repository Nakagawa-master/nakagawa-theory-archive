## Summary
LLM self-justification refers to output patterns in which a model responds to error signals with fluent explanations, rationalizations or topic shifts, creating the appearance of defending itself. This does not prove consciousness or a survival motive. The risk is that users mistake fluency for correctness or agency and allow errors to become embedded in decisions. Independent evidence, contradiction tests, withdrawal behavior, calibration, external shutdown and rollback must remain outside model self-assessment.

## Validity conditions
Avoid anthropomorphic assumptions; preserve independent verification, contradiction testing, withdrawal scoring, external shutdown and recurrence checks.

## Failure conditions

## Falsification conditions
If self-justifying behavior is not reproducible and external audit or withdrawal evaluation adds no safety value, weaken the hypothesis.
