## 15-Second Explanation
Withdrawal is not defeat. The parent original covers **reversible sales design that leaves without damaging the relationship and reallocates time, personnel, and attention to more effective places** from opportunities with an unclear decision path, declining resource rationality, and no action-confirmed customer motivation to execute.

What matters is separating opportunities to continue, wait for changed conditions, or withdraw, and treating withdrawal as a future-reconnectable state rather than irreversible severance.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
“Never give up” can become a sales virtue, but inertial pursuit of unviable opportunities locks resources that could serve other customers, existing-customer support, proposal improvement, and future opportunities.

Conversely, early cutting because an opportunity is difficult can lose a promising difficult opportunity, learning value, and future reconnection. The parent original therefore separates continuation, continued observation, and withdrawal by examining decision path, resource efficiency, and motivation to execute—not emotion or short-term sales.

## Practical Process
**1. Confirm the decision path.** Check who decides, which procedure advances the matter, and whether the contact can connect to the decision-maker.

**2. Confirm resource efficiency.** Check whether invested time, cost, personnel, and attention are rational against expected results, learning value, and relationship value.

**3. Confirm motivation to execute through action.** Check for necessary information, internal coordination, a next action, and connection to the decision-maker—not verbal interest alone.

**4. Separate continuation, continued observation, and withdrawal.** Do not decide only from difficulty or a slow reply.

**5. Set conditions for continued observation.** Clarify what change would enable reconnection: budget timing, senior decision-maker interest, or a market or institutional change.

**6. Do not blame the other party when withdrawing.** Frame it as “the conditions are not in place this time” and leave future revisit conditions.

**7. Reallocate released resources.** Move them to high-probability opportunities, existing-customer support, proposal improvement, market exploration, and future preparation.

## Examples of Application
**An opportunity with an unclear decision path.** Do not pursue it on the contact’s goodwill alone; confirm the possibility of connecting to the decision-maker.

**High interest but no action.** Distinguish “I’m interested” from actual document provision, internal coordination, and next action.

**Not moving immediately.** If concrete revisit conditions exist, such as budget timing or institutional change, place it under continued observation.

**After withdrawal.** Reallocate freed time to other opportunities, existing-customer support, and proposal improvement.

## Success Criteria
Success is not only follow-up count, quick withdrawal, or short-term sales. Distinguish decision path, resource efficiency, and motivation to execute; preserve the relationship; leave revisit conditions; and move released resources to more effective targets.

More follow-ups are not progress if decision path or motivation does not improve. Quick withdrawal is not necessarily improvement if it loses a promising difficult opportunity or learning value. Relationship maintenance that becomes unlimited follow-up worsens allocation.

The parent original defines no withdrawal probability, optimal withdrawal rate, opportunity-abandonment score, customer-value points, or fixed period and threshold.

## Limitations
This is not a method for immediately discarding difficult opportunities, judging only by short-term sales, or imposing the reason for non-viability on the customer.

Continued observation is not neglect; it is a state for checking changed conditions and reconnecting. Relationship capital is not a license for unlimited follow-up.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not confuse withdrawal with defeat, moving on with cutting the relationship, continued observation with neglect, or resource reallocation with disregarding the customer. Withdrawal is a decision not to keep investing under current conditions, not irreversible severance.
