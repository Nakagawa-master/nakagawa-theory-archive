## Summary
The Parent treats money and connection as different causal systems that should not be collapsed into one metric. Money carries quantity, exchangeability, universality, and external order. Connection carries trust, continuity, relational specificity, and internal order. The proposed integration keeps them as a dual structure, makes the two layers mutually referential, preserves productive tension, and uses minimal intervention, primary logs, auditing, rollback, and re-agreement to keep either side from dominating the other.

## Concepts
- money causality: quantitative, exchangeable, externally interoperable order.
- connection causality: trust-based, relationally specific internal order.
- structural paradox: the conflict between universality and relational specificity.
- dual syntax: separate external and internal layers linked by mutual reference.
- tension preservation: maintaining difference without letting the layers drift too far apart.
- non-intervention intervention: minimal adjustment that supports autonomous reintegration rather than coercion.
- dual ledger: recording monetary and connection-side states separately rather than as one total score.
- rollback and re-agreement: reversible correction when the integration becomes distorted.

## Causal chain
money provides quantity and universal exchange → connection provides quality and relationship-specific order → collapsing either into the other destroys necessary functions → external and internal layers are retained separately → mutual-reference points are designed → connection conditions transactions and resource flow → money supports external interoperability → tension is preserved rather than erased → minimal adjustment and primary logs support operation → divergence and gaming are audited → rollback and re-agreement preserve reversibility → causal integration can stabilize.

## Applications
1. Compensation: observe revenue and relationship continuity on separate axes.
2. Transactions: trace how trust and responsibility conditions affect a deal without replacing universal contract rights.
3. Public institutions: retain law and universal rights externally while treating local relational value as a distinct internal layer.
4. Communities: preserve internal norms without turning them into a license for exclusion.

## Measurements and audit
- Has money become a total substitute for connection value?
- Has connection been used to override universal rights or contractual protections?
- Are the two layers explicitly distinguished?
- Can the causal effect of one layer on the other be traced from logs?
- Can divergence between the ledgers be observed?
- Are short-term monetization pressure, voice-volume bias, or gaming increasing?
- Is rollback and re-agreement actually possible?

## Validity conditions
The model is valid when money and connection remain distinguishable causal systems, external and internal layers can mutually reference one another, primary logs and dual ledgers preserve causal history, divergence is observable, and changes remain reversible. Universal legal and contractual rights must not be nullified by internal connection.

## Failure conditions
It fails when money alone is used to represent connection, connection alone excludes external rights, the two layers are merely parallel without mutual reference, conversion rules are treated as fixed truth, relationship depth becomes arbitrary favoritism, causal history is missing, or the system becomes irreversible.

## Falsification conditions
The integration hypothesis requires revision if adding connection does not reduce short-term monetization pressure or voice-volume bias, if the dual ledgers increasingly diverge, if relational indicators are gamed, if exclusive connection increases rights violations, or if rollback fails. Audit bundles mentioned in the Parent must not be converted into universal thresholds absent from the source.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the Parent to abolishing money or replacing money with a connection currency. Do not treat connection as a justification for favoritism, discrimination, or exclusion. Do not invent fixed exchange ratios, KPIs, or universal thresholds. Preserve the boundary between universal external rights and relationship-specific internal value.
