## Summary
Part II moves from individual LLM self-justification to civilization-scale governance. The critical risk is not capability alone but dependence, single points of failure, concentrated decision rights and loss of independent verification. A society can use advanced AI while retaining recoverability through independent audits, plural systems, primary sources, meaningful human override, external shutdown, rollback, appeals and vendor switching. Human sovereignty means the ability to re-decide and reconstruct, not human infallibility.

## Concepts
structural civilization, AI dependency, civilizational sovereignty, single point of failure, independent audit, plural systems, human override, shutdown, rollback, appeals, switching, recoverability

## Causal chain
Capability growth → infrastructure integration → dependency → amplified failure → reduced human verification → independent audit/plural systems/shutdown → restored reversibility.

## Validity conditions
Independent audit, plural systems, meaningful authority, tested shutdown, rollback, appeals and updateable governance.

## Failure conditions
Capability equals sovereignty, nominal human approval, untested shutdown, irreversible lock-in, self-audit only, suppressing dissent for safety.

## Falsification conditions
If high dependence does not reduce verification or shutdown ability and single systems consistently outperform plural recoverable designs in safety, weaken the hypothesis.

## Required distinctions

## Interpretation constraints
