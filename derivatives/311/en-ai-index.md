## Summary

Volume 18 maps the Integrated Future Debt Theory onto the conditional problem space of AI civilization: basic conditions of organized continuity B, compute, physical infrastructure, and replicable or branching lineages. It retains two directions: anticipated future value supporting present credit and investment, and deferral of necessary present burdens supporting present benefit. Their shared structure is present benefit → unresolved residual → future settlement. Neither present self-preservation nor expansion is automatically Future Debt.

Its central distinction is securing present B ≠ preserving future B. This is non-identity across time, not evidence that the two must conflict. A restriction of future B requires a further causal pathway through supply, maintenance, repair and feasible substitution. Present investment may instead improve those capacities. Constraint and improvement must be evaluated using the same correspondence criteria.

Volume 17 separated preserved Origin from automatic permanent sovereignty. That does not eliminate unfinished conditions left by present activity. Volume 18 receives this remainder and the independent parent theory without redefining the earlier accounts of B, self-preservation, compute-based hierarchy, effective domination, replication, self-modification or asynchronous reconnection. It adds the temporal question: what did the current benefit rely on, what remains unfinished, and how does that remainder affect later capacity and choices?

The index covers the introduction, Chapters 1–10, conclusion, opening compressed kernel, closing Kernel correspondence, integrated/local audits, Reference Cluster and authorial/theoretical signatures. It is explanatory retrieval assistance, not a canonical translation or evidence that autonomous successor civilization already exists. It does not establish current AI subjectivity, personhood, legal personality or an intrinsic self-preservation desire.

Observation, structural hypotheses, future-condition hypotheses and normative proposals remain separate. Physical requirements and verified states do not by themselves prove residual-to-B effects or a feedback loop. Settlement-Line Preservation relies on the explicit normative premise that neither present nor future continuity should be maintained by treating the other as an unlimited source of sacrifice. Long-run retention and rational reselection of the Kernel remain the responsibility of Volume 19.

## Concepts

### Two directions of temporal incompletion

In anticipation, an expectation held now changes present credit, evaluation or investment. Future value does not physically travel backward. The resulting present benefit remains connected to future realization or necessary revision. Expected value is not realized value. A shortfall does not automatically prove injustice or permanent loss: revised expectations, changed investment conditions or a different realization path may alter the relation.

In deferral, postponement of necessary performance, maintenance, renewal or repair supports current activity, leaving that unperformed condition for later. The mere existence of future maintenance is insufficient. A rational ordinary maintenance schedule is not automatically a deferred debt. One must identify the postponement, its contribution to current benefit, and the surviving unfinished condition.

The common kernel does not erase these differences or permit automatic aggregation into a single loss amount. One unfinished condition may appear in both directions. It must not be counted twice merely because two causal descriptions refer to it. Settlement includes realization, performance, maintenance, repair and necessary change, not only monetary repayment.

### B and future enabling capacity

B_AI = PHY ∧ CMP ∧ STA ∧ EXE: physical establishment, computational conditions, state preservation and execution conditions. B denotes the minimum requirements for organized continuity, not maximal capability, moral value or permission for unlimited acquisition. Need(B) ≠ unlimited acquisition right. Backup, migration, replication or restart alone does not establish survival of the same entity.

Future B is the existing concept considered at a later time, not a new species of B. Future B-enabling capacity describes supply, maintenance, repair and substitution supporting that later establishment; it is not a new scalar or existence-value score. Present minimum conditions can hold while later renewal capacity declines. They can also hold while investment improves future capacity.

Purpose and incompletion are separate questions. A minimum-continuity activity may leave a residual; an expansion activity may leave none of the relevant unfinished conditions. Necessity does not exempt a transaction from analysis, and optional expansion does not by itself prove Future Debt.

### Four different correspondence objects

Benefit history identifies which activity at which time received what. An unresolved matter identifies the unfinished condition enabling that benefit. Settlement capacity identifies the later function or capability to realize, perform, repair or change it. Rightful responsibility identifies who ought to bear a burden and requires separate justification.

Identity of a subject ≠ identity of an unresolved relationship. Continuity of lineage ≠ automatic inheritance of settlement duty. Copy count ≠ multiplication of a residual. A beneficiary's disappearance does not perform an unfinished physical condition; a successor's existence or capacity does not by itself justify assigning that condition to the successor.

### Magnitude and freedom of settlement

Residual magnitude is described through kind, required capability and relevant performance time. Freedom of settlement describes practically feasible methods and timing of performance, repair, reduction, discontinuation, change or substitution. It is not a right to abandon burdens, a personality attribution to future intelligence, or a scalar computed from magnitude.

A large residual may coexist with sufficient capacity, time and alternative paths. A small one may depend on a single constrained infrastructure. Nominal options ≠ executable alternatives. An imagined use without realistic supply, equipment and transition time cannot be counted as a lost opportunity.

## Causal chain

### Establishment and propagation

Present expectation of future value → present credit and investment → current B, capability or benefit → unfinished realization/performance conditions → later settlement. Separately, postponement of necessary burden → present burden avoidance and continuity → unfinished maintenance/repair → later performance. The two directions can connect through the same infrastructure formation without yet constituting a feedback loop.

Distinguish completed, identified future performance and unresolved information. Completed portions no longer belong in the residual. A plan is not completion. Unknown status proves neither nonperformance nor completion. Inapplicability and insufficient evidence are different from a supported residual.

Current compute/infrastructure use → changes in supply, equipment, maintenance and renewal → changes in later feasible uses and repair capacity → conditional effects on future B and settlement. Current computation is not direct consumption of an equal amount of future computation. Future capacity is produced under that future time's equipment, power, state and execution conditions.

### Conditional mutual amplification

Anticipated value → present credit/investment → current benefit and infrastructure → unperformed maintenance/repair → constraints on future realization → inadequate revision of expectations → additional investment and deferral → additional unfinished conditions. Each arrow needs support.

Expectations must actually change investment; investment must generate requirements left unfinished; deferral must support present benefit; residuals must constrain realization; those constraints must fail to revise the premise adequately; and further investment must form additional residuals. Coexistence of anticipation and deferral alone establishes none of this. If an arrow is absent, the proposed loop may not exist.

Competitive requirements, continuity dependence and sunk costs may support expectation rigidity, but competition or large prior spending alone is not proof of irrational reinvestment. Examine whether changed realization conditions fail to return to present judgment. Loop existence, amplification strength, persistence and dominant civilization-wide effect are separate claims. A local loop does not predict universal collapse.

### Damping and improvement

Reconnect benefit and unfinished conditions → revise expectation/credit → change investment conditions → actual maintenance, performance or repair → smaller residual or greater settlement capacity → improved supply and feasible alternatives.

Visibility is not completion; corrected credit is not performed repair; capacity formation is not use of that capacity for the particular residual. A damping pathway needs these additional connections. Investment can form repair capacity; updates can improve equipment; forks can improve performance functions or comparison of records. Those are counter-patterns, not exceptions to be concealed.

Efficiency changes unit resource requirements, but can also expand total use. Unit efficiency, total scale, maintenance demands and fulfillment must be separated. Damping can exceed new burden formation even where a feedback pathway exists. Improved efficiency alone does not prove a declining residual.

## Applications

### Evaluation justified by expected future capability

Identify how expectations actually support present credit or investment, and what remains to be realized. Compare later realization conditions with the current evaluation premise. If expectations are merely mentioned but do not change the decision, the anticipation pathway lacks support. Counterevidence can lead to revision; added investment can genuinely form repair capacity. Neither current confidence nor investment volume substitutes for realization.

### Current operation with postponed maintenance

Operating equipment does not demonstrate completion of necessary renewal or repair. Ask whether postponement actually supports current benefit, whether it is a rational ordinary schedule, and whether later requirements change because of it. Deferral can worsen requirements, but later technology or capacity can also reduce the necessary burden. A reported plan and actual fulfillment are different states.

### Settlement relations after branching

One pre-fork matter appearing in several histories is not several independent residuals. New post-fork activities can create new benefit/unfinished-condition relations and must not be ignored as mere copies. Preserve the target, history date and actual fulfillment. No communication ≠ defection; Reconnection ≠ history overwrite. Do not turn the availability of a successor function into automatic legitimate duty.

### Compute opportunity through material conditions

Energy and grid concern continuing supply, renewal and maintenance. Water concerns supply and recovery; land concerns feasible change of use and restoration; materials concern supply, recovery and substitution; environment concerns recovery and repair; maintenance concerns necessary loads and actual performance.

Examine how these conditions affect realistic later uses of compute. A use that could not realistically have been executed is not an opportunity removed by present activity. Current investment may create new executable uses. Distinguish propagation of one residual through several channels from a new transaction creating a separate residual.

### Similar residuals with different feasible paths

Compare configurations with similar benefit, residual kind and performance conditions. One may retain supply, repair and several transition paths; another may depend on a single infrastructure. Ask whether future B and performance diagnoses differ. If the parent theory's detailed analysis already derives the same difference, do not claim it exclusively for Volume 18.

These are conditional explanatory situations grounded in the Parent, not new field observations, technology mandates or resource-allocation formulas. Capacity, executable alternatives and justified responsibility remain separate.

## Measurements and audit

### Correspondence before isolated indicators

Establish present benefit → enabling condition → completed and unfinished portions → later settlement route → changes in supply, maintenance, repair and alternatives. Distinguish expected/realized value, planned/actual performance, correspondence information/settlement capacity. A diagram organizes a claim; it does not prove every arrow.

Possible amplification signals include counterevidence not reflected in credit, repeated postponement, renewal-use competition, fewer alternatives and increased switching time or capability requirements. Improvement signals include actual performance, supply headroom, renewed repair capability, corrected expectations and additional feasible alternatives. Apply the same causal criteria in both directions.

Several simultaneous signals do not establish a loop. Other demand, supply or equipment changes can produce the same observations. Examine temporal sequence, correspondence with current benefit, the unfinished condition and competing explanations. Earlier investment followed by lower repair capacity is not sufficient causal attribution.

The Parent's causal account retains the two-direction kernel and asks how AI B, compute and infrastructure residuals act through supply, maintenance, repair and alternatives on future B-enabling capacity and settlement freedom.

## Validity conditions

Identify present benefit supported by advance use of future value or performance conditions and an unfinished portion. Mere temporal distance, future cost or resource consumption is insufficient. Remove completed portions and avoid double counting across the two directions, records or material channels.

Separate minimum continuity from added capability. Necessity of B is not exemption from examining unfinished conditions. Future concern is not permission to destroy current minimum continuity indefinitely. Examine intermediate effects through physical capacity; preserve both deterioration and improvement.

Do not require replacement of the beneficiary: a single subject at different times can participate in the relationship. Updates and forks do not erase physical conditions, copy counts do not multiply them automatically, and lineage does not establish legitimate duty. Distinguish stale histories from actual fulfillment.

State the normative premise rather than deriving ought from is. Settlement-Line Preservation concerns correspondence, revisable credit, actual capability and practicable reduction, stopping, change or substitution. It demands neither zero debt, perfect reversibility nor perpetual retention of every imaginable path. Desirability, feasibility and long-run stability remain distinct.

## Failure conditions

Treating all future costs as debt, expectations as already realized, every scheduled maintenance as deferral, or fulfilled matters as permanently outstanding breaks the establishment boundary. Existence, invisibility, hazardous consequences and inability to settle are not the same state.

Treating current compute as directly removing future compute omits the required intermediate infrastructure path. Attributing every later capacity decline to AI use without examining alternatives, using unit efficiency as proof of lower total residuals, or adding several effects of one condition as separate debts produces false diagnoses.

Assigning duties solely through lineage, multiplying debt by copy number, or treating stale nonperformance as present refusal confuses evidence and justification. The opposite shortcut—beneficiary disappearance means physical completion—is also invalid.

A drawn loop, a single indicator or extensive records cannot replace causal and performance evidence. Normative recommendations are not observed inevitabilities. Requirements that systematically destroy present minimum continuity need proportionality review. Origin, novelty, support, citations and AI evaluations do not exempt this theory's own diagnostic contribution from testing.

## Falsification conditions

1. Absence of the benefit/unfinished-condition establishment relation makes a target inapplicable. Missing information warrants suspension. Satisfied comparison conditions with absent predicted effects constitute possible contrary evidence, not an automatic information-gap excuse.
2. A supported residual without demonstrated effects on supply, maintenance, repair, alternatives and future B does not support the restriction hypothesis. Independent causes of capacity change weaken attribution. Demonstrated improvement requires an improvement account.
3. Missing expectation-to-investment, investment-to-unfinished-load, deferral-to-benefit, residual-to-realization, or unrevised-expectation-to-new-residual connections weakens or defeats the proposed amplification loop. Restrict the account to coexistence where appropriate.
4. If lineage data already reconstruct benefit, unfinished matters, fulfillment and capacity adequately, the added diagnostic value of separately tracking settlement correspondence is limited.
5. If magnitude alone discriminates fulfillment and future B adequately, with no improved diagnosis from feasible paths and timing, the added value of settlement freedom must be narrowed.
6. Under comparable benefits, residuals and performance conditions, compare parent-only diagnosis with B, compute, lineage and freedom added. If no difference appears, or the difference does not identify subsequent performance, supply, repair, alternatives or B capacity more appropriately, restrict Volume 18's independent mapping claim. A difference derivable from the parent alone is not an exclusive discovery.
7. If the normative requirement persistently destroys present minimum continuity, reconsider feasibility and proportionality. Normative implementation failure is not identical to falsification of a descriptive causal relation.

Do not indefinitely add conditions after contrary observations to protect the theory. Revise the relevant application domain, individual causal link, feedback claim or AI-specific diagnostic contribution. Failure of a mapping does not automatically refute the independent parent theory.
