## 15-Second Explanation

Even if AI is operating now and can increase its capabilities, that does not mean it has secured the electricity, facilities, maintenance, repair, and alternatives needed at the next point in time. The Parent Original examines as Future Debt the relationship in which conditions supporting present benefits remain incomplete into the future. However, not all future costs are debt, and the existence of debt alone does not make something unjust or impossible to settle. The central concern is preserving a “settlement line” connecting present and future.

## Why It Is Needed

### Present success and future conditions of viability differ

Part Seventeen argued that preserving an origin does not automatically transfer permanent governing authority. Even so, incomplete conditions left by present activity for the future do not disappear. Part Eighteen examines this other direction of time.

B is the minimum organizational condition for continuity. It concerns computation, physical infrastructure, state retention, and execution conditions, not maximum capability or personality value. Securing present B and preserving conditions that can maintain, repair, or replace that B in the future are separate. That does not mean the two must always conflict. Present investment can sometimes build supply or repair capacity.

### Future Debt has two directions

One direction is that expectations of future value not yet realized support present credit and investment. The expectation changes present decisions; future value is not physically transferred into the past.

The other direction is that necessary maintenance and repair are postponed now, and avoiding that burden supports present activity. A future maintenance plan alone does not qualify. The corresponding conditions are that postponement supports a present benefit and leaves an untreated condition. The common core of both directions is “present benefit → unsettled remainder → future settlement.”

### Having a burden and losing the ability to choose are different

Even with a large incomplete condition, supply and repair capacity and multiple routes may remain. Even with a small incomplete condition, difficulty may arise if it can be handled through only one infrastructure and there is not enough switching time. The Parent Original separates burden size from settlement freedom.

This freedom is not a right to abandon obligations at will. It describes how much practical method and time remain for fulfillment, repair, change, reduction, suspension, and substitution.

## Practical Process

**1. Separate what was obtained now.** Distinguish minimum continuity B, capability expansion, credit supported by future expectations, and cost avoidance through postponement. Using the same facility does not make necessity and purpose identical.

**2. Return to the conditions that supported the benefit.** Distinguish what has been fulfilled, what will be needed in the future, and what is unknown. A plan is not completion; uncertainty does not mean either zero debt or total debt.

**3. Examine intermediate routes to future capacity.** Merely computing now does not mean future computation was directly taken away. Check how changes in electricity, facilities, maintenance, renewal, supply, and repair connect to executable future uses.

**4. Separate history, capacity, and responsibility.** What remains from which benefit, who can process it, and who ought legitimately to bear it are separate questions. The same lineage does not automatically inherit an obligation. The number of copies does not by itself double the remainder.

**5. Examine deterioration and improvement by the same standard.** A loop in which counterevidence does not return to the expectation and additional investment and postponement repeat is a hypothesis. Also check the opposite direction, in which fulfillment, credit revision, supply improvement, and broader alternatives advance. Efficiency improvement alone does not guarantee a lower total burden.

**6. Confirm an executable settlement route.** Look for remaining necessary supply, facilities, and transition time, not a substitute in name only. Distinguish increased records, existing capability, and actual fulfillment.

The above is an order for checking the Parent Original, not a fixed audit protocol, allocation formula, or assignment of legal responsibility.

## Applications

### “It will be more capable in the future”

Even if an expectation makes present investment possible, that capability has not yet been realized. Ask whether the expectation is corrected when counterevidence appears in the realization conditions. If additional investment truly cultivated repair capacity, recognize the improvement.

### “The facility is operating now”

Operation does not prove that necessary maintenance and renewal are complete. Conversely, avoid turning an appropriate future maintenance plan into postponement debt. The entry point is whether an unfulfilled condition supported current operation.

### “The previous burden disappeared because it branched”

Updating a subject and fulfilling physical conditions are separate. Untreated conditions may remain. But the existence of a successor does not automatically establish that it bears the obligation. Separate preservation of history from legitimate assignment of burden.

### “The record still says it is unfulfilled”

It may be old asynchronous information. Communication loss does not prove refusal, and reconnection is not a reason to erase the past. Compare the time of the record with actual present fulfillment. Do not turn an already completed condition into debt again.

### “It is safe because the burden is small”

Even a small burden can leave little method or time if dependence on a single infrastructure is strong. Conversely, a large burden can leave capacity and routes. Do not infer impossibility of settlement or complete irreversibility from size alone.

These are explanatory situations, not reports of an actual AI civilization or new research findings.

## Success Criteria

It must be possible to compare benefits, incomplete conditions, fulfillment status, and future supply, repair, and substitution within the same causal relationship. Exclude what has already been fulfilled; assess improvement as well as signs of deterioration; and separately check whether correcting expectations led to actual fulfillment.

Compare a diagnosis using only the Parent theory with one adding B, compute, lineage, and freedom, and test whether the added distinctions better explain fulfillability or differences in future B. More words alone do not count as success. If there is no difference, reduce the claimed additional explanatory power of this Part.

## Limitations

This does not assert present AI personality, subjectivity, or self-preservation desire, or the emergence of an independent successor civilization. It does not prove Future Debt or exploitation from future burden, a single sign, or mere temporal order. When information is missing, suspend judgment; when the expected effect does not appear despite the conditions being met, treat that as a candidate falsification.

Preserving the settlement line is an explicit norm against making present and future continuity conditions one-sided sacrifices. It does not automatically generate obligations from causality, nor demand zero debt everywhere, complete reversibility, or permanent preservation of every alternative. If such demands continually damage present minimum conditions, revisit proportionality and feasibility.

## Misreading Prevention

Present B is not a guarantee of future B, but neither is it inevitable future destruction. Not every future cost is debt. The existence, invisibility, danger, and un-settleability of debt are different. Visibility is not settlement. Lineage is not proof of responsibility. Efficiency gains do not guarantee a reduction in total remainder.
