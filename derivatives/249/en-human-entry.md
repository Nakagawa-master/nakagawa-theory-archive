## 15-Second Explanation
Part I addresses how to reverse a condition in which social success is separated from contribution and value absorption or externalization is rewarded more strongly. Its design direction is expressed as `S=C`, meaning “reconnect success S to contribution C.” This does not mean scoring everyone by contribution points. The center is building a Civilization Kernel that records contribution as causality, connects it where needed, returns it to value circulation, and prevents monopolization by evaluators, enclosure, surveillance, and conversion into a single score.

## Why It Is Needed
In society, the actor creating value and the actor receiving reward, authority, or ownership from it are not always the same. If only short-term profit remains as success, burdens sent into the future and repair costs in the field become invisible. Repetition then biases learning about “what produces success” toward the position of value acquisition rather than contribution itself.

Yet simply introducing “contribution points” does not solve it. The actor with the right to define points may become a new power; participants may game evaluation; care and exploration that are not evaluated may disappear; and the institution may become impossible to leave. The Parent Original therefore treats changing the value function and preventing reversal as one matter.

The Civilization Kernel includes not only what counts as contribution, but who observes, which history is preserved, who can object, whether a connection can be removed, and whether rules can be revised. It is necessary to bring success and contribution closer while making the institution itself re-auditable.

## Practical Process
### 1. Confirm the substance of success
Confirm what the organization or institution treats as “success.” Look not only at surface indicators such as sales, growth rate, volume, and participation, but also at actions that actually lead to promotion, budgets, authority, and continued investment.

### 2. Separate contribution and value acquisition
Separate who created value from who acquired it. Restore and read the causality necessary for establishment: credited name, actual work, coordination, verification, care, and repair of failure.

### 3. Record contribution causally
Record “what was acted upon and what changed,” not personality or popularity. Do not conduct more individual surveillance than necessary; retain the minimum provenance that can return to grounds later.

### 4. Design connection destinations
Even when contribution is recorded, circulation does not occur unless it connects to evaluation, resources, knowledge, or the next project. Make clear where it should connect for value to be reused.

### 5. Confirm return flow
Check whether rewards, authority, reinvestment, and learning opportunities actually returned to the contribution side. Confirm that substantive value has not concentrated elsewhere under the guise of honor.

### 6. Place reversal prevention
Audit single-score conversion, concentration of authority in a central evaluator, inability to exit, blocking objections, and ex post rule changes. Also examine whether the new institution rewards the same behavior as the old one under another name.

### 7. Enable re-agreement and reconnection
Do not permanently fix participation conditions or evaluation methods. Keep withdrawal, departure, reconnection through another route, re-agreement, and institutional revision actually usable.

## Examples of Application
**Internal improvement.** Track not the number of people who proposed improvements, but which friction decreased, whose time recovered, and to whom the result was attributed. If managers alone acquire the result, correct value circulation.

**Research and creative work.** Do not close results under the author’s name alone; preserve different causal contributions such as research, design, review, editing, verification, and translation. This does not require converting every contribution into one score.

**AI collaboration.** Do not equate AI output with contribution. Separate human questions, verification, and final judgment from AI exploration, organization, and draft generation. Make responsibility and origin traceable.

**Community.** Observe multiple contributions needed for a community to function, including consensus-building, knowledge preservation, repair, and connection generation, rather than speaking frequency or popularity.

## Success Test
- Grounds for contribution can be revisited.
- Divergence between value creator and value acquirer can be explained and corrected.
- One score does not dominate every judgment.
- Objection, withdrawal, departure, and reconnection are actually usable.
- Rule changes and agreement history are traceable.
- Externalized burdens return to success evaluation.
- The new institution does not reproduce the old value absorption under another name.
- The Kernel itself can be re-audited and corrected.

Success is not a state in which everyone participates in the institution. If high participation arises from inability to leave or conformity pressure, it is failure. What matters is that the connection between contribution and success improves while agency and institutional reversibility remain intact.

## Limitations
`S=C` is not a model that expresses every value in real society through one formula. How to handle values that rarely appear as short-term results—care, exploration, rest, safety, and preserving future options—requires consideration in each context.

Also, because Part I handles the Civilization Kernel, it does not settle the details of accounting, allocation, justice, or relief by itself. Fixing in advance issues handed to Part II or connection-value accounting confuses upstream Kernel responsibilities with downstream institutional responsibilities.

Contribution records also have limits. Causal contribution cannot always be measured completely, and some contributions cannot be observed. Records therefore must not become absolute evaluations; they must remain open to objection and revision.

## Misreading Prevention
This theory does not say “people who are not useful have no value.” Contribution C is not personal worth; it is a concept for auditing what social success is connected to in a particular context.

Nor does “making contribution visible” mean total surveillance. If collecting more information than necessary creates concentration of power, it contradicts reversal prevention. “Connection” is not enclosure either. Without free exit and reconnection, the conditions of a connection-reward society are not met.

The minimum core is **to reconnect success to contribution and audit the connecting apparatus itself so that it does not reverse into new value absorption.**
