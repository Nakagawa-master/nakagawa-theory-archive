## Summary
The Parent begins from a structural problem exposed by the Dark Equation: social success can become detached from contribution and instead reward extraction, value capture, externalization, and control over points of appropriation. Part I asks what must exist upstream if a civilization is to redirect recognized success toward contribution. It compresses that design direction as `S=C`. This is not presented as a universal empirical equality and not as a command to assign one contribution score to every person. It means that the causal reasons for success should be reconnectable to contribution rather than systematically severed from it.

The central object is a civilization Kernel. A Kernel is upstream of a particular compensation scheme. It governs how contribution is observed, how causal provenance is retained, how generated value is connected to relevant people, resources, decisions, and future work, and how value returns through reward, authority, reinvestment, learning, or other context-appropriate channels. The Kernel must also preserve origin, agreement, change history, objection, exit, reconnection, and revision. Without those anti-inversion properties, a system created to reward contribution can become a new extraction machine.

The anti-inversion requirement is therefore not an optional safety appendix. It is part of the positive design itself. If one actor monopolizes the definition of contribution, if one score governs every decision, if records become surveillance, if connection becomes lock-in, or if exit is technically available but economically or socially impossible, then the new system can reproduce the same structural pattern that it intended to replace.

## Concepts
- **Civilization Kernel**: an upstream structure that determines what counts as success, how causal value is retained, how it is connected, and how it can be audited and revised.
- **S=C**: a design direction that reconnects success S to contribution C. It is not a universal measured equation and not a one-dimensional ranking formula.
- **Contribution trace**: a record of what actor affected what object, under which context, and what change followed, while retaining uncertainty where causal attribution is incomplete.
- **Value creation / value capture separation**: an analytical distinction between producing value and occupying the position from which value is appropriated.
- **Connection**: linking generated value to relevant people, resources, institutions, knowledge, or decisions so that value does not remain isolated.
- **Value return**: the path by which connected contribution returns as compensation, authority, reinvestment, learning opportunity, reuse, or another appropriate form.
- **Origin and agreement memory**: the ability to trace who created, modified, agreed to, or rejected a structure and under what conditions.
- **Distributed observation**: preventing evaluation, logging, allocation, and audit from becoming one opaque operation controlled by a single actor.
- **Reversibility**: real capacity for withdrawal, revocation, renegotiation, and reconnection.
- **Anti-inversion**: mechanisms that detect whether a contribution-oriented system is becoming a hierarchy, surveillance system, lock-in mechanism, or extraction structure.
- **Externality reconnection**: returning displaced costs and affected-party information to the success evaluation that produced them.
- **Gaming**: behavior optimized for a fixed metric rather than the underlying contribution. Gaming is treated partly as a design signal rather than only as personal misconduct.

## Causal chain
```text
Dark-Equation-like success structure
→ identify the gap between contribution and recognized success
→ separate value creation from value capture
→ place S=C as a civilizational design direction
→ record contribution as causal provenance, not personality ranking
→ connect recorded contribution to relevant nodes and resources
→ return value through compensation, authority, reinvestment, and learning
→ preserve origin, consent, and change history
→ distribute observation and keep objection/re-audit available
→ preserve exit, revocation, renegotiation, and reconnection
→ audit concentration of authority, metric gaming, and surveillance drift
→ block inversion into extraction and lock-in
→ hand downstream accounting, justice, and operational-loop details to Part II
```

The chain matters because merely defining a better value function does not implement it. Each bridge can fail independently. Contribution can be recorded but never rewarded. Reward can exist but be allocated by one unaccountable authority. Exit can exist in policy but not in practice. A valid implementation must therefore inspect the complete chain rather than declare success from the presence of a new label or score.

## Applications
1. **Organizational improvement.** Trace which interventions reduced friction, restored time, preserved knowledge, or enabled a process, rather than rewarding only the manager who owns the final metric. Return value to causal contributors while keeping attribution contestable.
2. **Research and creative work.** Preserve heterogeneous roles such as investigation, design, criticism, editing, verification, translation, and maintenance. Do not force all roles into one comparable score when the causal functions differ.
3. **Human-AI collaboration.** Separate human questions, verification, judgment, and responsibility from AI exploration, synthesis, and drafting. Keep origin and decision provenance so that AI use does not erase accountability.
4. **Communities.** Observe contribution to coordination, repair, knowledge preservation, and connection generation rather than equating activity counts or popularity with contribution.
5. **Institutional design.** Evaluate not only participation but also exit friction, objection, value return, authority concentration, and externalized burdens. High participation caused by lock-in is not evidence of S=C.
6. **Compensation and reinvestment.** Examine whether value created in one layer is systematically captured in another and whether reinvestment reaches the conditions that generated the value.
7. **Rule revision.** Preserve the reasons, scope, and consent history of rule changes so that revision remains legitimate rather than becoming retroactive manipulation.

## Measurements and audit
Audit the distance between value creators and recipients of reward, authority, ownership, or reinvestment. Check whether third parties can return to the evidence behind contribution attribution. Measure whether a single metric effectively dominates all decisions even when multiple indicators nominally exist. Inspect concentration of evaluation, audit, and allocation authority. Confirm whether objection can actually change a record or rule rather than merely register dissatisfaction.

Reversibility must be tested as practice, not policy text. An exit path that destroys accumulated rights, identity, or access may be formally present but structurally unusable. Reconnection should not depend on discretionary forgiveness by the same authority that controls evaluation. Externalized losses should be reattached to the success record so that short-term gains cannot remain clean by transferring repair costs to other people or future periods.

Also inspect data proportionality. Provenance needed for causal accountability must not silently expand into continuous behavioral surveillance. Finally, compare what behavior is rewarded before and after reform. If the same extractive behavior remains advantageous under new terminology, the Kernel has not changed the causal success function.

## Validity conditions
The interpretation is valid only if `S=C` remains a design direction rather than a universal measured equality or social ranking formula. Contribution must be attributable to causal changes with uncertainty preserved where evidence is incomplete. Observation, evaluation, connection, value return, and audit must be mutually inspectable instead of collapsed into one opaque authority. Origin, agreement, and modification history must remain accessible. Objection, revocation, exit, reconnection, and revision must be practically usable. Externalized losses must not disappear from the success record. The Kernel itself must remain auditable and revisable.

A further validity condition is that non-market or non-output values are not erased. Care, maintenance, exploration, protection, rest, and preservation of future options may not appear as immediate output but can be structurally important. The Parent does not license a society in which only visible production receives moral standing.

## Failure conditions
Failure occurs when contribution becomes a permanent score attached to personal worth; when the evaluator becomes an unchallengeable authority; when provenance expands into surveillance; when connection becomes lock-in; when withdrawal is formally allowed but practically punitive; when agreement history disappears; when rules are changed retroactively; when anti-inversion becomes a justification for suppressing dissent; or when the system keeps rewarding value capture while describing the reward as contribution.

It also fails if the equation is implemented as a single optimization target. A system optimized only for a quantified C can generate Goodhart-like behavior, discard unmeasured values, and reward those best able to manipulate the measurement interface. Anti-inversion therefore requires continuous observation of the measurement system itself.

## Falsification conditions
The causal hypothesis must be revised if stronger contribution provenance and value-return mechanisms do not reduce persistent separation between value creation and value capture. If distributed observation does not reduce concentration of authority, the independence and practical powers of the observation points must be reconsidered. If reversibility exists but participants remain unable to leave without disproportionate loss, the design has failed at the practical layer.

The theory should also yield to simpler alternatives when they produce equal or better value return, autonomy, and auditability with less data collection and institutional complexity. Anti-inversion is not a reason to preserve a complex mechanism for its own sake. Evidence that a different arrangement reconnects success and contribution more effectively is a reason for revision.

## Required distinctions
- record ≠ surveillance
- connection ≠ lock-in
- contribution evaluation ≠ evaluation of human worth
- `S=C` ≠ one-dimensional social scoring
- value return ≠ monetization of all value
- distributed observation ≠ disappearance of responsibility
- reversibility ≠ absence of responsibility
- anti-inversion ≠ exclusion of opponents
- origin preservation ≠ unconditional inheritance of old institutions
- Part I Kernel ≠ completed Part II institutional machinery

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reinterpret this Parent as a meritocratic ranking theory. C is contextual causal contribution, not a measure of a person's intrinsic value. Do not infer that every activity must be measured, logged, or monetized. The minimum sufficient provenance for audit is preferred over total observation. Do not claim that high adoption proves the new system is valid; adoption can be produced by dependency or lock-in. Do not pre-commit the details of accounting, allocation, justice, and remedy that the Parent intentionally hands to subsequent work.

When applying the theory to AI, avoid transferring final normative authority to the model that performs the measurement. AI can help organize evidence, but the right to object, inspect, revise, and leave must remain explicit. The point is not to replace old authority with algorithmic authority.
