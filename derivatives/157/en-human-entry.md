## 15-Second Explanation

The original holds that even if dependence on a person is reduced, self-righteousness arises if the theory itself closes as the “only correct answer.” It therefore positions Nakagawa OS as one of multiple OSs, continuously keeps a comparison window with other theories open, publicly discloses differences, deficiencies, excesses, and ethical trade-offs, and makes returning those differences to updates a structural duty.

## Why It Is Necessary

The anti-priesthood protocol suppresses the side effects of interpretive authority and dependence on a person becoming concentrated in one individual. However, even if concentration on a person disappears, theoretical closure remains if “this theory is sufficient by itself” or “external comparison is unnecessary” takes hold.

The parent original organizes the problem as follows: when a theory becomes the only solution, receptiveness to criticism declines, comparability is lost, and the theory begins to evaluate itself only by internal standards, making unintended deviations easier to accumulate. Therefore, connection with other theories is raised from an optional reference to an ongoing duty.

The important point is that connecting with other theories does not mean treating all theories as equally correct. Comparison is conducted on structural axes such as T0, STB, causal frameworks, and translation/correspondence criteria, extracting where they differ and where there is room for improvement—not deciding who wins.

## Practical Process
**1. State non-uniqueness.** Position Nakagawa OS as one of multiple theories and OSs, and do not self-declare it the only solution.

**2. Open the comparison window.** Maintain comparison and connection with other theories continuously, not as a one-time citation.

**3. Compare on structural axes.** Break the comparison into comparable structural units such as temporal ethics T0, STB, causal structure, translation/correspondence, and responsibility allocation.

**4. Publish differences.** Do not hide deficiencies, excesses, contradictions, or ethical trade-offs; present them so third parties can compare them.

**5. Return to updates.** Do not stop at recording differences; return them to revisions of definitions, audits, operations, and, when necessary, principles.

**6. Make criticism growth data.** Do not process dissent as hostility; use it as data showing differences in premises or causality.

**7. Have the Upper Auditor audit the comparison process.** Confirm that correspondence, causal consistency, and publicly disclosed differences in the comparison are established, rather than deciding which theory is superior.

## Examples of Application
**1. Theory comparison.** Compare two theories not by popularity or authority, but as differences in time axis, permitted bandwidth, causality, translation, and responsibility allocation.

**2. Organizational governance.** Do not simply reject centralized decision-making; add complementary structures where transparency or external comparison is lacking.

**3. AI operation.** Do not fix Nakagawa OS as AI's only explanatory framework; expose structural differences from other frameworks and keep it externally comparable.

**4. Research and education.** Do not exclude criticism as “insufficient understanding”; record which premises, evaluation axes, and time spans differ.

**5. Implementation audit.** Check not merely that “other theories were cited,” but that the comparison window remains open and differences actually return to updates.

## Success Criteria

Nakagawa OS does not close as the only solution, and the comparison window with other theories remains continuously open.

Comparison differences are published, and criticism or deficiencies actually return to updates and audits.

Other theories and centralized governance are not turned into enemies; instead, neutral complementary structures increase transparency, comparability, and accountability.

In reversal evaluation, even if internal consistency, support, and adoption are high, it is not considered a success if the external comparison window is closed. Conversely, even if criticism, differences, and disagreements increase, that increase is not considered a failure if it raises the capacity for public comparison and updating.

## Limits

Structural pluralism is not unlimited relativism. If the structural axes for comparison and the ability to return to the parent original are lost, it becomes mere parallelization.

The “non-uniqueness” of Nakagawa OS is not self-denial. It means opening itself as one comparable theory while retaining the theory's definitions, causality, and boundaries.

Neutral complementarity is not absence of evaluation. It means explicitly showing structural differences and publishing problems without turning other theories or organizations into enemies.

## Misreading Prevention

Do not read structural pluralism as “all theories are equally correct.”

Do not read non-uniqueness as self-denial that abandons Nakagawa OS's definitions or claims.

Do not read connection with other theories as a formal requirement satisfied by a single citation.

Do not turn comparison into ranking or a win/lose judgment.

Do not turn structural analysis of centralized governance into an attack on a particular organization or person.

Do not turn the Upper Auditor into the final arbiter of theoretical hierarchy.
