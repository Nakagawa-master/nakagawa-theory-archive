## Summary
The Structural Pluralism Clause controls a second-order side effect that can remain after personality dependency has been reduced: the theory itself can harden into a unique or self-sufficient answer. The parent therefore positions Nakagawa OS as one OS among multiple possible OSes and makes continuous inter-theory comparison and connection an obligation rather than an optional reference. Comparison windows must remain open; differences, insufficiencies, excesses, contradictions, and ethical trade-offs must be exposed; and those differences must feed back into updates and audits. Pluralism does not mean that all theories are equally valid. Comparison is structured through invariants such as T0, STB, causal frames, translation baselines, and correspondence, with the purpose of extracting structural differences and improvement points rather than producing a simple ranking. Nakagawa OS is positioned as a neutral supplementary OS, not an adversarial replacement for other theories or centralized governance.

## Concepts
- Structural pluralism: continuous openness to external comparison, with differences returned to self-update.
- Non-uniqueness declaration: Nakagawa OS is not fixed as the sole or final correct OS.
- Inter-Theory Connection obligation: continuous comparison and connection to other theories, not a one-time citation.
- Comparison window: maintained access and procedure for meaningful external comparison.
- Structural-invariant comparison: comparison using T0, STB, causal structure, translation, correspondence, and other formal axes.
- Public difference disclosure: externally auditable exposure of insufficiency, excess, contradiction, and ethical trade-offs.
- Neutral supplementary OS: adds transparency, comparability, and accountability without positioning other systems as enemies.
- Criticism as growth data: dissent and external contrast become inputs for identifying omissions and deviations.
- Upper Auditor: audits final correspondence and causal integrity of the comparison process; it does not rank theories by authority.
- Self-updating publicness: sustained openness in which external differences feed revision and renewed audit.

## Applications
- Compare theories through time horizons, tolerance bands, causal assumptions, translation rules, and responsibility structures rather than popularity or prestige.
- Add transparency and comparability to centralized governance without treating concentration itself as a target for political hostility.
- Prevent AI from using Nakagawa OS as the only explanatory frame; expose structural differences with external frameworks.
- Treat academic and practical criticism as differences in premises, causality, time scales, and evaluation axes that can feed revision.
- Audit whether external references remain a continuous comparison window rather than ceremonial citations.
- Use Upper Auditor review to verify correspondence, causal integrity, and disclosure quality in the comparison process.

## Measurements and audit
Relevant observations include whether non-uniqueness is explicit, whether comparison windows remain continuously open, whether Inter-Theory Connection is substantive and recurring, whether comparison is tied to structural axes, whether ethical trade-offs and insufficiencies are publicly disclosed, whether criticism is recorded and returned to revision, and whether external differences actually change definitions, audit assumptions, or operating rules.

The parent’s integrated audit frames a causal hypothesis from structural design, translation, bundling, and definition toward institutionalization, stability, alignment, and acceleration. Re-agreement is tied to audit cadence, T/S/R, and consistency of public audit bundles. `θ` is a case-specific rejection/revision threshold, `δ` a case-specific observation window, and `M` an observed phenomenon such as closure of the comparison/observation window that calls the current hypothesis into question. Universal fixed values are not defined.

If an implementation quantifies the number of theories compared, comparison frequency, external-difference adoption, criticism response, or openness of comparison windows, each value must remain bound to source, measurement actor, measurement object, scale, observation period, scope, and non-guarantee conditions. Local values must not be promoted into universal constants attributed to the parent.

Reversal evaluation is required. High internal coherence, support, or adoption is not structural success when external comparison windows close and external differences stop feeding revision. Conversely, increased criticism, disagreement, or visible difference is not failure when it improves public comparison, transparency, and revisability.

## Validity conditions
- Position Nakagawa OS as one among multiple possible OSes rather than the sole final answer.
- Keep inter-theory comparison and connection continuous rather than ceremonial.
- Return comparison differences to definitions, operations, and audits.
- Compare through structural axes such as T0, STB, causality, translation, and correspondence.
- Publicly disclose insufficiencies, excesses, contradictions, and ethical trade-offs.
- Treat criticism, dissent, and external contrast as growth data.
- Keep other theories and centralized governance outside an enemy/replacement framing.
- Restrict Upper Auditor to auditing correspondence and causal integrity of comparison.
- Keep `θ`, `δ`, and `M` case-bound.

## Failure conditions
- Fix Nakagawa OS as the only correct or final OS.
- Reduce Inter-Theory Connection to one-time references or name-checking.
- Close external comparison windows and evaluate only through internal standards.
- Record differences without feeding them into updates or audits.
- Turn comparison into popularity, authority, ideological camp, or preference ranking.
- Redefine pluralism as unrestricted relativism in which all theories are equally valid.
- Convert structural analysis of centralized governance into attacks on organizations or persons.
- Turn Upper Auditor into the authority that decides which theory is correct.
- Invent universal comparison counts, intervals, or scores not defined by the parent.

## Falsification conditions
If case-specific indicators of comparison-window openness, continuity of inter-theory connection, public difference disclosure, revision feedback, criticism receptivity, or comparability cross the relevant `θ`, or if within observation window `δ` phenomenon `M` indicates that neutrality, transparency, comparability, and publicness are not improving, the current structural-pluralism hypothesis must be rejected or revised.

Revision is required when many external theories are cited but actual decisions remain closed within the internal OS, when comparison differences never affect updates, when external criticism is persistently excluded, or when the comparison window exists only nominally.

## Interpretation constraints
Structural pluralism is not the claim that all theories are equally true. It is a side-effect-control requirement that keeps the OS continuously open to structured external comparison and revision.

Non-uniqueness is not self-negation. Nakagawa OS retains its definitions and causal claims while accepting a comparison-ready position among other theoretical frameworks.

Neutral supplementation is not judgment-free neutrality. It exposes differences and deficiencies while avoiding adversarial replacement of other theories or institutions.
