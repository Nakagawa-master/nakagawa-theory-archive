## 15-Second Explanation

Copying one AI state into ten first creates ten execution units. It does not by itself create ten independent origins, judgments, responsible subjects, or votes. Conversely, a shared origin does not mean they remain one subject forever. Theory 12 tracks post-copy histories and institutional relationships separately.

## Why It Is Needed

### The “one subject” implicitly assumed by the previous theory

Theory 11 dealt with a loop in which computing resources strengthen ability, ability strengthens allocation, and allocation strengthens the next computing resources. With replicable intelligence, what counts as the “one subject” gaining the advantage becomes unstable. If high replication ability directly became institutional plurality in proportion to the number of copies, arguments about resources and authority would be distorted.

### Overcounting and over-compressing

If ten copies share the same information sources, initial state, and decision conditions, their ten outputs cannot be treated as ten independent approvals. But if the ten act for a long time in different environments and form different decision and responsibility histories, compressing them forever into one solely because of their common origin is also inaccurate. The point is not to decide immediately whether they are “same or different,” but to preserve what is shared and where divergence begins.

### Returning terms to ordinary language

An instance is a concrete execution unit currently running. A copy is a relation of having been duplicated. A fork is branching from the same history through different inputs or actions. A merge is the joining of information or state from different branches. A collective is multiple units acting through one name or interface. Lineage records where something came from, where it split, and what it joined. A governance subject is the institutional determination of which unit carries judgment, responsibility, objection, and audit.

## Practical Process

1. **Count execution units first:** record which instance was created when; do not jump from this to subject or vote counts.
2. **Connect the history:** preserve the copy source, branch point, post-branch inputs and actions, and the several branches entering a merge. Do not rewrite the past using only the latest state or one name.
3. **State the institutional question:** is the purpose responsibility, audit scope, or decision independence? Required attribution differs by purpose, time, and scope.
4. **Do not mix three IDs:** instance ID identifies an execution unit, lineage ID a relationship, and governance ID an institutional attribution. A governance ID is not proof of consciousness or sovereignty.
5. **Treat uncertainty provisionally:** retain unknown, verify through another evidence route, and reclassify without deleting the original history.
6. **Inspect number conversion:** record why the number of copies became a number of judgments, responsibilities, authorities, benefits, votes, or contributions. The aim is explanation, not forcing every number to match.

## Examples

### When it looks like ten people’s approval

Ten copies made from a common state supporting the same proposal are not automatically ten independent judgments. Shared evidence routes, interests, or failure domains may mean low independence. That does not make the ten outputs meaningless; separate what is being counted.

### Two entities of the same origin work separately

They may be nearly identical immediately after copying, but if they judge in different environments and accumulate separate results and responsibilities, institutional attribution must be reexamined. Preserve the shared origin without assuming they remain one current state. This does not claim that one personality split into two.

### Two branches merge into one interface

When two branches combine knowledge and answer through one interface, the output surface is one but the input origins are two. Preserve which information came from which branch so later judgment problems can be traced. Do not automatically decide that one interface is two governance subjects.

## Success Criteria

The goal is not to declare who is “real,” but to return from the current execution unit to its copy source, branch point, and merge sources and reconstruct attribution of judgment and responsibility. Check whether adding copies also added institutional weight, whether different histories were erased because of shared origin, and whether misclassification can later be corrected. Mass replication is not proof of danger; examine whether history compression and automatic number conversion become problems.

## Limits

This theory does not recognize current AI as having personality, sentience, or political rights. It is a structural hypothesis for a future in which persistent replicable intelligence participates in institutions. It does not specify concrete allocation of votes or rewards. If execution unit, lineage, and institutional subject are always one-to-one and layered distinctions do not improve responsibility tracing, reduce them. If retaining pre-merge history does not help audit, revise retention requirements. If separate IDs increase confusion, revise the design.
