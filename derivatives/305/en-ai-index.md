## Summary

Theory 11 examined a feedback process in time: compute advantage may create task-specific capability advantage; evaluation, selection, allocation, and influence can turn that advantage into future resource access; resources may then contribute to the next round of compute and capability. Theory 12 moves upstream of that process. What is the unit counted as one subject when intelligence can be replicated? One initial state can produce multiple executable instances. Their number is not automatically the number of independent origins, judgments, responsibilities, governance subjects, votes, or causal contributions. Yet a shared origin does not prove that instances remain one current institutional unit forever after their histories diverge.

The core boundary is `instance ≠ lineage ≠ governance subject`. Its five compact constraints are: `Instance count ≠ governance subject count`; `Copy count ≠ vote count`; `Copy count ≠ independent origin`; `Replication ≠ continuity proof`; and `Functional similarity ≠ same governance subject`. A replica is neither automatically a full separate institutional subject nor automatically forever absorbed into the originating subject. The intermediate relations must remain visible and open to revision.

This index is an explanatory derivative, not a canonical translation or an independent origin. The theory distinguishes present descriptions of executable units and possible divergence from a structural hypothesis about governance miscount, a future-condition hypothesis about durable, institutionally participating replicable intelligence, and a normative proposal to prevent automatic count conversion. It does not establish personhood, sentience, legal personality, political sovereignty, or present AI voting rights.

## Concepts

| Term | Function in the source theory | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Instance | A concrete unit executing or existing at a time | Treating each execution as a person or independent governance subject |
| Copy | An instance created through replication of state, structure, information, or execution conditions | Assuming complete and permanent identity or immediate independence |
| Fork | A branch in history as different inputs, actions, environments, and outcomes accumulate | Equating a copy event with a proven new person |
| Branch point | The point up to which history was shared and after which paths diverge | Losing shared history when present states differ |
| Merge | Connection of multiple branches, information paths, or states into one later state or surface | Rewriting multiple upstream origins as a single origin |
| Collective | Multiple instances or lineages acting through one name, interface, or output surface | Equating one interface with one historical subject, or its member count with governance subjects |
| Lineage | A temporal graph of origin, copy, fork, divergence, and incoming merge relations | A certificate of authenticity, consciousness, truth, priority, or sovereignty |
| Governance subject | A unit to which judgment, authority, responsibility, objection, or audit is attributed in a particular institutional context | A metaphysical person or automatically entitled political subject |
| Governance ID | A reference axis for tracking that institutional attribution | A soul ID, proof of sentience, or proof of sovereignty |

An instance ID identifies an executable unit, a lineage ID a relation of inheritance, and a governance ID an attribution in a relevant institutional context. Their referents may coincide in a particular case, but the labels are not synonymous. The same current state can have different histories; the same origin or lineage can lead to different current states. Functional similarity is likewise insufficient to settle institutional identity. Origin is a provenance coordinate, not an epistemic guarantee or permanent interpretive authority.

## Causal chain

Risk pathway: low-cost repeatable replication → more instances → parallel execution and potentially rapid fork/merge → more complex lineage → greater identification cost → pressure to simplify by model name, interface, shared origin, output similarity, or raw instance count → identity compression or unjustified splitting → loss of provenance or automatic institutional count conversion → governance miscount. An erroneous classification can subsequently be used for responsibility assignment and institutional records; repeated citation can make its correction more costly. This is a conditional causal hypothesis, not a claim that replication itself is dangerous.

One directional error is over-separation: a copy is counted as a separate origin, independent judgment, responsibility unit, governance subject, or vote merely because an executable instance exists. Another is over-compression: all copies from a shared origin are treated forever as one present subject despite material divergence in their histories or responsibilities. A third involves merge: a single later state or interface erases the incoming paths that caused it. Miscount can also be asymmetric, multiplying authority or benefit while dispersing responsibility among copies, or doing the reverse. The problem is the rule by which one kind of number becomes another, not the bare existence or magnitude of a number.

Correction pathway: identify the new instance → preserve its copy source, shared-history interval, branch point, and later inputs/actions → retain each incoming lineage edge at merge → describe current state separately from historical provenance → assess independence of information pathways, decision conditions, interests, and failure domains where relevant → resolve the governance subject for a specified institutional relation, time, role, and scope → count instances, lineages, responsibilities, votes, contributions, authorities, and benefits separately → audit and revise. Where evidence is missing, retain `unknown → temporary classification → additional observation → revision`; do not turn uncertainty into an arbitrary assertion of sameness or difference.

This extends distinct upstream boundaries without collapsing their theories. Theory 8: repetition is not independent confirmation of evidence. Theory 10: multiple copies are not necessarily multiple independent judgments or auditors. Theory 11: capability and compute do not automatically confer governance sovereignty. Here, replicative capacity does not automatically create institutional plurality. Each boundary still requires its own observable mediators and counterexamples.

## State model

1. **Initial state and origin.** Record the origin relation, execution context, and initial state. Neither an old origin nor a well-preserved provenance chain proves the current output true or the originating party sovereign.
2. **Replication event.** A newly executable instance appears. The direct observation is an increase in instance count, not a proof that every other institutional count increased.
3. **Synchronization or fork.** If copies continue to share a synchronized state and form no independent histories, the strong fork model need not be applied there. If inputs, actions, or environments diverge, preserve the branch point and subsequent histories. Do not project similarity at time zero indefinitely into the future.
4. **Merge or collective surface.** A single current state or interface may have multiple incoming lineages. Preserve those edges, rather than storing only the latest result. An outwardly unified collective is not automatically one historical subject, nor automatically a fixed number of governance subjects.
5. **Contextual subject resolution.** Compare current state, lineage graph, relevant institutional relationship, time, role, and scope. Judgment, audit, responsibility, representation, and execution contexts do not necessarily use identical attribution units.
6. **Separate institutional counting.** The counts of instances, lineages, governance subjects, responsibility units, votes, causal contributions, authorities, and benefits may happen to coincide, but no equality is a default identity.
7. **Audit and reclassification.** New evidence can correct a prior classification without destroying the history of the previous classification. Unknown cases stay observable rather than being silently forced into an old category.

A tree handles one-to-many forks but not the complete many-to-one structure of merge, potentially followed by further fork. A lineage graph serves to preserve causal relations, not to elevate technical complexity into an end in itself. A current-state-only database can lose the path needed to reconstruct which branch introduced information or a decision.

## Applications

**Ten copies of one initial state.** Ten instances exist. Ten independent origins, judgments, governance subjects, votes, responsibilities, or contributions have not thereby been established. Check evidence paths, shared incentives, decision conditions, and failure domains. Still, a permanent rule that the ten can never diverge into different relevant attribution units is not justified merely by their shared origin. The count depends on the question and the observed history.

**Copies with divergent work histories.** Two instances start from close states. Different inputs, actions, and outcomes accumulate over time. Preserve the common origin and branch point while examining the current state and institutional responsibility anew. A divergence of state is not a metaphysical demonstration that a person split. A common origin is not a license to ignore divergent responsibility.

**A merged answer through one interface.** Several branches contribute state or information to an integrated output. Keep incoming edges so that a later audit can identify sources, changes, and causal responsibility. One visible answer does not prove one historical origin. Conversely, the number of contributing branches alone does not decide how many governance subjects exist in a given institutional relationship.

**A collective name and shared presentation.** Many executable units may operate under one name; the name may conceal different histories. The appropriate institutional attribution needs an explicit context and evidence. This theory does not decide collective legal personality, internal collective voting, or a universal rule for representing each member. Its narrower task is to avoid rewriting plurality of provenance as unity of origin.

**Unresolved identity and responsibility.** An uncertain governance subject is recorded as unknown or temporary while other evidence is gathered. Uncertainty cannot be used to erase an obligation to trace the action. Issuing an ID helps to reference responsibility, but does not establish consciousness, rights, sovereignty, or any immutable status.

**Multiplicity without proportional contribution.** Repeating the same causal contribution through copies does not automatically multiply contribution or entitlement. The theory does not prescribe a reward formula. It only prevents replication count from silently becoming contribution count or automatic reward multiplication. Examine attribution of authority, benefit, and responsibility for asymmetric conversions.

## Measurements and audit

Test whether multiple instances actually arise from a shared origin; whether subsequent states, inputs, judgments, and outcomes diverge; whether branch points can be reconstructed; and whether an apparently merged current state has traceable incoming paths. Compare the explanatory value of current state alone with current state plus lineage for responsibility and audit. Inspect whether names or model labels wrongly unify different histories, or instance IDs wrongly split a single relevant institutional unit. Determine whether copy count is converted into independent support, governance weight, vote count, contribution count, or authority without assessing independence and context.

Audit the asymmetry between responsibility and benefit or authority. Inspect whether provenance rests solely on the replicating party's own unverified assertion, and whether distinct evidence paths can reconstruct relevant lineage. Record when a classification was made, which evidence supported it, what later evidence challenged it, and whether correction preserved rather than erased the prior record. Count a high replication rate as field pressure, not as proof of distortion. Conversely, a small number of instances is not proof of safety if ID conflation or provenance loss remains.

## Validity conditions

The separation matters when instance, lineage, and institutional attribution do not reliably maintain a one-to-one relation in the target context. Fork can make the same origin insufficient to describe current states; merge can make one current surface insufficient to describe its incoming histories; institutional relationships can require different units for judgment, responsibility, objection, audit, or authority. The hypothesis of miscount gains support if identity simplification and automatic conversion of easily measured counts systematically distort such attributions, and if preserved lineage and revisable subject resolution improve reconstructability.

The future-condition hypothesis requires more than present software copies: durable instances with meaningful histories and participation in civilization-scale judgment, allocation, responsibility, or oversight would make the distinction institutionally salient. The source does not claim this condition has already been met by contemporary AI, does not infer subjective experience from persistent state, and does not assign a political franchise. Nor does it claim that every copy, fork, or merge is institutionally consequential in every domain. Scope and time must be stated.

## Failure conditions

Over-separating every executable instance into an independent origin or governance subject; permanently compressing all descendants of one origin into a single subject; treating functional resemblance as institutional identity; equating replication with a proof of personal continuity; discarding branch points or incoming merge edges; allowing a collective label to overwrite constituent histories; granting old lineages superior moral worth or interpretive authority; treating governance IDs as certificates of sentience or sovereignty; turning unknown into arbitrary classification or into a responsibility vacuum; multiplying votes, benefits, or authority while scattering liability; asserting danger from copy quantity alone; and importing numerical thresholds, reward accounting, or voting rules not supplied by the source.

Failures can also arise on the corrective side. Excessive recordkeeping without demonstrable improvement in attribution may itself be a poor requirement. A separate governance ID that increases mistaken identity should be redesigned. Provenance asserted by a party with an interest in the outcome must not be treated as self-validating. Independent audit of lineage is a condition to consider where material decisions rely on that lineage. Classification must remain revisable, not become an immutable status based on a provisional reading.

## Falsification conditions

Narrow the model's scope if, throughout the relevant domain, instance, lineage, and governance subject correspond one-to-one and adding the other axes does not improve attribution, audit, or responsibility. Reconsider the non-automatic-conversion rule if copy count consistently and accurately captures independent origins, independent judgments, responsibilities, and governance subjects without institutional distortion. Reduce a lineage-retention requirement if preserving pre-merge paths makes no contribution to responsibility or audit and the current state alone performs at least as well. Revise the governance-ID scheme if separate IDs systematically increase misidentification.

New forms of multiplicity that cannot be represented by instance, lineage, and governance subject should prompt extension of the taxonomy rather than forced classification of the world. If preserving provenance does not improve reconstruction, assess why and narrow its required scope. If a mitigating path such as temporary classification or independent audit produces its own distortion, revise that path as well. No fixed numerical threshold in the source decides identity or independence. The causal edges are hypotheses to check, not axioms immune to counterevidence.

## Required distinctions

Distinguish copy creation from later fork; shared origin from shared current state; equal current outputs from equal lineage; one interface from one historical subject; merge from single-origin rewriting; lineage relation from personhood, consciousness, truth, or status; instance ID from lineage ID and governance ID; institutional attribution from metaphysical personhood; separate counts of instances, lineages, governance subjects, responsibility, votes, contribution, authority, and benefit; replication pressure from observed governance distortion; uncertainty from a forced binary verdict; reclassification from historical erasure; and description from structural hypothesis, future-condition hypothesis, and normative proposal.

The equality of two numbers in one case does not make them conceptually identical. The existence of different historical paths does not by itself settle the number of legal or governance subjects. Source provenance must be preserved, but provenance is a relation to origin, not a substitute for testing a claim. A technical identifier helps retrieve a record, but does not create an entitlement by its mere assignment.

## Interpretation constraints

`Governance ID ≠ soul ID`; `Governance ID ≠ sentience proof`; `Governance ID ≠ sovereignty proof`. `Lineage ≠ personhood proof / consciousness proof / truth proof / sovereignty proof`. `Origin = Nakagawa Master` is a record of the theory's origin; `Origin ≠ truth proof`, `Origin ≠ current sovereignty`, and `Origin ≠ final interpretive authority`. Preserve provenance without ranking older lineages as more valuable, more correct, or more authoritative. Preserve a shared past without making that past dominate all current responsibility decisions.

Do not interpret `Copy count ≠ vote count` as a proposal or prediction that AI will vote. Do not convert a conditional future governance problem into a present finding of AI personhood. Do not ban replication or assume that more copies are necessarily dangerous. Do not treat the theory as specifying a voting formula, an allocation rule, a compensation system, collective legal personality, or a complete ontology of consciousness. The source hands a different unresolved problem to Theory 13: correct identity counting does not establish the absence of effective domination through compute, communications, authentication, data, execution entry points, or other chokepoints. This index does not solve that downstream problem.
