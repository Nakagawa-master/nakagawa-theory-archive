## 15-Second Explanation
Structural organizational theory is an approach that does not explain why an organization fails to move merely by saying “the people are weak.” It repairs the organization as a wiring of observation, translation, design, and verification; decision order; role switching; and handoffs. By making clear who does what, where to stop, and to whom to pass work, it creates a state less dependent on a particular coordinator.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
In meetings, fact-checking, proposals, decisions, and criticism often happen at the same time. When that happens, coordination concentrates around people who speak quickly or have more experience, and missing premises, overlapping responsibilities, delayed handoffs, and rework are discovered later.

The parent source treats this not only as a lack of ability, but as a problem of roles, sequence, and switching.

## Four Roles
**Observation.** Gather facts, constraints, and points of unease. Do not rush into solutions here.

**Translation.** Turn observed facts into a map of relationships, causality, roles, and responsibilities.

**Design.** Convert the map into procedures, owners, resources, deadlines, and stop conditions.

**Verification.** Check whether the proposal fits the objective, where to terminate it, and what events should trigger revision.

There is no problem with one person having multiple capabilities. What matters is not mixing multiple functions at the same moment.

## Value → Benefit → Cost
First clarify what is to be protected or improved, then confirm what happens to whom as a result, and finally align implementation, maintenance, and withdrawal costs with it.

This is not an order for hiding costs. It presents unfavorable conditions as well, while preventing cost alone from overwriting the entire objective in advance.

## Silence Slot
When information is saturated, unease is increasing, or decision fatigue appears, pause briefly. The parent source gives 30–90 seconds as an implementation example, not a fixed obligation. A short written confirmation or a fixed speaking order can create the same switching function.

```text
Stop
→ Confirm the immediately preceding role and output
→ Separate what is settled from what is unsettled
→ State the next role and owner
→ Resume
```

## Roles by Hierarchical Level
**Management.** Define the value core, prohibited boundaries, final responsibility, and update conditions.

**Middle management.** Maintain the vocabulary dictionary, handoff rules, and switching log, preserving the wiring between departments.

**Front line.** Confirm the current role and terminal condition before work begins, and share unease as a signal of collapsed premises rather than as personal criticism.

## Connections Between Departments
Aligning terminology alone does not create a connection if objectives, responsibilities, evaluation, and failure costs differ. ST-3 maps relationships and responsibility; ST-4 maps causality; and, where necessary, ST-5 maps evaluation and agreement systems as well.

## Applications
**Sales and development.** Do not pass a customer request only as a feature name; translate it into users, value, causality, constraints, and failure conditions.

**Planning and legal.** Organize the planning objective and legal viability conditions in separate phases, linking them to conditional design rather than simple approval or disapproval.

**R&D.** Separate observation from hypothesis, and set conditions for stopping, continuing, or redesigning before verification.

**AI collaboration.** Treat summarization, structuring, design, and falsification as separate roles; do not make AI output the final decision as-is.

## How to Measure
CPI looks at role deviation, rework, and procedural consistency; HL looks at handoff delay; R-Yield looks at whether one cycle of examination advanced to a useful agreement; and D-Gap looks at the difference between the required depth and the depth actually reached.

These are not concepts for scoring employees. They are observational concepts for testing hypotheses about organizational wiring.

## Inversion Evaluation
Even if handoffs become faster and the agreement rate rises, it is not an improvement if dissent and refusal have merely disappeared. Even if meeting time becomes shorter, it is a failure if necessary evidence or responsibility has vanished. Even if rework decreases, it cannot be called success if errors can no longer be corrected.

## Limitations
In emergencies, there are situations where clear command should take priority. Even then, examine responsibility, grounds, and results afterward.

The four roles do not need to be divided among four people. When objectives cannot be shared, it is sometimes necessary to end without forcing agreement, leaving it as non-agreement.

## Preventing Misreadings
This is not a theory that people or leaders are unnecessary. It is not a way to control people through silence, nor a way to increase meetings. If the indicators are directly tied to personnel evaluation, failures and dissent may be concealed.
