## 1. Summary
Structural Organizational Theory treats an organization as wiring of roles, sequence, switching, handoff, responsibility, and evidence rather than as the sum of talented individuals. Observation, Translation, Design, and Verification are separated as one active role at a time. Value → Benefit → Cost, silence slots, cross-department structural translation, primary logs, termination conditions, and re-agreement make organizational function reproducible and auditable.

## 2. Concepts
- Four roles: Observation, Translation, Design, Verification.
- One role at a time: phase discipline, not permanent job assignment.
- Value → Benefit → Cost: an ordering principle, not price concealment.
- Silence slot: a bounded pause used to close one role and declare the next.
- Handoff architecture: output, responsibility, unresolved items, evidence, and next role.
- ST-0 to ST-5: cross-department structural-translation depth classification.
- CPI / HL / R-Yield / D-Gap: observation concepts for organizational wiring.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
role mixing
→ coordination concentrates in highly capable people
→ rework, responsibility overlap, and handoff delay increase
→ separate four functions
→ declare sequence, switching, and handoff
→ translate departments at the required ST depth
→ audit wiring with primary logs and named observations
→ terminate, re-agree, and rewire
→ reproducible organizational function
```

## 5. Applications
- Sales ↔ development: translate customer requests into users, value, causality, constraints, failure conditions, and responsibility.
- Planning ↔ legal: separate purpose from legal conditions and reconnect them through conditional design.
- R&D: separate observation, hypothesis, design, and verification with stop/rework criteria.
- AI collaboration: separate summarization, structuring, design, and falsification; do not transfer final accountability to AI output.
- Emergencies: prioritize accountable command when life or safety requires it, then review evidence and outcome afterward.

## 6. Measurements and audit
- **Four roles** are a source-explicit classification of four functions, not a four-point score.
- **30–90 seconds** is a source-explicit implementation example for a silence slot, not a universal required duration or optimal threshold.
- **ST-0 to ST-5** is a six-level mapping classification, not a ranking of departments, people, or cultures.
- **CPI** concerns process integrity through role deviation, rework, and procedural coherence.
- **HL** concerns handover latency between roles or departments.
- **R-Yield** concerns whether a cycle produces effective agreement; it is not obedience or popularity.
- **D-Gap** concerns the difference between designed depth and actual depth reached.

Reversible evaluation is required: shorter HL, higher R-Yield, or improved CPI do not count as improvement when dissent, refusal, correction, evidence, safety, or accountability deteriorate.

## 7. Validity conditions
- Inputs, outputs, prohibited actions, and termination conditions exist for each role.
- One role at a time is functionally maintained.
- Value → Benefit → Cost includes unfavorable conditions rather than hiding them.
- Switching triggers and a functional silence slot exist.
- Current role, switching reason, next role, and unresolved items are recorded.
- Departments use the ST depth required by the dependency.
- Vocabulary, primary logs, handoffs, and decision evidence remain retrievable.
- Refusal, correction, reversibility, and bounded emergency command remain possible.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Role labels exist while multiple functions are performed simultaneously.
- Silence becomes intimidation, concealment, or avoidance of responsibility.
- Value language hides price or unfavorable conditions.
- Meetings or approval layers increase without termination or handoff architecture.
- Coordination concentration in talented people is mistaken for structural repair.
- Cross-department connection stops at terminology matching.
- Metrics are tied directly to employee ranking.
- Responsible emergency command is avoided when required.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- Rework and responsibility overlap do not improve after role separation.
- Lower HL reduces evidence quality or handoff completeness.
- Higher R-Yield comes from superficial assent or suppression of dissent.
- Lower D-Gap comes from skipping necessary depth.
- Better CPI reflects only formal compliance rather than organizational function.
- ST-3 to ST-5 still fails to connect causality, responsibility, or evaluation across departments.

## 10. Required distinctions
- role vs title/personality
- capability vs organizational wiring
- one role at a time vs one permanent role per person
- silence vs intimidation or neglect
- ordering vs price concealment
- command vs agreement
- ST depth vs merit ranking
- CPI vs employee score
- HL vs speed-only optimization
- R-Yield vs obedience
- D-Gap vs human ability
- natural function vs laissez-faire

## 11. Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the theory to “talent is unnecessary,” “leadership is unnecessary,” meeting etiquette, or static job division. Do not universalize the 30–90 second example. Do not convert CPI, HL, R-Yield, D-Gap, vocabulary consistency, or cycle compliance into human-worth scores or invented thresholds.
