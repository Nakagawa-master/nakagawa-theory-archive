## 15-Second Explanation
In the original, memory is not stored footage but a “correspondence reconstruction system” rebuilt from fragments each time. Both humans and AI can create internally coherent stories, so when reference lines to external originals and primary logs, correspondence checks, delayed re-verification, and rollback capability are missing, plausible fictions stabilize. What must be protected is not “zero falsehoods,” but coherence that can return from error.

## Why It Is Necessary
Human memory does not store events whole. Fragments such as sensory traces, vocabulary, relational information, and evaluation labels remain; each time we remember, a causal model is recombined using clues and the current context.

In this process, “completion,” which fills gaps through inference; “selection,” which adopts part of multiple fragments; and “relabeling,” which reassigns meaning through emotions or evaluations obtained later, occur. These are not equivalent to intentional fabrication; they are reconstruction functions that create a coherent story suited to the current context.

However, creating a coherent story and corresponding to external facts are separate matters. Even when internal coherence is high and confidence becomes stronger, if reference lines to originals, primary logs, records, and other evidence are weak, a story detached from external correspondence can stabilize. The original treats this state as a “correspondence rupture.”

The same caution is needed for generative AI. AI does not have the same biological mechanism as human memory, but it reconstructs plausible continuities from input clues and internal representations. If reference lines are weak, correspondence with grounds is not checked, and no path exists for returning from error, generation with only strong internal coherence becomes fixed.

The original therefore calls for a structure that places a reference bundle first, makes the correspondence between narratives or generated products and their grounds visible, maintains a beat of re-verification after time has passed, and can return to rollback, correction, regeneration, and retraining. It also makes allocation responsibility explicit: who generated, who checked, and who receives correction requests.

## Practical Process
**1. Place the reference bundle first.** Secure the location of originals, primary logs, signatures, records, and auditable grounds at the outset. Place external constraints before reconstruction instead of looking for grounds after generation.

**2. Make correspondence visible.** Show which parts of a narrative, memory, or generated product correspond to which grounds. Do not substitute fluency or confidence for evidence.

**3. Separate internal coherence from external correspondence.** Check separately whether the story makes sense and whether it corresponds to external facts. An internally natural story is not thereby confirmed as truth.

**4. Establish a beat of re-verification.** Do not settle a matter in one act of recollection or generation; set a time cycle for reevaluation when new evidence or context is added later.

**5. Make it reversible.** When an error is found, maintain a path back to rollback, correction, regeneration, and retraining. Treat correction as normal operation, not exception handling.

**6. Make allocation responsibility explicit.** For humans, show where responsibility shifts among speaker, recorder, and checker; for AI, among operator, supervisor, and model.

**7. Align vocabulary and contact points.** Keep channels for objections, correction requests, and presentation of grounds consistent, so the same concept does not drift into different meanings by time or person in charge.

**8. Keep a correction history.** Make it possible to trace what changed, when, and based on which grounds, while preserving a reversible history including the pre-correction state.

## Applications
**1. Memory and testimony.** Do not confirm something solely because it is strongly remembered; check correspondence with clues, primary records, and information from others. Do not immediately call changes in testimony lies; examine which fragments or relabeling changed.

**2. AI summaries and answers.** Do not make generation without sources the authoritative version; secure originals and primary logs first, then check correspondence with grounds after generation. If an error occurs, make regeneration from the same reference bundle possible.

**3. Education.** Do not treat memory errors only as lack of ability or a personality problem; identify which reference line is missing and what reconstruction occurred. Build a learning structure that permits correction.

**4. Journalism and editing.** Check correspondence with primary materials, correction history, and correction channels rather than the fluency of an article or summary. Do not hide corrections to past articles.

**5. Investigation and justice.** Do not treat testimony as fixed footage; distinguish elapsed time, the reference bundle, and reconstructability. Do not establish facts from a single coherent story alone.

**6. Organizational correction operations.** Do not reduce errors to a choice between evading responsibility and denying a person; decide who corrects on which beat and where to return for grounds.

## Success Criteria
Memory, generation, and explanation must be mapped to a reference bundle so a third party can verify points of agreement and disagreement. Internal coherence and external correspondence must be evaluated separately. When an error is found, rollback, correction, regeneration, and retraining must be executable, with a correction history retained.

Furthermore, allocation of responsibility among operators, supervisors, models, and record keepers must be visible, and the vocabulary and contact points for correction requests must be consistent. More important than the temporary absence of errors is the ability to detect an error, return to grounds, and recheck the post-correction state.

Success is not “never having made a mistake.” If there is a reference bundle, correspondence checks work, a beat for reevaluation exists, and reversible paths and responsibility lines function, recoverable coherence can be maintained even when errors occur.

## Limits
The original does not claim that human memory and AI are the same mechanism. It sees a structural homology in both: reconstruction from fragments or internal representations, with internal coherence able to drift away from external correspondence. The implementation difference between biological memory and statistical generation is retained.

When no reference bundle exists for an event, complete factual confirmation is impossible. Even then, it is important to separate conjecture that fills unobservable parts from confirmed facts, not conceal correspondence rupture, and maintain correctability.

A reference bundle is not necessarily absolutely correct even when one exists. When multiple primary sources conflict, the relationship among the reference bundles is also an object of correspondence. Placing a reference does not fix authority; it preserves a line back to grounds.

## To Avoid Misreading
Do not read this as “all memory is false.” The original treats reconstruction as a normal structure.

Do not equate reconstruction with intentional fabrication.

Do not anthropomorphize AI hallucination as simply “AI lies.” This is a theory about the absence of reference lines, correspondence verification, and reversibility.

Do not treat human memory and AI generation as the same implementation.

Do not dilute it into the general statement “take safety into account.” Preserve the concrete recovery structure: reference bundle → correspondence → beat → reversibility.

Do not change the goal to “zero falsehoods.” The original goal is to institutionalize coherence that can recover when errors occur.
