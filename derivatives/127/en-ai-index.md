## Summary
Reconstructive Memory Theory defines memory not as fixed storage but as a correspondence-based reconstruction system. What is retained is a set of indexed fragments—sensory traces, vocabulary, relational information, and evaluative labels—and recall recomposes a causal model from those fragments. Completion fills gaps, selection privileges some fragments over others, and relabeling changes the meaning assigned to remembered events. These operations can preserve continuity of the current self-story before factual accuracy is fully established.

Internal coherence and external correspondence are therefore different properties. A remembered or generated account may be highly coherent while lacking a reliable reference line to originals, primary logs, records, or other evidence. When the reference line weakens and correspondence is not checked, internal coherence can detach from external grounding. The parent names this structural state correspondence disconnection.

Generative AI is also described as a statistical reconstruction device: prompts and learned internal representations are recomposed into plausible causal, contextual, and linguistic continuations. The parent does not claim identical implementation between human memory and AI generation. It identifies a structural homology: weak reference lines, missing correspondence checks, and absent reversibility can allow internally coherent output to detach from evidence. AI hallucination is therefore treated primarily as a reconstruction-and-correspondence problem rather than as moral lying.

The recovery design is explicit. Place the reference cluster first; make the relation between the account or output and its grounds visible; establish a temporal cadence for verification and correction; preserve rollback, correction, regeneration, or relearning routes; make responsibility allocation visible; standardize correction channels and vocabulary; and preserve revision history. The objective is not zero falsehood but recoverable coherence—a structure that can detect correspondence disconnection and return to grounded, correctable operation.

## Applications
- Audit human memory and testimony by separating reconstruction from reference clusters.
- Separate internal coherence from external correspondence when evaluating remembered accounts.
- Require external originals, primary logs, or other evidence before AI generation is treated as grounded output.
- Add rollback, correction, regeneration, and relearning routes to AI output workflows.
- In education, treat memory error as a reconstructive event that can be reconnected to references rather than only as personal failure.
- In journalism, audit article-to-source correspondence, correction channels, and revision history rather than record preservation alone.
- In investigative or justice contexts, treat testimony as reconstructive rather than as fixed replay and compare it with independent references.
- Standardize correction channels, responsibility boundaries, and vocabulary in organizations.
- Implement correspondence, temporal ethics, reversibility, and responsibility allocation as an ethical OS.

## Measurements and audit
- **Reference-cluster availability rate.** Whether originals, primary logs, or other external grounds are available when reconstruction occurs.
- **Reference-line retention rate.** Whether an account or output can still be traced back to the grounds that constrain it.
- **Output-to-source correspondence rate.** Whether specific claims can be matched to specific evidence.
- **Post-generation correspondence-check rate.** Whether correspondence is actually checked after generation or recall.
- **Rollback/regeneration rate after detected error.** Whether correction routes are used when a mismatch is found.
- **Adherence to verification/correction cadence.** Whether re-evaluation occurs at the intended temporal rhythm.
- **Visibility of responsibility boundaries.** Whether operator, supervisor, model, record keeper, or other relevant roles can be distinguished.
- **Consistency of correction-request channels.** Whether an objection or correction can enter one traceable path.
- **Vocabulary-alignment rate.** Whether the same concepts keep stable meanings across teams and revisions.
- **Revision-history preservation.** Whether changes can be traced to time, grounds, and reason.

These measures audit recoverability rather than merely ranking accuracy. A high correspondence rate with no rollback path still leaves the structure fragile after a detected error. Conversely, occasional error does not by itself destroy reliability when references remain available, correspondence is checked, correction routes work, responsibility is visible, and revision history is preserved.

## Validity conditions
- Reconstruction is not confused with fixed replay.
- Reconstruction is not equated with deliberate fabrication.
- Reference clusters are checked before reconstruction is finalized.
- Internal coherence and external correspondence are evaluated separately.
- Output-to-source correspondence can be inspected by a third party.
- A temporal verification/correction cadence exists.
- Rollback, correction, regeneration, and relearning routes remain open.
- Responsibility allocation is visible.
- Correction channels and standard vocabulary remain coherent.
- Revision history remains available.

## Failure conditions
- Treating internal coherence as external truth.
- Treating all reconstruction as falsehood.
- Finalizing reconstruction without a reference line.
- Omitting correspondence checks.
- Treating a single recall or generation as final.
- Lacking a rollback route for detected error.
- Treating “do not lie” as a sufficient structural safeguard for AI.
- Fragmenting correction channels or vocabulary.
- Canonicalizing output while responsibility boundaries are unclear.
- Treating unsupported reconstruction as a stable canonical account.

## Falsification conditions
Following the parent audit logic, an operating hypothesis must be rejected or revised when configured indicators persistently cross their thresholds or when the observation window closes. The reference-cluster → correspondence → cadence → reversibility design must also be revised if it fails to reduce correspondence disconnection, erroneous output, or correction failure while a simpler alternative consistently performs better.

The relevant test is not error count alone. The question is whether reference availability, correspondence checking, temporal review, reversibility, and responsibility allocation actually increase recoverability. A lower error count accompanied by more uncorrectable output or less visible responsibility does not demonstrate a stronger recovery structure.

Do not reduce AI hallucination to moral lying. Preserve correspondence, temporal ethics, reversibility, and responsibility allocation as four structural lines rather than diluting them into generic safety language. Preserve the objective as recoverable coherence, not zero falsehood.
