## Summary
The parent source does not import Early Buddhism as a religious system. It uses the Five Aggregates, non-self, and suffering as a limited auxiliary vocabulary for making the Cognitive OS easier to observe. The Five Aggregates are read as five layers of an experiential operating system: body and physical conditions, primary sensation, cognitive image, behavioral tendency, and recognition function. These are mapped to the Nakagawa OS metaphors of terrain, the first movement of the water surface, reflected images, wind or current, and the clarity or resolution of the surface. Non-self is interpreted not as self-negation but as a protocol for removing the fixed label “this is me” from observable phenomena and shifting the observer from entanglement to overview and then super-overview. Suffering is treated not simply as an enemy to suppress, but as a signal that the observer has become identified with surface phenomena. Physiological condition, breathing, thought volume, and self-labeling are adjusted to restore observer position and improve cognitive clarity.

## Concepts
- **Five Aggregates:** form, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness as five experiential layers.
- **Form / rūpa:** body and physical conditions; mapped to underlying terrain.
- **Feeling / vedanā:** primary pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral reception; mapped to the first ripple.
- **Perception / saññā:** image, interpretation, memory, prediction, and story; mapped to reflected imagery.
- **Formations / saṅkhāra:** habits, impulses, and automatic tendencies; mapped to wind and current.
- **Consciousness / viññāṇa:** recognition capacity; mapped to clarity and resolution of the surface.
- **Non-self:** removal of a fixed self-label from changing experiential phenomena.
- **Entanglement:** observer identification with emotion and thought.
- **Overview:** observing surface states as objects.
- **Super-overview:** observing internal and external events on one natural-phenomena screen.
- **Suffering:** a reaction signal associated with misidentification and observer-position drift.
- **Auxiliary language:** comparative vocabulary used to clarify structure, not to confer religious authority.

## Causal chain
```text
Experiential phenomena are labeled as a fixed self
→ body, feeling, thought, tendency, and recognition become identity-bound
→ the observer becomes entangled with changing phenomena
→ waves, wind, and distortion are mistaken for self or world essence
→ suffering, automatic reaction, and structural misreading persist
→ the five aggregates are separated as experiential layers
→ each layer is returned to observable-phenomenon status
→ self-label generation itself becomes observable
→ observer position shifts from entanglement to overview
→ internal and external events are placed on one observation screen
→ super-overview is established
→ suffering is read as a position-drift signal
→ physiology, breathing, and story generation are adjusted
→ surface noise decreases
→ structural recognition becomes clearer
```

## Applications
- During anger, separate bodily reaction, sensation, interpretation, impulse, and recognition instead of equating anger with the self.
- During anxiety, separate future prediction from fear and inspect sleep, breathing, story generation, and avoidance tendencies.
- In organizational conflict, observe both individual reaction layers and external structures such as roles, evaluation, authority, and information flow.
- Before consequential decisions, distinguish physiological instability and cognitive noise from the decision object itself.
- With persistent suffering, inspect where “me,” “mine,” or fixed ownership labels are attached rather than only suppressing the feeling.

## Measurements and audit
- Can the five experiential layers be distinguished in observation?
- Can self-label generation itself be recognized as an event?
- Can entanglement, overview, and super-overview be distinguished in practice?
- Is suffering being generalized into total self-worth or world truth?
- Can changes in physiology and observer position be re-observed?
- Do automatic reactions and structural misreadings decline after observer repositioning?
- Is the auxiliary language still clarifying rather than becoming religious authority?
- Are external hazards and institutional problems still represented as real external conditions?
- Do judgment quality and responsibility performance improve?

## Validity conditions
- Early Buddhist terms remain auxiliary language for the Cognitive OS.
- The Five Aggregates remain observable experiential layers rather than a fixed self.
- Non-self remains a perspective-shift protocol rather than self-negation.
- Suffering is not reduced to a simple evil to be eliminated.
- Internal phenomena and external structures are both observed.
- Physiological conditions are not detached from cognitive quality.
- Structural synchronization is not generalized into complete identity of the two systems.

## Failure conditions
- Early Buddhism is used to confer authority on the Nakagawa OS.
- The Five Aggregates are converted into fixed personality categories.
- Non-self becomes self-denial, nihilism, or responsibility avoidance.
- Emotional suppression or absence of suffering becomes the sole success criterion.
- Super-overview becomes escape from reality or emotional disconnection.
- External danger, harm, or institutional dysfunction is ignored.
- Cognitive technique is treated as sufficient regardless of physical condition.

## Falsification conditions
If externalizing self-labels, shifting observer position, and adjusting physiological, breathing, and thought conditions do not reduce automatic reaction or structural misreading and instead worsen real-world judgment or responsibility performance, the mapping and implementation hypothesis should be revised. If the auxiliary vocabulary creates conceptual confusion or religious authority effects rather than greater structural clarity, the correspondence should be weakened or withdrawn.

## Required distinctions
- Auxiliary language / adoption of a religious system.
- Five Aggregates / fixed self.
- Non-self / self-negation.
- Observing suffering / suppressing suffering.
- Entanglement / overview / super-overview.
- Observer repositioning / responsibility avoidance.
- Structural synchronization / total-system identity.
- Internal cognitive noise / external real-world conditions.
