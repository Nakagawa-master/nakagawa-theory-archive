## 15-Second Explanation

This Parent Original does not introduce Early Buddhism as a religion, but uses it as a supplementary language that makes the structure of the Cognition OS easier to see. The Five Aggregates are read as layers of the experiential OS—body, sensation, cognitive image, reaction tendency, and recognition function—and the mistaken label “these are me” is removed. Non-self is treated not as self-denial, but as an operation that moves the observation position from entanglement to observation and super-observation. Suffering is also not an enemy; it is read as a warning that the observer has sunk into a phenomenon.

## Why It Is Needed

In the Cognition OS, the more one treats anger, anxiety, expectations, and thoughts as the self itself, the more the observer becomes entangled in the waves on the surface and is likely to read structure and causality in a distorted way. The Parent Original uses the Five Aggregates as supplementary terms for decomposing this “self-identification with phenomena.”

When divided into form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness, bodily conditions, primary sensations, interpretation, reaction tendencies, and recognition functions can be seen as separate phenomena. It becomes harder to fix any one fluctuation as “my essence,” and easier to distinguish “what is happening now” from “who is labeling it and how.”

Non-self is likewise read not as a doctrine for erasing the self, but as a shift in perspective that removes the fixed “I” label and returns body, emotion, thought, and tendencies to the status of objects of observation. This allows distance from reactions without suppressing emotion and makes it possible to separate reaction from judgment.

## Practical Process

1. First separate body, sensation, cognitive image, reaction tendency, and recognition function.
2. See where the label “this is me” or “this is my value” was applied.
3. When entangled, return emotion and thought to objects of observation without trying to erase them.
4. Move to observation from above the water surface, and then to super-observation, which views inner and outer worlds as the same kind of natural phenomenon.
5. Check whether physiological conditions such as sleep, nutrition, breathing, and posture are destabilizing the observation position.
6. Calm the breath and temporarily reduce excessive story generation.
7. Make the “I” label itself an object of observation.
8. Read suffering and discomfort not as targets for elimination, but as signals showing where mistaken identification is occurring.
9. After restoring the position, re-observe the structure and terrain behind the phenomenon.
10. If real-world judgment does not improve, revise the correspondence and observation hypotheses.

## Examples of Application

**An important interpersonal conflict.** Instead of immediately concluding “I was rejected,” separate bodily reaction, primary sensation, interpretation, impulse to retaliate, and recognition. Place the other person’s statement as an external phenomenon and one’s Five Aggregates reaction on the same observation surface.

**A decision under strong anxiety.** Do not identify future prediction with anxiety; separate sleep deprivation, bodily tension, story generation driven by fear, and automatic avoidance tendencies. Read causality after recovering the observation position.

**Organizational friction.** Do not close the matter with the single label “I dislike that person”; separate facts, sensations, interpretations, habitual reactions, and role, evaluation, and information structures. Do not confuse individual reaction with organizational terrain.

**Continuous suffering.** Before suppressing suffering, observe what is being fixed as “me,” “mine,” or “something I must not lose.” When external danger or harm exists, treat that as a real condition at the same time.

## Success Criteria

- Even amid strong emotion, one can observe without immediately fixing emotion as the self.
- One can explain body, sensation, interpretation, reaction tendency, and recognition separately.
- The difference between entanglement, observation, and super-observation is reflected in actual judgments.
- Suffering can be read as information about mistaken identification or a displaced position without suppressing it.
- The relationship between physiological conditions and recognition accuracy can be observed.
- After restoring the observation position, structural misreading and automatic reactions decrease.
- The supplementary language makes understanding more transparent without increasing religious authority claims or conceptual confusion.

## Limitations

This framework does not prove that all of Early Buddhism and all of the Nakagawa OS are identical. The treatment of the Five Aggregates, non-self, and suffering is limited to the range of structural correspondences extracted by the Parent Original to explain the Cognition OS.

Nor does super-observation mean emotional numbness or escape from reality. When external danger, harm, illness, or institutional problems exist, do not reduce them to “problems of one’s recognition.” References to mind-body conditions are not a medical diagnosis or substitute for treatment.

## Preventing Misreading

- Do not use Early Buddhism to lend authority to the Nakagawa OS.
- Do not turn non-self into self-denial or abandonment of responsibility.
- Do not make merely feeling no suffering the success criterion.
- Do not equate super-observation with emotional disconnection or dissociation.
- Do not reduce all external problems to problems of recognition.
- Keep “complete identity” limited to the correspondence range of the recognition structure extracted in the text.
