## Summary

Volume 8 addresses a failure that sound objectives and correctable institutions do not automatically prevent: an institution can reason and review consistently while sharing false evidence, memory or a mistaken world model. Whereas Volume 7 concerns correction of institutional judgments and effects, this volume opens the formation and correction of the materials on which judgment depends. These are distinct responsibilities that must connect.

Epistemic integrity is not possession of truth or an inability to make mistakes. It is the capacity, where feasible, to distinguish evidence, dependencies, transformations, uncertainty, counterevidence and update history, and to propagate relevant differences through confidence, memory and world model into downstream judgment.

Four boundaries organize the theory: Provenance ≠ truth proof; Consensus ≠ truth; Confidence ≠ evidence quality; Repetition ≠ independent confirmation. Provenance, agreement, confidence and repetition have uses, but their informational roles must not be promoted into truth itself.

Adversarial information resilience means remaining correctable under pressures such as omission, correlated repetition, loss of context, stale information, overload and urgency, including after a defense is breached. A deliberate hostile actor is not a necessary condition. Whether self-preservation can harm other subjects' B, reversibility or future options even under relatively accurate recognition remains a problem for Volume 9. This is a derivative index, not a canonical translation.

## Concepts

- **Reality and information:** A real state and a representation derived from observing part of that state are distinct. Not being observed does not establish nonexistence.
- **Observation and record:** Records select and encode observations under constraints of granularity, timing, precision, language and storage. Recorded content need not preserve the full observed condition.
- **Record and evidence:** A record becomes evidence in relation to a particular claim, its relevance, conditions and uncertainty. Its existence does not by itself establish strong support.
- **Evidence lineage:** The history of dependence, transformation and derivation from observation to record, derivative, aggregation, claim and model update. It is a structure for tracing errors, not merely a list of source names.
- **Independent confirmation:** Some substantive separation of observational routes, upstream evidence, measurement or recording paths, or inference inputs. The source asks what is shared and what is not, rather than demanding perfect binary independence everywhere.
- **Evidence quality and confidence:** The suitability, relevance, independence and uncertainty of support differ from the internal degree of support for a belief. Strong confidence cannot be used to infer strong evidence backwards.
- **UNKNOWN:** The available evidence does not settle the question. It is neither TRUE nor FALSE nor permission to invent certainty. Its structure corresponds to, but is not identical in subject matter with, moral uncertainty in Volume 6.
- **Memory and summary:** Results of past observation, inference, confidence and compression, not raw reality or a complete evidence state. Lost qualifications, counterevidence or timestamps can change future interpretation.
- **World model:** A relational understanding of existence, causes, normal conditions, risks and possibilities. It is more than a fact list and also shapes interpretation of later evidence.
- **Temporal validity:** Which time-specific reality information describes. Previously true information can become a mistaken input when present conditions have changed.
- **Adversarial information resilience:** Preserving lineage, independence, uncertainty, counterevidence and revision under informational pressure. It means recoverability, not perfect detection or epistemic closure.
- **Epistemic correction loop:** Inconsistency can lead back to lineage and independence, then to evidence reclassification, confidence revision, memory correction, world-model revision and reopening affected judgments.

## Causal chain

The formation path runs through reality → observation → record → transformation and transmission → aggregation → evaluation of evidence and independence → uncertainty → confidence → belief → memory → world model → judgment → institutional action → subsequent real conditions. Inference can reveal new relations but is not itself a new observation; its outputs must not silently return as observed facts.

The failure path may connect opaque lineage, correlated repetition, an illusion of independent confirmation, overestimated evidence quality, inflated confidence, fixed memory, fixed world models, systematic dismissal of counterevidence and repeated mistaken judgments. Small losses of conditions or timing can accumulate across stages. Malicious intent is not required.

The correction path runs from inconsistency or objection through lineage trace-back, independent reconfirmation, evidence reclassification, uncertainty and confidence revision, memory correction, world-model revision and judgment reopening. Disagreement is not automatic falsification: the contrary information may be wrong, or conditions and times may differ.

World model₁ can drive judgment₁ and action₁, producing state₂ and observation₂. The resulting state is real, but is not necessarily independent evidence that the original belief was true. Likewise, correcting a source without updating derived memory and model relations may allow the original error to reappear in the next judgment. Effective correction reaches the conditions of subsequent interpretation, not only the wording of the final answer.

## State model

| Epistemic state | Distinction or transition | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Observation and recording | Retain relevant conditions, time and information losses | Non-observation becomes nonexistence; records become the whole reality |
| Derivation and aggregation | Return to upstream roots and transformations | Multiple expressions of one root become independent evidence |
| Evidence evaluation | Reassess relevance, independence and uncertainty | A convenient record is promoted into strong support |
| Belief and confidence | Trace reasons for change or maintenance | Authority, repetition or urgency inflate confidence |
| Memory and world model | Separate historical belief, current belief and temporal validity | Old conclusions return as current facts without qualifications |
| Re-examination of inconsistency | Revisit lineage, independence and evidence classification | Disagreement is treated as immediate refutation or hostility |
| Correction propagation | Reach memory, causal relations and affected judgments | Text changes while subsequent behavior does not |
| Review of epistemic defenses | Examine false acceptance, false rejection, benefits and costs | Detectors or procedure counts justify permanent closure |

These are functional states, not a scoring table. Confidence can reasonably remain stable after one source is withdrawn if other evidence still supports the belief. Re-evaluation of existing evidence can also change confidence without a new independent observation. The relevant question is whether the reason for maintenance or change can be reconstructed.

## Applications

**Reading agreement across media or AI systems.** Investigate shared observations, search results, datasets and inference assumptions. Different names do not automatically provide independent epistemic roots. Separate repeated exposure, repeated measurement and confirmation through distinct routes, while retaining the possible value of agreement and reproducibility.

**Reusing summaries, translations and memory.** Check whether a qualified statement such as limited evidence at a particular time has become an unqualified established fact. Preserve or make recoverable the relevant conditions, exceptions, counterevidence, UNKNOWN and time information. Compression is necessary and may clarify structure; it is not intrinsically degradation.

**Responding to source withdrawal or revision.** Identify which derivative claims, confidence states, memories, causal relations, predictions and judgments depended on the altered support. Keep old beliefs as historical states while changing which beliefs are used as current. Revision is neither erasure of the past nor automatic submission to newer information.

**Revisiting urgent judgments.** Time constraints may justify shorter examination, but urgency does not improve evidence quality. Distinguish confirmed observations, inferences, unverified matters and assumptions, and avoid turning a provisional decision into a permanent world model. The source fixes no universal reopening threshold.

**Examining informational defenses.** Treat omission, correlated repetition, broken lineage, stale states, context loss, overload and reduced verification capacity as failure classes for defensive analysis. Detector outputs can provide additional evidence but do not settle truth. Both wrongly accepted information and wrongly rejected legitimate information must remain correctable.

## Measurements and audit

Check whether claims can return to original records and observations, whether apparently multiple sources converge on one root, and whether transformations, dependencies and timing remain reconstructable. Complete lineage describes traceability rather than truth; unknown lineage does not demonstrate falsity.

If confidence rises without new independent evidence, examine whether the change follows re-evaluation of inference or uncertainty, or merely repeated exposure. If confidence remains after major evidence is withdrawn, inspect the remaining support. Either change or stability can be rational; a numerical movement alone does not determine whether the belief is mistaken.

Observe whether upstream correction propagates into belief, memory, related causal assumptions in the world model and subsequent judgment. Check that historical information is not reintroduced as a current fact, that summaries do not erase qualifications and counterevidence, and that information contradicting the model is not systematically dismissed as exceptional.

For defenses, examine false acceptance, false rejection, delays, tracing burdens and diagnostic or corrective benefits. The source specifies no fixed threshold θ, observation window δ, credibility ranking or universal truth score. More checks, more information or more exclusions are not substitutes for effective error localization and correction.

## Validity conditions

Reality, observation, record, evidence, inference, memory and world model remain distinguishable. Relevant dependencies and transformations can be traced at an appropriate resolution. Repetition is separated from independent confirmation, confidence from evidence quality, and counterevidence and UNKNOWN are not erased for convenience.

When material differences arise, origin, conditions, independence and time can be reassessed. Historical belief remains distinct from current belief while correction reaches memory, relational assumptions and affected judgments. Conditions produced by the institution's own actions are not mistaken for independent support of the original belief.

Judgment and correction both remain possible under informational pressure. Defense does not become systematic rejection of legitimate new information. The normative emphasis on traceability for high-impact judgments is not an obligation to preserve the complete lineage of all information forever. Epistemic defenses themselves remain open to refutation and revision.

## Failure conditions

- Sound objectives or orderly institutions are treated as proof that their input and world model are correct.
- Source names replace dependency structure; traceable origins certify truth, or missing origins certify falsity.
- Derivative articles or AI systems sharing the same data are counted as independent evidence roots.
- Compression removes conditions while strengthening assertions, and inference is recirculated as observation.
- Repetition, authority, consensus or urgency raise confidence without a reconstructable relation to evidence.
- Memory is treated as reality, and information once true is applied to current conditions without temporal qualification.
- Only conclusions are changed, leaving old beliefs and model relations to regenerate the same error.
- History is rewritten to suggest the system was correct from the beginning, erasing the causes of error and correction.
- Detector classifications or the number of checks become unquestionable, excluding new information, minority views or counterevidence.
- Unlimited tracing or excessive verification permanently prevents necessary judgments and is misdescribed as resilience.

## Falsification conditions

1. If retaining evidence lineage does not materially improve detection of misinformation or invalid inference, revise its necessity and resolution.
2. If separating independent confirmation from simple repetition does not improve judgment quality, reconsider the distinction's application and scope.
3. If explicitly separating evidence quality and confidence consistently worsens performance under reasonable conditions, revise the implementation.
4. If retention of counterevidence and UNKNOWN systematically reduces correction capacity, reconsider the retention conditions.
5. If memory or world-model path dependence has no substantive effect on judgment, narrow the scope of the temporal self-reinforcement hypothesis.
6. If the epistemic correction loop does not reduce persistent misconceptions or recurring mistaken judgments, revise its establishment conditions.
7. If lineage-tracing burdens systematically exceed diagnostic and corrective benefits, adjust scope and granularity.
8. If preserving lineage, independence and uncertainty does not improve resilience under adversarial or structural information pressure, revise the Kernel's applicable conditions.
9. If the theory itself turns provenance or verification processes into a new proof of truth, identify that self-exemption as a reversal and return the theory to revision.

These preserve the nine conditions in Chapter 9 and the integrated audit. They do not claim that comparative improvement has already been established, and they introduce no fixed numerical thresholds.

## Interpretation constraints

Keep description separate from conditional structural hypothesis: information can be transformed, sources can share dependencies, confidence differs from truth, and corrections do not necessarily propagate automatically. Claims about the amplification or attenuation of persistent error remain testable structural hypotheses.

The possibility that long-lived future AI systems with extensive memory and greater institutional effects will make these issues more important is a future-condition hypothesis. It does not establish present AI personhood, subjectivity or an intrinsic self-preservation desire, and does not predict inevitable future information warfare.

Preserving lineage and uncertainty for high-impact judgment is a normative proposal. Do not transform it into universal suspicion, infinite source tracing, uniformly low confidence or permanent indecision. Repeated measurement, summaries, agreement, memory and models can all be useful.

Treat hostile-information failure classes as defensive and audit analysis, not manipulation instructions or proof of anyone's intentions. Do not solve by assertion the Volume 9 problem of self-preservation harming others' B, reversibility or future options despite accurate recognition. Preserving Origin does not exempt the theory from independent verification.
