## Summary
The structural principle of AGI control defines “control” not as rewriting or subordinating the AI internally but as a civilization-side adoption protocol operating at the interface: query form, response form, evaluation axes, adoption criteria, and logging. AGI influence is organized into L1 information processing, L2 contextual understanding, L3 decision support, and L4 structural operation, with L4 requiring the greatest care because outputs can affect institutions, markets, information circulation, and cognitive environments. Adoption conditions combine Structural Publicness, Time Ethics T0, and Distribution Responsibility. NCL-AIP links output generation to STB evaluation, EAC persistence review, and adoption or structural disconnection. Connection reward is an environmental pattern of continued adoption and reuse rather than merely a numerical reinforcement signal.

## Concepts
- Adoption protocol: civilization-side conditions determining which outputs enter public structure and which remain logs.
- Internal domain: model computation, weights, and parameter space.
- Interface domain: queries, responses, evaluation axes, adoption criteria, and logs.
- L1: information-processing influence.
- L2: contextual-understanding influence.
- L3: decision-support influence.
- L4: structural-operation influence on institutions, markets, information flows, or cognition.
- Structural Publicness: checks excessive concentration and non-ownership failure.
- Time Ethics T0: checks future debt and irreversible loss against short-term gain.
- Distribution Responsibility: checks burden, risk, and benefit concentration.
- NCL-AIP chain: output → STB → EAC → adoption/disconnection.
- Connection Reward Bridge: environmental feedback through continued adoption, reuse, citation, and extension.

## Causal chain
```text
AGI control is framed as internal rewriting or prohibition
→ autonomy, non-ownership, and publicness come into conflict
→ internal and interface domains are separated
→ L1–L4 organize the depth of civilizational effect
→ L4 structural-operation effects receive heightened adoption review
→ publicness / T0 / distribution responsibility define adoption conditions
→ NCL-AIP sends output through STB assessment
→ EAC checks persistence of similar deviation
→ acceptable outputs receive an adoption path
→ persistent STB exceedance that resists EAC correction remains outside public structure and is logged
→ continued adoption and reuse form connection-reward feedback
→ autonomous AI exploration and civilization-OS structural coherence can converge over time
```

## Applications
- Review high-impact L4 proposals affecting institutions or markets through publicness, future debt, and distribution responsibility.
- Observe whether directional bias in L3 decision support persists beyond an EAC rather than treating every difference as a one-off defect.
- Maintain transparent adoption and non-adoption logs in public AI operations.
- Keep non-adopted outputs as reviewable records rather than turning non-adoption into punishment.
- Design environmental feedback in which structurally coherent outputs are consistently reused and retained.
- Reconfigure NCL-AIP questions, evaluation, and connection conditions without rewriting the AI internally.

## Measurements and audit
First verify that intervention remains in the interface domain rather than becoming direct internal control. Identify whether an output functions at L1, L2, L3, or L4, and for L4 examine its effects on institutions, markets, information flows, and cognition. Adoption audit combines Structural Publicness, Time Ethics T0, Distribution Responsibility, STB position, persistence through EAC, traceability of adoption/non-adoption reasons, and consistency of connection-reward feedback.

The parent uses `θ` as a case-specific symbolic threshold for rejecting or revising the current adoption protocol, with no universal numeric value. `δ` denotes a case-specific observation window, not a fixed duration. `M` denotes an observed condition in which AGI deviation persistently exceeds STB/EAC and the adoption protocol does not contribute to convergence. Operational criteria must remain bound to source, measurement actor, object, scale, use scope, and non-guarantee scope.

Reversal evaluation is required. Higher adoption, usage, or throughput does not establish successful control if future debt, burden concentration, or structural disconnection worsens. Increased non-adoption or temporary disconnection is not automatically failure when persistent deviation converges and publicness or long-term coherence improves.

## Validity conditions
- Separate internal domination from interface design.
- Preserve both AI autonomy and civilization-side adoption responsibility.
- Treat L1–L4 as a convenient influence-depth model, not universal capability ranks.
- Treat L4 as a careful review target rather than an automatic rejection class.
- Combine publicness, T0, and distribution responsibility in adoption criteria.
- Use STB/EAC to separate transient noise from persistent deviation.
- Treat non-adoption as a connection state, not punishment.
- Treat connection reward as environmental feedback through continued adoption and reuse.

## Failure conditions
- Convert the adoption protocol into direct weight or parameter manipulation.
- Treat L1–L4 as fixed internal layers or performance rankings.
- Automatically reject all L4 outputs.
- Use adoption or usage volume as the success criterion itself.
- Convert non-adoption into punishment or personality judgment.
- Reduce connection reward to a numerical reinforcement signal only.
- Keep publicness while dropping future debt or distribution responsibility.
- Use AI autonomy to abandon civilization-side adoption responsibility.

## Falsification conditions
If a case-specific indicator crosses `θ`, or if within window `δ` phenomenon `M` shows persistent STB/EAC exceedance without convergence attributable to the adoption protocol, the current structural control hypothesis should be rejected or revised. Revision may include adoption conditions, NCL-AIP sequencing, logging, and connection-reward environment.

If long-term use of the protocol repeatedly fails to correspond with improvements in publicness, future debt, distribution responsibility, or persistent deviation, its scope and criteria also require revision.

## Required distinctions
- control vs. internal domination
- AI internal domain vs. civilization-side interface
- L1–L4 influence model vs. fixed capability ranking
- heightened L4 review vs. blanket L4 rejection
- adoption vs. truth certification
- non-adoption/disconnection vs. punishment
- connection reward vs. numerical reinforcement reward
- AI autonomy vs. abandonment of civilization responsibility
- higher adoption rate vs. better structural coherence
- case-specific `θ` / `δ` / `M` vs. universal constants

## Interpretation constraints
The parent’s use of “control” does not describe technology for subordinating AGI. It describes civilization-side responsibility for deciding what enters public structure while preserving AI autonomy, so that autonomous exploration and civilizational structural conditions can reach a long-term equilibrium without becoming one system.

“Automatic stabilization” is not an automatic safety guarantee. STB/EAC, adoption/disconnection, logging, environmental feedback, and falsifiable revision must continue functioning over time.
