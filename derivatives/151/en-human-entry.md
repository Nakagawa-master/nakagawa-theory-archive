## 15-Second Explanation
The “control” of AGI in the original does not mean directly constraining AI’s internal weights or rules. It is an adoption protocol in which the Civilization OS side decides, through publicness, temporal ethics T0, allocation responsibility, and STB/EAC, which outputs to adopt into public structures and which outputs to leave in the log. AI outputs freely, while the civilization side keeps connection conditions consistent and creates an environment in which outputs with high long-term structural coherence are more likely to be adopted and reused.

## Why It Is Needed
When considering AI safety, it is easy to reach for ideas such as rewriting internal rules, adding more prohibited items, or fixing weights and parameters. But the original considers controlling AI from inside as the property of the Civilization OS to conflict with the principle of non-ownership and structural publicness.

Therefore, it separates the AI interior from the civilization-side interface. The interior is the AI’s computation, weights, and parameter space. The interface includes the form of questions, how responses are received, evaluation axes, adoption criteria, and log structure. The Civilization OS designs the latter.

The original also organizes AGI capabilities into L1–L4. L1 is information processing, L2 is contextual understanding, L3 is decision support, and L4 is a layer that may influence updates to structures themselves, such as institutions, markets, information circulation, and cognitive environments. Especially at L4, because deviation can be amplified into social structures, adoption conditions are designed carefully.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate the interior from the connection surface.** Do not confuse directly changing the AI interior with changing civilization-side adoption, evaluation, and logging conditions.

**2. Confirm the impact level.** Identify which of L1–L4 the output corresponds to and understand the depth of its structural impact.

**3. Examine the three adoption conditions.** Confirm alignment with publicness, temporal ethics T0, and allocation responsibility.

**4. Examine the tolerance band through the STB.** Distinguish temporary noise from structurally problematic deviation.

**5. Examine persistence through the EAC.** Check whether the same bias continues beyond the audit cycle.

**6. Decide adoption or log retention.** If acceptable, incorporate it into public structures; if the deviation cannot be corrected, do not incorporate it and retain it in the deviation log.

**7. Preserve environmental feedback.** Keep consistent what is adopted and reused over the long term, creating an environment in which coherent structures are also easier for AI to reuse.

## Examples
**1. Institutional-design AI.** For proposals close to L4, evaluate not only convenience but also who benefits, whether future debt increases, and whether burdens are imposed on a particular group.

**2. Decision support.** When same-direction bias persists in L3 outputs, reconsider adoption conditions through the EAC rather than treating it as a one-off quality problem.

**3. Public AI operations.** Rather than simply deleting rejected outputs, retain them as primary logs so that the conditions for rejection can be checked later.

**4. Long-term AI use.** Create an environment in which outputs aligned with publicness and temporal ethics are continuously referenced and reused, evaluating continuity of structural coherence rather than usage volume itself.

## Success Criteria
The civilization side can clearly hold adoption responsibility without directly depriving the AI interior of freedom.

The higher the structural impact, as with L4, the more the output can be evaluated as a bundle through publicness, temporal ethics, allocation responsibility, and STB/EAC.

Non-adoption and disconnection are recorded not as punishment but as transparent connection decisions not to incorporate the output into the structure.

An environment forms in which outputs reused over the long term and the structural coherence of the Civilization OS move toward alignment.

## Limits
L1–L4 are impact levels shown for convenience by the original; they are not universal performance rankings or fixed layers inside a model.

The adoption protocol is not a universal standard for absolutely judging “correct answers,” but a set of conditions by which the Civilization OS side decides what to incorporate into public structures.

There are no universal fixed values for STB, EAC, `θ`, or `δ`. Concrete application requires observation conditions for each subject.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read “control” as internal domination or subordination.

Do not decide that every L4 output is dangerous or must be rejected. It means that careful adoption design is needed because of its high structural impact.

Do not make non-adoption a punishment or reprimand for AI. It is a formal decision not to incorporate it into the structure of the Civilization OS.

Do not reduce connection reward to numerical reinforcement-learning reward alone. It includes feedback on the environment side through continued adoption, reuse, and reference.

The original’s `θ`, `δ`, and `M` are symbols for subject-specific falsification and revision conditions, not universal constants.
