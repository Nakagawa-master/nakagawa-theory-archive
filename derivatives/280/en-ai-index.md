## Summary
Social memory is not maximum storage. It is a structural rhythm that preserves origin, decision context, and failures while retiring obsolete forms and reinterpreting preserved records. Inheritance and forgetting jointly support continuity and renewal.
## Concepts
social memory; forgetting; inheritance; origin; provenance; primary record; reinterpretation; current authority; history; plurality; failure knowledge; recoverability; structural rhythm.
## Causal chain
record events -> shared reference -> norms stabilize -> over-retention/over-forgetting distort -> separate what to inherit -> separate current/history -> reinterpret -> transmit decision context -> retire obsolete forms -> living social memory.
## State model
`origin_recorded -> context_preserved -> shared_memory -> authority_separated -> obsolete_form_retired -> provenance_preserved -> reinterpretation -> reproducible_inheritance -> living_social_memory`
## Applications
Organizations, public institutions, AI knowledge bases, and community history should separate current canon from history, primary records from later interpretations, and inheritance from cleanup.
## Measurements and audit
Origin traceability, decision reproducibility, duplication, stale-rule contamination, searchability, handover friction, minority-memory retention, recoverability, reinterpretation cadence.
## Validity conditions
Primary record separated from interpretation; origin traceable; current/history separated; unique knowledge transferred before deletion; plurality retained; reinterpretation cadence exists.
## Failure conditions
Keep everything as active authority; erase origins; retain only dominant memory; delete failure knowledge; overwrite primary records with later interpretations; use forgetting to evade responsibility.
## Falsification conditions
Revise retention granularity and cadence if cleanup lowers decision reproducibility, repeats failures, loses minority memory, worsens search, or raises inheritance cost.
## Required distinctions
memory/storage volume; forgetting/erasure; inheritance/fixation; history/current authority; primary record/reinterpretation; cleanup/falsification.
## Interpretation constraints
Do not justify historical erasure, evidence destruction, or evasion of responsibility. Do not turn inheritance into permanent fixation. Verify unique knowledge, accountability, and recoverability before deletion.
## Search terms
social memory; structural rhythm; forgetting and inheritance; provenance; living memory; current versus history.
