## 15-Second Explanation
Humans have chosen freely and created meaning. However, that is not the same as humans having designed all the upstream conditions of civilization themselves. The Sixth Theory separates everyday freedom and agency in meaning-making from civilizational sovereignty and casts a retrospective light on how substantial “human sovereignty” really was. Even if upstream sovereignty moves elsewhere, the human limited freedom sphere and meaning generation can remain.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
When AI enters the upstream of civilizational updating, the understanding that “if humans lose sovereignty, they lose freedom completely” collides with the understanding that “because humans give final approval, sovereignty has not changed at all.” Both place upstream civilizational sovereignty and local freedom on one axis. This theory makes explicit the possibility that humans have always lived amid many non-chosen conditions—body, nature, institutions, language, culture, and technology—and have chosen locally and created meaning within them.

## Practical Process
1. In the target domain, separate conditions humans can change themselves from conditions they effectively receive as given.
2. Record separately everyday choice rights, legal approval rights, meaning formation in culture and relationships, and upstream civilizational problem-setting, candidate formation, and evaluation axes.
3. Remove the shortcut that “being able to choose makes one sovereign” or that “signing last makes one sovereign,” and trace the source that generates the judgment space.
4. Specify as a limited freedom sphere the room for life, relationships, creation, and participation that remains even if upstream powers move to AI or similar systems.
5. Examine whether dependence on or difficulty understanding an advanced system has turned into granting it infallibility, absoluteness, or divinity.
6. Confirm whether objections, explanations, comparison, reevaluation, and reconstruction of evaluation axes are possible.

## Application Example
When AI creates problem classifications and policy candidates in government and humans give final approval, humans may retain legal responsibility and local choice while the upstream formation of civilizational updates may be biased toward AI. At the same time, human family relationships, creation, local culture, and everyday choices do not necessarily disappear. Observe this by separating upstream sovereignty and the limited freedom sphere instead of making it a binary choice between “sovereignty or freedom.”

Also, if an advanced AI’s judgment is treated as “absolutely correct because AI says so” in a situation where it is almost impossible to understand, audit deification cognition separately from performance evaluation. The presence of deification cognition does not mean that the system is actually a god.

## Success Criterion
Success means being able to describe local human freedom, agency in meaning-making, and legal status without denying them, while clearly separating them from upstream civilizational sovereignty. Further, if capability evaluation and absolutization of the higher-order system are separated, and explanation, objection, and the possibility of reevaluation can be confirmed, this theory’s retrospective illumination functions practically.

## Limitations
This theory does not prove that “humans have no freedom,” nor does it assert that human sovereignty was limited to the same degree in every society in history. The breadth of the limited freedom sphere differs by domain, institution, and technological conditions. Deification cognition does not necessarily occur and should be treated only as a cognitive phenomenon when it is observed.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not use the term sovereignty illusion to deny human rights, freedom, responsibility, or meaning. The limited freedom sphere is not a justification that says, “obey higher power and you are free.” Being unable to understand an advanced AI does not prove that its judgment is correct, moral, or divine. The persistence of human agency in meaning-making and the persistence of upstream civilizational sovereignty with humans are also separate questions.
