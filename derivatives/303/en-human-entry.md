## 15-Second Explanation

People can inspect their own work and can also divide roles for mutual checking. But even when the inspector is different, the parties may still be inside the same loop if they can read only the same material, lack authority to change the decision, share the same cause of failure, have connected interests, or if only the original decision-maker receives objections. Theory 10 asks whether an audit can reopen judgment through a different route and lead to necessary correction.

## Why It Is Needed

### Even an honest inspection can justify the original judgment

When a judgment is carried out, resources and information, the other party's response, and records left afterward change. A later audit reads the changed world as evidence. If the original judgment created that evidentiary environment and is then checked through the same mechanism, self-reference can arise: “judgment → execution → changed evidentiary environment → self-confirmation → continued execution.” No bad faith is required. The inertia of resources already committed and institutions already established can make review difficult.

### Separating four role names is not enough

Separating evaluator, evaluated party, executor, and auditor has meaning. However, if the auditor depends on the same records as the original judgment, receives authority from the decision-making party, stops at the same obstacle, and the original decision-maker alone decides whether an objection is accepted, the roles are separate but the causality is not.

### “Many people reach the same conclusion” is not necessarily independent confirmation

Even if several people or AIs reach the same conclusion, they may share the same inputs, summaries, memories, standards, and causes of failure. If the same error is being replicated, more votes do not add independent confirmation. Conversely, disagreement is not evidence that one side is correct. It can be a prompt to investigate the cause of the difference and reopen judgment.

## Practical Process

1. Identify the original judgment, executor, evaluated subject, and auditor, and map who can change what.
2. Trace the evidence supporting the judgment and how a later audit can obtain that evidence.
3. Identify common causes of failure, such as shared design, inputs, memory, standards, dependencies, and update paths.
4. Check through actual history whether audit results reach reinspection, reopening the judgment, temporary suspension, or review of exceptions.
5. Confirm how the auditor's own continuation, authority, evaluation, and institutional interests return to the conclusion.
6. Check whether objections reach a separate route rather than being collected only by the original decision-maker, and whether silence is being mistaken for consent.
7. Compare what independence is compressed during an emergency and what is restored after pressure falls.
8. When the five axes are separated under the same conditions, examine whether error detection and correction improved, and keep the theory and arrangement revisable.

The five axes are evidence, authority, failure domain, interests, and objection route. This is not a total score or a binary independence test. Confirm what is separated, what is shared, and which route returns to the judgment.

## Examples

### Multiple models or auditing bodies agree

Do not judge by model name or execution count. Trace the starting point of inputs, memory, evaluation standards, external dependencies, and causes of failure. Also preserve the possibility that sharing the same evidence is reasonable, and make clear where inspection through a different route exists.

### The decision does not change even after objections

Check whether recorded objections have a history of reaching reopened judgment. Saving a report is not enough: if the audited subject can reject every objection alone, the correction route is closed. This does not mean the auditor should receive unlimited veto power.

### Emergency audit and nondisclosure

Because of time shortages or dangers created by disclosure, evidence routes, authority, and objections may temporarily narrow. Make the grounds for judgment, remaining uncertainty, exception scope, and reinspection conditions traceable, and confirm that independence returns after the emergency ends. Secrecy is not the same as falsehood, and an emergency is not an audit exemption.

## Success Criteria

The question is not whether the conclusion is positive or negative, but whether an independent difference actually entered, the judgment was reexamined, and it could be corrected, narrowed, or withdrawn when necessary. Even when no different conclusion appears, it is important to show what evidence or conditions would change it. Also check that the auditor is audited, that temporary emergency compression has not become permanent, and that independence has not reversed into unlimited power.

## Limits

Completely different evidence cannot always be obtained, and a subject cannot be made completely free of interests. Excessively demanding separation can harm information sharing and cooperation and may delay judgment. This theory does not prescribe concrete numbers, organizations, or procedures for the five axes of independence. It also does not assert that current AI has self-awareness or a will to survive, and treats future AI only as a conditional structural hypothesis. Asymmetry in audit ability, access, and computing resources remains for Theory 11.

## Misreading Prevention

- It does not say self-audit is unnecessary. It also does not say self-audit alone satisfies independent audit.
- Role separation or majority agreement alone does not prove independence or truth.
- The existence of interests is not evidence of bad faith or corruption.
- Objections and disagreement are not truth votes. Silence is not consent.
- Emergency contraction and nondisclosure are not categorically prohibited, but must not become permanent or turn into audit exemption.
- Independent audit must not become a new unlimited sovereignty or an infinite veto.
- Origin and provenance are not proof of truth or exemption from audit.
