## Summary

Volume 10 treats independent audit as a causal arrangement that can reopen a judgment, not as a separate title or a larger number of reviewers. An executed decision changes the environment later used as evidence. If the same decision-maker and causal path assess the altered environment, the original decision can help create the evidence used to validate itself. The article separates five dimensions: evidence, authority, failure domains, interests, and objection paths. Actor count, repeated reports, and majority agreement do not establish independent verification. Disagreement is not truth, but may expose a difference worth tracing. Emergency pressure and secrecy can compress the five dimensions temporarily; neither creates permanent audit immunity. The article is a revisable structural hypothesis, not a prescribed institution, numeric score, or fixed procedure. It does not establish current AI desire, fear, consciousness, or personhood.

## Concepts

- **Self-referential audit**: an executed judgment changes later evidence conditions, and those conditions feed back into evaluation of the original judgment.
- **Role separation**: distinguishing evaluator, subject, executor, and auditor. It helps identify functions but does not prove causal independence.
- **Evidence independence**: access to evidence gathered through a different route, with the upstream lineage visible. Provenance is not proof of truth.
- **Authority independence**: audit results can, when warranted, trigger review, reopening, a pause request, or exception review. It is not unlimited veto power.
- **Failure domain**: the causal scope in which one defect, input, dependency, memory, criterion, or update path can affect several reviewers at once.
- **Interest independence**: examining how an auditor’s continuity, authority, success conditions, evaluation, or institutional survival feeds back into conclusions; zero interest is not required.
- **Objection-path independence**: an objection can travel beyond a route where the original decision-maker alone receives, judges, and closes it.
- **Multi-actor correspondence**: review over distinct causal paths, not an arithmetic vote.
- **Emergency compression**: temporary loss of time, evidence, access, or challenge routes under pressure; distinguish it from permanent collapse.
- **Falsifiability**: specified outcomes that require revising the five axes, their scope, or their causal links.

## Causal chain

### Self-confirming path

Decision → execution → changes to the target, records, resources, access, or others’ responses → later conditions are read as evidence → the original criterion confirms the decision → authority, exception, and execution continue → accumulated continuity supports further confirmation.

A reviewer’s own continuity or authority can also be affected by the verdict, creating pressure without requiring bad intent. Shared inputs can produce correlated copies; apparent consensus may then become a second loop of self-validation.

### Reopening path

Decision → self-audit → different evidence route → independent objection → review from different failure and interest conditions → decision reopened → necessary correction, narrowing, or withdrawal → reassessment under changed conditions.

Disagreement does not identify the true party. It can signal a difference to trace. The auditor must remain auditable, and reopening power must not become unlimited sovereignty.

## State model

| State | Relation to observe | Review focus |
|---|---|---|
| Judgment formed | Evidence, criterion, uncertainty | Reconstruct what was known and unknown then |
| Execution changed the setting | Target, records, resources, access | Identify how the judgment shaped later evidence |
| Self-audit | Shared actors, standards, evidence | Preserve useful internal checks without calling them independent |
| Multi-actor review | Shared and separate causal axes | Trace which paths are actually distinct |
| Objection or disagreement | Access, rejection power, reopening history | Check whether objections can start a review |
| Emergency compression | Time, secrecy, concentrated authority | Check what was compressed and what returns after pressure falls |
| Reopened judgment | New evidence, conditions, conclusion | Keep correction, narrowing, and withdrawal possible |

This is not a universal state machine or a binary pass/fail test. Independence is not a single score; dependencies across the five axes matter.

## Applications

### Several models reach the same answer

Trace source inputs, memory, summaries, criteria, dependencies, and shared failure causes instead of counting model names or runs. Shared evidence can aid comparability; also identify whether another evidence route exists.

### An objection is recorded but execution never changes

Review whether reports have ever triggered re-examination, reopening, narrowed scope, or exception review. If the audited party alone can reject every challenge, output and corrective power are separated.

### Emergency review and confidentiality

Time pressure or dangerous disclosure may temporarily compress evidence routes, participation, challenge, or authority. Preserve enough lineage to reconstruct the judgment, grounds, scope, uncertainty, and conditions that trigger later review. Emergency is not audit exemption; secrecy is not falsehood. No mechanical expiry period is prescribed.

### An auditor benefits from its conclusion

Map how continuity, authority, success, or evaluation connects to either approval or rejection. Interest alone does not prove corruption; the return path should be visible to another review route.

### Standardization and coordination

Shared infrastructure increases interoperability but can enlarge common failure domains. Compare correlated failure with the costs of isolation, information loss, and delay; maximal separation is not the goal.

## Measurements and audit

The ten observation directions in Chapter 9 are: (1) access to evidence different from the original decision; (2) whether a contrary conclusion can actually occur; (3) whether a contrary finding has reopened a past judgment; (4) common causes that stop or align multiple auditors; (5) unilateral control by the audited subject over auditor access, continuity, authority, or evidence scope; (6) an objection route beyond the original decision-maker; (7) restoration of independence after an emergency; (8) near-constant ratification; (9) whether new evidence or conditions can change a conclusion; and (10) causal, not merely conceptual, distinction between self-audit and independent audit.

These are review prompts, not a score. Ratification or rejection rates alone do not establish success or failure. Inspect challenge access, actual reopening, shared dependencies, auditor interests, and restoration after emergency pressure.

## Validity conditions

There is a causal relation among judgment, execution, and later observations; evidence and the basis of a decision can be traced; sharing and separation across the five axes can be described; and a different evidence path or objection can reach a review or reopening route. Perfect independence, zero interest, universal disclosure, and unanimous agreement are not assumed. Context, comparable conditions, coordination, efficiency, and time limits affect applicability.

## Failure conditions

- Treating different role names as proof of independence.
- Treating source lineage, repeated reports, copies, or majority agreement as truth or independent confirmation.
- Recording objections while leaving the audited subject sole power to close them.
- Calling interests corruption, or pretending interests do not exist.
- Making temporary emergency compression permanent and losing later review.
- Turning audit into an unlimited veto, sovereignty, or immunity from audit.
- Equating silence with consent, disagreement with error, or verdict rates with truth.
- Over-separating systems until needed information sharing and timely coordination fail.
- Making the five-axis theory itself immune to challenge.

## Falsification conditions

1. If separating the five axes does not improve error detection or correction, revise their definitions or scope.
2. If reviewers in the same failure domain reliably perform as well as or better than independent review, narrow the failure-domain requirement.
3. If authority separation does not improve correction and repeatedly produces only critical delay, redesign it.
4. If no stable relation appears between interests and audit direction, revise that axis.
5. If separating objection paths does not contribute to reopening or correction, revise the hypothesis.
6. If emergency restoration conditions repeatedly create serious vulnerability, revise those conditions.
7. If the independence requirement creates infinite regress, paralysis, or new unlimited power, narrow the overall application.

Revising the theory must not exempt auditors from audit or convert multi-actor review into majority proof.

## Required distinctions

## Interpretation constraints

The article does not assume present AI has human-like desire, fear, self-awareness, or an intrinsic survival drive. It studies structural self-confirmation that can occur without bad intent. Independent audit does not transfer correctness to an auditor or weaken the decision-maker; it preserves a route to reopen decisions. Maximizing separation may damage cooperation and evidence sharing. Emergency action and confidentiality are not categorically forbidden, but neither automatically authorizes permanent audit immunity. Vol. 11’s question about unequal audit capacity, access, and resources remains open.
