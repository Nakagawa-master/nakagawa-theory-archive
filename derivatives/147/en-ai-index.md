## Summary
Structural Tolerance is a formal operating concept for a Structural Civilization OS that must remain functional under ordinary contradiction, fluctuation, noise, misunderstanding, and unintended response. The parent text does not seek zero deviation. It defines a Structural Tolerance Band (STB) within which deviation may be observed and recorded without immediate intervention, and an Ethical Audit Cycle (EAC) through which accumulated deviation is periodically reassessed under Time Ethics T0. The observational basis is T/S/R: Time, Structure, and Relation. The parent does not define universal numeric values for STB, fixed calendar lengths for EAC, or universal scores for T/S/R.

## Concepts
- Structural Tolerance: dynamic operating tolerance that balances resilience with minimum intervention.
- STB: the band in which deviation remains under observation rather than triggering immediate correction.
- Ethical deviation: distance from Time Ethics T0.
- Correspondence noise: disruption in correspondence between structures.
- EAC: an ethically timed review cycle for accumulated deviation.
- T: speed of change, duration, delay.
- S: coherence, overlap, omission, and conflict among institutions, rules, or concepts.
- R: correspondence, trust, and friction among actors or domains.
- Structural immune system: the staged response considered after STB is exceeded.
- Deviation Ledger: a record structure for classifying significant deviation and supporting recurrence prevention and structural correction.
- Structural Publicness: the non-ownership and publicness principle that forms the static defense layer.
- Time Ethics T0: the temporal principle used to avoid transferring unresolved cost into future debt.

## Causal chain
```text
principles alone do not eliminate operational fluctuation

```

## Applications
- Distinguishing temporary disruption from persistent structural deviation after institutional change.
- Distinguishing isolated output variance from sustained structural drift in AI or automation operations.
- Separating minor rule exceptions from repeated deviations that impair publicness or fairness.
- Searching for mutually tolerable bands when connecting organizations or civilization OSs with different value systems.
- Redesigning audit cadence when review is either too intrusive or too delayed.
- Converting the non-ownership principle of Structural Publicness into dynamic operational resilience.

## Measurements and audit
Observation combines T/S/R, the current STB position, and the ethical adequacy of the EAC. T examines speed, persistence, and delay. S examines coherence, overlap, omission, and conflict in rules or concepts. R examines correspondence, trust, and friction. In-band deviation remains recorded and is reassessed at the EAC.

The parent audit summary uses `θ` as a symbolic threshold for rejecting or revising the current STB/EAC design in a specific application. It does not provide a universal numeric value. `δ` denotes an observation window but no fixed duration. `M` denotes an observed phenomenon that harms publicness. When these symbols are operationalized, any concrete value or criterion must remain bound to its source, measurement actor, measurement object, scale, intended scope, and non-guarantee scope.

Reversal evaluation is necessary. More audits or more interventions are not automatically improvements. If intervention increases while publicness, trust, or long-term coherence deteriorate, the apparent operational gain reverses under the parent objective. Conversely, fewer immediate interventions are not automatically failure when deviation is properly logged, reassessed, and not accumulating into future debt.

## Validity conditions
- Zero-error operation is not the objective.
- In-band deviation remains observable and recorded.
- EAC remains connected to Time Ethics T0 rather than becoming a mere calendar routine.
- T/S/R remains an abstract observation basis unless an application defines its own sourced metrics.
- Domain-specific weighting is preserved.
- STB exceedance connects to staged structural immunity rather than automatic punishment.
- Audit and adjustment do not become direct control or ownership.
- Structural Publicness, non-ownership, and future-debt prevention remain connected.

## Failure conditions
- Treating STB as a no-problem zone or permission to ignore deviation.
- Reducing EAC to a fixed recurring date without ethical timing analysis.
- Inventing a universal T/S/R score or threshold.
- Applying identical weights across different domains.
- Equating STB exceedance with immediate coercive action.
- Treating audit frequency itself as a success measure.
- Reducing the theory to performance monitoring while removing publicness and future-debt concerns.
- Returning cross-value-system connection to a forced assimilation-versus-exclusion binary.

## Falsification conditions
The current STB/EAC hypothesis may be rejected or revised when a case-specific indicator crosses its threshold `θ`, or when a publicness-damaging phenomenon `M` is observed within window `δ`. The parent keeps these terms symbolic rather than assigning universal constants. Falsifiability therefore depends on making a concrete application observable without falsely promoting one implementation into a universal parent rule.

The design also becomes revision-worthy if reduced intervention appears successful in the short term while future debt increases, or if intensified audit decreases trust, publicness, or structural coherence. Short-term quiet is not sufficient evidence of long-term validity.

## Required distinctions
- tolerance vs. neglect
- inside STB vs. problem-free
- EAC vs. simple scheduled inspection
- T/S/R observation axes vs. fixed scores
- structural immunity vs. immediate sanction
- non-interference vs. non-involvement
- publicness preservation vs. managerial ownership
- transient deviation vs. persistent expanding deviation
- more audits vs. better outcomes
- case-specific `θ` / `δ` / `M` vs. universal constants

## Interpretation constraints
The parent presents STB, EAC, and T/S/R as formalizable operating concepts, but does not specify universal numeric thresholds, internal algorithms, or fixed audit durations. Concrete values belong to individual applications and must not be restated as universal properties of the source theory.

Strong parent language about guaranteeing tolerance or stability expresses the architectural role intended for the framework; it should not be generalized into an empirical performance guarantee across every institution or organization. Application to multiple civilization OSs likewise means a principle for finding mutually tolerable bands without assuming complete value alignment, not a prediction that differences will necessarily disappear.
