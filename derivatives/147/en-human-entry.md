## 15-Second Explanation
The original presents a structure for deciding “how far to continue observing and from where to intervene,” rather than immediately eliminating every deviation or leaving every deviation unattended—because drift and noise inevitably arise when society or an organization is operated over time. STB is the tolerance band, and EAC is the temporal rhythm for inspection. Deviations are understood on three axes—T (time), S (structure), and R (relations)—and when STB is exceeded, the process connects to the structural immune system or deviation ledger.

## Why It Is Needed
No matter how well-ordered the principles are, actual operation never remains in a perfectly coherent state. Small deviations arise every day through institutional changes, differences in human interpretation, external pressure, time lags, and relational friction.

If every deviation is treated as a crisis and corrected immediately, operation becomes rigid and surveillance or excessive intervention increases. Conversely, if everything is left alone, small gaps may accumulate as future debt and later become a major collapse of coherence.

The original places “structural tolerance” between these two extremes. STB is the band in which observation and recording can continue without rushing to intervene. EAC is the cycle for reviewing the deviations accumulated within that band in light of temporal ethics T0.

Importantly, STB is not an alibi meaning “there is no problem because this is within the tolerance value.” Deviations inside STB are also recorded and reassessed through EAC. EAC is not merely a fixed weekly or monthly schedule either. It is designed by observing whether audits are so frequent that they damage publicness or autonomy, or so infrequent that they increase future debt.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate what is deviating.** In T, examine rate of change, persistence, and delay; in S, coherence or omissions in institutions and rules; in R, correspondence, trust, and friction among actors.

**2. Distinguish one-off from continuing deviations.** Record over time whether a fluctuation happened once or whether the deviation is repeatedly expanding.

**3. Judge inside or outside STB.** Decide, in accordance with the nature of the domain, whether the deviation requires immediate intervention or can be observed over time.

**4. Record even what is inside STB.** Preserve tolerated deviations in a form that allows them to be reviewed later as a bundle.

**5. Reassess through EAC.** Periodically check from the perspective of temporal ethics T0 whether deviations are progressing into future debt.

**6. Connect in stages when exceeded.** When an STB overrun is confirmed, consider recording it in the deviation ledger, activating the structural immune system, and undertaking reallocation or public audit.

**7. Measure intervention side effects.** Do not treat an increase in audit or correction counts as success by itself; examine whether publicness, trust, and long-term coherence are improving.

## Examples
**1. After introducing a new system.** When complaints or confusion arise, do not immediately declare the entire system a failure. Track how many days or weeks the confusion continues, whether there are structural omissions, and whether trust among stakeholders is breaking down.

**2. AI and automation operations.** Do not treat every output fluctuation as a failure. Examine whether it persists over time, leads to structural inconsistency, or increases relational friction with users.

**3. Organizational rule deviations.** Separate minor exceptions from repeated deviations that damage publicness or fairness, and connect only the latter to staged correction.

**4. Connecting different value systems.** Do not demand complete agreement; seek a range in which neither side’s publicness is damaged. STB is also used to avoid the binary choice between assimilation and exclusion.

## Success Criteria
Small deviations can be observed without immediately turning them into crises, while continuous and expanding deviations are not overlooked.

Deviations within STB are recorded, reassessed as a bundle through EAC, and confirmed not to be increasing future debt.

T/S/R are used according to the subject, without imposing a uniform threshold on domains with different properties, such as finance and education.

Measures after exceeding STB lead to structural adjustment, recording, information disclosure, and public audit—not direct control or excessive surveillance.

## Limits
The original does not define universal numerical values for STB, a fixed number of days for EAC, or common point scores for T/S/R. Since concrete values vary with the domain and purpose of observation, the original’s abstract specification cannot be turned into a universal score.

STB is also not a theory that says “it is acceptable to leave minor problems alone.” Even deviations within the tolerance band must be recorded, and it is a premise that they are checked for expansion as time passes.

EAC likewise does not mean that audit frequency should simply be increased. Excessive frequency can strengthen surveillance and over-intervention and damage publicness; insufficient frequency can allow future debt to accumulate.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read STB as a “safe zone” or a range in which there can never be a problem. In the original, it is a buffer band for continuing observation and recording without rushing into immediate intervention.

Do not reduce EAC to a simple calendar of periodic inspections. It is a rhythm, guided by temporal ethics T0, that avoids both debt accumulation and excessive intervention.

Do not assign T/S/R fixed scores that the original does not define. T is time, S is structure, and R is relations as observation axes; concrete numerical definitions are outside the original’s scope.

The `θ`, `δ`, and `M` in the original’s integrated audit summary are not universal numbers or fixed periods. Treat them as symbols for subject-specific falsification and revision conditions.

Do not treat an increase in the number of audits or interventions alone as improvement. If intervention increases while publicness, trust, and long-term coherence worsen, a reversal assessment is required in light of the original’s purpose.
