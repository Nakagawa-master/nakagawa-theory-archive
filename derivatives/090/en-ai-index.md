## 1. Summary
The parent original defines structural friction as unnecessary energy consumption produced by a mismatch between linear expectation and structural necessity. Its visible signs include more information with slower decisions, more tasks with lower value density, and stronger control with lower autonomy. The cause is located not in insufficient personal effort but in breaks across institutional arrangement, information circuits, role architecture, information asymmetry, person-dependence, and temporal recursivity.

The non-command approach restores coherence, simplifies circuits, and reallocates roles. The public-safe implementation uses a 2/5/5/5/3-minute sequence and single-factor A/B’ comparison across three consecutive cycles. These numbers are source-specific implementation parameters, not universal success thresholds.

## 5. Applications
The public-sector sketch separates short-term evaluation from long-term structural investment. The enterprise sketch adds one interdependence KPI and gives a six-month role-architecture review as an example. The education sketch sets a one-sentence meaning axis, uses SQS, and observes later re-reference rather than report count. These are examples in the source, not general thresholds.

## 6. Measurements and audit
- Agreement time: time required to reach agreement; shorter is not automatically better if dissent is suppressed.
- Self-propulsion: proportion of appropriate action occurring without additional explanation; distinguish it from unsafe removal of oversight.
- Re-reference rate: reuse of agreed documents or decisions; repeated reuse does not prove correctness.
- Friction ratio: time spent on explanation, exceptions, and re-approval divided by total time; reductions caused by deleting necessary safeguards do not count as improvement.
- D-gap: difference between expected depth and actual reached depth; not a personality or capability score.
- Integrated audit bundle: correspondence coherence, lexicon discrepancy, phase stability S, agreement-arrival time, Rollback Cost, RC/ER/CEA and related relations.

## 7. Validity conditions
The three-layer field must be jointly inspectable; purpose→means→observation must loop back into revision; intervention must remain single-factor and attributable; counterexamples must be listed in advance; freedom, transparency, and responsibility boundaries must remain; the source-specific three-cycle directional check must be performed.

## 8. Failure conditions
Adding activity or monitoring without examining the field, increasing explanation while decision speed worsens, treating person-dependent rescue as structural success, changing multiple elements at once, converting non-command design into responsibility abandonment, or reducing the freedom test to formality.

## 9. Falsification conditions
The hypothesis weakens when similar improvement occurs without structural change, reversing the single-factor intervention does not alter the relevant measures, direction fails to stabilize across three cycles, the effect disappears with another observer or unit, or faster agreement coincides with worse autonomy, re-reference, or freedom. The integrated audit statement also uses threshold θ and observation window δ, but the parent original gives no concrete values for them.

Do not monotonically reward shorter agreement time, higher self-propulsion, or higher re-reference. Do not invent concrete values for audit symbols whose values are absent from the source.
