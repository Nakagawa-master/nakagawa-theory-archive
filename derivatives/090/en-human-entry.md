## 15-Second Explanation
Structural friction is not a state of insufficient effort. It is a state in which the connections among institutions, information, roles, and time are distorted, turning invested effort into explanation, renewed approval, exception handling, and duplicate work. The parent original presents reverse phenomena such as decisions becoming slower as information increases, value density thinning as tasks increase, and autonomy falling as control is strengthened as signs of friction.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
When a problem occurs, adding meetings, documents, rules, KPIs, or monitoring does not necessarily solve it. If the linear expectation that “increasing A increases B” is misaligned with the actual structure, where multiple interests, time horizons, and roles are intertwined, the added force enters the same distorted channel. As a result, measures taken to address the problem multiply.

The parent original views a social field in three layers. Institutional arrangement consists of rules, procedures, and resource allocation. Information circuits consist of recording, disclosure, reference, and reuse. Role architecture is the functional arrangement and responsibility boundaries of humans, organizations, and AI. A state in which these three layers connect without contradiction and purpose → means → observation can circulate is “field coherence.”

Time is also not treated as one-way. Rather than only past → present → future, the design begins by placing present standards from the structure that should be established in the future, then rereads the past as a learning asset. This does not rewrite past facts. It updates which experiences are reused in present design according to future conditions.

## Practical Process
**1. Place the meaning axis first.** In the parent original’s public-safe edition, agree in one sentence on the standard to protect this time, using two minutes as a guide.

**2. Arrange the issues.** In five minutes, make proximity, contrast, and hierarchy visible; do not advance the discussion through speaking volume alone.

**3. SQS.** Use five minutes of Silence–Question–Silence to wait for autonomous completion through question → silence → renewed question.

**4. Minimum change.** In five minutes, limit what is changed at once to a single factor so that the scope of impact can be observed.

**5. Freedom test.** In three minutes, check whether the situation has been closed into Yes/No and whether multiple goods remain.

This 2/5/5/5/3-minute sequence is the original’s specific 20-minute frame. It is not a universal success time; treat it as a published implementation form.

## What to Measure
Agreement time, self-running degree, rereference rate, friction ratio, and the D-gap are the parent original’s main indicators. Shorter agreement time is not always better. If it was shortened by suppressing dissent, friction was merely hidden. Nor does greater self-running degree count as improvement if unsupervised operation increases where responsibility boundaries or confirmation are necessary. A high rereference rate has no value if it only means that an incorrect document is repeatedly used.

Compare interventions with A/B’. Compare prior method A with B’, which changes only one element under the same conditions, and check whether an effect in the same direction appears for three consecutive cycles. Three cycles are the original’s verification specification, not a general statistical guarantee.

## Applications
In public decision-making, separate short-term evaluation axes from long-term investment axes. Do not collapse short-term operating KPIs and long-term structural indicators such as standard-compliance rate, update cycle, and demographic-fit degree into one axis.

In business operations, one example incorporates an interdependent KPI into departmental KPIs. Observe delay time caused by other departments and whether local optimization has become friction for the whole. The original also gives an example of reviewing role architecture every six months.

In education and development, observe the number of times later rereferenced rather than lecture volume or number of report pages, after sharing the meaning axis in one sentence and securing question depth with SQS.

## Success Criteria
Judge structural improvement not only by outcomes but by channel health. Read the bundle of changes such as agreement advancing without increasing explanation, appropriate spontaneous action increasing without additional commands, documents and decisions being reused, the ratio of exception handling and renewed approval falling, and the gap between expected depth and reached depth shrinking.

At the same time, freedom, transparency, and responsibility boundaries must not be lost. If speed increases but conformity pressure also increases, if things become easier by removing relief or confirmation, or if the result cannot be reproduced when the person in charge changes, do not treat it as structural improvement.

## Counterevidence and Limits
If improvement of the same degree continues without a structural change, the result does not change when a single-factor change is reverted, the direction does not stabilize over three cycles, or the effect disappears when the person in charge changes, the explanatory power of the assumed structural fulcrum weakens.

In the integrated audit summary, correspondence coherence rate, lexical discrepancy rate, phase-stability S, time to agreement, Rollback Cost, RC/ER/CEA, and threshold θ and observation window δ are placed in counterevidence relations. The original does not provide concrete values for θ or δ, so do not guess and add them.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not reduce structural friction to simple stress or likes and dislikes in human relationships. Do not turn structural operation into a hidden technique for making people act as intended. Do not treat non-command as abandonment of responsibility, or simplification as indiscriminate deletion of safety controls. Do not aim for zero friction; retain necessary dissent, confirmation, and audit.
