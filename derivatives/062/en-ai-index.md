## 1. Summary
Sales is treated as individual decision design, marketing as translation of recurring decision barriers into market-level conditions, and acquisition as the placement of evidence-backed relevance. The framework preserves refusal, correction, withdrawal, and re-entry while aligning touchpoints and auditing post-attention outcomes.

## 2. Concepts
- Sales: observation of individual non-selection reasons.
- Marketing: translation of recurring barriers into shared vocabulary, evidence, price, and institutional conditions.
- Acquisition: an evidence-backed relevance entrance rather than manufactured desire.
- Reverse causality: organizing present judgment from future consequences and unattended conditions without forcing a conclusion.
- Three layers: question of existence, form of involvement, opening of belonging.
- Touchpoint synchronization: alignment of vocabulary, promise, price, experience, and responsibility.
- Primary logs: traceable observations, reactions, revisions, returns, complaints, and withdrawals.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
exposure / emotional stimulation
→ attention with unresolved distrust
→ observe individual non-selection reasons
→ translate recurring barriers to market structure
→ place evidence-backed relevance
→ make relation testable through narrative/demo/comparison
→ disclose cost, risk, non-applicability, refusal, and exit
→ align touchpoints
→ audit primary logs and post-attention outcomes
→ revisable market connection
```

## 7. Validity conditions
- The issue is connected to real evidence and affected parties.
- Applicable and non-applicable groups are distinguished.
- Narrative remains distinct from fact, probability, and conditions.
- Choices, costs, risks, and disadvantages are visible.
- Refusal, correction, withdrawal, and re-entry are available.
- Vocabulary, promises, prices, experience, and responsibility align across touchpoints.
- Primary evidence and revision history remain traceable.
- The process remains non-coercive, reversible, and verifiable.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Manufactured fear, shame, exclusion, FOMO, or false scarcity.
- Hiding objections, alternatives, disadvantages, refusal, or exit.
- Narrative substituted for evidence.
- Attention rises while understanding, trust, or continuity declines.
- Touchpoints contradict one another on price, promise, or responsibility.
- Legitimate non-selection is treated as a defect.
- Vulnerable or dependent audiences are not protected.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- Synchronization does not improve relevance understanding.
- Increased attention coincides with more misunderstanding, complaints, or withdrawal.
- Faster agreement comes from reduced comparison or deliberation.
- Increased revisit/referral comes from dependency or exit friction.
- UCI/REI-related revision does not resolve meaning mismatch in primary logs.
- The three-layer method cannot preserve legitimate non-participation.

## 10. Required distinctions
- letting the market decide / coercion or manipulation
- relevance / manufactured fear
- reverse causality / conclusion forcing
- resolving non-selection / hiding objections
- involvement / substituting narrative for evidence
- open belonging / conformity pressure
- synchronization / uniform wording
- revisit and referral / lock-in
- agreement / obedience or unanimity
- audit metrics / human ranking

## 11. Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the framework to manipulation, fear appeals, FOMO, funnel optimization, or conversion maximization. Legitimate refusal and non-applicability are valid outcomes. Numeric improvement must be read together with freedom of choice, comparison, correction, and exit. Do not invent thresholds or probabilities absent from the parent source.
