## 15-Second Explanation
This approach understands sales, marketing, and customer acquisition not as techniques for “moving people,” but as one continuous design that creates a state in which the other party understands their relationship to themselves, sees the evidence, cost, risks, and options, and can choose for themselves.

## What “Making Someone Decide” Means
“Making someone decide” here does not mean psychological manipulation or coercion. It means arranging the information needed for judgment while hiding no reasons not to choose, showing conditions of non-applicability, and preserving refusal, correction, and exit.

```text
Strong exposure, discounts, and emotional stimulation
→ Attention is gathered
→ But distrust, a sense of irrelevance, and reasons for refusal remain
→ Observe why people do not choose
→ Translate this into barriers shared by the market
→ Present real relevance as a question
→ Make it verifiable through stories, demos, and comparisons
→ Disclose costs, risks, non-applicability, refusal, and exit as well
→ A state is reached in which people can decide for themselves
```

## The Connection Between Sales, Marketing, and Customer Acquisition
**Sales** deals with one person’s decision. It observes reasons not to choose, such as price, anxiety, lack of understanding, timing, responsibility, and withdrawal conditions.

**Marketing** converts refusal reasons and distrust that recur across individuals into issues of market-wide vocabulary, evidence, touchpoints, pricing, and systems.

**Customer acquisition** creates an entry point for considering “Why does this relate to me, society, and the future?” before rushing to stimulate the desire to buy.

## Reverse-Causality Customer Acquisition
Instead of starting with “Do you want it?”, this approach organizes current decision material from the consequences of leaving something unattended, future conditions, and the scope of impact. However, it is not a method of forcing a conclusion by asserting the future or artificially amplifying fear.

## Three-Layer Structure
### 1. The Question of Existence
Pose an issue that is genuinely relevant to many people as a question, together with evidence and conditions of non-applicability.

### 2. The Form of Involvement
Use stories, demos, comparisons, personification, trials, and other forms so that an abstract problem can be examined as a relationship to oneself. Keep presentation distinct from fact.

### 3. Open Attribution
Show which values or objectives one can participate in, while also leaving routes for not participating, exiting, rejoining, and stating dissent.

## Synchronizing Touchpoints
If an advertisement is attractive but the promise changes in sales, the experience differs in the product, and responsibility disappears in support, the market connection breaks.

- Use vocabulary that returns to the same value core.
- Do not reveal price or disadvantage only afterward.
- Make the promise and the actual experience consistent.
- Align the scope of responsibility and withdrawal conditions across touchpoints.
- Make it possible to trace reactions and reasons for revision from primary logs.

## Applications
### B2B
Separate refusal reasons among users, decision-makers, and administrators, and connect unattended work, responsibility, cost, and implementation and withdrawal conditions.

### B2C
Do not incite trends or anxiety; make it possible to check the use case, comparison criteria, price, and refund and cancellation conditions in the same place.

### Social Issues
Show evidence, opposing views, scope of impact, and the choice not to participate at the same time, so that many people can consider their relationship to themselves.

### Theory and Thought
Rather than a definitive slogan, show what is explained, under what conditions it holds, and what it does not explain, then return to the parent source.

## How to Measure
The parent source treats understanding of relevance, movement toward decision-making, persistence of reasons not to choose, touchpoint alignment, UCI/REI, agreement time, revisits, referrals, misunderstandings, complaints, retractions, and exits as objects of observation.

These are not standalone success scores. Even if attention or conversion increases, it is a failure if comparability or free refusal decreases. Even if agreement becomes faster, it is not an improvement if the cause is omitted information or pressure. Even if revisits increase, it cannot be called success if they result from difficulty exiting or lock-in.

## Easily Misread Points
**This is not a method that uses FOMO.** Artificial urgency, exclusion, shame, and false scarcity are outside the parent source’s conditions of validity.

**This is not a method for eliminating reasonable refusal.** Resolve misunderstanding with evidence, and preserve legitimate disagreement as non-agreement.

**This is not a method for replacing facts with stories.** A story is an entry point for understanding; it does not replace evidence, probability, conditions, or limits.

**This is not a theory that leads everyone to the same conclusion.** The existence of people who are outside the target, do not participate, or withdraw is a premise.

## Boundaries and Limitations
- Without a real issue and evidence, it is impossible to create “relevance that cannot be ignored.”
- People with large power imbalances, including children, patients, and dependent persons, require special protection.
- In medicine, finance, law, and other fields, specialized regulations and duties of explanation take priority.
- Immediate action may take priority in an emergency warning, but factual warning must be distinguished from fear-based staging.
- Where refusal or exit is impossible, the ordinary market decision-making design cannot be applied as-is.
- Do not improve only the indicators while closing the observation window for dissent and failure.
