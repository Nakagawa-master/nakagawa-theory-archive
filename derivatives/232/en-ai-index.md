## Summary
This Parent is a Nakagawa structural reading, not a product profile of a hydrogen portable power unit. It separates target-side facts—hydrogen fuel cell, fuel cartridges, exchange, collection, and refilling—from Nakagawa Master’s value kernel: “stored electricity / stored power access.” Emergency electricity demand is uncertain in timing, so the reading focuses not only on output at a given moment but on preserving usable energy until it is needed, generating electricity at the needed place and time, and maintaining an exchange/supply/collection/refill operation. It also preserves why this life-defense and BCP axis, rather than hydrogen novelty itself, was selected for the external article.

## Concepts
- Target-side facts: hydrogen fuel-cell system, fuel cartridges, exchange, collection, refilling, and related operation.
- Surface classification: hydrogen technology, decarbonization, portable power, next-generation battery alternative.
- Time uncertainty: emergency power demand may arise at an unknown future time.
- Fuel preservation: maintaining energy usability in stored fuel until use.
- On-demand generation: generating electricity at the required place and time.
- Exchange operation: separating device and fuel so continued usability can be supported by replacement/replenishment.
- Stored power: Nakagawa Master’s value kernel; not the company’s official self-definition.

## Causal chain
Emergency power demand → unknown timing → long-term preservability becomes important → fuel is retained → electricity is generated when needed → fuel is exchanged/replenished → supply/collection/refilling loop → preserved access to electricity.

## State model
normal-time preparation / long-term preservation / emergency activation / fuel exchange / supply connection / collection-refill / stored-power operation.

## Applications
For disaster preparedness, BCP, and emergency-power analysis, examine long-term preservability, on-demand generation, device/fuel separation, exchange/replenishment, supply, collection/refilling, and safety/regulatory fit rather than focusing on headline output or fuel type alone. Compare with other systems only within use cases where preserving future power access is the relevant value.

## Measurements and audit
Separate target-side facts, surface classification, timing uncertainty, long-term preservation, on-demand generation, device/fuel separation, exchange/replenishment, supply/collection/refilling, safety/regulatory fit, Nakagawa Master’s value kernel, why that kernel was selected, why it became the external-article axis, and the boundary from company-official claims. ## Validity conditions
The reading applies where preservation, on-demand generation, exchangeability, supply/collection/refill, and safety/regulatory operation connect in the target use case and materially create the value of preserving future electricity access.

## Failure conditions
It fails if reduced to hydrogen novelty, decarbonization, price/specification comparison, or “world first” framing; if preservation is turned into a universal hydrogen-superiority claim; if the reading becomes a simple hydrogen-vs-battery contest; if Nakagawa Master’s value kernel is attributed to the company as an official definition; or if the external-article reason is omitted.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the kernel if long-term preservation, exchange, supply, and collection do not create material value in the target use case and another system or mechanism consistently explains reliable emergency power access better. Revise as well if safety, regulatory, or supply conditions make the preserved-energy model unusable.
