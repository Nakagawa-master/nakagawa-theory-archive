## Summary
The ethical burden of a structural custodian is an operating ethic for people who design or maintain structures. It does not make the custodian an unquestionable authority or a heroic self-sacrificing center. The custodian must continuously audit whether intervention is creating dependence, silence, concentrated responsibility, or an inability to hand over the role. “Stillness at the origin” is not neglect: it is observation of initial conditions and available freedom before acting. Intervention should be limited to the minimum effective action compatible with safety, with reasons and responsibility boundaries made visible. Dissent, pause, exit, alternatives, handover, and rollback must remain usable. Success is measured by autonomy, dependence, responsibility clarity, dissent availability, and handover capacity rather than the custodian’s presence. Strong emergency intervention can be justified when necessary, but emergency authority must not silently become permanent control.

```text

↓

↓

↓

↓
Audit autonomy and dependency
↓
Rollback or redistribute authority
```

## Applications

**2.** AI operations owners retain emergency stop authority without centralizing routine decisions.

**3.** Communities institutionalize dissent, exit, and administrator replacement.

**4.** Founders audit whether the system can operate without their constant presence.

## Validity conditions
- Authority and responsibility are explicit.
- Intervention necessity can be observed before action.
- Minimum effective action can be selected.
- Dissent, exit, and handover are practically usable.
- Reasons can be independently reviewed.
- Dependency and autonomy are measured.
- Rollback or handover is possible.

## Failure conditions
- Custodian judgment becomes untouchable.
- Self-sacrifice is treated as proof of ethics.
- Dissent is classified as structural destruction.
- Success is used to normalize intervention.
- Authority and responsibility are vague.
- The structure becomes dependent on an irreplaceable person.
- Emergency authority expands into routine control.

## Falsification conditions
- Reducing intervention does not improve autonomy.
- Increased handover capacity consistently destroys the structure.
- Centralized control consistently outperforms on safety, responsibility, and durability.
- Dissent channels do not reduce dependency or silence.
- Custodian self-audit does not prevent authority concentration.

## Required distinctions

- moderation / inaction

- emergency intervention / permanent control

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not romanticize leader self-sacrifice.
- Do not use good intentions as immunity for domination.
- Do not turn stillness into avoidance of explanation.
- Do not treat dissent or exit as disloyalty.
- Do not create a privileged custodian class.
- Do not prohibit strong intervention when immediate safety genuinely requires it.
