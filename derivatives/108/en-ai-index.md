## Summary
The ethical burden of a structural custodian concerns preserving origin, boundaries, rhythm, temperature, margin, auditability, and reagreement without turning stewardship into authority worship, hidden control, or permanent self-sacrifice.

## Central thesis
Successful custodianship preserves other actors' agency and makes the structure progressively less dependent on the custodian through minimum reversible intervention, explicit responsibility, records, and handoff.

## Causal chain
Shared structure → boundary/origin drift → personal control increases dependency → observe origin + intervene minimally → preserve dissent/exit → record and hand off → self-running structure.

## Measurements
Decision concentration, autonomy, handoff time, dissent availability, rollback success, personal dependency, burnout risk.

## Failure conditions
Charismatic authority, mandatory silence, self-sacrifice as policy, succession failure, dissent suppression, opaque responsibility.

## Falsification conditions
If custodianship systematically increases centralization, dependency, and burnout compared with ordinary distributed governance, the claim should be revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Stewardship ≠ ownership. Responsibility ≠ martyrdom. Stillness ≠ forced silence. Resonance ≠ obedience.
