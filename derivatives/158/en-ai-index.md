## Summary
AI and Environmental Side-Effect Control treats AI and environmental systems as different high-risk domains that share one audit structure: when a serious deviation crosses a line of irreversibility, recoverability can be lost. The line is associated with the Structural Tolerance Band (STB) and temporal ethics T0, and is placed above short-term gain, efficiency, and revenue. The parent states three principles: irreversible loss takes priority over gain; the line of irreversibility is not to be moved opportunistically through negotiation; and protecting it requires strong accountability. The No-Scapegoat Clause prevents decision responsibility from being shifted onto AI output, while the No-Immunity-By-OS Clause prevents use of the structural OS from becoming an environmental excuse. Decision responsibility remains with a human actor or a human+AI co-subjective decision structure. External audit, an Upper Auditor, and the deviation ledger preserve irreversibility protection, responsibility traceability, and public accountability.

## Concepts
- Line of irreversibility: a boundary beyond which recoverability is lost.
- STB: the structural tolerance band used to audit approach to or exceedance of the boundary.
- Temporal ethics T0: a frame that compares present gain with future debt.
- Priority protection of irreversibility: placing avoidance of unrecoverable loss above short-term optimization.
- Three principles: priority of unrecoverable loss, non-negotiability of the boundary, and high accountability for protective decisions.
- No-Scapegoat Clause: AI output cannot erase human-side decision responsibility.
- Human actor: the human responsibility holder for adoption, approval, and execution.
- Human+AI co-subjectivity: a decision structure that includes AI without allowing responsibility to disappear.
- No-Immunity-By-OS: using an OS or completing its procedure does not create immunity from environmental responsibility.
- External audit: comparison against standards or evaluative frames outside the internal OS.
- Upper Auditor: a higher-level review role for decisions that may cross an irreversible boundary.
- Deviation ledger: a corrective record for boundary approach, responsibility shifting, immunity claims, and missing external audit.

## Applications
- Separate AI output from human adoption, approval, and execution responsibility in consequential decisions.
- Evaluate short-term revenue together with distance to irreversible environmental loss.
- Audit AI infrastructure by connecting compute efficiency, energy/resource burden, irreversibility, and responsibility rather than treating them as separate scorecards.
- Check whether “we used the OS” or “we completed an audit” is being used as an immunity claim.
- Route high-risk decisions to external review and Upper Auditor review.
- Record responsibility ambiguity, immunity claims, and boundary approach in the deviation ledger.

## Measurements and audit
Audit targets include distance to an irreversible boundary, STB status, future debt, short-term gain pressure, separation between AI output and human adoption responsibility, explicitness of the responsibility holder, attempted OS-immunity claims, effectiveness of external audit, completion of Upper Auditor review, and corrective use of the deviation ledger.

The parent does not define universal numeric values for the irreversibility line or STB. If an implementation quantifies environmental load, AI deviation, boundary distance, responsibility ambiguity, or audit effectiveness, it must state the value, source, measurement actor, measurement target, scale, observation period, scope, and non-guaranteed range.

The integrated audit summary uses audit cycles, T/S/R, and consistency of the public audit bundle as re-agreement conditions. `θ` is a case-specific reject/revise threshold, `δ` is a case-specific observation window, and `M` is a case-specific phenomenon that triggers reconsideration. They are not universal constants.

Reversal evaluation is required: higher revenue, efficiency, throughput, or automation does not count as structural success if irreversibility risk, future debt, responsibility ambiguity, or weakness of external audit rises. Conversely, lower short-term revenue or speed is not by itself failure if irreversible risk falls and responsibility/auditability improves.

## Validity conditions
- Place irreversibility protection above short-term gain, efficiency, and revenue.
- Define the irreversible boundary and its evidence for the specific domain.
- Connect present gain to future debt through T0.
- Do not use AI output to erase responsibility.
- Anchor decision responsibility in a human actor or human+AI co-subjectivity.
- Do not use OS adoption as environmental immunity.
- Preserve external standards and third-party audit.
- Route consequential boundary-risk decisions to Upper Auditor review.
- Use the deviation ledger as a corrective record.
- Treat `θ`, `δ`, and `M` as case-bound falsification structures.

## Failure conditions
- Ignore approach to irreversible loss because short-term gain is high.
- Move the irreversible boundary opportunistically for business convenience.
- Say “the AI decided” in order to erase the responsibility holder.
- Assign ethical or institutional responsibility to AI alone.
- Treat OS adoption or completed audit as environmental immunity.
- Close high-risk decisions inside internal self-audit only.
- Reduce external audit to a nominal form while excluding external standards.
- Turn the deviation ledger into a punishment register.
- Introduce universal fixed thresholds not defined by the parent.

## Falsification conditions
If case-specific measures of boundary distance, STB status, future debt, responsibility ambiguity, or external-audit effectiveness cross their configured `θ`, or if a phenomenon `M` is observed within window `δ`, and the clause still fails to improve irreversibility protection, accountability, and external auditability, the current hypothesis must be rejected or revised.

Revision is especially required if exceptions to irreversibility protection become routine for short-term gain, responsibility becomes less explicit as AI use grows, OS use becomes an immunity claim, or external audit becomes merely nominal.

## Required distinctions
- line of irreversibility vs. temporary performance loss
- STB exceedance vs. ordinary operational error
- structural homology of AI/environmental side effects vs. identity of the phenomena
- priority protection of irreversibility vs. blanket shutdown of activity
- human / human+AI responsibility vs. AI-only responsibility
- AI autonomy vs. decision responsibility
- No-Scapegoat Clause vs. prohibition of AI use
- No-Immunity-By-OS vs. rejection of the OS
- external audit vs. internal self-evaluation
- short-term rationality vs. long-term homeostasis
- higher gain/efficiency vs. structural success
- case-bound `θ` / `δ` / `M` vs. universal fixed values

## Interpretation constraints
Do not expand irreversibility protection into a general rule that any risk requires total shutdown. AI and environmental damage are not the same phenomenon; the commonality is the audit structure of irreversible-boundary exceedance. The No-Scapegoat Clause is not a ban on AI use. Co-subjectivity does not assign standalone responsibility to AI. No-Immunity-By-OS does not negate the OS; it prohibits responsibility exemption through OS use. Do not invent a fixed external-audit institution, cycle, STB value, `θ`, `δ`, or `M` when the parent does not define one.
