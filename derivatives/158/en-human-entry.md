## 15-Second Explanation

The parent original understands the different high-risk domains of AI and the environment through a common audit structure: crossing an irreversibility line means losing the possibility of recovery. The irreversibility line is a boundary of no return indicated by STB and is placed above short-term profit, efficiency, and revenue. At the same time, it prohibits eliminating human decision responsibility on the grounds of AI output, or treating use of the structural OS as exempting one from responsibility for environmental burdens, and fixes accountability through third-party audits and the Upper Auditor.

## Why It Is Necessary

Short-term rationality can undervalue long-term losses that eliminate the possibility of recovery. Especially for AI and the environment, current efficiency or profit may look high, but once the irreversibility line is crossed, future permanence is lost.

The original connects this problem to STB and temporal ethics T0, establishing the priority “protect irretrievable losses before profit.” This is not a general argument for completely stopping activities that carry risk. It is a structure that defines the meaning and evidence of irreversibility for each subject and audits approach to that boundary from the perspective of long-term consistency.

Another problem is the disappearance of responsibility. As AI becomes more advanced, explanations such as “AI decided” or “it was AI's fault” become more likely. The original separates AI autonomy from decision-making responsibility and fixes the responsible subject as human, or as a joint subjectivity of human + AI.

On the environmental side as well, it prohibits treating the fact that one “uses the structural OS” or “has followed the audit procedure” as a pardon. The OS is not a device that lightens responsibility, but a structure for clarifying responsibility lines and auditability.

## Practical Process
**1. Define the irreversibility line.** State what would cause the possibility of recovery to be lost, together with the subject, evidence, and observation period.

**2. Observe distance from STB.** Distinguish ordinary operational error from approach to or exceeding of the irreversibility line.

**3. Put short-term gains and future debt in the same decision table.** Do not close judgment around profit, speed, or efficiency; compare long-term losses through T0.

**4. Separate AI output and decision responsibility.** Make it traceable who is responsible at each stage: AI recommendation, adoption, approval, and execution.

**5. Do not use OS adoption as grounds for exemption.** Do not accept explanations such as “it is safe because the OS was used” or “there is no responsibility because it was audited.”

**6. Connect third-party audits.** Do not close high-risk judgments with internal evaluation alone; retain comparison with external standards and evaluation indicators.

**7. Send major decisions to the Upper Auditor.** Re-audit decisions approaching the irreversibility line from the perspectives of long-term consistency and correspondence structure.

**8. Record in the deviation ledger.** Record approach to the irreversibility line, ambiguity of responsibility, pardon-seeking behavior, and absence of external audit as structural deviations, making correction possible.

## Examples of Application
**1. Decision-making using AI.** Even when adopting an AI recommendation, do not erase the responsible subject on the grounds that “AI produced it.”

**2. Businesses with environmental burdens.** Even when profitability is high, if approach to the irreversibility line is suspected, prioritize auditing recoverability and future debt.

**3. AI infrastructure.** Do not evaluate AI processing efficiency and energy, resource, and environmental burdens separately as successes; consider irreversibility and responsibility lines together.

**4. Governance.** Do not justify high-risk acts merely by the formality of OS adoption or having been audited; maintain external audits and explainability.

## Success Criteria

The meaning and grounds of the irreversibility line are explicit, and approach to STB is audited ahead of short-term profit.

Even when AI is used, the responsible subject for recommendation, adoption, approval, and execution is traceable, and the responsibility line is not severed by an explanation of “AI's fault.”

Use of the OS does not become an environmental pardon, and third-party audits and external standards function substantively.

Even if short-term profit or speed declines, that decline alone is not judged a failure if irreversibility risk, future debt, and ambiguity of responsibility decrease.

## Limits

The parent original does not establish common fixed thresholds for all environmental burdens or AI deviations. Irreversibility lines, STB, `θ`, `δ`, and `M` must be defined and observed by subject.

AI and the environment are not identical as phenomena. What they share is the audit structure of exceeding an irreversibility line.

Also, the parent original's fixing of responsibility is not a ban on AI use. It separates AI autonomy and decision-making responsibility so that responsibility does not disappear.

## Misreading Prevention

Do not broaden protection of the irreversibility line into a general argument that all activity must stop if there is even slight danger.

Do not turn the ban on AI scapegoating into a claim that AI must not be used.

Do not turn joint subjectivity into a concept that assigns responsibility to AI alone.

Do not turn No-Immunity-By-OS into a denial of the structural OS itself.

Do not arbitrarily specify third-party auditing as a fixed obligation of a particular institution.

Do not add fixed values undefined by the original to the irreversibility line, STB, `θ`, `δ`, or `M`.
