## 15-Second Explanation

The deviation ledger is not a blacklist that exposes violators and preserves the memory of punishment. It is a recovery device in Layer B that handles suspected loss of correspondence with the origin through a reversible flow—**observation → under review → refutation/correction → confirmation only when necessary → recovery → removal**—and records listing and removal with equal weight.

## Why It Is Necessary

When provenance evidence such as the origin signature, NCL-ID, audit summary, and correspondence block is severed in an external implementation, recording nothing makes it impossible to verify origin evaporation or recurrence. On the other hand, if the other party is exposed, judged malicious, and moved toward sanctions as soon as suspicion arises, defensive reactions and conflict increase, closing the very entrance to correction and recovery.

The parent avoids this either-or choice. It first establishes an “under review” state and opens an official channel for refutation and correction rather than a maliciousness judgment. It then confirms intentionality, willingness to recover, and direct structural impact. If the party recovers through prompt correction, definitive listing is in principle unnecessary. Only clear deviations are made public to the minimum extent necessary; once recovery occurs, “removed” is announced at the same level and with the same weight as the listing.

The deviation ledger is Layer B of a three-layer structure. Layer A registers positive connections such as co-creation logs, supervision, and certification in the NCL Registry; Layer C handles self-correction in which the theory itself is amended and re-signed in response to external observations. It is important not to confuse external implementation audits with self-correction of the theoretical body.

## Practical Process

**1. Observe a suspected deviation.** Confirm facts identified by the parent as examples, such as intentional deletion of the origin signature, severing provenance evidence including the NCL-ID and audit summary, or malicious repeated recurrence after recovery procedures have been presented.

**2. Place it “under review” first.** Do not determine guilt or maliciousness from a report alone. Show the reported area or difference and open an official channel for refutation and correction.

**3. Examine three determination axes.** Confirm intentionality, willingness to recover, and direct structural impact. In particular, when willingness to recover is shown and correction is made promptly, definitive ledger listing is in principle unnecessary.

**4. Publish the minimum only when confirmation is necessary.** Only when human review or equivalent judgment finds a clear deviation, publish only necessary information such as the target URL, Diff-ID, recovery procedure, and removal status.

**5. After recovery, show removal with equal weight.** Do not bury “removed” in a past log; announce it at the same level and with the same layout as the listing.

**6. Minimise personal information.** As a rule, do not publish personal names or private contact details of corporate officers. After removal, delete personal information unnecessary for identification and retain only the minimum summary and recovery time required.

**7. Audit recovery capacity monthly.** Review the number of listings, number removed, removal rate, average response days, and self-correction rate as indicators of the ledger’s recovery operation, not as sanction scores.

## Applications

**1. A citation error corrected promptly.** The parent excludes it from definitive listing. It values the fact that correction and recovery worked more than the error itself.

**2. Criticism, verification, or rebuttal with an origin signature.** Being critical of the parent is not itself a deviation. Research and educational use that preserves sources and correspondence is also excluded from definitive listing.

**3. Intentional severing of provenance evidence.** When origin signatures or NCL-IDs are intentionally removed and malicious repetition continues even after recovery procedures are shown, it may be considered for definitive listing.

**4. An external observation about the theoretical body.** This belongs to Layer C, not Layer B. The observer is treated as a co-creator who preserves theoretical consistency, not as an attacker; when necessary, the theory is amended and re-signed, leaving a record of correction and recovery.

## Success Criteria

The first condition is that “under review” truly functions as an entrance to refutation and correction, without moving from suspicion alone to personal evaluation or social sanction. Prompt voluntary correction must be able to avoid definitive listing, and even in confirmed cases, removal after recovery must be shown with the same visibility as the listing.

Monthly metrics concern the case state and recovery operation, not the danger level of a deviating person. The number of listings and removals is the number of cases during the target month; the removal rate is the ratio during the applicable period under the parent’s definition; average response days is response time with a defined starting point and target set; and the self-correction rate is the proportion of target cases that ended through voluntary correction after notification but before listing. These metrics must not be repurposed as sanction scores, credit ratings, or probabilities of legal violation for people or companies.

## Limits

Do not make definitive judgments about actual cases from the public explanation alone. The evaluation of evidence in an individual case, operation of under review/confirmed/removed states, and publication scope return to the parent and the official deviation ledger’s formal procedures.

Respect for anonymity and deletion of unnecessary personal information after removal are part of recovery design. Not permanently fixing a recovered person or organisation through past deviation information alone is connected to giving listing and removal equal weight.

## To Avoid Misreading

Do not turn “under review” into a guilt or maliciousness judgment. Do not turn the deviation ledger into exposure, a blacklist, a person ranking, or a sanctions register. The record concerns facts, times, Diff-IDs, recovery procedures, and removal status—not personality.

Do not turn monthly metrics into penalty points. When measured values are unavailable, do not add target values, thresholds, or probabilities of occurrence. Do not conflate Layers A, B, and C; preserve positive-connection registration, recovery from external deviation, and theoretical self-correction as distinct ethical positions.
