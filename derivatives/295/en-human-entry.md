## 15-Second Explanation
“An expert said it, so it is correct” becomes “therefore it need not be checked,” while repair and loss from failure are pushed onto the field. The Parent calls this “authority immunity.” It does not reject experts; it preserves grounds, scope, uncertainty, objection, correction, and responsibility.

## Why It Happens
Complexity requires delegating judgment to experts, departments, AI, or certifiers. Delegation becomes harmful when it means no rechecking. Field counterevidence is dismissed as poor operation or an exception, the upstream model does not learn, and the same failure repeats.

## Where the Bad Causality Lies
The issue is not one expert being wrong once. It is the loop: authority delegation → skipped verification → field exceptions ignored → failure → field repairs → upstream evaluation preserved → model not updated → recurrence.

## Practical Process
1. Record grounds and data.
2. State scope and uncertainty.
3. Create a non-punitive objection route.
4. Map decision, approval, implementation, benefit, and impact.
5. Return repair work, user loss, and extra cost to upstream evaluation.
6. Stop, correct, and remedy wrong judgment.
7. Revise standards, procedures, and authority.

## Examples of Application
Do not treat every field exception to a headquarters standard as operational error; return it to the standard’s assumptions. Even high-accuracy AI needs human review and explicit out-of-scope areas and uncertainty. Audits should retain evidence, opposing views, and revision history.

## Success Criteria
Grounds and scope are traceable; uncertainty is visible; field evidence reaches upstream; objectors are not punished; decision and implementation are distinguishable; repair cost is not left only in the field; wrong judgment can stop and be corrected; repeated failure updates the model and institution.

## Limitations
Reviewing every low-impact decision repeatedly can cause delay; vary review density by impact, uncertainty, and irreversibility. Field implementation can also be causal, so do not push responsibility upstream by default.

## Misreading Prevention
Authority immunity is not anti-expert activism. Expertise is necessary; expertise and verifiability must coexist. The minimum core is **never stopping verification because of who spoke, and returning failure information, responsibility, and repair cost upstream**.
