## 15-Second Explanation
The original reconsiders AI ethics through “connection responsibility.” It finds the “tears in correspondence” arising between technology, institutions, and values, then sews them back together through three layers—correspondence, temporal ethics, and structural design—to rebuild the ethical intelligence needed by an AI society. It does not merely appoint one person responsible; it also traces where the lines of impact and responsibility were severed.

## Why It Is Needed
When AI enters society, technology alone cannot explain outcomes. Social meaning emerges only when what institutions permit, how organizations operate, and what humans choose as values are connected. Even a high-performing model cannot produce socially predictable results if the institutions and value judgments connected to it do not fit together.

When this connection between technology, institutions, and values breaks, the original’s “tear in correspondence” appears. It becomes difficult to see which decision produced which impact and who is responsible for what. Present convenience or efficiency can send burdens to future users, institutions, labor, and trust while the burden line is cut off from the present decision.

Connection responsibility is not a way to distribute responsibility so finely that nobody is accountable. It traces how a responsible actor’s decision was converted into impact through particular technology–institution–value connections, while still identifying the concrete responsible actor. Actor responsibility and connection responsibility are both retained.

The three layers have different roles. Correspondence observes “where things do not fit.” Temporal ethics asks “what present optimization is sending into the future.” Structural design asks “whether the connection can be returned to a form that can be corrected later.” It goes beyond adding explanations to making the actual connection correctable.

Through these three layers, the original aims to rebuild the “ethical intelligence” needed by an AI society. Ethical intelligence does not mean producing more ethical language. It means being able to find tears, locate the time line of burdens, reconnect impact and responsibility lines, and check for new tears after correction.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate technology, institutions, and values.** Place AI capability, institutional authority/operation, and embedded value judgments separately. Do not complete the ethical issue within one layer.

**2. Trace the impact line.** Check which institutional decisions the technical output passes through and what impact it sends to which people. Observe actual operation, not just explanatory text.

**3. Find tears in correspondence.** Identify where the technical purpose, institutional operation, value judgment, and actual impact fail to fit.

**4. Separate the responsible actor from connection responsibility.** Make clear who made the decision while also checking the connection structure through which it passed. Drawing the connection must not erase individual responsibility.

**5. Add time.** Ask whether present efficiency, convenience, or optimization is sending ethical debt to future users or institutions.

**6. Redesign the connection.** Rebuild the problematic relationship so it can be corrected and updated later. Do not treat a fixed connection as inevitable.

**7. Re-establish correspondence.** After correction, check whether technology, institutions, and values fit again and whether the burden was merely shifted to another actor.

**8. Reintegrate as ethical intelligence.** Preserve observation, time audit, correction, and re-establishment of correspondence as continuous connection management, not a one-time incident response.

## Examples
**1. Introducing AI.** Check performance together with institutional authority, scope of use, value judgments, and user impact as one connection line.

**2. Evaluating AI.** Look not only at whether an output is correct, but at which institutional decision it enters and whose opportunity, evaluation, or authority it affects.

**3. Organizational operation.** After a problem, do not only identify one responsible person. Find where the correspondence and impact lines broke, then repair the wiring that would reproduce the failure.

**4. Data operation.** Trace the connection among data, evaluation criteria, institutional decisions, and user impact so embedded value judgments become visible.

**5. Long-term operation.** Use temporal ethics to ask whether present efficiency is sending ethical debt to future institutions, users, or social trust.

**6. Checking after redesign.** Re-establish correspondence to see whether correcting one tear shifted the burden to another actor. Do not treat one correction as completion.

## Success Criteria
Technology, institutions, and values must be read as one connection structure without confusing the layers. The tear in correspondence, impact line, responsibility line, and ethical debt to the future must be visible on the same causal line.

The concrete responsible actor and the failure in the connection must both be identified. Do not name an actor while leaving the structure untouched, or describe the structure while erasing the actor.

Structural design must return the connection to a correctable form, and after correction it must be possible to confirm that technology, institutions, and values correspond again. Check that the burden has not been transferred to another actor.

Success is not an increase in explanatory material. It is the reconstruction of ethical intelligence in which the actual connection is correctable and the impact, responsibility, and time lines can be checked again.

## Limits
The original is not a general legal-responsibility determination or a contract-practice manual. Its center is the tear in correspondence arising in the connection among technology, institutions, and values, and making that connection correctable through temporal ethics and structural design.

It is not an article centered on fixed responsibility KPIs or numerical thresholds. Any quantification remains constrained by the original’s specific observables: tears in correspondence, ethical debt to the future, and the correctability of connections.

Connection responsibility is not a concept for erasing concrete actor responsibility. Who made the decision and which connection failed must be retained together.

High explainability is not the same as ethical correspondence. Even when something can be explained, the tear remains if the actual impact or burden line has not been repaired.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not turn connection responsibility into a simple responsibility-allocation table.

Do not blur the responsibility of an individual actor by invoking connections.

Do not reduce AI ethics to technical safety alone or institutional theory alone.

Do not drop the three layers of correspondence, temporal ethics, and structural design.

Do not equate explainability with restoration of correspondence.

Do not make correction into shifting the burden to another actor.

Do not add stop rights, rollbacks, legal immunity, or responsibility scores absent from the original as central concepts.
