## Summary
Connection responsibility treats AI ethics as a chain linking purpose, data and model selection, known limitations, output, human adoption, deployment, external effects, appeals and remedies. Multiple actors do not imply that responsibility disappears. Each connection point should record what an actor knew, chose, could stop, and was obliged to correct. Affected-party feedback must travel upstream, and ethical quality is measured by remedy and recurrence reduction rather than explanation alone.

## Concepts
connection responsibility, AI ethics, responsibility graph, provenance, model limits, human adoption, affected parties, appeal, remedy, stop authority, recurrence prevention, correspondence

## Causal chain
Purpose → technical selection → output → human adoption → real-world effect → connection failure → appeal → responsibility tracing → remedy → redesign.

## Applications
Hiring AI, clinical support, public-sector AI, generative publishing, education assessment.

## Measurements and audit
Traceability, limit disclosure, adoption logging, appeal handling, remedy time, stop success, recurrence rate.

## Validity conditions
Signed purpose, known limits, adoption responsibility, affected parties, appeal, stop, remedy, upstream correction.

## Failure conditions
Blaming AI alone, blaming end users alone, missing connection logs, excluding affected parties, explanation-only responses, downstream warnings only.

## Falsification conditions
If connection responsibility does not improve remedy or recurrence and a simpler single-owner model consistently performs better, weaken the framework.

## Required distinctions
AI contribution/AI responsibility; generation/adoption; technical limit/operational error; shared responsibility/dilution; appeal/hostility; explanation/remedy; log/liability.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not automatically assign moral personhood to AI. Do not use the framework for vendor or user immunity. Avoid excessive surveillance logs. Legal responsibility requires proper procedures.
