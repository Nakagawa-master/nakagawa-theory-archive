## Summary
The parent redefines AI ethics through “Connection Responsibility.” Its source-specific center is the correspondence gap that appears among technology, institutions, and values, and the three-layer response of Correspondence, Temporal Ethics, and Structural Design. Ethical intelligence is reconstructed by finding where connections break, tracing the resulting responsibility and impact lines, and making those connections correctable rather than reducing ethics to model performance or a list of liable actors.

AI acquires social meaning only when technical functions enter institutions and are shaped by value judgments. A model can perform well while the institutional use of its output, the authority attached to that output, or the values embedded in evaluation create a different social effect. When those layers stop corresponding, responsibility and impact lines fragment. Present optimization can then transfer ethical debt into future users, institutions, labor, or social trust while the path from decision to burden becomes difficult to see.

Connection Responsibility does not erase responsible actors. It keeps the actor visible while adding the connection through which the actor’s decision becomes social impact. A single-actor account can be too narrow when the failure lies in the relation among technology, institutional authority, value criteria, and downstream effects. Conversely, mapping a complex connection cannot be used to make concrete responsibility disappear. Actor responsibility and connection responsibility must remain simultaneously inspectable.

The three layers perform different functions. Correspondence identifies mismatch among technical purpose, institutional operation, value judgment, and actual effect. Temporal Ethics places future ethical burden on the same responsibility line as present optimization. Structural Design changes the connection itself so it can later be corrected and updated. Redesign is not complete until correspondence is checked again and the correction has not simply transferred burden to another actor. Ethical intelligence is therefore the capacity to detect gaps, trace time and impact, repair connections, and recheck the resulting structure.

## Applications
- Review AI deployment by separating technical performance, institutional authority/operation, and embedded value judgments while tracing how they connect.
- Trace the impact line from AI output through institutional decision to affected people, opportunities, evaluations, or rights.
- Identify concrete responsible actors while separately locating the correspondence gap or connection failure that enabled the effect.
- Trace ethical debt transferred from present optimization into future users, institutions, labor, or social trust.
- Audit data, evaluation criteria, institutional decisions, and downstream effects as one connected structure without collapsing the layers into one another.
- Design connections so assumptions, authority, and operation remain correctable when circumstances change.
- Recheck the redesigned structure for restored correspondence and for new burden transfer to another actor.
- Evaluate explainability separately from actual correspondence restoration and correctability.

## Measurements and audit
The parent is not centered on a generic responsibility score or fixed KPI system. Audit attention therefore stays on the source-grounded connection structure:
- **Technology–institution–value connection.** Which technical function connects to which institutional operation and value judgment.
- **Correspondence-gap location.** Where intended purpose, institutional use, value criteria, and actual effect diverge.
- **Responsibility-line fragmentation.** Where the path from a decision-making actor to the resulting outcome becomes unclear.
- **Impact-line fragmentation.** Where benefits, burdens, constraints, or opportunities reaching affected actors become hidden.
- **Future ethical debt.** Burdens transferred from present optimization into future users, institutions, labor, or social trust.
- **Connection correctability.** Whether assumptions, authority, data use, or institutional operation can be changed after deployment.
- **Responsible-actor visibility.** Whether structural complexity is being used to hide the concrete decision-making actor.
- **Restoration of correspondence after redesign.** Whether technology, institution, value, and actual effect correspond more closely after correction.
- **Secondary gap or burden transfer.** Whether solving one gap merely moves the burden to another actor or future period.
- **Explainability versus actual repair.** Whether an improved explanation is accompanied by a real change in the connection and its effects.

A clearly identified responsible actor does not remove the need to inspect the connection if the same structure can reproduce the failure. A detailed connection map also does not satisfy Connection Responsibility when it makes actor responsibility disappear. Actor responsibility, impact, time, correctability, and connection structure must remain jointly visible.

## Validity conditions
- Do not close AI ethics within technical performance alone.
- Read technology, institutions, and values within one structure while preserving their distinctions.
- Observe correspondence gaps and downstream impact lines.
- Connect present optimization and future burden through Temporal Ethics.
- Treat the connection itself as an additional responsibility unit.
- Preserve both responsible-actor accountability and connection responsibility.
- Use Structural Design to restore correctable connections.
- Recheck correspondence after redesign.
- Check for secondary gaps and transferred burdens.
- Distinguish explainability from actual correctability and repair.

## Failure conditions
- Treating technical performance as the whole of AI ethics.
- Separating institutions and values as irrelevant external conditions.
- Reducing responsibility to finding one actor and leaving the enabling connection unchanged.
- Using connection complexity to erase concrete actor responsibility.
- Disconnecting present optimization from future ethical debt.
- Treating explanation alone as correspondence repair.
- Replacing Connection Responsibility with generic contractual or liability allocation.
- Treating one redesign as final without checking new gaps or burden transfer.
- Equating explainability with correctability.

## Falsification conditions
If expected correspondence gaps among technology, institutions, and values are persistently absent, future ethical debt from present optimization is not observed, and a single-actor account consistently explains responsibility and impact more accurately than a connection-based account, the Connection Responsibility reading should be revised.

The redesign account is also revisable. If applying Connection Responsibility, Correspondence, Temporal Ethics, and Structural Design does not improve visibility of responsibility/impact lines or correctability, and instead merely diffuses responsibility across a more complex map, the operating structure should be revised. Source-absent responsibility scores or fixed thresholds are not introduced to protect the hypothesis.
