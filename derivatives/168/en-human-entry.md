## 15-Second Explanation

Nakagawa-style sales is not a technique for persuading someone to buy a product. It is a sales OS that uses questions to read the absence and its causal structure that the person has not yet seen clearly, builds the most natural path to fulfillment from that absence rather than from the product, arranges both value and compensation, and creates a state in which the person can decide by their own will. The feeling that one wants to close is treated not as a signal to intensify persuasion, but as an inspection signal that some structure remains incomplete.

## Why It Is Needed

When sales results are poor, it is easy to turn toward downstream techniques such as asking more questions, adding more explanation, improving objection handling, or creating deadlines. The Parent Original instead removes the premise that sales is fundamentally a “technique for selling.” A customer’s words are only the surface; behind them are an unease not yet verbalized even by the customer, an undefined absence, how that absence arose, why it has remained unresolved, and the customer’s values and decision criteria. Questions in Nakagawa-style sales are not a trick for steering someone toward a product, but lightweight probes for reading this cognitive structure. “Calculating fulfillment” begins once the causal shape of the absence is visible: rather than merely deciding whether one’s own product fits, it considers what combination, order, and form of involvement would produce natural fulfillment without strain for the customer. If the offering naturally fits, it is presented quietly without adding excessive explanation or anxiety. If the offering alone is insufficient, the solution is neither to sell it while leaving the gap nor to detach from the matter as irrelevant; the fulfillment path is recalculated to include knowledge, networks, additional design, contract form, and time period. However, if the support scope is expanded without limit, the sales side becomes volunteer labor and cannot continue, so the boundaries of the basic contract, additional support, and compensation must also be made explicit as structure. When the absence, solution path, future image, and compensation structure are aligned, the decision is more likely to converge naturally from the person’s understanding rather than being produced by pressure. Thus, the moment one feels like closing is an audit point indicating that recognition, solution fit, explanation of compensation, future image, or remaining options may be incomplete. The Parent Original also positions Nakagawa-style sales and Structural Wu Wei Ziran not as a later combination of two theories, but as different-scale displays of a practical OS and a higher-level OS that were on the same structural line from the beginning. The natural-convergence rate and satisfaction level of the integrated audit, condition Z, T/S/R, θ, δ, and M are treated not as universal closing guarantees or fixed values, but as observation conditions for rejecting or revising connection model A for each subject.

## Practical Process

**1.** Do not treat the customer’s first request as the answer. Ask when and what has been missing, why it remains unresolved, and which decision criteria are involved; record the surface statement separately from the cognitive structure.

**2.** Organize the confirmed information as the causal shape of the absence, not as product features. Clarify it until the person can explain their own absence in their own words, without filling the blank with a sales-side assumption.

**3.** Starting from the absence, calculate the required elements, sequence, participants, period, and options. If one’s own product is only part of that path, show its position without exaggerating it.

**4.** If the offering alone is insufficient, consider knowledge, referrals, additional support, and contract design, while making the responsibility scope and compensation explicit so that unpaid overextension is not built into the structure.

**5.** Present the absence, solution path, future image, and cost and contract conditions in a form the customer can understand. Do not add excessive explanation, anxiety, or deadline pressure; leave room for independent comparison.

**6.** When you feel like closing, before using a technique, recheck which part of recognition, fulfillment fit, future image, compensation explanation, or options remains incomplete, and return to the stage before the proposal if necessary.

**7.** After the case, observe not only closing but also satisfaction, fit, cancellation, disputes over additional support, and dependence on forced closing by subject-specific δ. If θ or M is encountered, revise hypothesis A to A'.

## Examples of Application

**1.** In enterprise-system sales, even if a customer says, “I want an analytics function,” do not look only at the feature request. Check whether the delay in decision-making arises from missing data, permissions, or meeting structure. If the company’s own function cannot fill the gap alone, show another fulfillment path that includes operational design.

**2.** In a recruiting-support proposal, if the request to “increase applications” hides an ambiguous job definition or friction in the selection experience, do not simply increase advertising volume; return to a design that fits the starting point of the absence. Set the scope and compensation for any additional support.

**3.** With a customer hesitating over a high-priced personal service, do not use scarcity or fear to force a decision. Organize the person’s purpose, alternatives, financial burden, and expected change. If the service is found not to fit, not selling it is also part of the fulfillment calculation.

**4.** If existing customers begin making more requests outside the contract, do not respond indefinitely out of goodwill. Organize what the new absence is, and present an additional contract, referral to a specialist, or a reset of the support scope as options.

## Success Criteria

The customer can explain in their own words the previously undefined absence, its background, the proposed fulfillment path, and the cost and support scope, and can decide to choose or not choose without pressure.

When the offering does not fit the absence, it can be declined or redesigned into a path containing other elements; the gap is not hidden for the sake of closing.

When additional support is needed, the structure of value and compensation is explicit, maintaining both customer satisfaction and the sales side’s sustainability.

Beyond short-term closing, it is possible to observe whether mismatch, cancellation, forced closing, insufficient explanation, or compensation-boundary disputes are recurring through subject-specific δ, and to revise A→A' according to θ and M.

## Limitations

Even careful reading of cognitive structure does not eliminate failure to close caused by the customer’s budget, authority, external environment, competition, or timing. Natural convergence is not a guarantee of results; it is a way to reduce dependence on pressure through structural alignment.

The salesperson’s ability to understand an undefined absence is different from being permitted to create an absence that does not exist for the customer. Steering that damages the person’s recognition or ability to compare falls outside the Parent Original’s structural line.

Even when natural-convergence rates or satisfaction are measured, the Parent Original does not provide universal fixed pass rates or fixed θ and δ. Observations from individual cases or organizations must not be expanded into guarantees for the theory as a whole.

## Preventing Misreading

Do not interpret “closing is unnecessary” to mean that final confirmation of contract terms or agreement is unnecessary. What is unnecessary is pressure that pushes the decision.

Do not interpret “the product is only a component” to mean that product quality or the duty to explain is unimportant. The responsibility to accurately explain fit with the absence remains.

Do not turn “structural kindness” into unlimited free consultation. The Parent Original includes redesign of the compensation structure as a condition of sustainability.

Do not turn “other options naturally disappear” into practice that hides alternatives or obstructs comparison. Separate convergence through the customer’s own understanding from restricting information.

Do not add fixed numbers to the natural-convergence rate, satisfaction, θ, δ, or M. Numbers may be used only as subject-specific audit values accompanied by the measuring party, subject, period, purpose, and non-guarantee scope.
