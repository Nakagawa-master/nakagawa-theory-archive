## 15-Second Explanation
Resonant Perception Theory reverses the idea that a correct structure will be understood if it is pushed strongly enough. Recipients already have cognitive frames, and the more coherent a new structure is, the more it can trigger a defensive response. The theory therefore designs a bridge that preserves the causal skeleton while allowing the other person to move without losing their own standpoint.

Its basis is “show, wait, and be able to return.” Do not hide the content, but do not overexpose it all at once; provide room to reconsider and a way back. Embed reversibility, temporal ethics, and allocation responsibility into the three layers of language, images, and institutions, so that voluntary understanding and renewed agreement—not obedience—can be established.

## Why It Is Needed
People do not receive new information in a blank state. They process it through values, experience, behavioral norms, and affiliations they already have. When a new structure differs greatly from an existing stable structure, it may be treated as “input that breaks stability” rather than “information that should be understood,” even when its content is highly accurate. Rejection, silence, or conformity pressure can result.

Trying to break through by increasing information volume or logical force may silence someone in the short term, but it damages long-term trust and reversibility. Resonant Perception Theory treats this friction as a problem for the communicator to design, not as the recipient’s immaturity.

However, “adapting to the other person” does not mean thinning the truth. The original first fixes definitions and causal lines as the skeleton and requires that the skeleton not be cut away. It then adjusts the amount of specialist language, metaphors, cases, diagrams, notes, summaries, and points of renewed correspondence. It does not weaken meaning; it increases routes of movement.

Acceptance alone is also insufficient. If recipients cannot reread, question, withdraw, and reach renewed agreement after initially accepting, translation turns into obedience. For that reason, every text, image, and institution should contain a structure that allows return, with the temporal rhythm of publication→response→revision→republication.

## Practical Process
**1. Fix the skeleton first.** Fix in advance what is being defined, which causal line is being claimed, and where the points of falsification are. Before adapting to recipients, make the central structure that must not be lost explicit.

**2. Observe the cognitive frame.** Check the words, values, behavioral norms, and points where rejection occurs that recipients already have. Do not classify and manipulate people; observe which gap makes movement difficult.

**3. Adjust exposure and footholds.** Do not hide the skeleton; stage peripheral information. Provide escape nodes that lead to another depth, such as a short version, comparison table, glossary, diagram, or FAQ.

**4. Implement “show, wait, and be able to return.”** Present the content, allow time for recipients to rearrange it, and leave routes for rereading, renewed agreement, withdrawal, or re-signing. Do not decide that understanding has been achieved from one reaction.

**5. Align the three layers.** Keep the causal explanation in language, the composition of the image, and the contract conditions of the institution from pointing in different directions. Avoid saying freedom in words while making the institution irrevocable.

**6. Revise after publication.** Observe misunderstandings, deviations, and new knowledge, and cycle through publication→response→revision→republication. Preserve the initial version, revision differences, reasons for decisions, signatures, and references so changes remain traceable.

## Examples
**1. Public release of a difficult concept.** Do not expand every term at the start. Show the central definition and causal line through a short entry, then provide routes to the detailed version, glossary, and FAQ. Leave a route back to the original so simplification does not harden into misunderstanding.

**2. Organizational system change.** Instead of one-sidedly explaining the new system’s benefits, show what changed from the old system, what can be withdrawn, when it will be reviewed, and who carries the burden. Treat agreement as an ongoing procedure rather than a single affirmative vote.

**3. Structural explanation in video or slides.** Show causal relations through center of gravity, perspective, whitespace, and staged exposure rather than fixing attention through fear or shame. The original’s example of one or two levels is a media guideline for avoiding excessive control of the gaze.

**4. Public information guidance.** Do not offer only a long text; provide a summary, key-point diagram, Q&A, and detailed materials in parallel. The aim is not merely to reduce information, but to let different entrances reach the same skeleton.

## Success Criteria
Recipients must be able to move from their own standpoint to the new structure while retaining its central causality. Understanding must not depend on conformity, silence, fear, or an agreement that cannot be withdrawn.

The central proposition must not contradict itself across the short version, images, and institutional explanation, and recipients must be able to return to details, definitions, and the original when needed. Reversibility clauses and review routes must be usable in practice, not nominal.

Misunderstandings and deviations after publication must connect to revision, and what changed and how must remain traceable. The cost of understanding must not be excessively concentrated on some recipients, and the public window must function.

## Limits
Resonant Perception Theory does not claim that every refusal can be resolved through communication technique. When value conflicts or conflicts of interest themselves exist, reducing friction and reaching agreement are separate problems.

“Thresholds” are not defined as universal fixed numbers. Because friction changes with cognitive frame, medium, and situation, the original’s θ and δ are symbols for falsifiability, not concrete values.

The original’s one-to-two-level gaze guidance, three beats, three layers, and six-step flow are structures or examples in the description, not a standard that mechanically applies the same numbers or sequence everywhere.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read “reducing friction” as a technique for disabling psychological defenses and steering someone to an intended conclusion. The center is the recipient’s agency, reversibility, and ability to reach renewed agreement.

Do not equate “making it easy to understand” with deleting difficult parts. Simplification that cuts out the skeleton is a failure in the original.

Do not read “wait” as abandoning explanatory responsibility or leaving people unattended. It means securing time for recipients to rearrange the material after showing the necessary skeleton and a route back.

Do not read “be able to return” as withdrawal without responsibility. It is reversibility joined to signatures, revision history, and allocation responsibility.
