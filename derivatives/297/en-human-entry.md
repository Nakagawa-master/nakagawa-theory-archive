## 15-Second Explanation
Future debt is not simply “cost remaining in the future.”

When the present uses future-side value, capacity to fulfill, ability to maintain or repair, resources, or available choices in advance, and gains credit, benefits, or ability to act, some of the conditions that make that benefit possible remain unsettled on the future side. This theory addresses that time gap.

```text
Present benefit
→ Unsettled remainder
→ Future settlement
```

The important point is not to avoid using the future. **If the future has been used, continue tracking what the present gained and what it left for the future on the same causal line.**

## Why It Is Needed
Future debt is hard to see because the benefit appears first on the present side. Expectations about future value that has not yet materialized can create credit and make investment or cooperation possible. Delaying maintenance or repair can reduce present costs. From a short-term view, both may appear rational.

But a problem arises when the conditions that make the present benefit possible remain on the future side and are excluded from the present success evaluation. As time passes, people and organizations change, information is interrupted, and a future burden comes to look like “a different problem.” Then additional pre-use or additional postponement can appear even more rational, and the unsettled portion keeps growing.

This is neither a theory that morally prioritizes the future nor one that sacrifices the present. It is a temporal structure for checking whether the **connection between present benefit and future settlement** has been severed, without absolutizing either present or future.

## Two Temporal Directions
### 1. Taking future value in advance as present credit
This is the direction in which expectations of future value that has not yet materialized change present credit, investment, cooperation, resource allocation, and the start of action.

```text
Future value
→ Present credit
→ Present ability to act
→ Settlement through realization of future value
```

This is not necessarily bad. Research, long-term investment, joint ventures, and institution-building may begin by extending credit to future value in advance.

However, if information contrary to the expectation appears but present credit is not revised, and further resources are invested to protect the expectation, the advance use of future value becomes self-amplifying. The question is not “does an expectation exist?” but whether counterexamples return to present evaluation.

### 2. Sending present burdens into the future
This is the direction in which maintenance, repair, renewal, institutional responses, and other matters that should be handled in the present are postponed, reducing present cost or burden.

```text
Present benefit / present efficiency
→ Necessary burden postponed
→ Unprocessed portion remains in the future
→ Settlement through future fulfillment / repair
```

When something deteriorates over time, “doing nothing for now” is not always neutral. The benefit gained by not handling it now and the repair burden thereby increased for the future must be viewed together.

### The two directions are not the same
Taking future value in advance and postponing present burdens are different operations.

```text
A: Taking future value in advance as present credit
B: Postponing present burdens into the future

Common:
The present benefits first
→ Part of the conditions remains unsettled
→ Realization, fulfillment, maintenance, repair, etc. become necessary on the future side
```

This theory does not overwrite A with B or absorb B into A and erase it.

## What Becomes Invisible
Looking only at “amount” in future debt loses important things. What may be lost on the future side includes credit, repair capacity, maintenance capacity, reversibility, substitutability, resource capacity, institutional options, temporal coherence, and future freedom of decision.

Also, the present beneficiary and future settler are not necessarily the same. The department that records present results may differ from the department responsible for future maintenance. Even within one organization they may be separated over time by a change of personnel. Conversely, the same actor may bear both the present and the future.

Therefore, limiting future debt to “the present generation pushing burdens onto future generations” makes the theory’s scope too narrow.

## Amplification and Irreversibility
When the unsettled portion becomes hard to see, future debt is prone to self-amplification.

```text
Unsettled portion becomes difficult to see
→ Additional use / additional postponement
→ Present results look better
→ The decision looks correct
→ The unsettled portion becomes even harder to see
```

However, having future debt and being irreversible are not the same.

```text
Future-debt amplification
→ Higher rollback cost
→ Fewer alternative routes
→ future option loss
→ Less freedom to choose settlement timing and method
→ Closer to forced settlement
```

If revision, reduction, stopping, repair, or transition to another route remains possible, freedom to choose the settlement method remains. When examining irreversibility, check not only “how large is the debt?” but also **how many options remain on the future side.**

## Practical Process
**1. Identify the present benefit.** What is being gained now? Specify credit, resources, avoided cost, recorded results, action begun, maintenance of the status quo, and so on.

**2. Identify what is being used early on the future side.** Determine what the present is using first among future value, future fulfillment, maintenance capacity, repair capacity, resources, and available choices.

**3. Make the unsettled remainder visible.** Separate what has not yet been realized, fulfilled, maintained, repaired, or borne but is needed to establish the present benefit.

**4. View beneficiary and settler separately.** Who receives the present benefit, and who will carry future realization, fulfillment, or repair? Include changes of responsibility and separation of actors.

**5. Return counterexamples to present evaluation.** Make it possible to revise present credit and present success evaluation when expectations about future value fail or future burdens grow.

**6. Preserve reversibility and alternative routes.** Do not define reversibility only as complete restoration; check whether reduction, stopping, revision, or transition to another route is possible.

**7. Evaluate present and future in the same causal bundle.** Avoid one-sided evaluation of only present benefit or only future burden, and keep observing their connection.

## Applications
**Long-term investment and research.** Do not deny gathering present resources through expectations of future value; check whether credit can be revised when counterexamples appear.

**Equipment and infrastructure.** View present cost savings from postponed maintenance together with future repair scale, outage risk, and the reduction of alternative routes on the same causal line.

**Organizations and institutions.** Even when the actor receiving present results is separate from the actor responsible for future fulfillment or repair, preserve a state in which the unsettled relationship can be tracked.

**Technology infrastructure.** Check whether temporary measures or postponed upgrades have become permanent, raising migration costs or rollback cost and reducing future technology options.

**Resources and environment.** View present-use benefits together with future repair, regeneration, and substitution burdens. Do not automatically call something future debt merely because it has future effects.

## Success Criteria
It is possible to explain what the present received first, what remains unsettled, and who will settle it in the future.

Counterexamples to future value or increases in future burdens return to present credit and results evaluation.

Revision, reduction, stopping, repair, or transition to another route remains possible, and future option has not been reduced to one path.

Even when short-term results look good, future burdens are not excluded from evaluation, and present benefit and future settlement can be tracked in the same causal bundle.

## Limitations
This article does not establish a single numerical formula for future debt, a monetary-conversion model, universal numerical thresholds, or automatic judgments for individual cases.

Nor does it absorb future definition, meaning-convergence theory, reverse-causal rectification theory, time OS, or structural wu-wei-ziran into one theory. They may connect to the same temporal-structure system, but each retains an independent responsibility.

Legal subjecthood of future generations and specific unpublished AI-civilization theories are also not derived from this article alone.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not dilute future debt into the general claim that “something bad will happen in the future.”

Do not label the present effect of future value as bad debt by definition. Advance credit is not completed settlement, but it may also have a constructive role in creating long-term value.

Do not automatically classify investment, contracts, future costs, future forecasts, or future definitions as future debt.

Keep `未来負債 ≠ 不可逆点`; observe future option loss separately from the decline in settlement freedom.

Do not use “for the future” as an unlimited justification for sacrificing the present. Neither the present nor the future is absolutized.

Do not replace the final goal with zero future debt. The goal is not to lose the settlement line with the future when the future has been used.
