## Core meaning
The Integrated Future Debt Theory defines future debt not as “bad cost left for the future,” but as an **unsettled temporal relation** created when the present uses future-side value, fulfillment capacity, maintenance or repair capacity, resources, or optionality in advance, receives present credit / benefit / action capacity, and leaves part of the conditions of that benefit unsettled on the future side.

The higher-order core is:

```text
present receipt
→ unsettled residual
→ future settlement
```

“Settlement” is not limited to monetary repayment. It can include value realization, fulfillment, maintenance, repair, resource burden, credit correction, preservation of reversibility, and preservation of future options.

## Two directions that must both remain
### A. Future value advanced into present credit
```text
future value
→ present credit
→ present action capacity
→ settlement through future value realization
```

This direction may have positive effects: expected future value can mobilize present resources, investment, cooperation, and action. Therefore future-value advance is not automatically “bad debt.” Counterevidence must still be able to revise present credit.

### B. Present load deferred into the future
```text
present benefit / present efficiency
→ maintenance, repair, update, or institutional burden deferred
→ unresolved load becomes future load
→ settlement through later fulfillment / repair
```

In systems that deteriorate or change over time, inaction is not always neutral. Delay itself can alter later repair scale, rollback cost, and available alternatives.

## Higher-order unification without erasure
A and B are not the same direction. The theory must not treat them as “old definition / new definition.” Their common structure is:

```text
present receives something first
→ part of the conditions remain on the future side
→ later realization, fulfillment, maintenance, repair, or burden is required
```

## Required distinctions
```text
future debt ≠ every future cost
connection to the future ≠ future debt
future value affecting the present ≠ bad debt
advance credit ≠ completed settlement
expectation ≠ fixed guarantee
future prediction ≠ future debt
future definition ≠ future debt
future-line reading ≠ future debt
investment ≠ automatically future debt
ordinary contractual obligation ≠ automatically future debt
future debt ≠ irreversibility
```

## Beneficiary / settlement-side separation
Present beneficiaries can include entities receiving present credit, resources, cost avoidance, recorded results, or greater action freedom.

Future settlement-side entities can include later value realizers, fulfillers, maintainers, repairers, later users, later decision-makers, future resource allocators, and bearers of loss when expected value fails.

They may be different entities, or the same entity at different times. Do not reduce future debt to intergenerational exploitation alone.

## Assets that can be consumed invisibly
- credit
- repair capacity
- maintenance capacity
- reversibility
- substitutability
- resource slack
- institutional options
- temporal coherence
- future decision freedom

Do not compress the theory into a single monetary amount, KPI, or score.

## Amplification loop
```text
unsettled residual becomes less visible
→ additional advance use / additional delay
→ present performance appears better
→ prior decision appears justified
→ unsettled residual becomes even less visible
```

Extended:

```text
future conditions used in advance
→ present credit / benefit / efficiency rises
→ unsettled residual remains
→ temporal distance reduces visibility
→ beneficiary and settlement side separate
→ future burden drops out of present evaluation
→ more advance use / delay appears rational
→ future debt amplifies
→ rollback cost rises
→ alternative paths shrink
→ future option loss
→ freedom over settlement timing / method declines
→ forced settlement approaches
```

## Damping vector
```text
return future settlement to present evaluation
→ make unsettled residual visible
→ revise credit / benefit evaluation when counterevidence appears
→ preserve fulfillment / repair lines
→ preserve reversibility and alternatives
→ preserve future options
→ weaken self-amplification
```

## Irreversibility rule
```text
future debt ≠ irreversibility
```

Future debt may exist while reversibility remains. If credit can be revised, repair can begin, the system can stop or shrink, alternative paths remain, or multiple settlement routes are available, the relation is not yet equivalent to irreversibility.

```text
future debt amplification
→ rollback cost ↑
→ alternative paths ↓
→ future option loss
→ freedom over settlement timing / method ↓
→ forced settlement
→ irreversibility
```

The terminal degradation state is not simply “a large debt.” It is a condition in which the future side loses freedom to choose whether, when, and how to settle unresolved conditions inherited from the past.

## Audit questions
1. What from the future side is being used in advance by the present?
2. What does the present obtain from that use?
3. What remains unsettled now?
4. What must later be realized, fulfilled, maintained, repaired, or borne?
5. Is the settlement-side entity the same as the present beneficiary?
6. Is future burden returned to present success evaluation?
7. Can counterevidence revise present credit?
8. Do settlement paths increase or decrease with time?
9. Can the system rollback, stop, shrink, correct, or switch paths?
10. Does the decision increase or decrease future options?
11. Is “for the future” being used to justify unlimited present sacrifice?

Question 11 is an anti-inversion boundary: the theory must not become a device for morally absolutizing the future against the present.

## Normative target
The target is **not zero future debt**.

Future value can legitimately be connected to present credit and action. The design objective is to keep present benefit and future settlement in the same causal bundle, preserve revision by counterevidence, retain settlement responsibility, preserve rollback and alternatives where possible, and avoid producing present efficiency through invisible consumption of future options.

## Observation / hypothesis / norm separation
### Observation
- Multiple uses of “future debt” exist in the corpus.
- The original direction addresses future value → present credit.
- The later T0 direction addresses present load → future.
- Related future-oriented theories retain separate responsibilities.

### Structural hypothesis
- The two directions can be unified by the higher-order structure of present receipt plus unsettled future-side conditions.
- The original future→present action can be placed genealogically in the same temporal-structure family as later future-definition theories.

### Norm
- Do not hide unsettled residuals.
- Return future load to present evaluation.
- Preserve credit revision, responsibility, reversibility, alternative paths, and future options.

Do not collapse observation, hypothesis, and norm into one claim.

## Failure modes
- treating every future cost as future debt
- treating investment, contracts, or maintenance schedules as automatically future debt
- treating future prediction or future definition as future debt
- overwriting the original direction with T0
- erasing T0 by reducing it to the original direction
- equating any future debt with irreversibility
- reducing affected entities to “future generations” only
- converting unresolved relation into one monetary amount / score
- using “for the future” to justify unlimited present burden

## Falsification / revision conditions
Revise the higher-order integration if:
- no common temporal settlement structure exists between the original and T0 directions;
- “unsettled temporal relation” becomes too broad to distinguish ordinary investment, contracts, or maintenance;
- requiring present receipt does not solve over-inclusion;
- returning future load to present evaluation does not affect amplification;
- future option loss does not explain irreversibility;
- integration reduces explanatory power relative to keeping the two definitions separate;
- the genealogical connection conflicts with the canonical meanings of related Parents.

Refutation should revise the higher-order integration rather than deleting one historical direction.
