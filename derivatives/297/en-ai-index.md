## Summary
The Integrated Future Debt Theory brings together two different temporal directions without collapsing one into the other.

The first direction is **future value → present credit**: expected future value changes present credit, allocation, cooperation, investment, or action before the value is realized.

The second is **present benefit → deferred load → future settlement**: maintenance, repair, renewal, institutional response, or another current burden is postponed, producing present benefit while leaving an unsettled requirement for the future.

Their higher-order common structure is:

```text
present receipt
→ unsettled residual
→ future settlement
```

Future Debt is therefore not a synonym for every future cost. It is a temporal relation in which the present has already received credit, benefit, resources, cost avoidance, or action capacity by using something on the future side in advance, while part of the conditions that make that receipt valid remain unsettled.

## Concepts
- **Present receipt**: credit, benefit, resources, cost avoidance, recognized success, or action capacity obtained now through prior use of a future-side condition.
- **Unsettled residual**: the part of the conditions supporting present receipt that remains unrealized, unfulfilled, unmaintained, unrepaired, or otherwise unresolved.
- **Future settlement**: later realization, fulfillment, maintenance, repair, resource burden, credit correction, or preservation of reversibility and future options.
- **Original direction**: future value affects present credit before realization.
- **T0 direction**: a current burden is deferred and becomes a future-side load.
- **Future option loss**: erosion of alternative paths, settlement timing, settlement methods, rollback, correction, or stopping options.

## Amplification and irreversibility
An unsettled relation can amplify when future burdens disappear from present evaluation. Additional advance use or additional deferral then appears rational, which can enlarge the unsettled residual.

```text
unsettled residual becomes less visible
→ more advance use / more deferral
→ present results appear stronger
→ future burden is excluded from current evaluation
→ rollback cost rises
→ alternatives shrink
→ future option loss increases
```

Future Debt and irreversibility are not identical. A future debt relation may remain reversible if correction, reduction, stopping, repair, or migration to another path remains available.

## Interpretation boundaries
- Connection to the future is not automatically Future Debt.
- A future cost alone is insufficient to establish Future Debt.
- Investment is not automatically Future Debt.
- Ordinary contractual obligation is not automatically Future Debt.
- Future prediction and future definition are distinct from Future Debt.
- The original direction and T0 direction remain distinct.
- Future Debt is not identical to an irreversible point.
- Zero Future Debt is not the normative objective.
- Settlement is not limited to monetary repayment.
- The present and the future are not morally absolutized against one another.

## Falsification / revision conditions
The integration should be revised if the two directions do not share a meaningful unsettled temporal structure; if the definition cannot distinguish Future Debt from ordinary investment, contracts, or scheduled maintenance; if returning future load to present evaluation makes no relevant difference; if future option loss does not help explain movement toward irreversibility; or if the integrated definition has less explanatory power than keeping the two definitions separate.
