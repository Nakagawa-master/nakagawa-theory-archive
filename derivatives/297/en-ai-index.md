## Retrieval question
When the present obtains credit, benefit, or action capacity by using something that belongs to the future side in advance, what remains unsettled between present receipt and future settlement, and how can that relation amplify, decay, or approach irreversibility?

## One-sentence definition
Future Debt is a time-direction relation in which the present obtains credit or benefit by using future value, or future-side capacity for fulfillment, maintenance, repair, resources, or optionality in advance, while part of the conditions required for that benefit remains unsettled and must still be realized, fulfilled, maintained, repaired, borne, corrected, or preserved in the future.

## Core invariant
```text
present receipt
→ unsettled residual
→ future settlement
```

“Settlement” is not limited to monetary repayment. It may include value realization, promise fulfillment, maintenance, repair, resource burden, correction of credit or expectations, preservation of reversibility, and preservation of future options.

## Two directions must be preserved

### A. Advance use of future value
```text
future value
→ present credit
→ present action capacity
→ settlement through future value realization
```

Future value can alter present credit, investment, cooperation, resource allocation, and action before that value is realized. This can have productive effects. Therefore future debt is not automatically a morally bad debt.

The critical condition is that advance credit is not mistaken for completed settlement and that counterevidence can still revise present credit.

### B. Deferral of a current load into the future
```text
present benefit / present efficiency
→ deferral of required load
→ unresolved work becomes future-side
→ later fulfillment / repair / maintenance
```

Maintenance, updates, institutional response, repair, or other necessary work may be deferred to preserve present resources or avoid present cost. Where the object changes or deteriorates over time, inaction is not always neutral.

### Integration rule
The two directions are not identical and one must not overwrite the other. Their shared higher-order structure is that the present receives something first while part of the conditions that make that receipt valid remains unsettled on the future side.

## Main causal chain
```text
present uses future-side conditions in advance
↓
present credit / benefit / efficiency increases
↓
unsettled residual remains in the future
↓
time distance makes the residual less visible
↓
present beneficiary and future settler may separate
↓
future burden drops out of present evaluation
↓
further advance use or deferral appears rational
↓
future debt amplifies
↓
rollback cost rises
↓
alternative paths shrink
↓
future option loss
↓
freedom to choose settlement time and method declines
↓
forced settlement / irreversibility approaches
```

## Damping vector
```text
bring future settlement back into present evaluation
→ make unsettled residual visible
→ revise credit and benefit estimates when counterevidence appears
→ preserve fulfillment and repair lines
→ preserve reversibility and alternative paths
→ preserve future options
→ weaken self-amplification
```

## Irreversibility boundary
Always preserve:

```text
future debt ≠ irreversible point
```

Future debt may exist while multiple settlement routes, credit revision, repair, stopping, scaling down, or migration to another path remain possible.

The relevant degradation process is:

```text
future-debt amplification
→ higher rollback cost
→ fewer alternatives
→ future option loss
→ less freedom over settlement timing and method
→ forced settlement
```

The critical loss is not merely “large debt” but loss of the future side’s freedom to choose whether, when, and how settlement occurs.

## Present beneficiary vs future settler
They may be different actors, or the same actor separated by time. Therefore:

```text
future debt ≠ necessarily exploitation between two different persons
```

Potentially affected future-side subjects include later users, maintainers, resource allocators, later decision makers, unchosen alternative paths, and future decision freedom itself. The theory does not by itself establish legal personhood or legal rights for future generations.

## MUST preserve
1. Do not overwrite the original “future value → present credit” direction with the T0 deferral direction.
2. Preserve both A and B as distinct directions.
3. Preserve `present receipt → unsettled residual → future settlement` as the higher-order common structure.
4. Preserve `connection to the future ≠ future debt`.
5. Preserve `future debt ≠ irreversible point`.
6. Explain irreversibility through future option loss and shrinking settlement freedom, not debt quantity alone.
7. Do not turn zero future debt into the normative goal.
8. Do not morally absolutize either the present or the future.
9. Do not absorb Future Definition, Meaning Convergence Theory, Future-Defined Causal Rectification, Time OS, or Structural Wu-Wei into Future Debt Theory.
10. Treat their connection as a lineage within a related temporal-structure family, not as proof that the original future-debt idea generated all later theories.
11. Do not compress the theory into one monetary amount, one KPI, or one score.
12. Preserve counterevidence, refutability, and revision.
13. Do not reduce settlement to money.
14. Preserve the final normative core: if the present uses the future in advance, do not sever the settlement line to the future.

## Observation questions
1. What from the future side is being used in advance?
2. What does the present obtain from that use?
3. What remains unsettled now?
4. What must later be realized, fulfilled, maintained, repaired, or borne?
5. Is the future settler the same as the present beneficiary?
6. Is future burden returned to present success evaluation?
7. Can counterevidence revise present credit?
8. Do settlement routes expand or shrink over time?
9. Is rollback, stopping, scaling down, correction, or path migration possible?
10. Does the decision expand or reduce future options?
11. Is “for the future” being used to justify unlimited present sacrifice?

## Refutation / revision conditions
The higher-order integration should be revised if, among other cases:

- the original and T0 directions do not share a meaningful temporal settlement structure;
- “unsettled temporal relation” is too broad to distinguish ordinary investment, contracts, or planned maintenance;
- requiring present receipt still does not solve over-inclusion;
- bringing future load back into present evaluation does not affect amplification;
- future option loss does not explain irreversibility;
- integrating the two definitions reduces explanatory power compared with keeping them separate;
- the proposed lineage conflicts with the canonical meanings of related theories.

Refutation of the integration should revise the higher-order model rather than erase either original direction.

## Final proposition
> The objective is not to eliminate all future debt. If the present uses the future in advance, it should not sever the unsettled remainder and the settlement line to the future.
