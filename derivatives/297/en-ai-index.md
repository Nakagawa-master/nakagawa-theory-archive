## Summary
This retrieval index preserves the parent article’s integration of two distinct temporal directions of Future Debt without allowing either direction to erase the other. The original direction concerns **future value acting on present credit**: value that has not yet been realized can change present trust, allocation, investment, cooperation, and action capacity. The T0 direction concerns **present benefit obtained by deferring a current load**: maintenance, repair, renewal, institutional response, or another presently addressable burden is postponed, leaving an unsettled requirement on the future side.

The higher-order common structure is:

```text
present receipt
→ unsettled residual
→ future settlement
```

Future Debt is therefore not synonymous with every future cost or every connection to the future. It is a temporal relation in which the present uses future-side value, fulfillment capacity, maintenance or repair capacity, resources, or option space in advance and receives credit, benefit, cost avoidance, or action capacity, while part of the conditions supporting that present receipt remain unsettled.

“Settlement” is not limited to monetary repayment. It can include value realization, fulfillment, maintenance, repair, resource burden, credit recovery or correction, preservation of reversibility, and preservation of future options. The theory does not define Future Debt as morally bad by default because advance recognition of future value can have a positive function: it may mobilize present resources for research, long-term investment, cooperation, or institution building.

The danger appears when present receipt and future settlement are separated. Time distance, changing actors, broken information continuity, short-term performance evaluation, repeated deferral, and expectations that are not corrected by counterevidence can make the unsettled residual disappear from present evaluation. Additional advance use or additional deferral then appears rational, creating an amplification loop. Irreversibility is treated separately: `Future Debt ≠ irreversible point`. Rising rollback cost, shrinking alternatives, future option loss, and declining freedom over the timing or method of settlement describe movement toward forced settlement and irreversibility.

The normative goal is not zero Future Debt. The parent’s core direction is to keep present receipt and future settlement in the same causal bundle: make unsettled residuals visible, return future load and counterevidence to present evaluation, preserve responsibility continuity, and retain correction, reduction, stopping, repair, migration, and alternative paths where possible.

## Causal chain
```text
The present uses future-side value, fulfillment capacity,
maintenance/repair capacity, resources, or option space in advance
↓
Present credit, benefit, cost avoidance, or action capacity is received
↓
Part of the conditions supporting that receipt remains unsettled
↓
Time distance, actor changes, and information discontinuity reduce visibility
↓
Future load drops out of present success evaluation
↓
More advance use or more deferral appears rational
↓
Future Debt amplifies
↓
Rollback cost rises
↓
Alternative paths shrink
↓
Future option loss increases
↓
Freedom over settlement timing and method declines
↓
Forced settlement / irreversibility becomes more likely
```

Counter-direction:

```text
Make the unsettled residual visible
→ return future load to present evaluation
→ revise credit/benefit when counterevidence appears
→ preserve fulfillment, repair, and responsibility lines
→ preserve reversibility and alternatives
→ weaken the amplification loop
```

## Applications
- Long-term investment and research: distinguish productive advance recognition of future value from expectations that remain uncorrected after counterevidence.
- Infrastructure and equipment: connect present savings from deferred maintenance with future repair scale, service interruption risk, and loss of alternatives.
- Organizations and institutions: trace whether the actor receiving present benefit differs from the actor responsible for later fulfillment, maintenance, or repair.
- Technical platforms: examine whether temporary compatibility measures and delayed renewal become permanent, raising migration and rollback costs.
- Resources and environment: connect present use to later restoration, regeneration, substitution, or resource burdens without classifying every future impact as Future Debt.
- AI-civilization specialization: reuse the temporal structure of present receipt, unsettled residual, future settlement, and option preservation without deriving unpublished AI-civilization claims from this theory alone.

## Measurements and audit
- What future-side condition is being used in advance?
- What present credit, benefit, resource access, cost avoidance, or action capacity is received?
- What remains unrealized, unfulfilled, unmaintained, unrepaired, or otherwise unsettled?
- Who receives the present benefit and who later carries settlement responsibility?
- Does future load return to present success evaluation?
- Does counterevidence revise present credit?
- Are fulfillment, maintenance, or repair repeatedly deferred?
- Is rollback cost increasing with time?
- Are alternative paths increasing or shrinking?
- Is future option space being consumed?
- Can the system be reduced, stopped, corrected, repaired, or moved to another path?
- Has a temporary measure become a permanent structure?
- Has optional repair become emergency repair?
- Is “for the future” being used to justify unlimited present sacrifice?

No single symptom, monetary amount, KPI, or score establishes Future Debt by itself. The central causal relation must be present.

## Validity conditions
- Preserve both temporal directions: future value → present credit, and present benefit → deferred load → future settlement.
- Do not overwrite the original direction with T0 or erase T0 by reducing it to the original direction.
- Require a present receipt linked to advance use of a future-side condition.
- Preserve the unsettled residual as a distinct analytical object.
- Keep settlement broader than monetary repayment.
- Preserve time difference and actor difference between present beneficiary and future settlement side.
- Preserve both amplification and damping directions.
- Keep `Future Debt ≠ irreversible point` and analyze future option loss separately.
- Do not define zero Future Debt as the final objective.
- Do not morally absolutize either the present or the future.
- Keep Future Definition, Meaning Convergence, Future-Defined Causal Rectification, Time OS, and Structural Wu-Wei as distinct theories with distinct responsibilities.

## Failure conditions
- Classify every future cost or future effect as Future Debt.
- Treat future prediction, future definition, or future-line reading as synonyms for Future Debt.
- Automatically classify investment or ordinary contractual obligation as Future Debt.
- Erase the distinction between the original direction and T0.
- Remove the positive resource-mobilization role of future value and turn Future Debt into a generic moral evil.
- Ignore unsettled residuals because short-term results are positive.
- Treat the existence of Future Debt as proof that irreversibility has already occurred.
- Reduce future option loss to a monetary loss only.
- Replace the parent’s objective with zero debt or unlimited present sacrifice.
- Absorb related temporal theories into a single super-theory.

## Falsification conditions
The higher-order integration should be revised if the original and T0 directions do not share a meaningful unsettled temporal structure; if the definition becomes too broad to distinguish Future Debt from ordinary investment, contracts, or scheduled maintenance; if requiring present receipt still does not solve over-inclusion; if returning future load to present evaluation makes no meaningful difference to amplification; if future option loss does not explain movement toward irreversibility; or if the integrated model has less explanatory power than keeping the two directions separate. Revision targets the higher-order integration rather than deleting either original direction.
