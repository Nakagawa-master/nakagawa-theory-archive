## Summary

The parent original rejects a purely linear reading of causality in which action A simply produces result B. It redefines causality as a natural path of least resistance embedded in a structure formed by people, information, institutions, context, time, intention, and roles. When outcomes are poor, adding more effort, commands, budget, or downstream tactics often fails because upstream structural defects remain unchanged. Information asymmetry, contradictory incentives, broken temporal learning, or mismatched responsibility and authority can absorb added force and reproduce delays, rework, and side effects.

Nakagawa-style causal structure theory identifies three major determinants of the causal flow: structural coherence of the field, temporal recursivity, and role architecture. Structural coherence means that people, data, institutions, evaluation rules, and context are arranged without disabling contradiction. Temporal recursivity means that past experience is not merely imposed on the present; a desired future structure is used to reinterpret prior evidence and redesign the present while preserving the original record. Role architecture aligns responsibility, authority, capability, and verification routes so that each component can function without avoidable resistance.

Structural operation therefore means redesigning the conditions through which causality flows, not secretly manipulating a person or directly forcing an event. A safe implementation maps the causal field, identifies one upstream friction hypothesis, applies the smallest reversible intervention, and evaluates a bundle of outcome, friction, delay, exceptions, side effects, rights impact, rollback cost, and reproducibility. If the intervention does not produce a repeatable improvement, or if a simpler explanation such as individual ability or an external factor accounts for the result, the structural hypothesis must be weakened or revised.

## Causal chain

```text
Interpret poor outcomes through a linear A-to-B model and add downstream force.
↓
Upstream distortions in information, institutions, time, and roles absorb that force.
↓
Map people, information, institutions, context, time, and roles as one causal field.
↓
Separate defects in structural coherence, temporal recursivity, and role architecture.
↓
Apply one minimal reversible intervention to an upstream friction point.
↓
Measure outcome together with friction, delay, exceptions, side effects, and rollback cost.
↓
Test whether improved flow reproduces across another cycle or operator without added coercion.
↓
Integrate only reproduced structural conditions; return failed changes to the causal hypothesis.
```

## State model

```yaml
- linear_model: causality is reduced to a simple A-to-B chain
- downstream_force: more effort, command, budget, or tactics are added
- friction_detected: rework, delay, conflict, or exceptions become visible
- causal_field_mapped: structural elements and their flows are described
- coherence_checked: contradictions in the field are audited
- temporal_recursion_checked: past evidence and future conditions are reconnected
- role_architecture_checked: responsibility, authority, capability, and verification are aligned
- minimal_intervention: one reversible structural change is introduced
- bundle_audit: outcome and side effects are measured together
- natural_flow: desired action increases without additional coercion
- rollback_ready: the intervention can be reversed at known cost
- reproduced: the effect repeats across another cycle or operator
- integrated: reproduced conditions are incorporated into operations
- falsified_or_revised: the structural hypothesis is narrowed or changed
```

## Applications

**1.** In organizational performance, map information exchange, incentives, approvals, and handoffs before simply increasing sales activity. Change one upstream asymmetry and compare both results and rework.

**2.** In human-AI collaboration, tune role boundaries, source attribution, verification points, responsibility, and recursive logs before increasing prompt instructions. Test whether quality and reproducibility improve without more commands.

**3.** In public systems, audit application, review, rights, remedy, and time burdens before increasing sanctions or effort demands, and check that friction is not merely transferred to weaker participants.

**4.** In trust design, replace personality-only selection with transparency, reciprocal verification, incentive alignment, disagreement routes, and remedy, then test whether trust behavior survives personnel changes.

## Measurements and audit

- Structural friction: rework, duplication, conflict, explanatory burden, and exception handling.
- Flow time: delay across input, decision, implementation, outcome, and reevaluation stages.
- Information asymmetry: mismatch between actors holding necessary information and actors holding decision authority.
- Coherence rate: proportion of institutional, informational, evaluative, and role relationships without disabling contradiction.
- Role alignment: fit among responsibility, authority, capability, and verification routes.
- Recursive-use rate: degree to which past logs and future conditions actually alter present design.
- Natural-flow rate: desired action proceeding without extra command or exception handling.
- Side-effect bundle: exclusion, dependency, friction transfer, local optimization, and rights harm.
- Rollback cost: time, expense, residual effects, and re-agreement needed to reverse a change.
- Reproducibility: recurrence of the same directional effect across cycles or operators.

## Validity conditions

- The causal field is described upstream of the visible result.
- Coherence, temporal recursivity, and role architecture can be audited separately.
- Interventions are minimal, reversible, and comparison-ready.
- Outcomes and side effects are measured under the same audit.
- Disagreement, stopping, remedy, and rollback routes are genuinely available.
- Reproducibility is tested across another cycle or operator.

## Failure conditions

- Poor outcomes are attributed only to insufficient individual effort.
- Structural operation is used to invisibly narrow another person's choices or dissent.
- Multiple variables change together and causal contribution becomes unidentifiable.
- A temporary result improvement is treated as proof of the causal hypothesis.
- Friction is transferred to another group and counted as system improvement.
- A preferred future is used to rewrite or suppress inconvenient historical evidence.

## Falsification conditions

- Upstream structural change produces no repeatable difference in outcome, friction, or delay.
- Individual capability alone explains the result without added value from the structural hypothesis.
- Improved structural coherence does not improve natural-flow measures.
- Unobserved external factors consistently explain the outcome better than the proposed intervention.
- The effect disappears under another operator and is attributable to the designer's personal ability.
- Side effects, rights harm, or rollback cost exceed the claimed benefit.

## Required distinctions

- linear causality vs structural necessity
- outcome forcing vs structural tuning
- structural operation vs manipulation of persons
- structural coherence vs conformity pressure
- temporal recursivity vs retrospective justification
- role architecture vs rigid hierarchy
- natural flow vs neglect
- reproducibility vs designer dependence

## Interpretation constraints

- Do not transform structural operation into a hidden persuasion or behavioral-control technique.
- Do not interpret natural causal flow as guaranteed outcomes or determinism.
- Do not erase individual intent, negligence, or accountability by invoking structure.
- Keep reinterpretation of history separate from alteration of primary evidence.
- Do not count a design as successful when dissent, exit, remedy, or rollback become unavailable.
- When falsification evidence appears, narrow the claim rather than preserve it by renaming concepts.
