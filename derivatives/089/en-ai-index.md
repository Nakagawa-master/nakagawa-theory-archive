## Summary
The parent source treats causality not merely as a linear sequence in which A is followed by B, but as a flow produced by the arrangement of people, information, institutions, context, time, and roles. Adding more effort, commands, budget, or tactics downstream may fail when upstream asymmetry, contradiction, broken temporal learning, or responsibility-authority mismatch remains unchanged.

Its three central elements are structural coherence of the field, temporal recursivity, and role architecture. Structural operation is not covert manipulation of persons. It is the tuning of structural conditions so that a desired causal flow can occur with less friction and without turning the theory into a guarantee of outcomes.

```text
Poor outcome is attributed to insufficient downstream effort
↓
More effort, command, budget, or tactics are added
↓

↓
Rework, delay, exceptions, and side effects recur
↓
People, information, institutions, context, time, and roles are read as one causal field
↓

↓

↓
If the effect does not reproduce, the structural hypothesis is narrowed or revised
```

## Applications
- Organizations: examine information exchange, evaluation, approvals, and responsibility-authority placement before simply increasing activity.
- Human-AI collaboration: tune roles, information boundaries, verification points, responsibility, and logs instead of only increasing instructions.
- Public systems: inspect application, review, rights, remedies, and time burdens before increasing sanctions or effort demands.
- Trust design: structure transparency, reciprocal verification, incentives, disagreement, and remedy rather than relying only on personality selection.

## Measurements and audit
Measurement should remain tied to the parent claim: whether structural changes alter rework, delay, exceptions, friction, role mismatch, or reproducibility. Do not invent universal success rates, fixed thresholds, or generalized percentages that the source does not provide.

Terms such as Rollback Cost should remain observations about reversibility—time, expense, residual effects, or similar burdens—unless the parent source supplies a concrete value. A better outcome metric alone is not sufficient if friction is merely transferred, rights are harmed, or the effect disappears when personnel change.

## Validity conditions
- The causal field is described upstream of the visible outcome.
- Structural coherence, temporal recursivity, and role architecture remain distinct.
- The relation between structural change and outcome change can be examined.
- Dissent and side effects can act as falsification evidence.
- Strong personnel dependence limits generalization to structure.
- Primary historical evidence remains separate from later reinterpretation.

## Failure conditions
- Poor outcomes are attributed only to individual effort.
- Structural operation becomes covert manipulation of persons.
- Temporary improvement is treated as proof of the theory.
- Friction is transferred to another group and counted as improvement.
- Preferred future conditions are used to alter inconvenient primary evidence.
- Non-reproducing effects are described as structural necessity.

## Falsification conditions
- Upstream structural change produces no repeatable difference in outcomes, friction, or delay.
- Individual capability sufficiently explains the outcome.
- External conditions explain the result more consistently.
- Effects disappear under another operator or another period.
- Side effects or rights harms outweigh the claimed improvement.

## Required distinctions
- linear causality vs structural causal flow
- outcome forcing vs structural tuning
- structural operation vs manipulation of persons
- structural coherence vs conformity pressure
- temporal recursivity vs retrospective justification
- role architecture vs rigid hierarchy
- natural flow vs outcome guarantee
- reproducibility vs designer dependence

## Interpretation constraints
Do not turn structural operation into a hidden persuasion technique. Do not interpret natural causal flow as determinism or guaranteed success. Do not erase individual intent or negligence by invoking structure, and do not hide recurring structural defects by blaming only individuals. Narrow the claim when falsification evidence appears.
