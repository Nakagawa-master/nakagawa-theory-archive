## 15-Second Explanation
The Parent Original redefines KPI not as a “list of numbers to manage,” but as the causal core point that necessarily realizes the KGI. Rather than listing many numbers of calls or posts, the idea is to narrow the focus to one or two points that move the overall causality and concentrate the necessary action there.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
In ordinary KPI operations, multiple figures—such as number of calls, number of business meetings, number of visits, and number of posts—are listed, and movement in the numbers tends to be treated as progress. The Parent Original criticizes this approach: even if peripheral activity increases, results cannot be achieved if the true causal core point leading to the KGI is missed.

Therefore, make the KGI concrete, break down the causal chain leading to it, and find the fulcrum that moves the whole. The Original explicitly states that this point of action should be narrowed to one or two points. If five or ten KPIs are listed, its strong position is that these are not the true KPIs but peripheral management indicators.

In non-coercive governance, finding the core point does not mean pushing through by command or force. The ethical conditions are to preserve freedom, not embed a hidden correct answer in the question, share the purpose of measurement, and not repurpose it for individual evaluation.

## Practical Process
**1. Clarify the KGI.** Define the final goal concretely rather than with abstract words.

**2. Develop the causal chain.** Make the path to the KGI visible, and break down what causes what to move.

**3. Extract core-point candidates.** List the factors that could move the whole if acted upon.

**4. Narrow it to one or two points.** Do not confuse peripheral management indicators with KPIs; retain only the causal fulcrum.

**5. Design the necessary action.** Design the number of times, pressure, and intensity to apply to the core point.

These five stages are the Parent Original’s own procedure. “5” is the number of stages, not a quality score.

## Examples of Application
**1. Meetings.** The Original gives as an example that presenting a “meaning axis” at the beginning of a meeting can be a core point that naturally aligns the entire discussion.

**2. Education.** It presents the point at which the learner puts their own role into words as a core point determining retention of learning.

**3. Social consensus.** It gives as an example a single question that turns opposing views into resources as a fulcrum that transforms conflict into consensus.

**4. Sales.** Whether the customer’s value axis can be defined at the first contact is offered as a general example of a core point that affects the contract rate.

## Success Criteria
Judge success not by whether the number of management indicators increased, but by whether action on the selected core point has a causal connection to the KGI.

The core point narrowed to one or two points functions as a fulcrum that moves the whole, rather than as a mere correlation among peripheral numbers.

Even while approaching the KGI, freedom, non-leading practice, and transparency remain intact.

If the core-point hypothesis fails, return to the causal chain and review the core point rather than adding more peripheral indicators.

## Limitations
The Original’s text makes the very strong claim that “if sufficient action is applied to the core point, the KGI will necessarily be achieved.” For that reason, in actual operation it is important to keep falsifiable whether the one or two points identified as the core point are truly causal fulcrums.

The integrated audit summary uses multiple indicators—RC/ER/CEA/S, Consent Failure Rate, Rollback Cost, consensus-arrival time, and others—for operational auditing separately from the single core point in the text. This should not be read as returning to numerous KPIs, but as an auxiliary bundle for auditing the health of non-coercive governance.

## Preventing Misreadings
Do not weaken this into the general dashboard argument that “fewer KPIs are easier to see.” The Original’s claim is to redefine KPI itself as the causal core point leading to the KGI.

Conversely, this is not a theory that permits forcing people on the grounds of concentrating on the core point. Freedom, non-leading practice, and transparency are ethical conditions.

The expressions “five” and “ten” are misuse examples of listing many KPIs, not statistical values. One or two points is the Original’s normative number of core points; five stages is the number of design steps.
