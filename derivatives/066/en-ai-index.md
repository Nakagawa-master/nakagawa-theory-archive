## 1. Summary
The parent advances a strong structural hypothesis: sufficiently broad AI substitution contracts human labor demand, weakens the labor-to-money pathway, and transmits pressure through purchasing power, demand, production/distribution, and monetary credibility. It then presents transition toward a Connection Reward Society, centered on agreement memory, connection density, and reciprocal necessity, as the structural successor.

## 2. Concepts
- labor-money decoupling: breakdown of labor as the primary route to monetary access.
- monetary-society structural limit: pressure on a social foundation centered on wage-mediated participation.
- Connection Reward Society: connection itself functions as value and reward.
- agreement memory: relationship and agreement history supports subsequent decisions.
- connection density: structural relation between overlapping connections and trust strength.
- reciprocal necessity: mutual, circulating necessity rather than one-way dependence.
- four principles: circulation of necessity / agreement memory / reversibility / contradiction resilience.
- transition prolongation: bridge measures such as basic income, not the parent’s final structural solution.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
AI substitution expands
→ human labor demand contracts
→ access to money through labor contracts
→ purchasing power contracts
→ market demand contracts
→ production and distribution slow
→ pressure on monetary credibility and circulation
→ structural limit of monetary society
→ institutionalization of connection value
→ parent argues transition toward Connection Reward Society
```

## 7. Validity conditions
- AI substitution materially connects to contraction of human labor demand.
- Reduced labor income transmits to purchasing power, demand, and production/distribution in observable ways.
- Prolongation measures are distinguished from structural value transition.
- Connection reward includes agreement memory, reciprocity, and reversibility rather than popularity scoring.
- Connection and exit remain transparent.
- The parent’s strong forecast is distinguished from established external fact.

## 8. Failure conditions
- AI substitution expands without structurally reducing human labor demand, monetary income access, or purchasing power.
- Monetary society remains durably stable through other mechanisms after labor-money decoupling.
- Connection reward collapses into popularity, follower count, or centralized social-credit scoring.
- Connections become coercive, irreversible, or one-way dependencies.
- Basic income is elevated into the parent’s root solution.
- Other institutional theories replace the parent’s central causal structure.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- The relation between AI substitution and human-labor-demand contraction persistently fails.
- Purchasing power, demand, and monetary credibility remain durably stable despite reduced labor income.
- Connection value fails to produce social function through agreement memory, reciprocal necessity, and reversibility.
- More connections correlate with worse trust or social function.
- Long-run stable coexistence of money and connection value accumulates evidence against the parent’s necessary-transition hypothesis.
