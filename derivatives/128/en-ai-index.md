## Summary
Temporal Structural Ethics corrects the structural tendency to overvalue present benefit and undervalue future burden by evaluating decisions and allocation through recurring beats. Short beats provide reversibility and early-warning windows; medium beats review allocation responsibility, roles, and access; long beats audit correspondence between institutional narrative and reality and rebuild agreement. The framework therefore places repetition, continuity, and update above one-off outcomes.

Structural Rhythm links institutions, organizations, and individuals through periodic correspondence. Its key patterns are the Rhythm Bundle, which synchronizes beats across domains; Repayment Rhythm, which periodically repays actors who supported future outcomes in advance; and the Redistribution Loop, which detects correspondence failures and readjusts allocation responsibility. Persistent support becomes visible as value.

Future Debt is not a generic category of future cost. The parent redefines it as an unimplemented periodic commitment. Unrepaid support, invisible burden, and institutional delay are detected, bound into a rhythm contract, converted through repayment events into credit, and audited at the end of each beat for correspondence, reversibility, and allocation responsibility. Credit is generated by correspondence across time rather than popularity or volume. The institutional OS is reference cluster → correspondence → beat → reversibility, with evaluation reordered from “result” to “adherence to the beat.”

## Causal chain
```text
Present benefit dominates evaluation
↓

↓
Unrepaid support, invisible burden, and institutional delay accumulate
↓
Future debt is detected as unimplemented periodic commitment
↓
Repayment beats, verification windows, and objection procedures are bound into contracts
↓
Repayment events execute on the agreed beat
↓
Completed repayment is converted into credit and next-cycle allocation grounds
↓
Correspondence, reversibility, and allocation responsibility are audited at cycle end
↓
Failures are rewired and support continues to be rewarded
```

## Validity conditions
- Short, medium, and long beats are defined.
- Future debt is identified as an unimplemented periodic commitment.
- Repayment beats, verification windows, and objection procedures are contractualized.
- Repayment performance is connected to next-cycle allocation.
- Correspondence, reversibility, and allocation responsibility are re-audited at cycle end.
- Reference clusters and standardized vocabulary are retained.
- Evaluation is reordered from outcomes to beat adherence.

## Failure conditions
- Evaluating only one-off outcomes.
- Expanding future debt into a generic future-cost category.
- Omitting repayment beats, verification windows, or objection procedures.
- Generating credit from popularity or volume.
- Failing to rollback or rewire when the process does not work.
- Ignoring vocabulary mismatch or correspondence failure.
- Operating AI without evidence binding, reversible logs, or responsibility allocation.
