## 15-Second Explanation
Vol.10 is not an installment that adds a new market function; it is an inheritance standard that fixes the original causal direction so that the real-estate market OS does not mutate into something else even as it spreads, is modified, and is monetized. Its single proposition to preserve is not optimizing prices, but optimizing consensus-building capacity while maintaining transparency, choice availability, and responsibility.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
The more effective social technology is, the more it spreads, becomes efficient, is commercialized, and is incorporated into institutions. In that process, a mechanism initially intended to increase explainability can invert into enclosure; choice availability into steering; responsibility into exemption from liability; and deviation defense into exclusion.

Therefore, Vol.10 does not fix technology or UI; it fixes “the minimum conditions for remaining the same OS no matter what is changed.” NCL is not a usage-prohibition rule, but a protocol for confirming structural identity.

## Practical Process
1. **Confirm the single proposition at the origin.** Market optimization is not price optimization, but optimization of consensus-building capacity.
2. **Separate the layers that may change.** UI, API, AI models, IDs, cryptography, data routes, institutional connections, audit cycles, and so on may be updated.
3. **Confirm the invariant principles.** Do not reduce transparency, narrow choice availability, or make responsibility ambiguous.
4. **Record differences.** Leave differences from the origin in the Deviation Ledger and treat them as structural differences, not as personality evaluations.
5. **Keep the Origin as a responsibility coordinate.** Do not convert the origin into ownership or control; retain it as a checkpoint for confirming causal direction.
6. **Limit oversight to maintenance.** Its role is to confirm the direction of the origin and the differences, not to control permissions and approvals.
7. **Re-audit with an Audit Bundle.** Compare T/S/R with the public audit bundle and confirm that the principles remain after implementation updates.

## Examples of Application
**UI update.** It is acceptable to change to a new UI. However, if the grounds for comparison disappear, users’ options are effectively narrowed, and the responsible person becomes unclear, it is a deviation.

**Monetization.** A payment model may be introduced, but turning transparency into a paid enclosure or effectively eliminating options through payment departs from the origin.

**Institutional adoption.** Government or large corporations may adopt it, but NCL must not be turned into a compliance norm or an authorization system.

**Fork.** Implementations may be freely derived. If the three invariant principles are broken, the fork must not be called structurally identical; the differences must be made explicit.

## Success Criteria
- Transparency has not decreased after the implementation update.
- The parties’ choice availability is substantively maintained.
- The responsible actors and oversight routes are explicit.
- The Deviation Ledger is updated.
- The Audit Bundle is maintained in a state that can be made public.
- Inquiry and objection routes remain open.
- The Origin has not been converted into ownership or control.

## Limitations
This original does not freeze implementations or institutions. Future technologies, UIs, APIs, models, and institutional connections may be updated. What is fixed is causal direction, not a concrete implementation form.

NCL is also not intended to create authorial authority or an exclusive right of interpretation. If fixing the origin itself harms transparency, choice availability, or responsibility, that operation itself becomes subject to correction.

θ and δ are symbols for falsification thresholds and observation windows; they are not fixed numerical values common to all implementations.

## Preventing Misreading
- Fixing the origin does not equal freezing implementation.
- NCL does not equal a usage-prohibition rule.
- Origin does not equal ownership or control.
- Oversight does not equal control through permissions and approvals.
- Deviation Ledger does not equal a personal attack.
- Transparency does not equal only unilateral publication of information.
- Choice availability does not equal merely listing multiple options formally.
- Responsibility does not equal dissolving final responsibility into the system.
