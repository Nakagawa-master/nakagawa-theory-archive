## Summary
Real Estate Market OS Vol.1 redefines the property market not as a venue for accelerating transactions but as social infrastructure that aligns the preconditions for decision-making. It treats asymmetry not only in information availability but also in depth of understanding as a system defect, and places current value, future cash flow, tax, inheritance, law, maintenance, demand conditions, emotion, and ethical constraints on one decision plane. Its central move is to reverse the conventional supply-first sequence and visualize conditional latent demand before supply through a Demand-First Model. AI is an instrument for measurement, organization, comparison, and visualization rather than the decision maker; professionals retain domain interpretation and responsibility, while users make the final informed choice. Participant roles are separated, private implementation accumulates both transaction and non-transaction decision logs plus misuse and interface failures, and the target is neither to push sales nor suppress transactions but to create a market in which people can understand and choose.

## Concepts
- Market OS: infrastructure that aligns who understands what, at what resolution, before making a market decision.
- Understanding Asymmetry: differences in comprehension depth, not only differences in access to facts.
- Decision Plane Integration: placing present value, future cash flow, tax, legal issues, maintenance, demand, emotion, and ethics into one decision context.
- Demand-First Model: reversing the sequence so latent demand becomes visible before supply.
- Demand Signal: conditional interest, not a purchase commitment.
- Freedom Not to Move: preserving holding, leasing, renovating, postponing, or taking no action as rational options.
- AI as Instrument: AI gathers, structures, visualizes, and compares decision materials.
- Three-Layer Responsibility: separation of AI support, professional interpretation/responsibility, and user final choice.
- Participant Role Separation: separating demander, buyer, broker, manager, and other roles.
- Private Implementation First: beginning with revisable private-sector trials to accumulate decision evidence.
- Choice as Value: treating the ability to know, understand, compare, clarify responsibility, and choose as value in itself.
- Condition Z: a re-agreement condition involving audit cycle, T/S/R, and consistency of the public audit bundle.

## Applications
- Show owners not only a current valuation but also future cash flow, tax, maintenance, inheritance, leasing, renovation, and no-action scenarios in comparable form.
- Visualize latent demander conditions such as area, price range, and use case as demand signals without treating them as purchase commitments.
- Use AI valuation as a measurement and scenario-comparison surface rather than as a single-price oracle.
- Separate brokerage as contract/coordination/risk management from a party that has an acquisition interest.
- In private trials, log why a transaction did not occur as well as misunderstandings and UI failures, then feed those observations back into revision.

## Measurements and audit
- Are necessary decision materials presented on the same decision plane?
- Are valuation assumptions and numerical grounds explainable?
- Is demand visible before supply?
- Are demand signals kept distinct from purchase commitments and sales pressure?
- Is non-action a comparable option?
- Can responsibility among AI, professionals, and users be traced?
- Are broker and buyer interests kept distinct?
- Does operational reality feed future-cash-flow assumptions?
- Are non-transaction decisions, misunderstandings, and UI failures retained as learning evidence?
- Does compulsory disclosure or enforcement create a parallel/shadow market?
- Are phenomena M such as stagnation, overheating, or public backlash observed?

## Validity conditions
- Do not turn Market OS into a sales-promotion or price-manipulation mechanism.
- Align understanding preconditions without forcing one conclusion.
- Do not fragment decision materials.
- Visualize latent demand before supply.
- Do not convert demand signals into purchase commitments.
- Preserve both the freedom to transact and the freedom not to transact.
- Do not transfer decision authority or responsibility to AI.
- Make professional and user responsibilities explicit.
- Separate participant roles.
- Preserve revisability through private implementation.
- Maintain consistency across Condition Z / T/S/R / the public audit bundle.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing AI valuation to price fortune-telling.
- Using demand visibility to pressure owners into selling.
- Treating demand signals as commitments.
- Using transaction volume alone as the success metric.
- Treating non-action as failure.
- Making AI the responsible decision subject.
- Mixing brokerage and buyer roles.
- Freezing institutional specifications from the outset.
- Creating formal compliance but a shadow market through coercive disclosure or penalties.
- Increasing technical efficiency while preserving a distorted market structure.

## Falsification conditions

The parent does not explicitly expand the meanings of T/S/R in this article, so this index does not import expansions from another source. No universal fixed θ or δ value is presented.
