## 15-Second Explanation
Real Estate Market OS Vol.1 redefines real estate from a “market for selling” into decision-making infrastructure for understanding and choosing. It reverses the conventional sequence of listing properties first and then looking for buyers, making latent demand visible first in the form of “I would want it under these conditions.” AI does not set the price; it lays out options such as selling, holding, renting, renovating, inheriting, or doing nothing along numerical and time axes. Final judgment remains with humans, while the market OS is updated by accumulating decision logs and misuse cases through private-sector implementation.

## Why It Is Needed
In conventional real estate decisions, the current appraised price stands out, while future income and expenses, taxes, inheritance, legal matters, maintenance costs, repairs, and demand conditions are scattered in separate places. When owners, demanders, and specialists make decisions with different amounts of information and different levels of resolution, people can appear to have “chosen” even though they were actually forced to choose among conditions that could not be compared.

The original treats this not merely as a lack of explanation by individual companies, but as a defect in the market OS. If apps or AI are accelerated without changing the market’s premises, they may accelerate the information asymmetry and steering of a distorted market. Therefore, the foundation must first be rebuilt so that it aligns “who is deciding what, and after understanding how much.”

## Practical Process
1. **Bring the decision materials onto one surface.** Place the current price, future income and expenses, tax/inheritance/legal matters, maintenance and repairs, demand conditions, emotions, and ethical constraints in the same decision-making context.
2. **Make the premises of the appraisal visible.** Do not present only a single price; make the premises, period, and risks used comparable.
3. **Make demand visible first.** List conditions such as “I would consider it if it were in this area, price range, and use” first as latent-demand information, not as a purchase commitment.
4. **Do not pressure the owner.** Even when demand is visible, compare selling, holding, renting, renovating, inheriting, and doing nothing in parallel.
5. **Use AI as a measuring instrument.** AI organizes information and lays out multiple plans along numerical and time axes, but does not substitute for the conclusion.
6. **Retain specialist responsibility.** Specialists handle tax, legal, contract, and management checks; AI output must not blur responsibility.
7. **Separate participant roles.** Do not mix the positions of demanders, buyers acquiring for profit, brokers, and managers.
8. **Accumulate decision logs in the private sector.** Preserve not only reasons for selling, but also reasons for not selling, areas where demand exists but supply does not, and displays that were misread.
9. **Keep it updateable.** Revise the UI, numerical granularity, and explanation methods from actual use; do not freeze the initial specification.
10. **Make “being able to choose” the success criterion.** Look not at transaction volume, but at whether the conditions of knowing, understanding, comparing, and clearly assigning responsibility have been established.

## Use Cases
**Consultation about selling a home.** Do not conclude “you should sell now” from the current appraisal alone; compare holding for 10 or 20 years, renting, repairs, inheritance, and tax burdens. Visible demand is not an obligation to sell.

**Investment and business properties.** Demanders present conditions first, while acquiring businesses clearly state their desired price, required yield, and acceptable risk as buyers. Brokers return to neutral contract, process, and risk management.

**AI appraisal.** Instead of giving authority to a single point estimate such as “the appropriate price is ¥X,” make the input conditions and multiple scenarios visible as material for humans to choose after understanding them.

**Private-sector demonstration.** Logs showing that someone decided “I will not act for now” after seeing the numbers are also valuable data. Before connecting to national systems, secure an environment where misreadings, UI failures, and exceptions can be corrected.

## Success Criteria
Success is not limited to an increase in the number of sales or market turnover. It is a state in which owners and demanders can compare the necessary information, retain the freedom not to sell even when demand is visible, keep the responsibilities of AI, specialists, and users separate, make participants’ positions clear, and enable an informed human to make the final decision.

When the market should move, it moves without distortion; when not moving is more rational, people can also choose not to move. A “society of choice” in which both are possible is the original’s endpoint.

## Limits
- Demand-first is not a mechanism for purchase reservations or urging sales.
- AI appraisal does not replace specialist responsibility for legal, tax, contract, or other matters.
- Transparency is not intended to make everyone reach the same conclusion.
- Private-sector implementation is not a claim that permanently excludes public institutions; it is positioned as an updateable demonstration phase.
- The original places T/S/R under condition Z, but does not spell out the expanded terms in this text; do not borrow meanings from another original.
- Fixed θ values and δ periods are not presented as universal values.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read “demand becomes visible first” as meaning that properties not for sale may be placed into the sales market without permission. What becomes visible is the demand condition, which is separate from the owner’s intention to sell.

Also, do not read “make everything visible with AI” as “AI decides the optimal answer.” The original’s ethical design is the reverse: the more strongly AI takes on information organization, the more explicitly final judgment and responsibility remain with humans and specialists.
