## Structural role

A micro-implementation layer that translates institutionally accumulated trust into a proposal an individual recipient can understand, compare, and evaluate. It connects the institutional-design layer of Lecture 13 with the externalization-of-embodied-knowledge layer of Lecture 15.

## Structural summary

A proposal is not raw information delivery but structural translation. Product, benefit, and price form the basic sequence; questions, resonance, and silence shape the emotional flow; organizational and social consequences extend the proposal beyond a dyadic exchange. Requirements, semantic domains, vocabulary mappings, correspondence checks, version control, counterexamples, and rollback conditions are bound into a primary-log audit bundle so the proposal remains comparable and reversible.

## Central proposition

```text
Proposal quality is not determined by information volume.
It is determined by whether value, conditions, price, emotion,
and social meaning are translated into the recipient's context
with the right sequence and correspondence.
```

## Causal chain

```text
unedited information
→ price, details, and seller convenience dominate
→ judgment occurs before value is understood
→ resistance, misdelivery, or incomplete agreement
→ obtain recipient problems, decision language, and approval constraints
→ edit the product → benefit → price skeleton
→ edit emotional flow through question, resonance, and silence
→ connect individual effects to organization and society
→ verify requirement, vocabulary, and condition correspondence
→ record versions, reasons, counterexamples, and rollback conditions
→ comparable, reversible, self-directed decision
```

## Core concepts

- **Proposal editing:** reorganizing information into a decision-capable meaning structure.
- **Structural translation:** transforming content into the recipient's context while preserving facts, causal relations, roles, and conditions.
- **Skeleton editing:** sequencing value before price, typically product → benefit → price.
- **Emotional editing:** supporting thought, recognition, and reflection through questions, resonance, and silence.
- **Social-connection editing:** showing effects across individual, organization, users, industry, and society.
- **Versioning and rollback:** retaining changes, reasons, counterexamples, and recovery conditions.

## Required distinctions

- editing vs concealment
- translation vs paraphrase
- resonance vs compliance-seeking
- silence vs pressure
- social significance vs exaggeration
- reducing redundancy vs omitting material conditions
- reusable form vs fixed script
- closing a sale vs reaching a valid fit decision
- improvement vs semantic drift

## Validity conditions

- Primary statements and decision conditions are obtained from the recipient.
- Facts, prices, constraints, and risks are preserved.
- Value comprehension precedes price judgment.
- Refusal, delay, and disagreement remain possible.
- Social meaning has material support.
- Vocabulary and requirements correspond across parties.
- Versions, reasons, counterexamples, and rollback conditions are traceable.
- The proposal fits approval, compliance, and operational constraints.

## Failure / non-applicable conditions

- The offering itself does not fit the recipient's problem.
- Editing is based on seller assumptions rather than recipient context.
- Price or adverse conditions are intentionally concealed.
- Questions or silence are used as immediate-closing pressure.
- Information overload or removal of decision-critical information occurs.
- Social meaning is used to hide weak value.
- Semantic drift cannot be detected because version control is absent.
- The default sequence conflicts with legal disclosure, comparison-purchase, or emergency requirements.

## Interpretation constraints

- Do not reduce the theory to “put price last to sell more.”
- Do not classify it as generic emotional manipulation or storytelling.
- Personalization must not alter facts or conditions.
- Closing is not the sole success metric.
- Proposal editing cannot substitute for product–problem fit.
- Preserve reversibility and falsifiability from the audit layer.
