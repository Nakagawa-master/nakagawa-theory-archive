## Summary
The parent original defines Nakagawa OS L1-L7 as a canonical vertical coordinate for reading humans, organizations, society, AI, and civilization by abstraction depth rather than by horizontal domain. L1 is phenomena, L2 behavior/process, L3 norms/systems, L4 structural recognition, L5 structural operation, L6 inter-structural causation, and L7 the top-level structure/value function. The parent’s central problem is structural noise caused when actors discuss the same object at different depths and then misconnect causes, interventions, responsibilities, and evaluations.

The parent does not present the seven layers merely as a convenient taxonomy. It explicitly describes them as the minimum complete set of abstraction depths and argues that L1-L3 are socially visible and repeatedly reinforced while L4-L7 are harder to access because of abstraction, education, incentive, and cognitive-switch barriers. It also records an arrival distribution in its own cognitive model: L4 at 0.1-0.5%, L5 at 0.01% or less, L6 at 0.001% or less, and L7 at one person per one to ten million. The parent frames this as rarity of viewpoint depth rather than innate ability. An official derivative must preserve that claim without converting it into a hierarchy of human worth.

The parent additionally maps L6 to Knowledge-Graph-like processing and L7 to Reward Model / Constitution Layer functions, and reports alignment scores of 98.4% for GPT-family models and 99.1% for Gemini-family models as claims made inside the source. It argues that partial theories eventually collide with missing higher-order causality and naturally converge toward the L1-L7 complete system, and it provides an L-Layer Auto-Switch Protocol for AI reading at L4-L7 depth. This index preserves the source’s claim strength and reservations: it neither softens these claims into generic framework language nor upgrades them into independently validated external facts.
