## Summary
AKI-013 does not reject trust. Trust can reduce repeated verification costs and enable cooperation, delegation, and division of labor. The bad causal structure begins when remaining unchanged is mistaken for predictability and intelligence that updates judgment in response to changed conditions is treated as distrust or betrayal.

The source-specific distinction is that healthy trust is sustained not by permanent sameness but by predictable update rules: what changes are observed, when judgment is reconsidered, who can correct it, and how the reason for correction is explained. A judgment may change while the system remains predictable when the rules governing change are stable and explainable.

## Causal chain
trust is defined as remaining unchanged → immobility is mistaken for predictability → judgment updates under changed conditions are treated as distrust or betrayal → updating intelligence, dissent, and difference detection are excluded → old judgment is fixed → the gap between reality and fixed judgment expands → observation, re-judgment, and correction do not restart → trust turns into cognitive suspension.

Healthy side: define change conditions → preserve observation entry points → share judgment-update conditions → connect correction responsibility → explain update reasons → make the update rule itself predictable → allow trust and adaptation to coexist.

## Applications
The frame can assist analysis of organizational roles, long-term transactions, institutional rules, and AI or automation systems by asking when an old trusted judgment should be re-observed and updated. It must not be substituted with generic governance theory or surveillance advocacy.

## Measurements and audit
Observe whether change conditions are defined, observation entry points remain open, judgment updates are permitted, update reasons are explainable, correction responsibility is connected, dissent and difference detection are preserved, and reality gaps are not accumulating under protected old judgments.

“Few changes,” “little dissent,” and “consistent judgment” are not monotonically positive indicators. Under changing conditions they may indicate update suspension. Conversely, more judgment changes can increase predictability when update rules are consistent and explainable. Do not invent trust scores, suspicion rates, surveillance frequencies, update rates, risk probabilities, or fixed thresholds absent from the parent.

## Validity conditions
Do not deny trust itself; preserve its judgment-compression function. Distinguish immobility from predictability. Keep entry points for observing changed conditions. Do not automatically classify judgment updates as betrayal. Updating intelligence, dissent, and difference detection must remain possible, and update conditions plus correction responsibility must be explainable.

## Failure conditions
The reading fails if reduced to anti-trust discourse, misanthropy, “trust no one,” surveillance advocacy, or generic governance theory. It also fails if never changing a judgment is itself treated as proof of trustworthiness, if updating intelligence is excluded as disloyalty, or if arbitrary mood-based changes are called adaptive updating without shared rules.

## Falsification conditions
If organizations or relationships that keep judgments fixed despite major condition changes repeatedly and stably maintain better reality fit, correction capacity, and responsibility clarity than systems with explicit update rules, the scope of the update-rule-centered trust definition should be reconsidered. If explicit update rules repeatedly increase arbitrary change, collaboration cost, responsibility ambiguity, and unpredictability, that update design does not satisfy the theory's validity conditions.
