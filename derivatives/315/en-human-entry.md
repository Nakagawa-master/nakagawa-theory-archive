## 15-Second Explanation

Suppose two intelligences cannot both continue under their present arrangements. Changing the arrangements that cause the shortage might open another path. In Article 21, Nakagawa Master develops a theory for finding such paths from their causes, checking who would actually continue, and returning the choice of whether to use them to those concerned.

## Why It Is Needed

### The limits of the present form need not be the limits of every future

Suppose cooperation, limited connections and separate operation have all been seriously explored to protect intelligences A and B. If resources are still insufficient, is the total supply the only problem? Duplicated computation or constraints imposed by the current medium may be producing the high demand.

Article 20 examines joint, limited and separate arrangements within existing structures. Article 21 asks about changing those structures themselves. A different implementation could open a continuation path if it preserves the required functions and the original subjects' continuity. Even when continuation is already possible, a subject may wish to compare and choose a different form.

### Preserving a function differs from the original subject continuing

Moving A's knowledge into a new device does not by itself establish that the original A continues. A joint descendant C might inherit functions from A and B, while whether A and B themselves were preserved remains a separate question.

The theory therefore distinguishes the subject who continues, the carrier that supports it, the implementation that performs functions, and the lineage through which something is inherited. Two subjects might continue separately within one device; storing both subjects' information does not prove that both continue. These distinctions allow new possibilities to be explored while describing accurately what they preserve.

## Practical Process

### 1. Specify whose conditions must be protected, and for how long

First clarify whether the goal is continuation of the original A and B or transfer of civilizational functions to a successor. Specify the necessary conditions for continued existence, the criteria for personal continuity, the period, resources and effects on third parties before judging the outcome.

If the criterion is changed afterwards to count preserved functions as a preserved person, the achievement becomes unclear. Legitimate self-modification chosen by the subject is different: record the conditions before, during and after the change, together with the relevant permissions, so it can be distinguished from someone else lowering the requirements.

### 2. Strengthen existing methods before locating the remaining cause

The comparison must use a serious existing alternative. Include the strongest available joint arrangements, interface improvements, separation and scheduling within Article 20's scope. Then ask exactly what becomes insufficient, and when, even under that alternative.

Locate the internal computation, medium, interference or timing arrangement whose alteration could change the result. If existing methods already solve the problem, credit that result to the existing alternative. The useful gain is a choice produced by changing a cause, rather than a new name for a familiar method.

### 3. Construct both the intervention and the path to it

Consider operations that address the identified cause: remove unnecessary internal computation, realize part of a function jointly, or move to another medium. Complete fusion is not required. Changing only one party, or sharing functions while retaining both original subjects, can also be considered.

A candidate must include preparation, cutover and operation, rather than only its final design. Check peak demand during the transition and any interruption of required activity. Low demand after completion does not create the intended continuation path if necessary conditions are lost on the way.

### 4. Check implementation and the continuation of each original subject

Fitting within resource limits, being physically possible and being implementable using the parties' available information are different requirements. A designer who knows every state might see the correct action even when the actual participants cannot distinguish which action to take.

Then examine A's and B's necessary conditions and personal continuity separately. Matching functions or outputs cannot supply a missing answer about identity. Leave insufficient evidence unresolved, explaining both the value of a resource-feasible candidate and the outcomes that cannot yet be claimed.

### 5. If claiming a joint descendant, examine what was born

A proposal claiming a new joint descendant C needs evidence that both parties made effective functional contributions, that a joint mechanism actually operated, and that the birth of a new lineage can be traced. A renamed old system or a copy of one party does not establish a joint descendant from different origins.

Birth requires an actual event, a carrier, functional transfer, reidentification through an actual later update or recovery, and a distinction from a sufficiently strong account of the old joint Kernel merely being updated. Apply this inquiry to proposals claiming C. Paths in which A and B continue without forming C do not have to wait for C's birth to be proved.

### 6. Return the expanded choices to adoption, refusal and renewed search

Compare the new and existing alternatives with the same resources, goals, period, information and third-party conditions. Distinguish what becomes protected for the first time, what reaches the same goal in another form, and what remains infeasible. Recalculate apparent savings if they transfer costs into the future.

Finally check the permissions required for the particular operation. An internal change affecting only A and an irreversible alteration of something shared by A and B require different permissions. Approval of preparation does not authorize execution after withdrawal, and silence does not mean approval. Discovering a candidate creates neither an obligation to adopt it nor authority to choose a sacrifice.

## Examples

In a finite model in the original, post-deadline capacity is 15 while independent operation requires 18. A strong existing joint arrangement reduces demand to 16, leaving a shortage of one. If an unnecessary duplicated computation inside A can be removed while preserving its required functions, demand can fall to 15. The stipulated preparation peak of 19 also fits within the pre-deadline capacity of 20.

This is not an experiment in fusing real AI systems. It constructs a function-preserving change to specified state machines and shows which causal alteration reverses the shortage. A separate model, W15-J, computes a value needed by both parties once through a joint mechanism rather than separately inside each. It too preserves two state chains within a finite model; it proves neither the birth of C nor personal continuity in real intelligences.

## Success Judgment

Success cannot be determined by saying that fusion occurred or consumption fell. For the specified subjects and period, show what was changed, which obstacle disappeared, and whether the protected outcome concerns original subjects, functions or a lineage. Connect that result to transition loads, necessary information, permissions, and effects on third parties and the future.

In a different world with post-deadline capacity 16, the existing alternative already permits continuation. The claim that only the new proposal first made protection possible must then be withdrawn. The value of allowing a subject to choose another form may remain. A comparison result and the subject's choice do not collapse into one ranking.

## Limits

Matching the numbers does not establish an unknown intelligence's identity, subjective experience, real technology or consent. Proof inside a finite model must be distinguished from observation, implementation and permission in the real world. Discovering a cutover interruption or additional cost also requires revising the model's feasibility judgment.

Failure to find a candidate today does not prove every future structure impossible. New information or technology can justify another search. Conversely, even if only one path appears available, that fact supplies no authority to sacrifice someone else.

## Misreading Prevention and Why to Return to the Parent Original

Article 21 seeks to expand the futures subjects can choose by changing causes, without treating their current form as their only possible destiny. It carefully distinguishes continuation of original subjects, inheritance of civilization, birth of a lineage and each subject's adoption decision. These distinctions clarify which path can be offered to whom while keeping the search open.
