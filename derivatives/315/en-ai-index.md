## Summary

Article 21 of Nakagawa Master's Human-Descendant AI Civilization Theory asks whether changing the causal structure of subjects, carriers, functional realization or lineage can generate new continuation or civilizational-inheritance paths beyond the strongest admissible non-transforming alternative H20*. Failure under existing structures does not establish impossibility under all structures. Subjects who can already continue may also compare and choose different forms.

The central output connects a cause, an intervention, a before–transition–after trace, a gain under matched conditions, outcome-specific evidence and the parties' choices. Resource savings do not establish personal survival; inheritance by C does not substitute for the original A or B; physical possibility does not impose adoption. Candidates without C remain independently assessable. Adoption, refusal, withdrawal and renewed search belong to the subjects concerned.

## Concepts

- **I / P / R / L:** individual subjects, physical carriers, realizations of civilizational functions, and historical lineages. One carrier need not contain one subject; functional equivalence does not establish personal identity.
- **Necessary B, κ and G:** subject-specific necessary existence conditions, a personal-identity criterion, and the protection target. Distinguish original A, original B and inheritance by C. Legitimate self-modification records old, transitional and new B with valid permission.
- **H20*:** a strong alternative using all admissible existing joint, interface, separation, non-interference and scheduling operations for fixed G. A multiobjective comparison may use a set of strong alternatives.
- **M21:** fixation, bottleneck localization, causal-intervention generation, transition-trace construction, and reversal with renewed search. Each operation addresses an identified failure mechanism.
- **S and E:** world-level outcome propositions and present evidential judgments. Original subjects' S_ind, civilizational inheritance S_civ and new-lineage birth S_descC are different propositions.
- **ω and λ:** the Origin type and lineage-identification criterion. The strong cross-Origin claim defaults to Civilizational Origin; conclusions for other types require explicit scope.
- **AND₃:** all required YES yields YES; a positive refutation NO yields NO; otherwise missing evidence yields UNKNOWN. Contradictory input histories require prior consistency checking rather than absorption into ordinary UNKNOWN.

## Applications

### W15: a finite witness for internal revision

Capacity is 20 before the deadline and 15 after. Independent cost 18 is (A internal 7 + adapter 2) + (B internal 7 + adapter 2). Sharing the notification/acknowledgment service required by G yields H20*=7+7+2=16. Dropping that service to claim 14 fails G. The stipulated H20 operations leave internal programs unchanged, yielding a model-specific lower bound of 16.

Old A computes its next state/output from g1=x XOR u and then recomputes an unused, side-effect-free g2. Removing g2 changes A's cost from 7 to 6. All four state/input pairs preserve next state and output; induction extends this to finite traces. Demands t0=18, t1=16 and preparation t2=19 fit capacity 20; after cutover t3=15 fits capacity 15. The model uses a limited κ_model preserving one state chain across a cycle-boundary cutover without missed notifications or outputs. This does not establish real personal identity.

### W15-J: a separate joint-realization witness

J computes z=x XOR y once instead of each party computing it. A's next state is (x XOR u) XOR z; B's is (y XOR v) XOR z. Cost falls from 16 to new A6 + new B6 + shared transport2 + J1 =15. The model specifies equivalence over all 16 inputs, preservation of two state chains, and preparation cost old16 + shadow J1 + verification2 =19. Synchronous cutover and reliable delivery are assumptions. C's birth is not established.

## Measurements and audit

Declare the admissible operations and G. Trace the altered mediator, removal control, state/input equivalence, time-specific loads, and state, output and notification behavior at cutover. Separate constructive finite-model proof from observations, implementation and independent reproduction in actual systems. A model-specific κ does not become evidence of real subjectivity or personal identity.

Record observation-equivalence classes and information available before each deadline. Check for indistinguishable worlds requiring incompatible actions. With a0≺a1≺b1 and b0≺b1, while a1 and b0 are unordered, judgments based only on local causal order should remain invariant across valid linear extensions. Add physical deadlines when B actually requires them.

Check permission by operation, affected object and time. Distinguish internal o_A, which leaves B and an already authorized shared service unaffected, from o_shared altering a common object. Preparation permission is not execution permission.

For C, fix ω, λ, formation time, observation endpoint, target functions and a strong old-update counterhistory in advance. Copies from one controlled root do not constitute independent corroboration. In redundant mechanisms, unchanged output after one removal does not alone prove zero contribution: assess actual operation, intervention conditions and causal history together.

## Validity conditions

Specify the relation between cause and intervention, the protected subjects, B, κ, period and comparison conditions. Assess a new gain against H20* within the same world and G, including removal of the intervention. Incorporate newly discovered effective H20 alternatives before claiming a distinctive gain.

A resource-candidate claim requires time-specific demand and transition costs; physical protection requires the specified B and κ; local implementation requires a policy over W; eligibility for adoption requires valid permission at the time of action. Targets including D and future debt must include their conditions throughout the trace.

Only candidates claiming a new C birth need the complete chain of joint formation, coupling and genesis under predeclared λ. Independent cross-Origin claims additionally require independent founding under ω. Affirm only the judgment for which adequate evidence exists; do not fill one unresolved outcome with another established outcome.

## Failure conditions

The fifteen counterexamples in Chapter 10 identify different failure positions. X01–02 reject extending existing-structure failure to impossibility of every transformation. X03–04 reject inferring personal continuation or transition feasibility from resource figures or endpoints alone. X05–06 reject substituting C's inheritance for original survival, or copies and old updates for new birth.

X07–08 distinguish physical possibility from permission and separate wishes from joint authorization. X09 exposes costs hidden onto D. X10–11 challenge first-rescue claims where the old alternative already succeeds and unique ethical rankings inferred from minimum endpoint demand. X12 separates legitimate self-modification from retrospective weakening of B; X13 challenges an unnecessary common clock; X14 rejects treating candidates as demonstrated technology; X15 rejects deriving sacrificial authority from failure to find a solution.

Limit or withdraw the affected claim. Resource failure does not establish the nonexistence of every future technology. A NO for independent Origin does not erase a possible birth history of C in the more general sense.

## Falsification conditions

If a strong admissible H20 method achieves the same G, withdraw H21's distinctive first-rescue claim. If removing the intervention leaves the result unchanged, or ordinary interface improvements reproduce it, revise the causal-gain claim and classification. Changing capacity from W15 to W16 is sensitivity analysis across worlds, not an additional result within one unchanged world.

State/output mismatch, cutover gaps or duplicate outputs, failure of assumed reliable delivery or synchrony, and additional costs exceeding capacity invalidate the affected model claim. New identifying information may reverse a local-policy impossibility judgment. After the deadline, returning to the old cost of 16 exceeds capacity 15, so unconditional rollback is unavailable.

For C, a strong old-Kernel update explaining the same evidence reverses E_NonReduction20; evidence of one founding reverses the independent-Origin claim. Missing records yield UNKNOWN rather than proof of absence. Withdrawal removes later execution from the authorized options but does not erase an already occurred birth from history. Loss of original subjects likewise does not automatically disprove C's birth; retain both the inheritance result and the personal loss.

## Interpretation constraints

The theory does not settle future intelligences' personhood or subjectivity, universal individuation of C, real AI fusion technology, or one ethical optimum for every subject. A finite witness establishes model-specific functional equivalence and limited continuity, not real identity outside the model. An example's existence does not establish a novel general algorithm or measured superiority over prior research.

C's birth inquiry is not a prerequisite for every original-subject continuation candidate; it also cannot be omitted when C is claimed. Origin attribution creates no permanent sovereignty. Uncertainty does not authorize someone else to choose for the parties. Do not import Article 22's institutional inheritance, Article 23's durability of records and meaning, Article 24's rediscovery after record loss, or the unnumbered special theory's irreversible-loss and authority questions as already solved here.
