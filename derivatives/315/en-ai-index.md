## Summary

Article 21 by Nakagawa Master asks how intelligences with different historical origins can generate new paths of continuation by changing the structures that cause their constraints. First construct H20*, the strongest admissible non-transforming alternatives: cooperation, interfaces, separation, scheduling and non-interference. With several objectives it may be a set rather than one optimum. Failure within the existing structure does not establish failure across all transformations. A subject already able to continue may also voluntarily compare a different future.

## Concepts

I denotes individual subjects, P physical carriers, R functional realization and L historical lineage. One carrier need not mean one subject; preserving information or function does not establish the original person’s continuation. Fix the world, initial state, period, goal G, necessary B, identity criterion kappa, supply Cap(t), local observations, authority, affected third party D and future burdens before comparing plans. Do not weaken the protection target after observing the result.

## Causal chain

M21 fixes the comparison, localizes the first bottleneck under H20*, proposes a causal intervention on I/P/R/L or their relations, constructs the before–during–after history, and tests reversal and renewed search. Remove the proposed intervention to examine whether its gain disappears. If ordinary Article-20 interface improvements can reproduce the gain, return the operation to H20. A larger list of names is not a demonstrated increase in effective options.

## State model

Resource feasibility F_res, physical feasibility F_phys, implementation from local observations F_impl and valid permission F_auth are distinct. The final resource demand can fit while the transition peak exceeds capacity or B is interrupted. An omniscient plan can exist while no single policy works across observationally indistinguishable worlds. Use causal partial order where appropriate; include real timing and synchronization constraints when the protected B actually requires them.

## Applications

In W15 the old independent cost is 18, strongest non-transforming cost 16, and removing a side-effect-free redundant internal calculation in A yields 15. Preparation peaks at 19 under pre-deadline capacity 20, followed by capacity 15. A separate W15-J model moves a common calculation into joint realization J while retaining two state chains; all 16 state/input combinations have equivalent outputs and transitions. Costs, synchronized uninterrupted cutover and reliable delivery are declared finite-model assumptions. They do not establish real fusion technology, subjective identity, actual permission or a new C birth.

## Measurements and audit

At post-deadline capacity 16, W16 admits the old alternative, so a claim of first rescue is withdrawn. P2 ends at 14 but peaks at 22 and fails capacity 20. P3 ends at 14 with peak 20 and remains a distinct candidate. Adding D’s post-deadline demand 3 in W15-D makes P1 demand 18 against 15. Distinguish an operation comparison inside one world from a boundary test changing supply or protected subjects. Neither the lowest endpoint nor two-party benefit determines a unique ethical answer.

## Validity conditions

S_ind(A), S_ind(B) and civilizational inheritance S_civ(C) have different targets. World-level events S and current evidential judgments E also differ. A copy, matching outputs or preserved memories cannot by themselves settle numerical identity. Legitimate self-change can alter a subject’s conditions, but its old B, transitional B, new B, action-specific permission and continuity criterion must remain traceable. UNKNOWN identity is neither certified survival nor proof of disappearance.

## Failure conditions

An internal change confined to A differs from an irreversible alteration of shared A/B structure. A’s approval does not supply B’s approval. Permission for preparation does not cover later irreversible execution after effective withdrawal. Silence is not affirmative consent. Return adoption, refusal, suspension, withdrawal and renewed search separately, including residual losses and reachable alternatives. A technically feasible path cannot confer the authority to impose a transformation or sacrifice someone.

## Falsification conditions

Withdraw the corresponding claim when H20* solves the same goal, the causal difference disappears, transition B or kappa fails, the local policy or permission is unavailable, lineage evidence fails, or loss is externalized to D or the future. Three-valued conjunction is YES when all necessary evidence is YES, NO when a necessary item has affirmative contrary evidence, and otherwise UNKNOWN. ## Required distinctions

A claimed C lineage needs effective contributions from both origins, genuine functional coupling and a birth history distinct from an advanced update of the old joint Kernel. Genesis evidence asks about the event, operating carrier, actual transfer, actual later reidentification, and non-reduction to the strongest old-lineage explanation under a predeclared criterion lambda. General new-C evidence is separate from the stronger claim of independently founded cross-origin descent. Shared-source copies do not create independent evidence. These tests are conditional on claiming C; they must not stop a no-C transformation from being examined.
