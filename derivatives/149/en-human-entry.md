## 15-Second Explanation
The original treats crises such as disasters and AGI deviations not as “destruction that suddenly arrives from outside,” but as structural deviations that have become observable: future debt, a break in correspondence, and structural disconnection accumulated inside the Civilization OS. Structural resilience decomposes the deviation through four lines—correspondence, temporal ethics T0, reversibility, and allocation responsibility—and uses the deviation ledger and structural immune system to return to the design of the next OS.

## Why It Is Needed
If a crisis is treated only as bad luck, the same structure remains after recovery and future debt reappears in another form. The original treats aging flood defenses, vulnerable urban infrastructure, delayed climate response, and postponement in AI governance as examples of debt pushed into the future.

AGI deviation and natural disaster are not physically the same phenomenon. However, they share a structural pattern: contradictions and debt accumulate in advance and, once a critical point is exceeded, become observable as an abrupt change. The original treats this isomorphism through a common frame of “deviation in the structural CPU.”

The center of crisis handling is the four lines. The correspondence line traces where the correspondence broke; the temporal-ethics line traces which debt was sent into the future and when; the reversibility line traces how to reconstruct after damage; and the allocation-responsibility line traces who takes on which burden.

## Practical Process
**1. Trace debt before the crisis.** Record what was postponed by whom and where the burden was sent.

**2. Confirm three breaks.** Examine in what order future debt, correspondence, and structural disconnection overlapped.

**3. Find the STB overrun point.** Trace when the deviation exceeded the tolerance band from subject-specific signs, rather than imposing a fixed value after the fact.

**4. Preserve it in the deviation ledger.** Record the route, context, scope of impact, and precursors.

**5. Reconstruct through the four lines.** Inspect correspondence, temporal ethics, reversibility, and allocation responsibility separately.

**6. Connect to the structural immune system.** Carry out the minimum necessary reallocation.

**7. Do not stop at restoration.** Update the structure so that the same weakness does not recur.

## Examples
**1. Disaster recovery.** Do not merely restore the previous state; redesign vulnerability, budget postponement, evacuation routes, and burden allocation.

**2. AI operations.** Do not end with treating problematic output as a failure of the model alone; trace correspondence gaps among training data, evaluation metrics, operational responsibility, and user expectations.

**3. Organizational misconduct.** Investigate the structure of incentives, audits, authority, and allocation of responsibility rather than condemning only an individual.

**4. Public policy.** Examine whether short-term fiscal stability creates future high costs or concentrates burdens on vulnerable groups.

## Success Criteria
It is possible to observe signs of deviation before a crisis, not only after one occurs.

It is possible to trace who bore what burden during crisis handling and where correspondence broke.

Recovery includes correction of weaknesses and the ability to reconstruct, rather than stopping at restoration of the previous state.

Deviation information returns from the deviation ledger to OS updates and is used in a direction that reduces future debt.

## Limits
Reading a crisis through internal structure does not mean that external factors do not exist. Even when there is a natural phenomenon or external shock, the expansion of damage, resilience, and uneven burden interact with internal structure.

AGI deviation and disaster are not the same phenomenon. What the original claims they share is the structural pattern of accumulated debt, critical overrun, and exposed deviation.

The four lines do not have universal fixed scores or thresholds.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not use “the result of the whole structure” as language that erases individual responsibility. The original makes responsibility and burdens transparent through allocation responsibility.

Do not read reversibility as simple restoration of the previous state. What matters is the ability to reconstruct while correcting weaknesses.

Do not treat a decline in the number of crises alone as success. If future debt or unfair burden allocation remains, structural improvement is incomplete.

The original’s `θ`, `δ`, and `M` represent subject-specific falsification and revision conditions; they are not universal constants.
