## Summary
The source redefines trust as low prediction-error variance E for future decisions rather than as affection, reassurance, moral approval, or personality judgment. Predictability may be localized in a subject or distributed into history. History openness H does not magically create trust; it relocates the basis of third-party prediction from a person to reproducible records. Observation bandwidth B is separate from E and can also introduce measurement bias. Sustainable structural trust is the state in which decisions remain reproducible even when the original subject is absent.

## Concepts
- E: prediction-error variance for future behavior or decisions.
- H: reproducible history openness covering inputs, transformations, outputs, exceptions, and differences.
- B: observation bandwidth, including frequency, resolution, proximity, and bias.
- Subject localization: prediction rules remain inside a person or organization.
- History distribution: decision rules are externalized so third parties can reconstruct them.
- Conservation/relocation: predictability shifts between subject and history rather than being treated as a mystical quantity that appears or vanishes.
- Spatial compression: high-B/low-H charisma state.
- Temporal compression: concealment that delays observation of prediction error.
- Structural trust: low-E reproducibility without subject dependence.

## Causal chain
Trust is explained through personality or affection → subjective confidence is confused with predictability → trust is translated into E → the prediction target, error rule, and evaluation window are fixed → subject-localized and history-distributed predictability are separated → reasons, conditions, exceptions, and diffs are externalized into H → third parties reconstruct the prediction model → misses become learning data → predictability shifts toward history → single-point dependence on the subject falls → decisions remain reproducible after subject replacement.

## Applications
In management, externalize executive judgment rules, inputs, exceptions, and diffs so decisions survive leadership change. In hiring and evaluation, separate reputation from task-specific prediction accuracy. In AI systems, evaluate task-level predictions, inputs, outputs, errors, and model-change diffs rather than anthropomorphizing a model as trustworthy.

## Measurements and audit
- Fix what decision or behavior E refers to.
- Pre-register predictions, inputs, timestamps, and update conditions.
- Fix the evaluation window δ before outcomes are known.
- Define error according to numeric, interval, or classification prediction type.
- Record B, including frequency, resolution, and bias.
- Evaluate H as reproducible disclosure of input / transformation / output / exception / difference.
- Keep a Prediction Log with prediction, basis, B, H, registration time, evaluation time, observation, error, and exceptions.
- Compare third-party E before and after subject disappearance or replacement.

## Validity conditions
The prediction target and error definition are fixed in advance; predictions are registered before outcomes; B, H, and E are recorded separately; the history allows third-party reconstruction; misses and exceptions remain visible; and decisions remain reproducible after subject replacement.

## Failure conditions
Using affection, reassurance, status, or reputation as a substitute for E; rewriting misses as exceptional stories; shaping predictions after the outcome; treating document volume as H; mistaking high-B/low-H proximity for high trust; treating stopped observation as stability; or allowing the decision model to disappear with the subject.

## Falsification conditions
Under Condition Z, the audit cycle, T/S/R principles, and public audit bundle must remain aligned. If low-H/high-B still yields persistently low third-party E, high-H/low-B yields reproducibly low E, or high-H/high-B still leaves E high, then the H definition, log authenticity, B measurement, error model, or subject-to-history relocation hypothesis must be revised.

## Required distinctions
Trust / reassurance; trust / affection or respect; E / subjective confidence; subject trust / history trust; creation of trust / relocation of predictability; publication volume / reproducible H; B / E; charisma function / structural trust; short-term pseudo-stability / long-term predictability; target change / observation-environment collapse.

## Interpretation constraints
The source does not deny human emotion or relationships. It keeps them outside the operational definition of E. Increasing H does not automatically reduce E: history must support third-party reconstruction. δ and numerical examples are context-dependent observation designs, not universal fixed thresholds.
