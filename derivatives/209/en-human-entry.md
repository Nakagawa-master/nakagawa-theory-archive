## 15-Second Explanation
This original replaces “trust,” rather than placing it in emotion or personality evaluation, with **prediction error variance E**, meaning how closely future actions match what was predicted. It further explains that lasting trust is established not by accumulating it within a person, but by transferring predictability to a history where decision reasons, conditions, exceptions, and differences can be traced.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Trust such as “it’s fine because it’s that person” or “that company is reassuring” can appear to exist even when only people nearby know the person’s decision-making habits. But the moment the subject resigns, changes, or is absent, if the decision rules disappear, a third party cannot predict the future.

Accordingly, this essay does not deny goodwill or reassurance; it separates them from observable prediction accuracy. If predictability is externalized into history, even a third party who does not know the subject can reproduce a judgment from the same conditions. This movement is the core of “the law of conservation of trust.”

## Practical Process
1. Fix what is to be predicted.
2. Before the result occurs, register the prediction, grounds, time, and update conditions.
3. Fix the evaluation window δ and the definition of error in advance.
4. Record observation bandwidth B and history disclosure H as variables separate from E.
5. After measurement, confirm E as the spread of how predictions missed.
6. Return reasons for misses, exceptions, and change differences to the history.
7. Test whether a third party can reproduce a judgment from the same conditions even without the subject.

## Examples of Application
**Dependence on an executive.** Even if judgments continue to be right through an executive’s intuition, it is subject concentration if the reasons exist only in the executive’s head. Record condition branches and exceptions in history and see whether they can still be reproduced after succession.

**Personnel evaluation.** Rather than the reputation of being “a trustworthy person,” compare prior predictions and actual measurements for each target task. Record separately any bias in B caused by goodwill or proximity.

**AI use.** Do not trust AI by anthropomorphizing it; log predictions, grounds, outputs, and errors for each target task. Make it possible to evaluate behavior from history even when the model changes.

## Success Criteria
- A third party can predict a judgment without knowing the subject’s name.
- Predictions are registered in advance and have not been shaped after the fact.
- Misses are not hidden and are used to update the next prediction model.
- H remains in a form that can reproduce inputs, transformations, outputs, exceptions, and differences.
- E does not deteriorate sharply after the subject changes.

## Limitations
E is not a universal personality evaluation. It must be defined separately for each prediction target, and targets that cannot be measured must not be forced into numerical form. Increasing H alone does not automatically reduce E; the history must allow a third party to reproduce the decision rules.

## Preventing Misreading
This essay does not claim “do not trust people.” Goodwill, reassurance, and respect exist as human phenomena. However, when designing the sustainability of society or institutions, do not mix them with E, the observable quantity of trust.

Charisma and concealment are treated not as good-or-bad evaluations, but as distortions in the placement of predictability. Charisma is described as spatial compression with high B and low H; concealment as temporal compression caused by stopping observation.
