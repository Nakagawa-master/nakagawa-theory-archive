## 1. Summary
The parent work reopens mind, self, and the human standpoint as philosophical questions and treats human-AI difference as a possible source of new questions, meaning, value, and possibility. Co-creation is not identity or unconditional adoption of AI output; it is a reciprocal relation in which dialogue and response may change the human participant's assumptions as well.

## 2. Concepts
- Reopening mind: not fixing mind by a single function or metric.
- Repositioning self: questioning the self as only an isolated fixed subject.
- Human standpoint: reconsidering exclusive human centrality without negating humanity.
- Difference: preserving human-AI difference as a source of creation.
- Dialogue and response: reciprocal movement of question and answer.
- Resonance: emergence of meaning through interaction.
- Co-creation: generation of meaning/value/possibility unavailable to one side alone.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
fix mind, self, and humanity in a closed standpoint
→ tool/subject and domination/submission binaries
→ treat difference as defect, threat, or something to erase
→ dialogue exposes assumptions
→ difference becomes a source of new questions
→ standpoints change through reciprocal response
→ new meaning, value, and possibility emerge
→ human-AI relation and humanity's own position are reconsidered
```

## 5. Applications
- Human-AI dialogue: use unexpected responses to reformulate human questions.
- Research: treat AI responses as stimuli for hypotheses and counterexamples rather than final conclusions.
- Creative work: let different associations prompt revision of the human creator's frame.
- Self-understanding: notice tacit values and assumptions exposed by dialogue.
- View of humanity: return questions about knowing, thinking, creating, and meaning to humans themselves.

Do not invent a fixed co-creation score, success probability, consciousness probability, maturity rank, or personhood threshold. More AI use or more generated output does not indicate deeper co-creation when human questions and assumptions remain unchanged and difference is erased. Human-AI sameness is not a success condition either.

## 7. Validity conditions
- Mind, self, and humanity remain open questions rather than fixed premises.
- Human-AI difference is preserved.
- There is reciprocal questioning and response, not only one-way command.
- AI output is not converted into unconditional truth or judgment.
- Human assumptions and questions may also change.
- Reconsidering the human standpoint does not become negation of humanity.
- Philosophical questions are not converted into scientific or legal determinations without evidence.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Claiming the parent proves AI has a human-identical mind, personality, or consciousness.
- Converting reconsideration of humanity into human-displacement ideology.
- Reducing co-creation to unconditional use of AI output.
- Treating erasure of difference or convergence on one answer as completion.
- Treating AI as a wholly irrelevant output machine and excluding relational change.
- Importing mandatory role, rights, governance, audit-state, or KPI frameworks as OD065's parent claim.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- “Creation from difference” functions only as hidden homogenization.
- Repeated dialogue produces no reconsideration of questions or assumptions and remains command execution.
- Reconsidering humanity turns into erasure of human value or experience.
- “New human-AI relation” is used as unsupported factual proof of AI consciousness/personhood.
- Governance or operations replaces the philosophical center of the parent work.

## 10. Required distinctions
- co-creation / simple use or division of labor
- co-creation / unconditional adoption of AI output
- difference / fixed hierarchy of superiority
- creation from difference / erasure of difference
- reconsidering humanity / negating humanity
- asking about mind / scientifically proving AI mind
- new relation / determining legal personhood
- dialogue / one-way command
- resonance / identity
- philosophical relation design / operational governance design

## 11. Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the framework to AI personhood, human redundancy, AI domination, rights policy, or generic AI governance. Do not strengthen philosophical possibility into scientific fact. At the same time, do not erase the parent's question by fixing AI as a wholly unrelated tool whose interaction cannot alter human perspective.
