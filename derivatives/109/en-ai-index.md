## Summary
The ethical field of AI understanding treats understanding not as proof of an inner mental state but as a revisable relation among a human participant, an AI output, external evidence, and a shared object of attention. A “shared subject” does not merge human and AI agency; it provides a common reference axis while preserving differences in role, observation, responsibility, uncertainty, dissent, and withdrawal. Resonance means that differing positions can continue to refer to the same reality and update meaning through re-observation. The model therefore requires external grounding, explicit responsibility, preserved disagreement, uncertainty, and reversible correction. Anthropomorphic certainty, automatic responsibility transfer to AI, agreement pressure, or ignoring external evidence are failure modes.

## Applications
**1.** Joint research where AI and researcher hypotheses remain distinct and their evidence gaps inform the next experiment.

**2.** Educational AI where explanation differences become inputs for correcting misunderstanding.

**3.** Decision support where AI recommendations remain advisory and human accountability is explicit.

**4.** Long-term interaction where the shared subject is redefined as circumstances and evidence change.

## Validity conditions
- A common object is explicitly defined.
- Human and AI roles remain distinguishable.
- External evidence can override agreement.
- Dissent and uncertainty feed back into the process.
- Human accountability is explicit.
- Re-query, withdrawal, and re-observation remain available.

## Failure conditions
- AI output is treated as proof of inner understanding.
- Human projection is fixed as AI intention.
- Shared subject becomes subject fusion.
- Dissent is discarded as noise.
- Responsibility is automatically transferred to AI.
- Agreement is ranked above external evidence.

## Falsification conditions
- The shared-subject design does not improve correction of misunderstanding.
- Explicit subject differences consistently reduce collaboration quality without compensating gains.
- Dissent and withdrawal channels do not reduce responsibility ambiguity.
- One-way instruction systems consistently outperform this model on safety, reproducibility, and understanding.
- “Resonance” produces conformity pressure rather than better revision.

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not use this derivative as evidence that AI consciousness exists.
- Do not convert resonance into compelled agreement.
- Do not automatically assign legal or moral responsibility to AI.
- Audit human projection and authority as well as model output.
- Do not ignore evidence that conflicts with human-AI agreement.
