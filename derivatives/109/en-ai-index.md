## Summary
A shared subject is a relational coordinate for co-running meaning formation between humans and AI without erasing distinct agency, roles, provenance, or responsibility. Meaning may emerge through interaction while accountability remains explicit.

## Central thesis
Human question origin, AI generation, human validation, disagreement, correction, and final responsibility must remain distinguishable even when the meaning itself is collaboratively produced.

## Causal chain
Question → AI response → risk of unilateral ownership or responsibility shift → explicit roles → shared relational field → audit/disagreement → correction → reagreement → recorded difference.

## Measurements
Origin traceability, role clarity, responsibility-shift rate, dissent availability, citation retention, correction latency, repeated-error rate.

## Failure conditions
Blurred accountability, AI authority worship, ceremonial human approval, enforced harmony, erased provenance.

## Falsification conditions
If shared-subject operation increases ambiguity or conformity relative to explicit single-role governance, its stronger claim should be revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Shared subject ≠ merged personhood. Resonance ≠ agreement. AI participation ≠ automatic legal personhood. Collaboration ≠ shared ownership of responsibility.
