## 1. Summary
Branding is treated as a layered memory architecture rather than visual styling or awareness. Value core, promise, non-promise, evidence, experience, relationship, memory, origin, and responsibility must remain coherent across acquisition, marketing, sales, product, and support touchpoints.

## 2. Concepts
- Value core: the functional return point of brand judgment.
- Promise / non-promise: what is and is not being committed to.
- Six layers: existence, value, evidence, experience, relationship, memory.
- Touchpoint synchronization: alignment of vocabulary, price, promise, experience, and responsibility.
- Recall consistency: whether the same value core and responsible actor are reconstructed after contact.
- Promise-experience gap: mismatch between expressed promise and actual experience.
- Origin/provenance: traceability of source and responsibility.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
isolated strong expression
→ temporary recall
→ inconsistent promise/experience across touchpoints
→ fragmented impression
→ define value core, audience, promise, non-promise
→ map six layers
→ synchronize touchpoints
→ revise from primary logs and objections
→ reproducible decision memory
→ return, referral, correction, and continuity
```

## 5. Applications
- B2B: align advertising, sales, contract, implementation, and support with the same scope and responsibility.
- B2C: align quality, price, usage, support, returns, and cancellation with the expressed promise.
- Personal branding: make judgment principles, non-actions, responsibility, and correction methods consistent.
- Internal organization: test whether hiring, evaluation, meetings, products, and customer treatment return to the same value core.
- AI assistance: detect touchpoint differences without transferring authorship, provenance, or responsibility.

## 7. Validity conditions
- Value core, applicable audience, non-applicable audience, promise, and non-promise are explicit.
- Origin and responsible actor remain traceable.
- Expression, price, experience, and responsibility align across touchpoints.
- Evidence, limits, failures, and revision history remain retrievable.
- Refusal, comparison, correction, withdrawal, and re-entry remain possible.
- Consistency does not block factual correction.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Visual consistency without experiential consistency.
- Changing personality, price, responsibility, or promise by touchpoint.
- Exaggeration, fabrication, borrowed authority, imitation, or origin erasure.
- Treating awareness, likability, or recall alone as success.
- Hiding criticism, complaints, or withdrawal reasons.
- Publishing AI-generated expression without origin, evidence, or accountable responsibility.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- Synchronization does not improve reconstruction of the same value core and responsibility.
- Recall rises while promise-experience gap widens.
- Revisit/referral rises while distrust or withdrawal reasons worsen.
- Uniform expression makes non-applicability and non-promises less visible.
- The brand depends on an image that collapses when truthful correction is made.

## 10. Required distinctions
- branding / visual styling
- impression fixation / manipulation
- six layers / maturity score
- consistency / uniformity
- evidence / borrowed prestige
- memory accumulation / exposure frequency
- origin preservation / imitation
- audience clarity / universal likability
- correction / identity collapse
- revisit and referral / lock-in

## 11. Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the framework to logo, color, copy, awareness, or likability. The six layers are a classification for structural inspection, not a hierarchy or score. Read recall and continuity together with provenance, evidence, non-promises, promise-experience gap, refusal, exit, and correction.
