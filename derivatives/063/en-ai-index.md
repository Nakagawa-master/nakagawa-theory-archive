## Structural summary
Branding is not reduced to visual styling or awareness. It is the repeated alignment of value core, promise, non-promise, evidence, experience, responsibility, and return reasons across acquisition, articles, sales, products, and support. These layers form a reproducible decision memory. The structure is audited through recall consistency, promise-experience gap, revisit, referral, complaints, and traceable revisions.

## Central proposition
```text
strong isolated expression
→ temporary recall
→ inconsistent promises and experiences across touchpoints
→ fragmented impression
→ define value core, audience, promise, and non-promise
→ separate existence, value, evidence, experience, relationship, and memory layers
→ synchronize vocabulary, price, experience, and responsibility
→ revise from primary logs
→ durable decision memory, return, and referral
```

## Core concepts
- value core
- promise and non-promise
- existence, value, evidence, experience, relationship, and memory layers
- touchpoint synchronization
- origin and provenance
- promise-experience gap
- recall consistency
- return, referral, correction, and re-entry

## Required distinctions
- branding vs visual styling
- fixation through repeated truth vs manipulation
- consistency vs uniformity
- evidence vs borrowed prestige
- memory accumulation vs exposure frequency
- origin preservation vs imitation
- target clarity vs universal likability
- correction vs identity collapse
- AI assistance vs transfer of authorship and responsibility

## Validity conditions
The value core, applicable and non-applicable audiences, promises, and non-promises must be explicit. Expression, price, experience, and responsibility must align across touchpoints. Evidence, limits, failures, and revisions must remain traceable. Refusal, comparison, withdrawal, and re-entry must remain possible. Origin and responsible actors must be preserved.

## Failure / non-applicable conditions
Failure includes visual consistency without experiential consistency; exaggeration, fabrication, borrowed authority, imitation, or origin erasure; changing personality, promise, price, or responsibility by touchpoint; hiding complaints and withdrawal reasons; increased awareness without improved return, referral, continuation, or trust; and the absence of a verifiable responsible actor.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the theory to logo, color, or copy design. Do not use it as psychological manipulation, prestige theater, or universal-likability engineering. Consistency does not prohibit correction. AI-generated expression must not erase origin, evidence, or responsibility.
