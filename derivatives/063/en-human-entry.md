## 15-Second Explanation
This approach designs branding not as work to create appearance or name recognition, but as a structure in which one can return to the same value, promise, evidence, and responsibility at every touchpoint, so that reasons for judgment accumulate as memories within the other party.

## Why Divide It into Layers
Even if something is appealing in an advertisement, impressions become fragmented if articles, sales, pricing, products, and support say different things. Therefore, impressions are divided into six layers so that one can observe at which layer the promise and the substance have diverged.

- **Existence**: Who one is and what one takes responsibility for.
- **Value**: What one makes possible and what one does not promise.
- **Evidence**: Track record, primary logs, comparison conditions, failures, and limits.
- **Experience**: What happens in actual use, dialogue, support, cancellation, and so on.
- **Relationship**: Whether consultation, refusal, correction, departure, and rejoining are possible.
- **Memory**: What remains as a reason for judgment after leaving the touchpoints.

The six layers are not six points out of six. They are a classification for checking where impressions are formed and fragmented, not a ranking of strength.

## Connection to Sales, Marketing, and Customer Acquisition
Customer acquisition places questions related to the value core into the market. Marketing aligns the vocabulary, judgment materials, pricing, and promises across multiple touchpoints. Sales enables an individual party to compare the promise with the substance. Products and support show whether that promise holds as an actual experience.

A brand is a memory structure that returns all of these to the same origin, value core, evidence, and responsibility.

## Examples
### B2B
If advertising promises efficiency, sales speaks of a different value, and a different scope of responsibility emerges after contracting, the impression collapses. Align the target, non-target, implementation conditions, responsibility, and withdrawal conditions across touchpoints.

### B2C
Do not encourage purchase through atmosphere or video alone; connect quality, price, conditions of use, inquiries, and returns and cancellations to the same promise.

### Personal Brand
Rather than titles or photographs, consistently show what one observes, how one judges, what one does not do, and how one corrects oneself when there is an error.

### Within an Organization
Check whether recruiting, evaluation, meetings, products, and customer support all return to the same value core and prohibited boundaries.

## How to Measure
From the structure of the parent source, one can observe recall consistency, the promise–experience gap, revisits, referrals, complaints, reasons for departure, and the alignment of vocabulary, pricing, and responsibility at each touchpoint.

Even if the numbers look good, that alone is not success. If name recognition or revisits rise while the gap from actual experience widens, or if refusal and departure become more difficult, it is a failure. A reduction in complaints is not an improvement if it results from removing the channels through which complaints can be made.

## Preventing Misreadings
**This is not a theory about logos and colors alone.** Visuals are one part of the entry point.

**This is not impression management.** It is not a way to push an impression through exaggeration, falsehood, borrowed authority, or imitation.

**Not everyone needs to like it.** It is also quality when people outside the target can leave correctly.

**Consistency does not prohibit change.** If facts change, correct them while retaining the origin and the reason for correction.

**AI does not replace authorship or responsibility.** It may be used to detect differences between touchpoints or organize vocabulary, but origin, evidence, and responsibility must not be erased.

## Boundaries and Limitations
- Do not fix a promise without substance as a brand.
- In medicine, finance, law, and other fields, prioritize regulation and duties of explanation.
- Do not use authority or another person’s expression of success in place of evidence.
- Do not exclude complaints, criticism, or reasons for departure from observation.
- Do not create revisits or referrals through dependency or lock-in.
- If a branding structure cannot be corrected, re-examine whether it depends on staging more than substance.
