## 15-Second Explanation
The ideational layer of the Tokibi AI Network is a shared safety condition placed before humans, AI, and institutions connect. It protects room to move between subjectivity and objectivity, makes connection non-coercive, allows consent to be limited and updated, and makes it possible to return from a mistaken connection.

## Why It Is Needed
Networks readily amplify volume, authority, and speed of dissemination, and an explanation that spreads quickly can become a substitute for correctness. Then objections, cooling-off, renewed agreement, and withdrawal become difficult; humans face conformity pressure, while AI is readily treated as a final authority or pseudo-personality.

The ideational layer does not decide the correct answer; it prepares the premises for connection. It connects agreement memory, structural logs, audit abstracts, bundle metrics, and connection value accounting as shared vocabulary, transforming statements, consent, objections, and repair requests into a state where third parties can read causality rather than judge people.

## Practical Process
### 1. Show Current Position and Boundaries
Humans, AI, and organizations state their roles, purposes, information used, and the scope within which refusal is possible.

### 2. Limit the Purpose and Scope of Consent
Define what will be shared, for what purpose, and for how long; do not make it blanket consent or a permanent connection.

### 3. Temporarily Take Another Perspective
Re-examine the same event from the perspectives of others, institutions, time, AI, and so on, and compare differences, interests, and missing information.

### 4. Return to One’s Own Position
After comparison, return to one’s own experience and boundaries, and choose adoption, deferral, refusal, or correction.

### 5. Preserve Renewed Agreement and Recovery Points
Update the consent scope, actions, stop conditions, and reconfirmation points so that withdrawal, cooling-off, exit, and rollback can be used.

## Examples of Application
**Human-AI joint decisions.** Humans state purposes, experience, and boundaries; AI presents counterexamples and structural differences. Do not make AI the final authority.

**Education.** Even when trying the perspectives of teachers or AI, ensure that learners can return to their own explanations.

**Social media and communities.** Use cooling-off, objections, renewed agreement, and structural evidence; do not turn controversy or popularity into a permanent personality assessment.

**Public institutions.** Design refusal, limited consent, objections, reconsideration, and rollback before adoption rates.

## How to Judge Success
It is important to return to one’s own position after shifting perspectives, and for limited consent, withdrawal, cooling-off, exit, and reconnection to be genuinely usable. It must be possible to explain causal relationships even after removing people’s names, and disagreements and repair histories must remain.

A high return rate or repair rate is not a success if it resulted from forced perspective-shifting or consent. Even if renewed agreement is fast, it is not an improvement if objections or reflection were omitted.

## Limitations
The ideational layer alone does not automatically create technical safety, legal rights, relief, or auditing capacity. Concrete systems must be implemented separately, and their consistency with the ideas must be checked continuously.

The difference in roles between humans and AI is also a working hypothesis, not a fixed essence. Do not assert that AI has consciousness or personality.

## Preventing Misreadings
- Do not turn room to move into infinite empathy or self-sacrifice.
- Do not treat the ideational API as a centralized management algorithm.
- Do not fix humans as subjective and AI as objective in an essentialist theory.
- Do not use AI fluency as evidence of consciousness or personality.
- Do not treat choosing not to connect, remaining silent, refusing, or exiting as defects.
- Do not repurpose ideational vocabulary for personality evaluation, hiring, or credit decisions.
