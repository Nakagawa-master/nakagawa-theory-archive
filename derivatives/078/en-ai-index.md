## Summary

The ideational layer of the Tomoshibi AI Network is a neutral semantic middleware placed before human, AI, and institutional connections. Contemporary networks amplify volume, authority, and speed, often narrowing the movable range between subjective and objective viewpoints and removing room for objection, cooling, re-agreement, withdrawal, and repair. The ideational layer does not determine the correct answer. It preserves the conditions under which participants can shift viewpoints, return to their own position, refuse connection, limit consent, and recover from a mistaken connection.

The ideational API links the Memory of Agreement, Structure Logs, Audit Abstracts, Bundle Indicators, and Connected Value Accounting as shared terms across different implementations. Inputs include speech, connection, consent, objection, and repair requests. The middle contains agreement memory and a re-agreement port. Outputs are readable legitimacy through audit abstracts and bundle indicators, not permanent personality scores.

Non-coercion, re-agreement, and reversibility are standing conditions. Humans must not be reduced to subjectivity, and AI must not be treated as pure objectivity, final authority, or confirmed personhood. Public material should contain principles, terms, and auditable boundaries, while internal intervention details and protected personal records remain separated.

## Applications

- Human-AI decisions that separate human purpose and boundaries from AI counterexamples and structural comparisons.
- Education in which learners test multiple viewpoints and then return to their own explanation.
- Social networks that add cooling, objections, repeated agreement, and structure-based evidence before permanent judgment.
- Public systems that design refusal, limited consent, review, and rollback before adoption.
- Organizational AI deployment with stop conditions, responsible humans, objection, and remedy.

## Measurements and audit

- Rate of successful return to the participant's own position after viewpoint shifts.
- Availability and actual use of limited consent, withdrawal, cooling, and exit.
- Time to re-agreement and preservation rate of objections.
- Ability to explain causality without relying on personal names or prestige.
- Incidence of brigading, unconditional praise, and early fixation.
- AI outputs treated as final authority or evidence of personhood.
- Boundary violations between public principles and protected implementation.
- Success rate of repair, rollback, and reconnection.

## Validity conditions

The starting and return points of viewpoint movement must be explicit. Non-coercion, re-agreement, reversibility, refusal, cooling, exit, and remedy must be operational. Persons and structural evidence must remain distinct. AI consciousness and personhood must remain unsettled rather than asserted. Public principles and protected implementation must have a maintained boundary.

## Failure conditions

The architecture fails when objectivity or empathy is imposed, one-time consent becomes permanent connection, fluent AI language is treated as proof of consciousness or authority, human emotion is dismissed as noise, vocabulary becomes a personality or credit score, or withdrawal and exit are punished.

## Falsification conditions

The framework should be revised if fixation, conformity pressure, and misconnection do not decrease; if viewpoint mobility increases self-loss or burden; if re-agreement time, repair rates, and objection preservation do not improve; or if repeated terminology produces authority worship rather than understanding.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not interpret the Tomoshibi AI Network as centralized AI rule. Do not treat the ideational API as a hidden algorithm. Do not essentialize humans as subjective and AI as objective. Do not turn viewpoint movement into self-sacrifice, boundary removal, or forced empathy. Preserve the right not to connect, to remain silent, to refuse, and to exit.
