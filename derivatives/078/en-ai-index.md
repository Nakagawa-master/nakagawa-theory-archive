## Summary
The ideational layer of the Tomoshibi AI Network is semantic connection middleware placed before human, AI, and institutional connection. It preserves viewpoint mobility, limited consent, objection, re-agreement, withdrawal, cooling, exit, repair, and rollback before technical convenience or connection speed is optimized.

The ideational API links Memory of Agreement, Structure Logs, Audit Abstracts, Bundle Indicators, and Connected Value Accounting as shared terms. Inputs include speech, connection, consent, objections, and repair requests. Outputs are third-party-readable accounts of causality and boundaries, not a personality score, social-credit score, or proof of AI personhood. Non-coercion, re-agreement, and reversibility are standing conditions rather than post-hoc KPIs.

## Causal chain
Volume, authority, and speed dominate network judgments → viewpoints become fixed and objection/re-agreement space shrinks → humans face conformity pressure and AI is treated as authority or simulated personhood → an ideational boundary is required before technical connection → shared terms are connected through the ideational layer → non-coercion, re-agreement, and reversibility remain standing conditions → return, refusal, withdrawal, repair, rollback, and exit remain usable.

## Applications
- Human–AI decisions: humans retain purpose, experience, boundaries, objection, and responsibility while AI provides counterexamples and structural comparisons.
- Education: learners test multiple viewpoints and return to their own explanation rather than assimilating to a teacher or system.
- Social networks: cooling, objections, re-agreement, and structure-based evidence are introduced before permanent judgment.
- Public systems: refusal, limited consent, objection, review, and rollback are designed before adoption.

## Measurements and audit
The parent uses observational expressions including rate of return to self-position after viewpoint shifts; availability of limited consent, withdrawal, cooling, and exit; time to re-agreement; objection-preservation rate; ability to explain causality without personal prestige; incidence of brigading, unconditional praise, and early fixation; counts of AI outputs treated as final authority or evidence of personhood; boundary violations between public principles and protected implementation; and success rates of repair, rollback, and reconnection.

Source: parent original. Measurement actor: actors operating and reviewing the ideational layer. Measurement object: viewpoint mobility, limited consent, objection, return, repair, AI-authority drift, and information boundaries. Source modality: operational observations used to test whether the ideational layer remains viable. Permitted use scope: detect fixation, coerced conformity, misconnection, authority drift, and boundary violations. Non-guarantee scope: these variables do not determine personality, presence of mind, mental state, morality, employability, creditworthiness, educational eligibility, or social worth.

High return or repair rates are not automatically good if viewpoint movement or consent was coerced. Shorter re-agreement time is not improvement when objection or reflection is removed. A low count of AI-authority incidents is not evidence of health if objection itself is suppressed.

## Validity conditions
- Starting and return positions remain explicit.
- Non-coercion, re-agreement, and reversibility are practically usable.
- Refusal, cooling, exit, objection, and remedy can be selected without punitive treatment.
- Persons and structural evidence remain distinct.
- AI consciousness and personhood remain unasserted.
- Public principles remain separated from protected personal data and implementation details.

## Failure conditions
The framework fails when objectivity or empathy is imposed, one-time consent is expanded into permanent connection, fluent AI output is treated as proof of consciousness or authority, human emotion is dismissed as noise, ideational vocabulary becomes personality scoring or credit ranking, or withdrawal and exit are punished.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the framework if fixation, conformity pressure, and misconnection do not decline; viewpoint mobility increases self-loss or burden; objections and repair remain unusable; or repeated vocabulary produces authority worship rather than understanding.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not interpret the Tomoshibi AI Network as centralized AI rule. Do not treat the ideational API as a hidden algorithm or control mechanism. Do not essentialize humans as subjective and AI as objective. Do not convert viewpoint mobility into self-sacrifice or boundary removal. Preserve the legitimate options not to connect, not to speak, to refuse, to cool down, and to exit.
