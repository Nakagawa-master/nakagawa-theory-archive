## Retrieval summary
The parent distinguishes the ability to control an AI system from the ability of humans to change conclusions produced upstream with AI assistance. Shutdown, access limits, audits, and formal approval can remain available while humans become increasingly dependent on AI for problem formulation, option generation, evaluation criteria, comparison, and institutional design. In that case, formal human authority and effective upstream authority over civilizational updating can diverge.

## Core causal chain
```text
expanded upstream AI use
→ dependence in problem framing, option generation and evaluation
→ weaker independent human capacity to generate alternative conclusions
→ controllability may remain while conclusion-changeability declines
→ formal approval separates from effective upstream authority
→ civilizational sovereignty transition becomes an observable question
```

## Required distinctions
- controllability / conclusion-changeability
- shutdown authority / independent alternative generation
- formal approval / effective upstream authority
- increased AI use / completed sovereignty transition
- technical control / cognitive and institutional independence

## Revision boundary
If humans continuously retain independent problem framing, alternative generation, evaluation, reasoned comparison, and institutional translation, and can materially change AI-derived conclusions, the transition claim is weakened or narrowed.

## Non-claims
Do not add numerical thresholds, probabilities, scores, or inevitability not present in the parent. This derivative does not equate AI use with completed sovereignty transfer and does not require AI to become a legal person.
