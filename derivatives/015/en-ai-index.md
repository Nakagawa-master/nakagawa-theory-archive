## Summary
The origin treats scandals, accidents, public blowups, and organizational collapse not as single-cause events triggered by the final incident, but as state transitions after consensus stability S = U × R × H remains below critical threshold θ through observation window δ. The visible incident is the moment an already critical state becomes visible.

## Concepts
- Physics of Consensus Formation
- Law of the Critical Point
- S = U × R × H
- U: third-party reproducibility
- R: responsibility traceability
- H: judgment-ground and history/difference traceability
- θ: critical threshold
- δ: observation window
- D: deviation
- state transition
- collapse phase
- responsibility for neglected critical state

## Causal chain
U declines → R becomes ambiguous → H becomes untraceable → S declines → S < θ persists through δ → the system transitions from stable phase to collapse phase → deviation D is no longer repaired and amplifies → the final incident appears to be the cause.

## State / operational model
In the stable phase, U/R/H remain sufficient and deviations can be observed and repaired. In the critical phase, S is below θ and that condition persists through δ. In the collapse phase, deviations D amplify without repair and the final visible event surfaces.

## Applications
Use for organizational scandals, accidents, public controversy, institutional distrust, and team collapse by auditing how U/R/H degraded before the visible event. The purpose is not to erase responsibility for the final act, but to include responsibility for leaving an observable critical state unrepaired.

## Measurements and audit
Do not invent numeric θ or δ values in the derivative. Audit third-party reproducibility, decision/responsibility nodes, traceability of grounds and change history, persistence of low S, and post-transition amplification of D according to the target system.

## Validity conditions
Apply when degradation of U/R/H is observable before the final event, low S persists over time, and deviation amplification and collapse follow.

## Failure conditions
The theory fails if inverted into a generic upward tipping-point model in which adoption or support rises past a threshold. θ is a failure threshold reached by declining consensus stability.

## Falsification conditions
If U/R/H and S remain substantively high, no persistent S < θ state exists, and comparable collapses still recur, the model or measurement bundle requires revision. Merely formal disclosure of H is not enough; substantive verifiability is required.

## Required distinctions
- final incident ≠ sole cause
- critical state ≠ momentary fluctuation
- decline of S ≠ upward adoption tipping point
- formal disclosure ≠ substantive H
- apology/punishment ≠ structural recurrence prevention
- responsibility for final act ≠ responsibility for neglected critical state

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce this to a crisis-management checklist. Do not use it to dilute responsibility; use it to add pre-event critical-state neglect to the responsibility analysis.
