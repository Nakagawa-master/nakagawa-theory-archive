## Summary

Bundle metrics is a measurement language designed to prevent one number—revenue, views, popularity, rankings, or a single KPI—from governing complex judgment. It does not combine many indicators into a more sophisticated total score. Instead, it preserves heterogeneous axes as a visible bundle: causal integrity, reversibility, capacity for renewed agreement, independence from brigading and volume, audit-summary quality, linkage to primary structural logs, separation from personal authority, and suitability for public deliberation. Each axis must connect to evidence and a defined reading method. Public information includes axis names, meanings, interpretation rules, evidence links, and revision conditions. Weights, thresholds, aggregation procedures, and individual assessments may remain protected to reduce gaming, but protected details still require independent audit, change logs, and challenge procedures. Bundle metrics links quantitative agreement, which supports decision speed, with legitimacy agreement, which tests validity. Its purpose is to expose shape, gaps, tensions, and change over time rather than to produce a winner.

## Central Thesis

Legitimate measurement requires preserving multiple evidence-linked axes as a bundle rather than collapsing them into one score, so decisions remain resistant to gaming, personal authority, and irreversible optimization.

## Causal Chain

Single metric → target gaming and short-term optimization → divergence between displayed value and real condition → separate heterogeneous axes → show bundle shape and missing axes → connect to audit summaries and primary logs → monitor over time → revise through challenge and renewed agreement.

## State Model

proposed / defined / evidenced / bundled / interpreted / applied / monitored / contested / reconsensus_required / revised / retired

## Applications

Policy evaluation, organizational decisions, media assessment, public deliberation, and AI-output audits.

## Measurements

Evidence-link rate, definition changes, re-compression into one score, gaming incidents, missing-axis duration, audit mismatch, re-agreement time, and authority drift.

## Validity, Failure, and Falsification

Validity requires heterogeneous axes, evidence, bundle display, disclosure boundaries, and revision conditions. Total-score collapse, arbitrary definitions, evidence-free scoring, or social-credit use are failures. Increased gaming, reduced explainability, persistent contradictory results, stalled re-agreement, or continuing authority dependence falsify the operating claim.

## Required Distinctions

Distinguish bundle metrics from total scores, multi-axis evaluation from indicator proliferation, transparency from publishing gameable assessment rules, measurement from adjudication, and comparison from ranking persons.

## Interpretation Constraints

Do not convert the bundle into a total score, personality ranking, social-credit system, opaque discretionary assessment, or permanent classification.
