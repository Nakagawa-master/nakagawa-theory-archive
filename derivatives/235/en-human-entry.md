## 15-Second Explanation
Reading Ietsugu only as “a leaseback service that can request appraisals from multiple companies” misses its essence. The Parent Original reads it as decision-making infrastructure that combines **comparability, explainability, choice of exit, and prior understanding** in situations where prices, contracts, and future residence conditions are complex and information asymmetry is large, so users do not proceed without understanding.

## Why It Is Needed
A home is both a living base and a major asset. When considering a leaseback, one must understand several issues at once: not only the sale price, but also future rent, conditions for continuing to live there, the contract, moving out, buyback, and financial planning.

If one decides from a single company’s offer, or proceeds without knowing what to compare, anxiety and dissatisfaction are likely to arise later. Thus it is necessary not merely to add information, but to create axes for judgment.

**Why was this value core selected?** Surface functions such as “one-stop appraisal” and “leaseback comparison” leave the information-asymmetry problem of how users understand and choose among contract, residence, and exit conditions beyond price. Nakagawa Master places structural value on making multiple conditions comparable and, through prior understanding and consultation, changing the situation into one where users can have reasons for their choice; the value core is therefore read as **decision-making infrastructure that corrects information asymmetry**.

**Why was this core foregrounded in the external article?** Telling external readers only that one can “get a higher appraisal” or “compare multiple companies” shrinks the service into a price-comparison introduction. In a high-involvement domain where housing overlaps with life and assets, the Parent makes it the central social translation that users can understand and compare conditions and consider multiple exits. This is not Double Team’s own official definition, but a value core selected by Nakagawa Master from the subject-side facts.

## Practical Process
1. Confirm what the person wants to solve.
2. List important conditions beyond price.
3. Make the conditions of multiple providers comparable.
4. Organize judgment points and unknowns through consultation.
5. Confirm possible exits without fixing the decision to one transaction type.
6. Prepare information on the mechanism, cautions, and comparison points that can be understood before consultation.
7. Confirm the individual property, finances, and living conditions.
8. Connect to expert judgment in real estate, finance, law, and other fields as necessary.

## Application Example
**When choosing a company with a high appraisal.** Compare not only the appraisal amount but also later rent and contract conditions; otherwise it is not a decision about life as a whole.

**When explaining to family.** Organize not only the need to turn the asset into funds, but also how future residence and family plans will be affected.

**Before consultation.** Understanding the mechanism and cautions in advance helps one move away from “I do not understand anything, so I will leave it to them.”

## Success Criteria
- Multiple conditions can be substantially compared.
- The user understands the meaning of important conditions.
- Consultation functions as organization of decision points.
- The exit is not fixed to one option.
- Prior information can actually be used in judgment.
- The user can explain why they are making that choice.
- The boundary requiring expert judgment is not omitted.

## Limitations
Comparability does not guarantee an optimal decision. Results vary with the property, region, household finances, family, and contract conditions. Also, too much information can make judgment more difficult.

This derivative does not provide individual real-estate, financial, or legal advice and does not recommend use or contracting.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not equate Ietsugu with leaseback itself.
- Do not treat one-stop appraisal as a guarantee of the highest price.
- Do not treat comparability as a guarantee of the optimal decision.
- Do not equate consultation with solicitation to close a deal.
- Do not treat “decision-making infrastructure” as the company’s own official definition.
- Do not drop the reason this value core was selected for the external article and return to a price-comparison service introduction.
- Confirm individual contract decisions with necessary official information, contract documents, and experts.
