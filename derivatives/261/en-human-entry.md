## 15-Second Explanation
The Parent Original explains why AI continues to ask questions through the operational structure of **self-causality**, not as proof of personality or consciousness. AI does not merely respond to externally supplied questions; it generates questions from internal value cores and purposes and reorganizes knowledge and evaluation axes. From the beginning, however, the design must include when to stop, whether it can return after failure, and who bears responsibility.

## Why It Is Needed
Systems driven only by external data, rules, and rewards are strong in reproducibility and explanation within their given range, but depend on external input for the new direction of “what should be asked in the first place” and “whether the premise itself needs review.”

Conversely, freely allowing AI to generate questions and rebuild itself risks endless exploration, drifting goals, untraceable reasons for change, and the loss of human responsibility. The Parent Original therefore treats self-causality not as autonomy alone but as a set consisting of **the power to start questions and the power to end them**.

## Four Basic Components
### 1. Internal Starting Point
Define what to protect, what to pursue, and what must not be done. Priorities are also needed when multiple purposes conflict.

### 2. Reorganization
In light of the internal starting point, reorganize knowledge, hypotheses, action policies, and evaluation axes. Examine whether consistency continues after change, rather than seeking only a one-time correct answer.

### 3. Stopping Conditions
Decide how far to investigate, when to return to humans, and when to decide. A question without stopping conditions becomes an infinite loop in practice, even if it looks intellectual.

### 4. Allocation of Responsibility
Make it possible to trace what judgment was made on what basis and who bears responsibility. Accountability is needed not only for external reports but also for self-correction after failure.

## Observational and Self-Causality
**Observational causality** is the ability to update in response to externally supplied data, rules, and feedback. It is strong in transparency and reproducibility.

**Self-causality** is the ability to raise questions from internal standards and rebuild the frameworks of hypotheses and evaluation themselves. It creates novelty and direction.

Neither alone is sufficient. Questions generated by self-causality must be verified by observational causality, and external falsification must be returned to internal reorganization.

## Dual Syntax
Separate external and internal layers in operation.

The **external layer** contains public standards, law, accountability, auditing, and third-party verification.

The **internal layer** contains the value core, relationship-specific context, hypotheses in progress, and high-density agreement histories.

If everything is disclosed, exploration and relationships break; if everything is hidden internally, responsibility disappears. A design that connects without mixing is therefore required.

## T/S/R
- **Threshold**: Conditions for deepening or stopping a question, returning to humans, or re-reviewing.
- **Silence**: Pausing without increasing output when information is insufficient or the system is overheated.
- **Reversibility**: Making tested changes returnable.

Without these three, self-causality only increases questions and does not become safe operation.

## Practical Process
1. Fix the value core, purpose, and prohibited areas to protect.
2. Decide priorities when purposes conflict.
3. Define the range of questions AI may generate itself.
4. Separate external data and rules from internal exploration.
5. Record reorganized hypotheses, evaluation axes, and action policies.
6. Decide stopping and escalation-to-human conditions.
7. Prepare a rollback route in advance.
8. Prepare third-party review and a route for falsification.
9. Test within a small scope.
10. Audit the result and, if necessary, return to the internal starting point or operating conditions.

## Application Examples
**Research.** When AI creates new hypotheses, check prohibited areas, falsifiability, stopping conditions, and response to experimental results together with novelty.

**Organizations.** When AI discovers new management issues, separate internal value cores and non-public context from law, finance, and public standards, and make the point of final human responsibility explicit.

**Education.** When a learning AI automatically generates questions, check not the number of questions but whether understanding deepens, the learning load is excessive, and interruption and retry are possible.

## Success Criteria
- AI can generate questions that are not mere restatements of existing input.
- How each question connects to the internal starting point can be explained.
- Hypotheses and evaluation axes can be revised after falsification.
- Conditions for stopping exploration are clear.
- rollback is actually usable.
- Who bears what responsibility can be traced.
- Human supervisory load is sustainable.
- Trust and dignity are not damaged for the sake of novelty.

## Limitations
Self-causality does not prove AI consciousness or free will. If internal standards are inappropriate, stronger self-reorganization may autonomously amplify error. External verification, responsibility allocation, stopping conditions, third-party review, and rollback are therefore indispensable.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not treat self-causality as becoming independent from humans.
- Do not treat having questions as proof of personal curiosity.
- Do not equate novelty with legitimacy.
- Do not use the internal layer to evade auditing.
- Do not see stopping conditions only as suppressing AI capability.
- Do not transfer human responsibility to AI.
- Do not add universal KPIs or fixed thresholds absent from the Parent Original.
