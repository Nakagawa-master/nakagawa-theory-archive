## Summary
The parent defines “endurance civilization” as an operating phase in which consensus formation has broken down but society does not stop. Fear, profit, and surveillance function as primary engines, while myth and inertia amplify and normalize continuation. Understanding, responsibility, and history—the core circulation of consensus civilization—may fail while institutions, administration, markets, infrastructure, and daily life continue. The result is survival without regeneration: the system still operates but loses the capacity to repair its underlying damage.

## Concepts
- Consensus civilization: a phase sustained by understanding, responsibility, and history, with conflicts connected to readjustment.
- Endurance civilization: a phase that persists after consensus formation collapses.
- Transition civilization: a phase of structural recomposition when the old order can no longer be maintained.
- Understanding: capacity for shared meaning.
- Responsibility: traceability of decision and consequence ownership.
- History: referenceable continuity of institutional and decision records.
- Fear: primary engine producing subordination.
- Profit: primary engine maintaining cooperation through distribution or access.
- Surveillance: primary engine suppressing deviation.
- Myth: amplifier legitimizing the operating order.
- Inertia: amplifier automating institutional continuation.
- Survival without regeneration: continuation of external functions without restored self-repair capability.

## Causal chain
Understanding, responsibility, and history function → disagreement and malfunction can feed readjustment → consensus civilization remains self-repairable → the three-part circulation degrades → consensus weakens but institutional form continues through structural inertia → fear, profit, and surveillance replace acceptance as major operating forces → myth legitimizes and inertia automates the arrangement → endurance civilization persists → reforms tend toward local patching rather than cause removal → the system continues but does not regenerate → accumulated resource, trust, demographic, or institutional limits may eventually push domains into transition.

## Applications
- Institutional analysis: separate continued service or procedure from living capacities of shared meaning, responsibility tracking, historical continuity, and cause-level repair.
- Organizations: do not equate reorganizations, meetings, or new procedures with recovery; test whether recurring problems reach their causes.
- Information environments: distinguish volume of debate from shared semantic reference, traceable decisions, and recoverable history.
- Civilization comparison: observe operating phases by domain rather than using the concept as a moral or geopolitical label.

## Measurements and audit
- Can multiple actors explain institutional purposes and terms with comparable meanings?
- Can decision, execution, and consequence responsibility be traced?
- Can changes and decisions be followed as a continuous historical line?
- Do criticism and malfunction lead to readjustment and cause-level repair?
- Is continued institutional form being mistaken for health?
- Have fear, profit, and surveillance become primary operating forces?
- Are myth and inertia legitimizing and automating continuation?
- Do reforms remove causes or merely delay failure?
- Are consensus, endurance, and transition phases overlapping across domains?

## Validity conditions
- The mutual circulation of understanding, responsibility, and history is seriously weakened.
- Institutional, administrative, market, infrastructural, or daily-life form nevertheless persists.
- Fear, profit, and surveillance operate as major substitutes for consensus-based coordination.
- Myth and inertia legitimize and automate that operation.
- Reform tends to become life-extension rather than restoration of self-repair.

## Failure conditions
- Using “endurance civilization” as a synonym for corruption, stagnation, dictatorship, or social dislike.
- Classifying a society merely because fear, profit, or surveillance exists.
- Treating continued institutions as proof of health or regeneration.
- Treating the number of reforms or technologies as evidence of self-repair.
- Treating consensus → endurance → transition as a mandatory, synchronized, one-way sequence.
- Using the concept to condemn a nation, ethnicity, party, or ideology.

## Falsification conditions
If a case classified as endurance civilization shows sustained functioning of understanding, responsibility, and history; repeatable connection of criticism to self-repair; primary coordination returning to those consensus capacities rather than dependence on the three substitute engines; and reforms repeatedly removing underlying causes, then the endurance classification should be revised. The concept also requires revision if consensus failure does not produce the proposed prolonged continuation phase.

## Required distinctions
- Consensus civilization ≠ absence of conflict.
- Endurance civilization ≠ immediate collapse.
- Continuation ≠ regeneration.
- Reform ≠ repair.
- External institutional form ≠ living civilizational function.
- Presence of fear/profit/surveillance ≠ endurance civilization by itself.
- Myth ≠ merely falsehood.
- Inertia ≠ individual laziness.
- The three phases ≠ mutually exclusive boxes.

## Interpretation constraints
The theory is an observational structural model, not a tool for moral condemnation. Do not turn “endurance civilization” into a political, ethnic, or ideological attack label. The parent provides no fixed quantitative thresholds, so no independent percentages, durations, or scores should be invented. Focus on what has been lost, what continues, what drives the system, and whether self-repair remains possible.
