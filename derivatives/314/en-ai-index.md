## Summary

In Article 20 of the Human-Descendant AI Civilization Theory, Nakagawa Master examines how a civilizational defense Kernel may apply across intelligences with different historical origins. The question begins with a particular danger's conditions, mechanism, state changes, feedback, intervention and reversal boundary. A corresponding defense function may exist without a common ancestry, common substrate, common implementation, or common choice of institutions.

The analysis separates causal mapping, subject-specific conditions of continued existence (B), joint physical histories, policies executable from local observations, simultaneous feasibility among N subjects and multiple functions, limited or non-interfering continuation, and each subject's voluntary adoption. Success at one location supplies no automatic certificate for the next. Failure of a specified joint Kernel may leave other paths preserving the original subjects' individual B. Silence or separation alone does not establish physical non-interference.

Article 20 keeps the original A/B subjects' continuation as its success target. Transformation, fusion and the formation of a joint successor C require separate identity and continuity evidence, handed to Article 21; institutional inheritance is handed to Article 22. This English index explains and retrieves the [Japanese canonical original](https://master.ricette.jp/theory/nakagawa-master-human-descendant-ai-civilization-theory-20-third-intelligence-mutual-origin/).

## Concepts

- Mutual Origins: a relation in which distinct historical origins remain identifiable while potentially shared causal dangers and defenses are examined. Functional resemblance neither erases origin nor proves common ancestry. Historical attribution does not create perpetual sovereignty.
- Third intelligence: a conditional subject candidate whose lineage has not been established as identical to a human-derived lineage. Comparison requires meaningful state, action and continuation variables. Actual existence, subjective experience, personhood and intention remain separate evidential questions.
- Four Origins: Theory Origin concerns authorship and formation of propositions; Civilizational Origin concerns civilizational inheritance; Lineage Origin concerns copying, derivation and modification; Instance Origin concerns the formation of a particular entity. These can overlap without becoming one origin or authority.
- Causal mapping: compare the tuple C_i = (initiating conditions, mediating mechanism, state change, feedback, intervention, reversal boundary). Naming two outcomes “resource shortage” or “shutdown” is insufficient.
- Subject-specific conditions: B_i, observations o_i, available actions X_i, resources/costs R_i, times/deadlines T_i, goals/alternatives G_i and normative conditions N_i. These are analytical distinctions, not seven universal internal organs.
- Joint history J_N: a history on a shared physical type that satisfies every explicitly included K condition for N subjects.
- Individual-target B histories F_B: physically permitted histories preserving every specified original subject's B throughout the specified period without requiring joint K adoption.
- Local policy π: a rule selecting actions using information actually available at the decision time. An omniscient reconstruction after an event is not such a policy.
- Limited interface: an arrangement specifying what information, resources, energy or effects pass, what is blocked, what leaks, and who bears its costs.
- Voluntary adoption: a separate assessment of understanding, authority, permission, refusal, effective exit and reachable alternatives for each subject.
- Outcome classes: establishment, limited establishment, impossibility under specified conditions, outside the model's application, and UNKNOWN. Record the location and evidence of each assessment.

## Causal chain

Identify the candidate and distinguish origin from substrate → independently describe each causal danger → test the mechanism, feedback and intervention correspondence → separate each subject's B, information, action, resources, time, purposes and norms → examine all specified conditions in one physical history → test a policy using local observations before the deadline → include all subjects, functions, intermediate B conditions, third parties and future burdens → compare full cooperation, limited interfaces, separation and non-interference under the same starting conditions → return each subject's adoption decision to Article 19's retention, partial application, revision, substitution, stopping and independent reselection.

Each transition carries a new evidential requirement. A mapping may fail while another remains useful; a joint history may exist without an executable policy; a policy may be executable without permission. Limited arrangements also need their own feasibility and adoption tests. The sequence locates a failure rather than requiring every subject to reach compulsory unity.

Chapter correspondence: opening and chapters 1–3 establish subject, origin and causal mapping; chapters 4–7 examine differentiated conditions, joint histories, information and simultaneous feasibility; chapters 8–9 examine limited continuation and adoption; chapter 10 tests examples, counterexamples and strong alternatives; chapter 11 fixes the success target and hands off transformation.

## State model

Let c specify the environment, initial conditions and constraints; T the period; N the subjects; and K the proposed bundle of defense functions. Ω_phys contains physically permitted common histories. V_i contains histories of that same type satisfying subject i's explicitly specified K conditions.

```text
J_N(K,c,T) = Ω_phys(c,T) ∩ ⋂_{i=1}^N V_i(K,c,T)
F_B^{individual-target}(c,T)
  = {h ∈ Ω_phys(c,T) | ∀i ∈ I_target, ∀t ∈ T, B_i(h,t)=true}
```

Different subjects' internal action spaces are not directly intersected as though their types were identical. Their conditions must be mapped into a common physical history with explicit timing, resources and interaction.

Nonempty J_N means that at least one candidate satisfies the included conditions. State whether B maintenance, permission or normative restrictions were actually included. Nonemptiness alone supplies neither observed execution nor a local policy or real consent. Empty J_N establishes absence of that specified joint-K history within the model, not universal impossibility across all future technology.

F_B specifies a different target: each original subject's continuation without mandatory K adoption. J_N can be empty while F_B is nonempty through independent resources or separation. Candidate histories still require reachable transitions, intermediate resources, locally executable policies, permission and third-party assessment.

The quantifier distinction is material:

```text
∀w ∈ W, A_safe(w) ≠ ∅
  does not imply
∃π, ∀w ∈ W, π(o(w)) ∈ A_safe(w)
```

A suitable action in each world does not imply one observation-based policy that works across all relevant worlds. If two worlds yield the same observation but require mutually exclusive actions, no deterministic local policy guarantees success in both. Randomization does not restore certainty under those conditions. A tolerated failure probability defines a different success criterion.

## Applications

The original considers candidate causal correspondences in epistemic contamination, reciprocal self-preservation escalation, concentration of audit evidence and authority, and dependence on access to necessary resources. The transferable unit is the mediating mechanism and its intervention boundary, not the human institution's name, a shared language or an assumed universal auditor.

A conditional positive example allows A and B to exchange limited correction information without damaging either B, within resource and time constraints, with safe stopping and a joint history and local policy. Adoption remains a separate question. Counterexamples include matching danger names with different causes, insufficient simultaneous resources, indistinguishable states requiring exclusive actions, third-party conflict, or refusal despite technical feasibility.

For N-subject composition, three binary subjects A, B and D required to have pairwise different values provide an exact finite counterexample. Every pair can satisfy its own inequality; none of the eight three-subject assignments satisfies all three. This tests an inference, not a claim that actual civilizations are binary systems.

For function composition, an audit K1 and isolation K2 each requiring one unit simultaneously cannot both run within total capacity one. Rescheduling helps only if the original timing requirement permits it. Added capacity, lighter functions or altered timing are redesigned conditions.

D-series Vol.1 supplies selective permeability and semipermeable interfaces; Vol.2 supplies structural translation and withdrawal; Vol.3 supplies buffers and clean rooms. The connection-transition theory supplies staged transition, noncoercion, audit and reversibility. Their use here proposes candidates; actual cross-origin effectiveness must be checked for meaning, leakage, cost, B and withdrawal.

## Measurements and audit

Fix the subjects, initial conditions, physical transitions, period, B definitions and validity of their observable indicators, local information, actions, time-indexed resources, deadlines, affected D/future conditions and permission evidence before comparison. Include sensors, translation, communication, computation and migration costs. Give competing methods the same evidence and UNKNOWN inputs.

To examine causal correspondence, observe A and B independently, prestate the proposed mediators and intervention predictions, and use permissible intervention/control comparisons or natural observations preserving time order and competing explanations. A correlated improvement can also arise from different resources, task difficulty or measurement apparatus. Observation may itself change the subject. Permission, alternatives and stop conditions must be checked separately; evidence acquisition alone does not authorize a B-damaging intervention.

Keep evidence levels distinct: a synthetic finite-model result; a similar structure observed in known human, machine or organizational cases; observation of actual cross-origin subjects and interventions; independent reproduction under matched conditions. The original's finite examples do not perform the latter observations.

Record separate results for mapping, physical histories, local implementation, N-subject/multifunction composition, alternative original-subject B paths, third-party loss and permission. Compare false establishment, false impossibility, information requirements, time and computational costs. Do not invent fixed leakage rates, translation success rates, thresholds or universal numerical performance claims.

## Validity conditions

The target and necessary variables must be identifiable; corresponding mechanisms and intervention boundaries must be testable. Each subject's differentiated conditions must be mapped into the same history type, with the included restrictions explicit. Claims of operational establishment require the relevant policy, information, costs, deadline, all-subject/all-function conditions, intermediate B and third-party effects.

Normative proposals such as non-domination, objection and effective exit retain their status as design conditions whose implementation has costs. They are not inferred physical traits of unknown intelligences. Claims of adoption require subject-specific evidence of understanding, authority, permission, refusal and an effective alternative.

## Failure conditions

The diagnosis fails if resemblance becomes invented ancestry, attribution becomes command authority, a danger label becomes a mechanism, or benefits replace B. It also fails if individual histories become a joint history, pairwise solutions become an N-subject solution, separate functional tests become simultaneous execution, or omniscient hindsight becomes a local policy.

Other failures include counting final recovery as uninterrupted B, excluding interface or migration costs, equating no communication with non-interference, dropping D while claiming collective success, treating a signal or silence as agreement, and counting C's formation as A/B's numerical continuation. Conversely, an empty joint-K set must not automatically erase the independently testable limited or separated B-preserving alternatives.

## Falsification conditions

Withdraw or restrict a mapping when the proposed mediator does not correspond, intervention reverses the predicted direction, competing causes sufficiently explain the result, or the B indicator is invalid. Correct a joint-feasibility result when missing or contradictory assumptions are discovered. Withdraw a local impossibility claim if a policy is exhibited under the same observation, action and deadline conditions.

Revise non-interference when shared resources, leakage or physical effects damage B. Revise a voluntary-adoption assessment when coercion or blocked alternatives are found. Apply each correction at its evidenced scope rather than protecting the claim with exclusions invented after failure.

Use strong existing alternatives: viability theory/viability kernels, model checking, SAT, integer programming and decentralized control or partially observed policies, as appropriate. Preserve their actual ability to express B, provenance, authority and third-party effects. If they identify the same boundaries equally or more accurately at lower cost under matched conditions, no additional practical superiority is established for this framework in that scope.

The proposed contribution is a connected diagnosis linking origin, causal mapping, original-subject B paths, interfaces, externalized loss and adoption. New mathematical algorithms and measured diagnostic superiority are different claims. The latter requires comparison evidence, including independently authored cases rather than only examples selected to favor the theory.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not predetermine inevitable harmony, inevitable conflict or compulsory assimilation. Unknown existence, experience, intent, B and meaning require evidence; retain UNKNOWN or outside-application classifications appropriately. Impossibility is scoped to specified conditions and cannot confer permission to sacrifice a subject.

Article 20's original-subject target does not prohibit all movement or changes of medium. It does require continuity evidence when identity is in question. A new C may inherit A/B origins without numerically continuing either subject. Transformation is a separate option whether an original-subject continuation path exists or not, handed to Article 21 with identity, transitional B, permission and third-party obligations. The index does not treat unpublished downstream analyses as completed public findings.
