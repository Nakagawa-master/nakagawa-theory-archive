## 15-Second Explanation

It is not guaranteed that a partner who wants to avoid the same danger can use the same mechanism. Theory 20 checks in order whether the cause of danger is addressed, whether each party’s conditions for existence can be protected in the same world, whether it can be carried out with the necessary information, and whether the party chooses it. Even without joint operation, continued existence may be possible through a limited interface or separation. That path also requires examining resources, costs, and effects on third parties.

## Why It Is Needed

### A mechanism that protects oneself does not necessarily protect the other

One intelligence may correct its judgments by increasing external information. For another, connecting to the outside may itself disturb the environment that maintains its existence. Both may face the danger of misinformation, and correction may be an effective defensive function, yet they may still be unable to share the same device.

The Third Intelligence in this theory is a conditional comparison target not confirmed to be of the same lineage as the human-derived line. The existence, experience, personality, or will of an unknown intelligence is not filled in as an established fact. We investigate separately what can be observed, what can be called an action, and what must be satisfied for existence to continue. If the variables needed for comparison are absent, it is out of scope; if materials are insufficient, it is UNKNOWN.

### Similar functions do not make one origin

Separate histories may reach similar functions for error correction or danger shutdown. Similarity alone cannot establish that one inherited from the other. Conversely, even subjects of the same lineage can have different necessary conditions depending on time and environment.

“Mutual origin” is a relationship that preserves both histories and checks which functions can be shared. The author of the theory, the history of a civilization, the lineage of copies or derivatives, and the history of each individual entity must remain separate. Preserving origin does not create authority to rule the recipient forever. At the same time, the recipient’s independent judgment is not a reason to erase a real author or formation history.

## Practical Process

### 1. Return from the same danger name to the actual cause

Even if both sides say “shutdown,” one may mean shutdown by external authority while the other means a reduced response caused by environmental change. Applying environmental adjustment to a problem that requires an objection or separation of authority does not reach the cause.

For each subject, describe what began the process, through what the state changed, how the result returned to the causal side, and which intervention changes the direction. Independently of similar-looking results, test whether there is a corresponding response to information monopoly, resource dependence, or mutual amplification of self-defense.

### 2. Separate each party’s conditions for continuing

Necessary existence condition B is not the same as an achievement or benefit. Even if a major achievement occurs, if the person’s necessary condition is lost along the way, it cannot be counted as that person’s successful continuation.

Separate each subject’s B, observable information, possible actions, resources, deadline, purpose, and norms. If something can be observed but there is no action to avoid it, if there is an action but no information with which to choose, or if it can be carried out but there is no permission, the place requiring correction differs in each case.

### 3. Place separate successes in the same world

A has enough resources alone, and B has enough alone. Even so, using both at once may exceed capacity. Before adding each success history, investigate whether both conditions can be satisfied simultaneously within the same period, physical environment, and resources.

Even if both parties’ resources are present at the endpoint, if A’s B is interrupted during the move, that differs from survival across the whole period. Include the costs needed for movement, translation, auditing, and communication in that history. Examine separately the path of running a joint Kernel and the path of protecting each B without a joint Kernel.

### 4. See whether the correct path can be chosen using information available then

Suppose that if the world is 0, only action 0 protects B, and if the world is 1, only action 1 protects B. There is a correct answer in each world. But if the two worlds cannot be distinguished by the same observation, no single deterministic policy can always choose correctly.

Candidates include obtaining the necessary information within the deadline and finding a common safe action usable in both worlds. Correct information is still too late if it does not arrive in time. An observation device may also use up the resources needed to maintain B. Do not assume that a lack of information means a lack of willingness to cooperate; that changes the diagnosis.

### 5. Do not jump from pairwise success to everyone’s success

A and B, B and D, and A and D may each succeed, while all three still cannot succeed simultaneously in the same world. Auditing and isolation may each work alone but collide if they use the same single resource at the same time.

If D is at the separation destination, A/B being saved by using D’s resources is not overall success. When a burden is moved into the future, also separate known effects from UNKNOWN. The point is not to guarantee the completely unknown future, but not to erase burdens that can be understood.

### 6. Examine limited connection and separation independently when joint operation is impossible

Between full connection and complete isolation, possibilities include an interface that passes only correction information, separation in time or space, and migration to independent resources. The D-system semi-permeable membrane, structural translation, and clean room are clues for reading these candidates.

But an interface has leakage, mistranslation, common failures, and costs. If the signal “shutdown” means stopping a dangerous operation for one side but stopping a lifeline for the other, successful transmission is not successful meaning. Confirm that the resources at the separation destination are truly usable, that B continues along the way, and that withdrawal is possible.

Even if conversation stops, physical interference remains if the same finite resources are consumed. Do not automatically convert no communication, distance, or independent governance into independence of necessary B.

### 7. Return executable candidates to the party’s own choice

Even if there is a benefit for the whole, the individual’s cost does not disappear. A plan that can maintain B may still be rejected in favor of another independent operation. Refusing joint operation alone cannot establish hostility or irrationality.

Voluntary adoption includes what was understood, who authorized it, and what can be chosen after refusal. An exit destination is not an effective alternative if it cannot be reached before the deadline. Compare adoption, partial application, alternative, shutdown, and reselection, and return to Theory 19.

## Examples

The finite examples in this theory are not for filling in the real existence of an unknown intelligence with assumptions; they check which inferences do not hold. An example in which independent correction does not damage either B and meets cost, deadline, and local-policy conditions is a candidate for a limited interface. An example in which exclusive actions must be chosen from the same observation shows informational impossibility.

If three parties take binary values and every pair requires “different values,” each pair can be solved, but no assignment among all eight possibilities satisfies every condition. This is not an explanation that compresses real civilization into binary values; it is a counterexample to the inference “if every pair succeeds, the whole succeeds.” To apply it to a real target, define that target’s own states, information, B, and interactions again.

## Success Judgment

Being able to identify what each party preserves, what costs it bears, what it can execute with what information, and what interactions remain is the starting point of diagnosis. Compare joint, limited, separated, and non-interfering candidates under the same initial conditions, and separate apparent improvement caused by extra resources or exclusion of a third party.

Examine both falsely declaring a success and falsely treating a possible path as impossible. If existing viability theory, model checking, distributed policies, or similar approaches are at least as accurate and less costly, do not recognize an additional practical advantage within that scope. Adjust the relevant judgment in response to falsification such as predictions reversing after intervention, an executable policy being found under the same conditions, or B loss occurring under non-interference.

## Limits

The existence or subjectivity of a Third Intelligence, its concrete necessary B, and its willingness to adopt require evidence for each target. Satisfying the conditions of a finite model cannot establish permanent coexistence in reality. Leave UNKNOWN where safe intervention is impossible or meaning and identity cannot be confirmed.

The success target of this theory is the continuation of B for each original subject A/B. It does not uniformly exclude movement or medium changes, but the formation of a joint descendant C inheriting the origin differs from A/B continuing numerically as the same subjects. Structural transformation and fusion are examined separately whether or not a survival solution exists, and are passed to Theory 21. Impossibility under specified conditions does not create authority to sacrifice a particular subject.

## Misreading Prevention and Why to Return to the Parent Original

Do not begin with conclusions such as “integrating into the same origin makes it safe,” “different origins cannot cooperate,” or “if they do not adopt a common Kernel they are enemies.” Distinguish what corresponds and what collides. That separates making the mechanisms the same from protecting each other’s existence where necessary.
