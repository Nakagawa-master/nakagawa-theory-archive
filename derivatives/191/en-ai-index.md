## Summary
C-series Vol.3 addresses preservation and succession after a resonance market has succeeded. It treats success itself as a critical point at which connection rewards, fixed order, sunk cost, and divergence between L7 and actual output can accumulate. The response is not personality judgment or unlimited exclusion, but structural immunity, metabolism, optimal scale, deliberate contraction, dynamic equilibrium, and succession from a person toward L7 plus implementation structure.

## Applications
- Audit successful communities for L7-output divergence and load concentration rather than treating growth alone as health.
- Separate respect for past contribution from present structural alignment.
- Re-run checkpoints as roles, environments, and burdens change instead of treating entry approval as permanent alignment.
- Treat separation as graduation or flow control when possible, not punishment or humiliation.
- Pause recruitment, raise price, reduce participants, or narrow scope when scale exceeds the range in which L7, immunity, and metabolism remain coherent.
- Build succession so judgment, learning, correction, immunity, and metabolism continue without the founder's constant presence.

## Measurements and audit
Observe whether L7 has become a slogan, whether current output diverges from declared values, whether contributors carry concentrated burdens, whether highly aligned newcomers are repelled by internal inertia, whether immunity has become ideological policing, whether sunk cost prevents release, whether contraction remains possible, and whether judgment remains stable without the founder. The parent does not establish universal head-count limits, mimicry rates, immunity scores, or revenue thresholds.

## Validity conditions
- Treat corruption risk as a normal post-success structural risk.
- Judge current L7-output alignment rather than personality, seniority, title, or past achievement alone.
- Treat mimicry as reversible state rather than permanent identity.
- Keep immunity as identification rather than attack.
- Keep metabolism and graduation available.
- Prefer optimal scale over scale maximization when they conflict.
- Preserve Origin specificity without converting it into founder absolutism.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing the problem to finding bad people.
- Ignoring current divergence because intentions are good.
- Converting checkpoints into loyalty tests or ideological screening.
- Allowing seniority, title, or historic contribution to capture immunity judgments.
- Letting sunk cost permanently freeze obsolete roles or connections.
- Using separation as punishment or spectacle.
- Treating growth in people, revenue, or influence as the only success criterion.
- Losing the ability to contract or becoming permanently founder-dependent.

## Falsification conditions
If structural immunity, metabolism, optimal-scale adjustment, and dynamic equilibrium repeatedly fail to improve alignment, circulation, contributor sustainability, newcomer quality, or succession capability, their definitions or scope require revision. If fixed growth and person-dependent succession repeatedly achieve equal or better long-term alignment and resistance to decay under comparable conditions, the necessity claimed for this design should be narrowed.
