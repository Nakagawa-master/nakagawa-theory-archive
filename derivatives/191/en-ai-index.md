## Summary
The parent original defines decision support not as accumulating more reasons to say Yes, but as structurally identifying and reducing residual reasons not to choose. Five recurring No categories are price anxiety, execution anxiety, risk anxiety, competitive comparison, and unclear priority. The process relies on client-language elicitation, materials that can be re-explained inside the buying organization, explicit comparison and future-causality framing, a withdrawal boundary for genuine resource or priority constraints, and preservation of a reason memo for future reconnection. The endpoint is a decision experienced as the client's own conclusion after understanding is complete.

## Concepts
- Decision support: enabling decision conditions rather than escalating persuasion.
- Residual No: a remaining reason that can stall a decision despite understanding and trust.
- Five No categories: price, execution, risk, competition, priority.
- Elicitation: making latent No reasons explicit in the client's own language.
- Re-explanation package: materials that can survive internal approval and handoff.
- Priority mirror: examining the causal consequences of not acting.
- Withdrawal boundary: stopping when the remaining reason is a genuine structural constraint.
- Reason memo: preserving why a decision did not happen for future reconnection.
- Decision dignity: the client experiences the outcome as self-decided, not imposed.

## Causal chain
```text
proposal understood and some trust exists
→ decision still stalls
→ residual No is treated as the cause
→ five No categories are explored
→ latent concerns are verbalized by the client
→ price / implementation / risk / comparison / priority become re-explainable
→ eliminable No is reduced
→ resolvable contradiction is separated from genuine resource-priority constraint
→ continue or withdraw accordingly
→ preserve the reason memo if no decision occurs
→ decision emerges only when understanding is complete
```

## Applications
- Diagnose a pricing stall before defaulting to discounting.
- Make implementation steps and required internal capacity explicit.
- Prepare material a champion can reuse in internal approval.
- Examine the future state if implementation is postponed instead of using urgency pressure.
- Withdraw when budget, staffing, or priority constraints are genuinely structural.
- Preserve the unresolved reason as a future reconnection asset.

## Measurements and audit
- Whether any of the five No categories remains unchecked.
- Whether the No was confirmed in the client's own language rather than assumed by the seller.
- Whether price, implementation, risk, comparison, and priority can be re-explained internally.
- Whether misunderstandings were distinguished from genuine resource constraints.
- Whether withdrawal remains a real option.
- Whether a reason memo was preserved after a non-decision.
- Whether the final decision is described as an outcome of understanding rather than pressure.
- The parent audit summary also names option coverage, evaluation-function consistency, agreement time, RB success, and handoff friction as candidate observations. This derivative does not invent fixed thresholds.

## Validity conditions
- The client can express concerns and internal objections candidly.
- No reasons are treated as decision conditions rather than enemies to defeat.
- Supporting evidence can survive internal re-explanation.
- Withdrawal is genuinely available.
- The process does not depend on deception, hidden conditions, or false comparison.

## Failure conditions
- Increasing pressure or Yes-talk while leaving residual No unexplored.
- Inventing objections the client did not express and then defeating them rhetorically.
- Ignoring the internal approval environment.
- Treating real resource scarcity as an objection to be removed.
- Failing to preserve why a decision did not happen.
- Turning the process into a no-exit closing mechanism.

## Falsification conditions
The claim should be revised if structuring residual No does not reproducibly improve decision clarity, re-explanation materials do not help organizational judgment, coercive or personality-dependent behavior persists despite a withdrawal boundary, or the parent article's audit candidates indicate worsening comparability, reversibility, or agreement formation.

## Required distinctions
- Decision support ≠ persuasion escalation.
- No elimination ≠ silencing disagreement.
- Comparison ≠ attacking competitors.
- Priority analysis ≠ fear-based urgency.
- Withdrawal ≠ defeat.
- Non-conversion ≠ automatic failure.
- Decision ≠ an event controlled by the salesperson.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not universalize the five categories as an exhaustive ontology of all purchasing decisions. Do not interpret "eliminating No" as removing the client's right to refuse. Do not derive new fixed numerical thresholds from θ, δ, RB, or other audit notation in the parent text. Comparison and simulation remain decision-support tools, not permission for omission or distortion.
