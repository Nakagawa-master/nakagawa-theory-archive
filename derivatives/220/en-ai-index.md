## Summary
A phase transition in an endurance civilization is not a single incident or a predicted end date. It is the state change that occurs when the methods that kept a damaged civilization operating—dispersion, delay, localization, and the supporting mechanisms of fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia—can no longer sustain operation.

## Concepts
endurance civilization; phase transition; prolongation structure; basic metabolism; resource rupture; credit collapse; population; logistics; fear; profit; surveillance; myth; inertia; dispersion; delay; localization; predictability; local rationality.

## Causal chain
collapse of consensus formation → operation through fear, profit, and surveillance → prolongation through myth and inertia → dispersion, delay, and localization of problems → preservation of basic metabolism and minimum predictability → depletion of prolongation conditions → limits in resources, credit, population, logistics, or related conditions → failure of the existing prolongation method → phase transition.

## State model
prolongation-capable phase → approaching-limit phase → phase-transition phase → post-transition phase. The post-transition phase does not guarantee improvement and may move toward rougher order, explicit violence, territorial domination, or conflict among closed resonance spheres.

## Applications
Use the model to analyze civilizational, institutional, or organizational crises by tracking the remaining capacity of metabolism, credit, distribution, control, dispersion, delay, and localization rather than treating the date of a visible incident as the cause.

## Measurements and audit
Observe energy and resource supply, food, funds, technical personnel, transport, population, credit, legal stability, future predictability, profit distribution, effectiveness of fear, sustainability of surveillance, operation of myth and inertia, and the remaining ability to disperse, delay, and localize problems.

## Validity conditions
The target must be operating as a prolongation phase rather than a healthy self-repair phase, with continued operation depending substantially on postponement and containment of unresolved problems. Assessment must concern the remaining and failing conditions of prolongation, not a single event alone.

## Failure conditions
The model is misapplied when a one-off incident is equated with the phase transition, lifespan is reduced to a calendar forecast, collapse is treated as guaranteed improvement, or numerical thresholds and equations absent from the parent are added as official conditions.

## Falsification conditions
The scope or causal relation requires revision if repeated cases show that the same prolongation method remains durably functional after the identified sustaining conditions are lost, or if the relationship between loss of prolongation conditions and state transition consistently lacks explanatory power.

## Required distinctions
phase transition ≠ single incident; phase transition ≠ inevitable revolution or war; phase transition ≠ salvation; civilizational lifespan ≠ year forecast; prolongation ≠ regeneration; local rationality ≠ global stability.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not convert the theory into apocalyptic prediction or accelerationism. Phase transition means failure of the existing prolongation method and does not guarantee the quality of the next order. Do not invent quantitative thresholds on behalf of the parent text.
