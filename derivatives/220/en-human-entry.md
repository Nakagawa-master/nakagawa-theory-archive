## 15-Second Explanation
Endurance civilization does not stop immediately because it is broken. It continues operating for a long time by dispersing problems, delaying them into the future, and localizing them, through fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia. Phase transition is the point at which that very method of prolongation stops working.

## Why It Is Needed
If civilizational collapse is viewed only as “the day a major event happened,” the structure of prolonged deterioration and prolongation is missed. The Parent original views lifespan not as a number of years but as the remainder of the prolongation structure: whether resources can still be circulated, trust maintained, benefits distributed, fear and surveillance made to function, and problems localized. The more these are lost, the closer phase transition comes.

## Practical Process
1. First distinguish whether the target is a healthy regenerative phase or a prolongation phase that operates by postponing problems.
2. Check basic metabolism such as energy, food, funds, human resources, and transport.
3. See whether benefit distribution, fear, and surveillance still function to maintain order.
4. See whether myth and inertia support justification and automatic continuation.
5. See whether minimum trust in money, institutions, law, and forecasts of the future remains.
6. Identify where the room to disperse, delay, and localize problems is shrinking.
7. When multiple prolongation conditions begin to expire simultaneously, treat it as a state transition rather than a single event.

## Examples of Application
When resources fracture, basic metabolism itself stops even if meaning and legitimacy remain. When trust collapses, the minimum predictability that “tomorrow will operate at roughly the same level” is lost, making people’s defensive actions more likely to damage whole-society order. Changes such as being unable to distribute benefits, fear no longer working, being unable to maintain surveillance, myth losing meaning, and inertia no longer running are also read as expiration of the prolongation method.

## Success Criteria
Success is not correctly naming when collapse will occur. It is being able to explain, separately from the story of events, what is providing prolongation, how much of each prolongation condition remains, and where dispersal, delay, and localization become impossible.

## Limitations
Phase transition does not guarantee the birth or improvement of a new civilization. After transition, it may move toward coarser order, overt violence, simple sphere domination, or conflict between kyoken. Also, the Parent original is not a model that predicts a specific year of collapse.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not conclude “if it collapses, it will change” or “it would be better if everything broke.” Endurance civilization is persistent, and ending itself does not guarantee reboot. The object to examine is not a desire for collapse but phase-transition conditions and the remainder of the prolongation structure.
