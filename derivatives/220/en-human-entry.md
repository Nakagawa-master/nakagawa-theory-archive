## 15-Second Explanation
Endurance civilization does not stop immediately because it is broken. It keeps moving for a long time through fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia while dispersing problems, delaying them into the future, and localizing them. A phase transition is the point where that very method of prolonging life ceases to work.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
If the collapse of civilization is viewed only as “the day a major event occurred,” the structures of degradation and prolongation that continue for a long time are missed. The Parent Original views lifespan not as a number of years but as the remainder of the prolongation structure. Can resources still be circulated, trust maintained, profits distributed, fear or surveillance made to function, and problems localized? The more these are lost, the closer the phase transition comes.

## Practical Process
1. First distinguish whether the subject is a healthy regenerative phase or a prolongation phase that operates by postponing problems.
2. Check basic metabolism such as energy, food, funds, personnel, and transportation.
3. See whether profit distribution, fear, and surveillance still function to maintain order.
4. See whether myth and inertia support legitimation and automatic continuation.
5. See whether minimum trust in money, institutions, law, and forecasts of the future remains.
6. Identify where the room to disperse, delay, and localize problems is shrinking.
7. When multiple prolongation conditions begin to expire at the same time, treat it as a state transition rather than a single incident.

## Examples of Application
With a resource rupture, basic metabolism itself stops even if meaning and legitimacy remain. With a collapse of trust, the minimum predictability that “tomorrow will operate to roughly the same degree” is lost, making people’s defensive actions more likely to damage overall order. Changes such as being unable to distribute profits, fear no longer working, being unable to maintain surveillance, myth losing meaning, or inertia no longer turning should also be read as the expiry of a method of prolongation.

## Success Criteria
Success is not getting the timing of collapse right. It is being able to explain, separately from the narrative of events, what is prolonging the system, how much of each prolongation condition remains, and where dispersal, delay, and localization can no longer be performed.

## Limitations
A phase transition does not guarantee the birth or improvement of a new civilization. After the transition, it may move toward cruder order, overt violence, simple territorial rule, or conflict between shared spheres. Also, the Parent Original is not a model that predicts a specific year of collapse.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not conclude that “if it collapses, it will change” or “it would be better if everything broke.” Endurance civilization is tenacious, and ending itself does not guarantee a restart. What should be examined is not the desire for collapse, but the phase-transition conditions and the remainder of the prolongation structure.
