## 15-Second Explanation
The parent original integrates the preceding nine parts and summarizes sales not as a “technique for selling,” but as **cognitive support**: through questions and resonance, it externalizes the other person’s decision structure and creates conditions in which they can choose to decide, defer, or withdraw once understanding is complete.

When customers can obtain information themselves, the key role is organizing comparison axes, concerns, visions of the future, and implementation conditions so the person can see “what they are thinking and how.”

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Explanation is necessary when information is insufficient. But even with sufficient information, decisions do not advance if what is being compared, what is concerning, who decides, and implementation conditions are unorganized.

Questions externalize the decision structure and return the other person’s words in comparable form. Silence protects the time needed to form their own words; it is not intimidation or pressure.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate insufficient information from insufficient structure.** Determine whether facts or specifications are missing, or comparison criteria, concerns, the decision-maker, and implementation conditions are unorganized.

**2. Externalize the decision structure through questions.** Confirm “What do you want to change?”, “What are you comparing it with?”, “Who decides?”, and “What obstacles will there be during implementation?”

**3. Reflect the other person’s words back.** Separate their words, the sales-side interpretation, issues, grounds, and unresolved matters.

**4. Protect thinking time.** Do not pile on explanation immediately after a question; leave room for their own words.

**5. Create comparability.** Put options, concerns, visions of the future, and implementation conditions on the same decision axis.

**6. Keep options open.** Do not make closing the only outcome; preserve deferral and withdrawal as legitimate choices.

**7. Make it reproducible in the organization.** Use shared vocabulary, case comparison, and education to make cognitive support more than individual intuition.

**8. Limit AI to organizational support.** Humans retain meaning, direction, ethics, and final responsibility.

## Examples of Application
**Sales dialogue.** Organize comparison axes and unresolved matters in the person’s own words rather than assuming explanation is insufficient.

**Internal approval request.** Enable the contact to re-explain value, concerns, and implementation conditions to a third party.

**Education.** Treat questions, summaries, silence, comparison, and self-determination as observable, trainable structures—not a script for obtaining Yes.

**AI support.** AI may organize issues or comparison candidates, but its interpretation is not the other person’s definitive meaning.

## Success Criteria
Success is not only question count, information volume, silence duration, or closing rate. The person should explain decision reasons in their own words; questions should not become leading; comparison axes, concerns, and unresolved matters should be separated; and deferral and withdrawal should remain legitimate options.

More questions can worsen matters if leadingness increases. More information does not deepen understanding if the comparison axis stays unclear. Higher closing does not necessarily mean structural success if self-determination or trust is damaged.

The parent original defines no fixed numerical values such as understanding rate, optimal time to agreement, probability of success, or sales-philosophy score.

## Limitations
This is not advanced persuasion, psychological manipulation, or a spiritual doctrine that disregards sales. Cognitive support is bounded by the person’s ability to refuse, defer, or withdraw.

It may not directly apply where power differences are large or immediate decisions take priority, such as safety, legal compliance, or medicine. Social, educational, and policy applications are possibilities, not automatically proven effects.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not turn “do not make them decide; let the decision happen” into sales-side irresponsibility. Do not equate resonance with agreement, turn silence into intimidation, or turn AI support into delegated judgment.
