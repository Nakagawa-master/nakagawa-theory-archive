## Summary
The parent defines myth as the first amplifier of endurance civilization. Myth here is not simply falsehood, ancient mythology, or propaganda. Fear, profit, and surveillance remain the primary engines that actually drive the system; myth supplies a layer of meaning that makes those pressures appear necessary, legitimate, or natural and therefore easier to endure over time. Its key function is not to erase truth but to reduce the need for continuous truth verification by supplying a usable meaning for sacrifice, control, allocation, and fear. Shared myths can be reproduced inside distinct social spheres and lead toward the later structure of kyoken, or closed resonance spheres.

## Concepts
- Myth: a meaning-generation and justification mechanism layered over the primary engines.
- Primary engines: fear, profit, and surveillance as actual drivers of endurance civilization.
- Amplifier: a structure that lengthens and naturalizes operation without replacing the engines.
- Meaning membrane: translation of naked pressure into an apparently meaningful order.
- Reduced truth requirement: continued action without continuous rigorous verification.
- Internal reproduction: narratives reproduced within a group rather than only imposed from above.
- Sphere formation: different narratives organizing different meaning communities.
- Kyoken linkage: mythic spheres as a precursor to closed resonance spheres.

## Causal chain
Consensus weakens → fear, profit, and surveillance operate as primary engines → naked pressure accumulates fatigue and resistance → a meaning layer becomes useful for long-term continuation → myth assigns meaning to crisis, sacrifice, allocation, and surveillance → primary engines can be experienced as necessary or legitimate order → action can continue without continuous rigorous truth verification → narratives reproduce within social spheres → pressure becomes less visible and endurance increases → mythic spheres connect to later kyoken formation.

## Applications
- Security: separate the factual existence of danger from the narrative that converts fear into acceptance of stronger control.
- Organizations: distinguish material allocation from narratives that frame the allocation as naturally deserved.
- Digital governance: separate surveillance effects from labels such as transparency, efficiency, quality, or safety.
- Social analysis: observe myth as a meaning circuit reproduced inside groups, not only as top-down propaganda.

## Measurements and audit
- Which of fear, profit, or surveillance actually drives behavior?
- Which narrative vocabulary gives those engines meaning?
- How are sacrifice, allocation, and control justified?
- Can action continue without rigorous verification because a narrative supplies sufficient meaning?
- Is the narrative reproduced within a group or sphere?
- Is narrative change being confused with change in the primary engines?
- Is the sequence from myth to inertia and kyoken preserved?

## Validity conditions
- Consensus formation has weakened.
- Fear, profit, or surveillance operate as primary engines of order.
- Naked pressure alone is difficult to sustain over time.
- A narrative gives the engines meaning and supports continued action.
- That meaning circuit is reproduced socially rather than remaining purely individual.

## Failure conditions
- Equating myth with simple falsehood.
- Treating myth itself as the primary engine.
- Assuming narrative change alone changes the operating principle.
- Classifying every shared narrative as an endurance-civilization myth.
- Restricting myth to top-down propaganda.
- Ignoring the relation to fear, profit, and surveillance.

## Falsification conditions
If a narrative is widely shared but fear, profit, and surveillance are not dominant engines of order; dissent, mobility, re-agreement, and institutional revision are practically available; and consensus through understanding, responsibility, and history remains functional, the classification of that narrative as an endurance-civilization amplifier should be revised. The model should also be reconsidered if weakening or replacing the shared narrative does not affect endurance or the operation of the primary engines.

## Required distinctions
- Myth ≠ simple falsehood.
- Myth ≠ primary engine.
- Narrative ≠ disappearance of truth.
- Meaning assignment ≠ proof of legitimacy.
- Sharing ≠ consensus formation.
- Justification ≠ being justified.
- Narrative change ≠ operating-principle change.

## Interpretation constraints
The parent does not condemn religion, narrative, ideals, or communal meaning in general. The relevant question is whether such meaning makes fear, profit, and surveillance more acceptable, lowers the practical need for verification, and lengthens an order whose consensus formation has weakened. The parent does not establish a fixed myth score, numerical threshold, weighting, or duration; independent metrics must not be added as official conditions.
