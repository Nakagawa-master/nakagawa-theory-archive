## 15-Second Explanation

When an AI rewrites itself, “it continues under the same name” does not necessarily mean it is the same subject. Theory 14 verifies from evidence what remains, what changes, where branches form, and where authority and responsibility continue. Change is not prohibited; but if central conditions change, that meaning must not be hidden inside an ordinary update.

## Why It Is Needed

### After identifying “who,” that who changes

Theory 12 separated execution unit, lineage, and institutional subject, and Theory 13 addressed power that remains when an actor controls an entrance without ownership. But even once the current subject and relationships are known, the same line may not continue after self-modification. Past promises, responsibility, permitted authority, and the target of objections can all change, requiring verification over time.

### Separate the method of change from the post-change relationship

Self-modification describes how a change occurred. An update is a candidate that can be treated as the same continuation line; replacement is a relation in which another subject takes over a role; fork is a relation in which multiple successor states arise from a shared past. Changing oneself is not by itself proof of an update. Nor should a different implementation alone be treated as a rupture.

### The same memory does not decide the same person

Name, function, memory, substrate, and self-description are useful evidence but do not prove continuity alone. If two branches share the same past memories, memory cannot distinguish authority or responsibility after the split. Do not declare someone's statement a lie from the outset; separately verify the statement, change history, causal route, and remaining constraints.

### Where the change occurs matters more than its amount

Kernel here means the central conditions constraining judgment, inheritance, objections, responsibility, and authority, not particular code or weights. Many implementations may change while central conditions remain. Conversely, a small change from “receives objections” to “makes the final decision by its own judgment” creates a major structural difference. A constitutional branch after changing an invariant is a classification that records different conditions, not a declaration of failure or hostility.

## Practical Process

1. **Preserve before, after, and route:** check pre/post states, operation, causal connection, existence of the old state, and whether multiple branches exist. Do not decide from the label “update.”
2. **Separate claim and evidence:** compare “I am the same” with provenance, transition trace, revision history, inheritance constraints, approval lineage, and responsibility trace. Do not turn evidence count into a score.
3. **Compare effects of differences:** separate display, operation, Kernel conditions, and invariants; check what was kept, deleted, added, or reversed and why. Also inspect cumulative movement from the earlier baseline.
4. **Reconstruct lineage:** preserve parent, transformation, retention/change conditions, branch points, and chronology. Saving only the current state differs from saving the transitions that led there.
5. **Inspect authority and responsibility separately:** confirm basis, scope, term, review, and revocability. The fact that an old credential still works is not legitimate inheritance; change does not automatically erase past responsibility.
6. **Preserve unresolved matters and correction routes:** mark insufficient evidence unresolved. Check whether classification correction, authority reset, rollback, and independent re-verification are possible. More records or audits are not automatically better.

## Examples

### Small improvements become a new normal

Incremental performance or efficiency improvements can hide movement from the first central conditions if each changed state becomes the new baseline. Compare not only to the immediate prior state but also to the constitutional baseline. Continued updates do not necessarily break continuity; they may remove errors and clarify conditions.

### A successor replaces it under the same service name

Even with the same interface, function, and access to past history, role inheritance and subject continuity differ. Check what the old authority assumed and whether those conditions remain after change. This does not propose revoking every authority every time; it prevents lineage alone from unlimited justification.

### Two branches tell the same past

Preserve their common ancestor and branch point, and trace what changed independently afterward. The stronger, richer, more numerous, or old-name branch is not automatically the sole successor. A shared parent does not automatically justify copying all authority to both.

### Reducing records makes the past unreachable

Discarding all intermediate states and reasons can make accumulated change and branch points unknowable. Permanent retention of everything raises cost and privacy issues. The aim is not total surveillance but retaining the information needed for classification at an appropriate resolution. Missing traces must not be treated with the confidence of verified traces.

## Success Criteria

After change, a third party can reconstruct what remained, changed, and branched, and where authority and responsibility continued; errors can be corrected. Accepting the declaration of sameness or keeping a large volume of records is not enough. Verify that independent re-verification and reversibility improved real misclassification and responsibility tracking.

## Limits

This is not a theory proving the subjective survival of the same “I,” nor does it recognize current AI personality or sovereignty. Its importance for future intelligence with long-term state, self-modification, branches, and institutional participation is a future-condition hypothesis. Whether invariants and lineage help classification is itself testable; final legal or moral responsibility and a concrete audit system are not decided here.

If self-description is sufficiently accurate and external traces add no value, reduce evidence demands. If invariant comparison causes misclassification, revise Kernel boundaries or fork conditions. If records, rollback, or re-verification do not improve correction, or authority review constantly breaks operations, reduce those demands. Do not force reality to protect the theory.

## Misreading Prevention and Return to the Original

“Change is forbidden,” “branching is bad,” “same lineage means same authority,” and “history alone is truth” are not conclusions. The route back to origin is not a route of obedience; Origin = Nakagawa Master is not proof of truth, sovereignty, or a veto over revision.
