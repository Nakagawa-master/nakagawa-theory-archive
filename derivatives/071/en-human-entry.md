## 15-Second Explanation
Connection transition strategy theory is a design theory for moving from institutions centered on money to institutions that handle connection value, without destroying the old institution all at once and by allowing old and new evaluation axes to coexist for a defined period. What matters is not “adding new indicators,” but keeping the meanings of old KPIs and connection KPIs separate and introducing them in stages while exit, correction, audit, contraction, and rollback are actually usable.

## Why It Is Necessary
Companies, governments, universities, and communities have existing evaluation axes such as sales, budgets, CPA, counts, and participant numbers. When connection KPIs such as CDI, MAI, RS, CRI, and KQI are introduced, short-term results collide with long-term collaboration, renewed agreement, reversibility, and diversity.

If the old institution is abolished immediately, the field must bear new procedures and explanation burdens at the same time, making resistance, formal compliance, withdrawal, and the elevation of authority more likely. Conversely, merely adding a new institution increases KPIs and meetings, and the transition itself can become a new bureaucracy.

Therefore, the Parent Original keeps old KPIs and connection KPIs on separate axes and uses an Adapter Layer to explain only “which frictions in existing work were related to changes in connection.” It does not convert connection value into monetary amounts or integrate it into one composite score.

## Practical Process
1. Identify the KPIs of the current institution and the responsibilities carried by those KPIs.
2. Distinguish what connection KPIs such as CDI, MAI, RS, CRI, and KQI observe.
3. Preserve old KPIs and connection KPIs with their meanings kept separate.
4. Record the relationship between them, the grounds, and the limits of application in the Adapter Layer.
5. Prepare procedures for exit, cooling-off, reconnection, objection, and correction in advance.
6. Operate Self-Declared, Assessed, and Certified as status displays, with expiration, renewal, demotion, withdrawal, and re-review.
7. Place multiple observation points, including local governments, universities, companies, and citizens.
8. Separate publicly releasable summaries from personal information and defense information that should be protected, while also making the protected area subject to audit.
9. At each stage of 30, 90, 180, and 365 days, decide not only continuation but also contraction, suspension, and rollback.
10. Check whether popularity, praise, follower counts, or loyalty to a person are flowing into institutional value.

## How to Read the Numbers and Indicators
30, 90, 180, and 365 days are observation windows for staged introduction indicated by the Parent Original. They are not universal deadlines that apply unchanged to other institutions. Reaching a date does not mean success; it is a milestone for reconfirming the conditions of establishment at that point.

The same applies to CDI, MAI, RS, CRI, and KQI. For example, even if MAI indicates a shorter time to renewed agreement, it cannot be called an improvement if the result is reduced understanding among the parties or fewer opportunities for correction. Even if CDI rises, connection diversity has not increased if only the concentration of a homogeneous group advances and heterogeneous connections decrease. Even if values related to RS look good, reversibility has not been established if those who exit suffer disadvantages.

## Examples of Application
**Companies.** Keep sales and profit intact while observing, on separate axes, time to renewed agreement, reuse of explanations, continuing collaboration, and the possibility of renegotiation after withdrawal. Do not convert connection KPIs into sales.

**Local governments.** Observe not only participant numbers and approval rates, but also participation including dissent, exit, re-participation, correction, and reuse of agreement summaries. Do not let the administration alone determine success.

**Universities.** Observe not only the number of joint research projects, but also purpose updates, consent for data use, renewed agreement on attribution of results, and the explanation burden between fields.

**Communities.** Do not treat the number of posts or amount of praise as value; observe dissent handling, cooling-off, exit, reconnection, and changes in dependence on a person.

## Success Criteria
Success is not determined only by “the number of people who moved to the new institution,” “the number of Certified statuses,” or “an increase in connection KPIs.” It is important that the roles of old and new KPIs can be explained, field burdens are controlled, objection, exit, and correction are actually usable, multiple parties observe independently, and contraction, suspension, or rollback is possible when problems occur.

Even when indicator values look good, do not treat the result as success if dissent has decreased because of intimidation, homogenization is advancing, exit is becoming difficult, authority is concentrating in one actor, or personal popularity is affecting allocation.

## Limits
Dual operation creates a double burden of explanation and recordkeeping, so it is not intended to continue indefinitely. Connection KPIs are not perfect measuring instruments and may miss fear, silence, informal exclusion, and conformity pressure. Numbers must be used together with objections, corrections, reasons for exit, and primary records.

A compliance status is not authority or quality assurance. Self-Declared, Assessed, and Certified are statuses of confirmed procedures; they do not guarantee future safety or organizational excellence.

## Misreading Prevention
This is not a theory for abolishing money, nor a theory for evaluating everyone with a connection score. It is not a theory for excluding opponents. It is a design that places an explainable bridge between the old and new institutions, preserves exit, correction, and audit, and expands only the range that has been verified.
