## 15-Second Explanation
Across companies, the public sphere, AI, markets, and other domains, the definition, speed, and form of explaining “trust” can diverge. This design translates that fault line into three common questions—RC: can it be returned; ER: is it put in order before speaking; CEA: can it be reconnected without breaking causality—and returns the result to improvement through non-coercive Cross-Audit.

## Why It Is Needed
Trust may work inside one organization but change meaning the moment it connects to another domain. A company's “fast correction,” the public sphere's “accountability,” and AI's “rollback” are not the same scale; comparing them as one number erases differences in rights, responsibility, time, and resources.

The Parent therefore places RC/ER/CEA not as fixed scores but as shared vocabulary for translation between domains. Universality means not imposing the same threshold, but allowing each domain to redefine the questions “can it return, can it speak after ordering, can it change while preserving causality?”

## Practical Process
### 1. RC — Can it be returned?
RC is the differential effort, time, and impact range needed to roll back a decision safely. For a company it translates into organizational difference for trust recovery; for the public sphere, social time until policy correction; for AI, the burden of returning model behavior through structural differences. It is not merely monetary cost.

### 2. ER — Is it spoken after being put in order?
ER is not amount of speech. It looks at how much the agreement unit, sufficient conditions, responsibility boundary, dictionary, drawing, intent, and negative conditions have been ordered before output. The focus is traceable structural preparation before speaking, not lengthy explanation.

### 3. CEA — Can it be reconnected without breaking it?
CEA is adaptability to change, not the ability to change anything. It asks whether rights, burdens, explanation, and the causal chain can be preserved when KPIs, systems, or specifications change. It is editing ability that preserves causal connection, not freedom to change.

### 4. Use the three as a bundle
Low RC alone is dangerous if explanation collapses; high ER alone becomes rigid if change is impossible; high CEA alone is dangerous if withdrawal is impossible. The Parent reads the health of a setting as a bundle, not as three independent scores. Reducing it to one ranking destroys the audit idea.

### 5. Use mutual audit as improvement input
Different domains inspect one another's RC/ER/CEA, but the aim is an improvement recommendation, not punishment. In company ⇄ public, public ⇄ AI, and AI ⇄ company relations, translate the same terms into each domain's meaning and limit publication to bundle indicators and audit summaries.

### 6. Start with silence and return to design
Audit meetings begin by looking at drawings and dictionaries and aligning agreement units and sufficient conditions. Do not immediately evaluate someone; align structural resolution. If RC is poor, shrink the rollback unit; if ER is poor, prepare the dictionary, drawing, and summary; if CEA is poor, visualize causal connections and impact scope. Return audit results to the next design.

## Applications
In a company, read RC as organizational difference for trust recovery, ER as preparation of agreement units and responsibility boundaries, and CEA as preserving causality when KPIs or priorities change. In the public sphere, read RC as social time to policy correction, ER as explanatory preparation of dictionary, drawing, and summary, and CEA as institutional ability to change while preserving rights, burdens, and explanation. In AI, read RC as structural difference for reverting specifications, ER as declaration of intent, premises, and negative conditions, and CEA as update ability that preserves explainable causality.

Because domains are connected, the same indicator name can differ in meaning, so Cross-Audit always needs a translation layer. Since audit is improvement input, indicators must be differential information returning to the next question, silence, and redesign—not fixed labels for individuals or organizations.

## Success Criteria
Withdrawal can be done by differential unit, things are ordered before speaking, and causality does not break when changes are made. Together these allow trust to be treated not only as “whether to trust someone” but as “whether the structure can return, explain, and reconnect.”

Audit RC, ER, and CEA trends as a bundle, and also check inter-layer consistency, propagation delay, phase stability S, withdrawal success rate, lock-in health, and inter-layer disconnection or excessive synchronization in observation windows. Public output is limited to bundle indicators and audit summaries.

## Limits
If withdrawal or re-agreement does not improve, higher ER does not lead to accountability, higher CEA breaks causal connection, Cross-Audit becomes punishment or ranking, or meaning is lost in translation, revise the audit design.

Publish definitions, framework, bundle indicators, and audit summaries only. Do not invent specific thresholds, internal adjustments, individual implementation procedures, proper names, or internal logs in the derivative. Do not compare away differences in rights, resources, responsibility, or authority.

## Preventing Misreading
“Universal indicators” does not mean a scientifically established fixed score common to every domain. Do not crush RC/ER/CEA into one ranking number; retain each domain's translated meaning.

Cross-Audit is an improvement recommendation, not mutual domination or punishment. RC is not failure avoidance, ER is not amount of speech, and CEA is not unrestricted change. Removing the audit's non-coercion and publication boundary departs from the Parent's structure.
