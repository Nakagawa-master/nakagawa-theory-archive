## Summary
The Parent addresses trust fractures that appear when organizations, public institutions, AI systems, markets, and other domains use slightly different definitions of trust. RC, ER, and CEA form a shared audit vocabulary: RC asks how safely a decision can be reversed, ER asks how much structural preparation exists before speaking or deciding, and CEA asks whether causal links can be preserved while changing the system. The three must be interpreted as a bundle rather than as independent rankings. Cross-Audit is non-coercive and returns improvement proposals to the next cycle of questions, silence, and redesign.

## Applications
In companies, RC can describe the effort to restore trust after a decision, ER the preparation of agreement units and responsibility boundaries, and CEA the ability to change priorities while preserving causal logic. In public systems, RC becomes social time and effort required for policy revision, ER the readiness of dictionaries/maps/summaries, and CEA the ability to preserve rights, burdens, and explanation chains during institutional change. In AI, RC concerns rollback differences, ER concerns declared intent/premises/negative conditions, and CEA concerns updates that preserve explainable causality.

## Measurements and audit
Read RC trend, ER trend, and CEA trend as a bundle. Also inspect inter-layer alignment, propagation delay, phase stability S, rollback success, lock-in health, and whether observation windows show disconnection or excessive conformity. Public output is limited to bundle indicators and audit summaries; concrete internal thresholds, implementation procedures, and logs remain outside the public layer.

## Validity conditions
Domain-specific translations of RC/ER/CEA must be possible. The three indicators must not be collapsed into a single score. Audit must remain non-coercive and improvement-oriented. Agreement unit, sufficient conditions, and responsibility boundaries are aligned first. Shared map, dictionary, and minimal records preserve replayability. Audit results feed the next redesign cycle. Public summaries and internal details remain separated.

## Failure conditions
Using RC/ER/CEA to rank people or institutions; turning audit into punishment or exclusion; lowering RC by removing explanation or rights protection; redefining ER as speech volume or participation count; treating CEA as permission to change anything; drawing conclusions from one metric alone; inferring confidential thresholds from public summaries; erasing domain-specific differences in rights, resources, duties, or authority.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the multi-layer audit hypothesis if inter-layer alignment worsens, propagation delay rises, phase stability falls, rollback success drops, lock-in becomes unhealthy, layer disconnection or excessive conformity increases, or apparent RC/ER/CEA improvement does not improve explainability, reversibility, and causal preservation.

## Required distinctions
audit / punishment; metric bundle / single ranking; RC / failure avoidance; ER / speech volume; CEA / unconstrained change; Cross-Audit / mutual domination; trust translation / monetizing trust; public summary / internal log; improvement proposal / accusation; beneficial lock-in / unhealthy fixation.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not elevate “universal indicators” into scientifically established fixed scales or a universal formula. The Parent publishes definitions and relationships, not concrete thresholds or internal measurement equations. Do not compress the three indicators into one ranking value. Preserve domain-specific translation. Do not remove non-coercion and convert audit into authority. Do not infer confidential information or procedures from the public abstraction.
