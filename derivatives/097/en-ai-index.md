## Summary
The source reframes consensus as more than verbal persuasion or voting. When a non-contradictory structure repeats over time while preserving others' freedom, agreement may migrate from procedure to habit and eventually to a shared social terrain. Three principles constrain this process: temporal synchronization, ethical inviolability, and existential margin. Structural rhythm, observability and resilience to counterexamples create a provisional expectation that the same coherence will recur. Silence is not consent: dissent, exit and re-agreement must remain available. The theory is supported only if communication pressure can fall while cooperation, re-reference and autonomous participation persist.

## Concepts
silent consensus; resonance; temporal synchronization; ethical inviolability; existential margin; structural rhythm; social terrain.

## Applications

## Measurements
Speech/pressure load, dissent use, re-reference, re-agreement, autonomous continuation, repair time.

## Validity / failure / falsification
Freedom to dissent and exit is mandatory. Treating silence as assent, loyalty scoring, or excluding critics are failure modes. If repeated coherent structure does not improve durable cooperation, the hypothesis weakens.

## Interpretation constraints
Not covert persuasion, ideology enforcement, or a deterministic claim about future consensus.
