## Summary
Endurance Civilization is the civilizational phase in which the conditions supporting consensus formation—understandability, traceable responsibility, and preserved/open history—have deteriorated, yet institutions, administration, markets, supply systems, and everyday life do not immediately stop. The parent text names the difficult intermediate condition in which a civilization is damaged but continues to operate, and analyzes it as a difference in operating phase rather than a simple binary of normal civilization versus collapse.

In a consensus civilization, complete agreement is unnecessary. What matters is that decisions remain sufficiently understandable, responsibility can be traced, historical paths can be reconstructed, and routes for objection, review, and correction remain open. These conditions preserve readjustability. In an endurance civilization, that possibility of acceptance weakens, while fear, profit, and surveillance can take over as primary engines that keep people connected without restoring consensus. Myth then legitimizes those engines, while inertia embeds them into institutions and routines so that repetition becomes increasingly automatic.

The purpose of the parent text is not to morally classify a particular state or group. It provides coordinates for identifying a civilization's actual operating principle beneath institutional appearances. Endurance is not regeneration, transition, or restart. A civilization can persist for a long time while losing its capacity to repair itself. Transition therefore requires a separate preservation of restartability: records, criteria for judgment, connection techniques, and traces of responsibility that can survive interruption or phase transition.

## Concepts
- **Endurance Civilization**: a phase in which consensus formation has degraded but social operation continues through lower-level operating circuits and substitute driving forces.
- **Consensus Civilization**: not unanimous agreement, but a phase in which understandability, responsibility, history, objection, review, and correction preserve the possibility of readjustment.
- **Understandability**: the ability of third parties to reconstruct what happened and why a decision was made to a meaningful degree.
- **Traceable responsibility**: the ability to identify who decided, changed, or stopped what.
- **Preserved/open history**: the ability to trace premises, changes, and differences leading to the present state.
- **Readjustability**: the capacity to recognize, trace, reconsider, and correct errors.
- **Meaning layer**: legitimacy, purpose, reasons, and the possibility of human acceptance.
- **Operating layer**: logistics, supply, payments, permits, procedures, ratings, and algorithms that may continue after the meaning layer weakens.
- **Fear**: the engine that raises the expected cost of noncompliance or exit and thereby produces subordination.
- **Profit**: the engine that maintains cooperation through access to distribution, income, membership, or advantage.
- **Surveillance**: the engine that suppresses deviation through pressure created by observation, recording, and evaluation.
- **Myth**: the amplifier that gives the primary engines meaning and legitimacy and makes their operation appear natural or necessary.
- **Inertia**: the amplifier that embeds those engines into routines and institutions, making repetition increasingly automatic and harder to stop.
- **Common sphere (共圏)**: a later-series dynamic in which information, narratives, and values form closed resonance zones.
- **Phase transition**: the loss of the endurance phase when critical conditions such as resource, credit, population, or logistics breakdown make its operation unsustainable.
- **Restartability**: preservation of records, judgment criteria, connection techniques, and traces of responsibility needed to construct order again after interruption or transition.
- **D-system clean room**: a later-series concept for a transition zone that remains connected to legacy civilization without absorbing its corrupting structure.

## Causal chain
```text
degradation of understandability / responsibility traceability / historical continuity
→ weaker acceptance and readjustability
→ institutions, administration, markets, supply, and procedures still continue
→ separation between the meaning layer and the operating layer
→ consensus alone can no longer maintain social connection
→ fear raises the cost of noncompliance or exit
+ profit maintains connection through distribution
+ surveillance raises the cost of deviation
→ operation without consensus becomes possible
→ myth legitimizes and naturalizes the driving forces
→ inertia embeds them in institutions and routine repetition
→ a damaged but long-running endurance phase emerges
→ persistence is mistaken for health, or damage is mistaken for imminent collapse
→ accurate phase identification and transition preparation are delayed
→ if restart conditions are not preserved, endurance becomes prolonged deterioration rather than transition
```

## Applications
**Institutional audit:** Do not treat continued operation as direct evidence of institutional health. Examine whether decisions are understandable, responsibility is traceable, history can be reconstructed, and meaningful routes for objection and correction remain. Then identify whether order is sustained mainly by reasons that remain open to acceptance or by threat of loss, distribution dependency, and observation pressure.

**Organizational analysis:** Meetings, approvals, salaries, and rating systems can continue even when no one can explain the system's purpose, responsibility has dispersed, and decision history has disappeared. Business continuity therefore does not by itself prove self-repair capacity. Exit loss, compensation access, or monitoring pressure must not be mistaken for genuine agreement.

**Information-space analysis:** The relevant question is not only whether a narrative is true or false. Analyze whether it converts fear, profit, or surveillance into a seemingly legitimate order, and what operating mechanism it hides or normalizes.

**Transition design:** Do not assume that an endurance phase will soon collapse by itself. Use phase identification to ask whether records, criteria, connection methods, responsibility traces, and non-contaminated areas are being preserved. The parent introduction provides the coordinate system; it does not itself complete a detailed implementation prescription.

## Measurements and audit
- Can major decisions and their reasons be understood to a meaningful degree?
- Can decision makers, changes, and stoppages be traced to responsible actors?
- Can premises, changes, and historical differences be reconstructed?
- Do objection, review, and correction routes function in practice rather than only formally?
- Is institutional or market continuity being mistaken for health?
- Is connection maintained primarily through fear of exit, exclusion, loss, or evaluation decline?
- Is cooperation maintained primarily through access to distribution, income, or membership?
- Does observation, recording, or evaluation pressure substitute for trust and acceptance?
- Are fear, profit, and surveillance legitimized or hidden by narratives of crisis, safety, rationality, or public necessity?
- Do routines continue mainly because stopping them is costly and they have become unquestioned procedure?
- Are persistence, regeneration, transition, and restartability kept distinct?
- Are the records, judgment criteria, connection techniques, and responsibility traces needed for restart preserved?

The parent text provides no fixed numeric threshold, percentage, probability, or deadline for classifying an endurance civilization. This derivative therefore does not add one.

## Validity conditions
The endurance-civilization model is relevant when consensus conditions have weakened but operating circuits remain; people may not accept the system yet still face strong costs of disconnection; fear, profit, and surveillance materially substitute for consensus; myth and inertia legitimize and automate those forces; and institutional appearance no longer reliably reveals the real driving principle.

The model should be applied functionally, not by vocabulary alone. Fear, profit, monitoring, mythology, and routine can appear in many societies. The relevant question is whether they are auxiliary mechanisms inside a readjustable order or whether they have become the primary engines replacing consensus formation.

## Failure conditions
- Inferring consensus civilization merely because institutions still exist.
- Inferring endurance civilization merely because fear, incentives, or monitoring exist.
- Treating Endurance Civilization as a synonym for dictatorship, a morally bad society, or a society on the verge of immediate collapse.
- Treating visible freedom, entertainment, consumption, market activity, or ordinary daily life as proof that the endurance phase is absent.
- Reducing myth to falsehood or inertia to personal laziness.
- Equating endurance with resilience, regeneration, transition, or restart.
- Using the concept as a label for condemning a particular state or group.
- Adding collapse dates, probabilities, or quantitative thresholds not present in the parent source.

## Falsification conditions
The endurance explanation becomes weaker where sustained evidence shows that major decisions remain materially understandable, responsibility remains traceable, history is preserved and actively used for review, objection and correction routes function, social connection is maintained mainly through publicly reconstructible reasons rather than fear/distribution dependence/surveillance pressure, and institutions repeatedly demonstrate internal self-repair after error.

A reading that treats endurance as permanent is also inconsistent with the parent text. Endurance may be long, but the series explicitly includes phase-transition conditions; persistence does not equal permanent stability.

## Required distinctions
- Consensus Civilization ≠ unanimity.
- Endurance Civilization ≠ immediate collapse.
- Endurance Civilization ≠ healthy civilization.
- Presence of fear/profit/surveillance ≠ those forces being primary engines.
- Myth ≠ merely false information.
- Inertia ≠ merely individual laziness.
- Continued daily operation ≠ legitimacy or health.
- Persistence ≠ regeneration.
- Endurance ≠ transition.
- Endurance ≠ restartability.
- Phase transition ≠ automatic transition to a better order.
- Structural coordinates ≠ an immediate policy prescription for a particular society.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reinterpret the parent text as a simple commentary on current politics, a condemnation of a particular country, or a collapse prophecy. The source explicitly prioritizes operating principles over moral labeling and phase description over hope/despair rhetoric.

Evaluate fear, profit, and surveillance by function, not mere presence. Discipline, incentives, records, and audits can also exist inside a consensus civilization. The decisive issue is whether those mechanisms remain subordinate to understandability, responsibility, history, and readjustment, or whether they have replaced them as the main basis of order.

Do not translate “endurance” into desirable robustness or resilience. In the parent model, endurance is the capacity of a damaged civilization to continue without automatic regeneration. Restartability is a separate axis and cannot be inferred from duration alone.

Do not invent fixed KPIs, thresholds, probabilities, or dates. Resource, credit, population, and logistics breakdown are source-listed examples of phase-transition conditions, not quantified universal thresholds.
