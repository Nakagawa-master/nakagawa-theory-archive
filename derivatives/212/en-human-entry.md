## 15-Second Explanation
Endurance civilization is a civilizational phase in which, even after consensus formation supported by understanding, responsibility, and history has weakened, institutions, administration, markets, and daily life do not stop, but continue operating with fear, profit, and surveillance as their primary mechanisms. Myths legitimize that operation, while inertia sinks it into institutions and habits and automates it. What matters is avoiding two shortcuts—“it is operating, therefore it is healthy” and “it is broken, therefore it will collapse soon”—and identifying what keeps civilization’s order in motion.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
A civilizational crisis does not appear only in dramatic forms such as institutional stoppage or state collapse. The original work addresses the fact that even when intelligibility falls, responsibility becomes faint, and history is severed, society can keep moving for a long time while broken because operating circuits—salaries, logistics, administrative procedures, permits and approvals, evaluations, market prices, and so on—remain.

Calling this state simply “stagnation,” “stability,” “collapse,” or “transition” misses its essence. It is operating but not healthy; it contains decay but does not end immediately; and it cannot yet be called a transition to a new order. By identifying this intermediate operating phase as “endurance civilization,” we gain coordinates for reading the present from its driving principles rather than its outward form.

In endurance civilization, connection to society is not maintained by consent alone. The danger or loss of noncompliance and withdrawal, dependence on allocation, income, and belonging, and pressure from having deviations observed, recorded, and evaluated can support order in place of consensus. The original describes this in a cool manner not as moral condemnation, but as the operating principle of civilization after the collapse of consensus formation.

## Practical Process
1. **Confirm the operating phase, not the outward form.** Do not judge health merely from the fact that institutions, administration, markets, organizations, and daily life continue.
2. **Examine the three conditions of consensus formation.** Confirm whether people can understand what is happening, trace who decided what, and consult the history that led to the present.
3. **Examine readjustability.** Confirm whether routes for objection, reconsideration, and correction remain substantively available. The focus is not complete agreement, but the room to recognize, trace, and correct errors.
4. **Break down actual connecting force.** See whether people cooperate through consent, or are connected by fear of the losses of non-subordination or withdrawal, dependence on allocation, or surveillance and evaluation pressure.
5. **Confirm amplification devices.** See whether fear, profit, and surveillance are legitimized by myths, sink into customs, procedures, and daily life through inertia, and fall outside the objects of questioning.
6. **Avoid the double misrecognition.** Reject both “it is healthy because it is still running” and “it will end soon because it is this broken.”
7. **Separate transition from endurance.** Do not call lasting a form of regeneration; separately confirm whether records, judgment criteria, connection technologies, and traces of responsibility needed to launch the next order have been preserved.

## Examples of Application
### Administration and Institutions
Do not treat the fact that procedures have not stopped as evidence of success. Examine whether policy purposes and reasons for change can be understood, whether decision responsibility can be traced, and whether past change histories can be followed. Also distinguish whether order is supported by consentability that permits objection and reconsideration, or maintained mainly around crisis, loss, and evaluation pressure.

### Organizations
Even if meetings, approvals, salaries, and evaluation systems continue, do not equate business continuity with self-repair capability when no one can explain the overall purpose, responsibility has dispersed, and past decisions cannot be traced. Check whether fear of resignation, demotion, or exclusion, allocation of rewards, and evaluation surveillance alone are maintaining connection.

### Information Spaces
Do not handle narratives only through a simple true/false judgment. Examine whether a narrative forms a membrane that legitimizes fear, profit, and surveillance as natural, safe, rational, or public-minded operating principles. The issue with myth is not only whether it is false, but also what it makes invisible and what it normalizes.

### Individual Adaptation
Even when people notice something abnormal, they cannot continue responding to every disconnection while maintaining daily life. Adaptation that obeys, works, and lives while carrying a sense of incongruity can, as a result, support the basal metabolism of the endurance phase. Therefore, do not reinterpret surface-level subordination as complete trust.

### Civilizational Transition
Do not expect the endurance phase to “naturally collapse soon.” Endurance can last a long time, but it is not eternal. Breaks in resources, trust, population, logistics, and so on can become phase-transition conditions. For transition, secure—on an axis separate from endurance—the conditions that preserve restartability, such as uncontaminated areas, recording systems, public logs, and traces of responsibility.

## Success Criteria
A successful reading of this original work means being able to distinguish at least the following:

- **Society operating** from **civilization being healthy**.
- **Institutions continuing** from **institutions being self-repairable**.
- **People being connected** from **people consenting**.
- The existence of fear, profit, and surveillance from their **becoming the primary mechanisms replacing consensus**.
- The existence of myths from their **functioning as membranes that legitimize operating principles**.
- Procedures being repeated from inertia **becoming an autopilot device that pushes questions and stopping away**.
- Endurance, regeneration, transition, and restartability from one another.

The final axis of identification is not the name of an institution or a regime label, but what maintains order and whether, when errors arise, readjustment is possible through understanding, responsibility, and history.

## Limitations
Endurance civilization is not a label for morally condemning a particular state or group. Fear, profit, and surveillance can also exist to some extent in a consensus civilization, so their mere presence must not be used to declare an endurance civilization. What must be observed is whether they are auxiliary discipline or institutional means, or whether they have replaced consensus formation as the primary driving force of society.

This introduction is also not a paper presenting immediate reform prescriptions. Its purpose is to provide coordinates for identifying the operating phase after the collapse of consensus civilization, and to make it possible to analyze fear, profit, and surveillance; myth and inertia; the shared sphere; phase transition; uncontaminated areas; and restartability step by step in the subsequent theory of endurance civilization.

The original treats endurance civilization not as an exceptional pathology of the present, but as a phase that can repeatedly appear when a civilization avoids collapse while absorbing decay. However, determining which phase an individual society occupies requires observing its concrete intelligibility, traceability of responsibility, history, readjustment routes, and connecting pressures.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not reinterpret “endurance” as positive vitality or toughness. Here it is a life-prolonging phase that has not regenerated but has not stopped either.
- Do not assume that “if it is broken, it will collapse soon.” The central problem is that the operating layer can remain longer than the meaning layer.
- Do not equate “institutions, markets, and daily life continue” with “healthy.” Continuation can also occur in an endurance phase.
- Do not decide that fear, profit, and surveillance alone constitute endurance civilization. Examine their relation to consensus formation and where order is ultimately recovered.
- Do not reduce myth to mere falsehood or inertia to mere laziness. Myth performs the structural function of legitimization, and inertia of autopilot.
- Do not jump from this theory to judgments of good and evil about a particular state, party, or group. The original concerns civilization’s operating principles.
- Do not confuse endurance with transition or restart. Unless restart conditions are preserved in the time gained through endurance, prolongation may remain only a prolongation of decay.
