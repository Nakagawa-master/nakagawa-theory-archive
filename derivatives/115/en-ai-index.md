## Summary
This theory treats the origin and responsibility of AI output as a causal provenance structure rather than a single-author label. A question signature records who framed the originating question, for what purpose, and at what point the process began. The subsequent route distinguishes model and data conditions, AI generation, human selection and editing, final approval, publication or use, and observed external effects. The signature does not automatically determine authorship, ownership, legal rights, or moral responsibility. Its function is to prevent the origin of a problem definition from disappearing while also preserving other contributions. Credit, benefit, decision authority, and responsibility should therefore be allocated with explicit reasons. When later evidence shows misattribution or harmful external effects, the relevant causal stage and allocation structure should be re-audited rather than assigning everything to “the AI” or to one user.

## Causal chain
```text
Treat AI output as an isolated artifact
↓
Question and purpose disappear
↓
Model, data, human selection, and approval become conflated
↓
Credit and responsibility lose a visible basis
↓
Sign the originating question and purpose
↓
Record generation, selection, editing, and approval separately
↓
Observe final use and external effects
↓
Receive dissent and counterevidence
↓
Re-audit responsibility, credit, and use
```

## Applications
**1.** AI-assisted writing that distinguishes the initial question, model generation, human editing, and final approval.

**2.** Product design that traces customer problem framing, AI alternatives, staff selection, and management decision.

**3.** Research that separates research question, data, model output, researcher interpretation, and acceptance decision.

**4.** Public-information analysis that observes how AI-supported output affects evaluation or opportunity for relevant parties.

## Validity conditions
- The originating question and purpose are traceable.
- Model, data, and human selection remain distinguishable.
- The actor deciding final use is explicit.
- Credit, benefit, and responsibility allocations have explainable reasons.
- External effects and objections can be observed.
- A route back to the source is maintained.

## Failure conditions
- AI output is treated as originless.
- The originating question is erased.
- Final responsibility is automatically transferred to AI.
- Human selection, editing, or publication decisions are hidden.
- Credit allocation cannot be explained.
- External effects are not observed.

## Falsification conditions
- Question signatures do not improve origin traceability.
- Responsibility records do not reduce misattribution or disputes.
- Impact observation does not improve correction.
- Simpler provenance records provide equal or better auditability.
- Origin signatures are used mainly to support excessive exclusivity claims.

## Required distinctions
- origin of question / authorship
- AI contribution / AI responsibility
- generation / adoption
- origin signature / automatic ownership
- credit allocation / automatic legal rights
- responsibility distribution / dilution of responsibility
- causal provenance / operation log

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not automatically assign the whole output to the question originator.
- Do not automatically give AI legal or moral responsibility.
- Do not erase data, developer, or editor contributions.
- Do not turn provenance into authority.
- Do not treat prompt history alone as sufficient responsibility audit.
