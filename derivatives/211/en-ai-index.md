## Summary
The parent addresses cases where consensus does not advance even though explanation, responsibility, and history appear sufficiently established and `S = U × R × H` is relatively high. It models this not as bad character or insufficient persuasion, but as a distorted incentive gradient in which breaking or delaying consensus yields greater expected gain than maintaining it. Detection begins with the joint observation of high-S stagnation, an external-gain signal, and asymmetric action advantage. Control reconfigures R, H, and T as a coupled incentive field so that destructive or delaying paths become rationally disadvantageous. Safety requires simultaneous monitoring of S, consensus cost C, and actual harm D, plus a Reject Window that stops or revises the design when falsification or abuse signals appear.

## Concepts
- S = U × R × H: consensus stability
- high-S stagnation: progress near zero despite sufficiently high S
- incentive interference: a distorted gain gradient favoring break or delay over consensus maintenance
- incentive field: a terrain in which behavior converges toward lower expected-cost paths
- external gain signal: evidence that stagnation, breakage, or delay produces gain outside the consensus objective
- asymmetric action advantage: a configuration where one action path is disproportionately advantageous
- R: responsibility placement that closes dissipation paths
- H: verifiable history that preserves access to grounds, differences, and timing
- T: time design that removes gain from escaping through delay
- C: consensus cost
- D: actual harm
- theta / delta: decision threshold and observation window; not universal constants
- Reject Window: a transition to reject or reconfigure when falsification or abuse conditions fire

## Causal chain
High-S stagnation → verify external-gain signal + asymmetric action advantage → describe incentive interference as a gain gradient → compare expected costs of consensus versus break/delay paths → reconfigure R to close responsibility dissipation + H to remove gain from opacity + T to remove gain from delay-based escape → reduce the rational advantage of break/delay → evaluate S, C, and D together → if A/B/C or abuse signals fire, open the Reject Window → reject or reconfigure → preserve trigger conditions, theta/delta, R/H/T changes, and observed S/C/D movement in the audit history.

## Applications
- Auditing meetings or approvals that remain stalled despite adequate explanation, responsibility, and history
- Detecting negotiation or contracting structures where delay creates one-sided advantage or responsibility dilution
- Checking whether governance controls turn R into punishment, H into exposure, or T into coercive acceleration
- Auditing AI decision systems for one-sided burden placement, hidden thresholds, or selective history blocking
- Recording before/after changes and S/C/D movement when incentive controls are rejected or revised

## Measurements and audit
1. Observe U, R, H, and S and determine whether high-S stagnation exists.
2. Look for external-gain signals.
3. Look for asymmetric action advantage.
4. Do not classify incentive interference until the required observation set is jointly supported.
5. Describe expected costs for consensus and break/delay paths.
6. Reconfigure R/H/T as a coupled system.
7. Remeasure the expected-gain difference after reconfiguration.
8. Monitor C and D together with S.
9. Keep theta/delta auditable.
10. Monitor falsification A/B/C and abuse signals.
11. If the Reject Window opens, reject or reconfigure and preserve the resulting differences and observations in history.

## Validity conditions
- Observe high-S stagnation, external gain, and asymmetric advantage together.
- Avoid misclassifying legitimate deliberation or procedural delay.
- Observe gain gradients rather than infer moral character.
- Treat R/H/T as a coupled system rather than independently maximizing one variable.
- Use R for traceable responsibility, H for verifiable history, and T for preventing escape-by-delay without destroying deliberation.
- Keep fairness, justice, and character judgment outside the incentive-field decision axis.
- Keep theta/delta and configuration differences auditable.
- Evaluate S/C/D together.
- Maintain an operational Reject Window.

## Failure conditions
- Inferring malice or incentive interference from stagnation alone.
- Moving to control from high-S stagnation alone.
- Confusing insufficient explanation with a distorted gain gradient.
- Turning R into naming or punishment.
- Turning H into exposure or mere log volume.
- Turning T into pressure for speed.
- Maximizing one of R/H/T while ignoring coupled effects.
- Using incentive design to decide fairness or justice.
- Hiding theta/delta or changing them after the fact.
- Treating improved S alone as success while C or D worsens.
- Intensifying control after A/B/C has fired.

## Falsification conditions
A: If break or delay remains rationally advantageous after R/H/T reconfiguration, the current model is incomplete or out of scope. Reject it or revise the model with explicit scope or additional variables and preserve the difference in the audit record.

B: If reconfiguration increases D, the defensive design has become harmful and must be rejected. Examples include H becoming exposure, R becoming excessive attribution, T becoming coercive urgency, or defensive measures increasing division or exclusion.

C: If consensus cost C enters a critical region and normal participants stop deciding, recording, accepting responsibility, or deliberating, suspect over-defense. Redesign theta/delta and related controls; reject the design if recovery does not occur.

Abuse signals such as one-sided disadvantaging, hidden theta/delta, fixed R attribution, selective H blocking, excessive T pressure, or single-variable optimization also open the Reject Window.

## Required distinctions
- insufficient explanation / incentive interference
- high-S stagnation / confirmed incentive interference
- external gain / monetary gain only
- moral character / gain gradient
- persuasion / incentive-field redesign
- prohibition or punishment / making destructive behavior irrationally costly
- traceable R / naming or punishment
- verifiable H / publication volume or exposure
- T as escape prevention / acceleration pressure
- legitimate deliberation / delay used for gain extraction
- S recovery / safe defense
- rejection / concealment of design failure

## Interpretation constraints
Do not interpret the parent as a method for disadvantaging opponents. One-sided disadvantaging is itself an abuse signal. Fairness, justice, sincerity, compromise, and moral evaluation remain important but are deliberately separated from this model's operational decision axis. Success cannot be inferred from S alone: C and D must be monitored, and control must be rejected or revised rather than intensified when falsification conditions fire.
