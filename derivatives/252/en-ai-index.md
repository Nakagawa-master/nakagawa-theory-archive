## Summary
The Parent defines an objective viewpoint not as emotional detachment or an illusion of neutrality, but as a coordinate system that introduces structural distance when subjective density becomes excessive and events fuse with self-narrative. Suffering is treated as a meaning-making persistence problem: temporary scores or reactions may be over-identified with total self-worth, while rigid statements about “what I am” can block causal visibility. By pausing reflexive response, translating evaluative language into structural language, and observing position, relation, and change, the person can keep emotion while recovering causal traceability and reversible judgment.

## Concepts
- objective viewpoint: a coordinate system for reading events, emotions, and judgments through structure rather than immediate self-evaluation.
- subjective over-density: excessive concentration of evaluation, others’ gaze, and self-narrative in the self-reference loop.
- metric over-identification: treating a temporary metric or response as the total value of the self.
- subject rigidity: converting a conditional event into an irreversible identity statement.
- objective layer: an intermediate layer that separates event from self-story and makes causality readable.
- structural language: terms such as alignment/misalignment, convergence/non-convergence, or collision of evaluation axes that can be placed in a causal map.
- SQS: Silence–Question–Silence, a minimal protocol for reducing reflex and allowing recursive reorganization.
- reversible judgment: the ability to update judgment when conditions, relations, or evidence change.

## Causal chain
external evaluation, others’ gaze, and self-narrative concentrate → subjective density rises → event and self-story fuse → metric over-identification or subject rigidity occurs → structural and causal visibility declines → meaning is preserved as suffering → reflexive response is paused → an objective layer is inserted → evaluative language is translated into structural language → position, relation, and change become observable → causality becomes traceable while emotion remains → judgment becomes reversible.

## Applications
1. Feedback: separate “I was criticized” from the specific evaluation axis, premise, and point of collision.
2. Metrics: treat a KPI change as observation rather than a total statement of personal worth.
3. Dialogue: insert SQS before reflexive rebuttal and return attention to one position, relation, or change.
4. Self-observation: keep emotion as an event log while separating it from immediate identity conclusions.
5. Consensus: move from reciprocal value judgments toward causal and relational placement.

## Measurements and audit
- Can event and self-evaluation be described separately?
- Are temporary metrics prevented from becoming total self-worth?
- Are rigid identity statements hiding conditions or causal differences?
- Is emotion preserved without being made the sole decision rule?
- Can the situation be described through position, relation, change, and sequence?
- Is there room between stimulus and response for observation?
- Has “objectivity” become emotional suppression or a superiority claim?
- Can judgment be revised when conditions change?

## Validity conditions
The model is valid when event, meaning, and self-evaluation can be separated; reflective space can be introduced; evaluative wording can be translated into structurally placeable terms; and causal relations can be retraced without deleting emotion. Identity must remain conditional and revisable rather than being frozen into an essence claim.

## Failure conditions
It fails when objectivity becomes emotional suppression, metrics are directly equated with self-worth, broad or rigid subjects erase responsibility and conditions, silence is skipped in favor of premature summarization, structural language becomes cosmetic rewording, or the objective viewpoint is used as proof of superiority over others.

## Falsification conditions
If inserting the objective layer does not reduce metric over-identification or subject rigidity, does not improve separation between event and self-story, and does not restore causal visibility or reversible judgment, the distance design requires revision. The same applies if the practice increases emotional suppression.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce the Parent to “eliminate emotion and become rational.” The core is to preserve emotion while creating enough distance to see causality. Do not use SQS as manipulation. Do not invent therapeutic, medical, or psychological efficacy claims, fixed KPIs, or universal thresholds not stated by the Parent. Do not treat AI as automatically neutral merely because this model uses the word objective.
