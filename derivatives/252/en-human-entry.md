## 15-Second Explanation
When suffering intensifies, it can be that, rather than the event itself, the meaning “this shows my worth” has become over-dense within the subjective viewpoint. An objective viewpoint is not a method for erasing emotion; it is a coordinate that creates distance between an event and a self-narrative and rereads causality as position, relation, and change.

## Why It Is Needed
When evaluation notices, other people’s reactions, numbers, and self-narratives overlap, a temporary event can easily become an evaluation of “oneself.” The Parent Original understands this as over-identification with indicators and hardening of the subject.

The problem is not that emotion exists. It is that the path from emotion to meaning-making and judgment becomes direct, and the distance for observing causality disappears. Therefore, an objective layer is inserted between subjectivity and the event.

## Central Structure
```text
Events, evaluations, and reactions from others
↓
Flow into subjectivity at high density
↓
Self-narrative and event fuse
↓
Numbers and reactions are over-identified with self-worth
↓
The subject hardens and causality can no longer be read
↓
Suffering is preserved as meaning
↓
Stop the reflex with silence
↓
Ask about position, relation, and change
↓
Translate into structural terms
↓
Regain causality while retaining emotion
↓
Restore the reversibility of judgment
```

## Practical Process
1. **Stop the reflex.** Do not immediately proceed to evaluation, rebuttal, or self-denial.
2. **Separate the event.** Separate what happened from the meaning you assigned to it.
3. **Remove the fixed subject.** Instead of “I am no good,” see what happened under which conditions, relations, and evaluation axes.
4. **Return to structural terms.** Rather than good/bad, place it as consistency/inconsistency, convergence/non-convergence, or a conflict between evaluation axes.
5. **Ask only one question.** Ask about one of position, relation, or change, and make room for information to be reorganized.
6. **Trace causality.** See what connects to what and where the meaning-making of suffering arose.
7. **Update the judgment.** Do not fix one’s essence; choose the next action according to conditions.

## SQS
The Parent Original places Silence–Question–Silence as the minimum form for creating an objective viewpoint as a field.

```text
Silence
→ One-sentence question
→ Silence
```

Silence is not for ignoring emotion, but for decompressing reflexive responses. The question is not to corner the other person; it returns attention to one point among position, relation, and change.

## Application Examples
**When evaluated.** Do not begin with “I was rejected”; check which evaluation axis collided with your assumptions.

**When numbers worsen.** Do not connect numbers to self-worth; examine which conditions, arrangement, and actions connected to the result.

**When dialogue heats up.** Do not immediately rebut; insert silence and ask one question about which relation or position changed.

## Success Criteria
- Explain events and self-evaluation separately.
- Trace causality without erasing emotion.
- Do not expand a temporary indicator to one’s entire self-worth.
- Speak in terms of conditions and arrangement rather than a fixed subject.
- Revise a judgment afterward.
- The objective viewpoint has not turned into superiority that “only I am right.”

## Limitations
An objective viewpoint is neither a technique that makes emotion unnecessary nor a guarantee that suffering will always disappear. When an event has physical, institutional, or relational problems, those problems themselves also require action.

Also, merely replacing words with structural terms is insufficient. The purpose is not to make language look neat, but to return causality to a visible state.

## Preventing Misreading
- Objective does not mean cold.
- Emotion is not noise.
- Silence is not neglect.
- Bad numbers do not mean low self-worth.
- Do not equate the self-definition “I am ...” with fact.
- Do not use an objective viewpoint to judge others.
