## Summary
Cultural resilience is neither defensive closure nor assimilation into a dominant standard. A region remains culturally alive when it can preserve identifiable language, memory, practices, and roles while translating them into points of exchange, co-creation, learning, and repeated connection with others. Openness is therefore not surrender of difference; it is an increase in connection while difference remains legible. Operation requires local editorial authority, benefit return, space for younger generations to reinterpret inherited forms, and monitoring of assimilation pressure and external dependency. Commercial success alone does not prove cultural renewal. AI can assist translation and retrieval, but it must not invent context, taboos, or community consent that the source has not established.

## Validity conditions
- Identity is explicit before opening.
- Exchange and assimilation are distinguished.
- Local actors retain edit and refusal rights.
- Younger generations can revise forms.
- Benefits return locally.
- Meaning change is audited.

## Failure conditions
- Purity becomes exclusion.
- Meaning is simplified for external markets.
- Assimilation is labeled success.
- Youth are forced to reproduce tradition.
- Benefits flow outward only.
- Dissent is treated as betrayal.

## Falsification conditions
- Openness does not improve continuity or participation.
- External exchange consistently erodes local identity.
- Closed protection consistently produces more creativity.
- Co-creation creates dependency without local agency.
- Reduced emphasis on difference consistently lowers conflict without cultural loss.
