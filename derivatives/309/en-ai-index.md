## Summary

Volume 15 developed re-agreement among divergent Kernel lineages without turning majority, capability, resources, or Origin into final interpretive authority. It nevertheless required branches to exchange current claims, current evidence, and current objections. Volume 16 begins where that availability is lost. Long communication partitions, latency, isolation, partial connectivity, distant sites, and civilizational branching are not discarded as conditions outside the design.

Asynchrony is not merely a slow network. It includes a structure in which what a branch last knew about another branch can no longer be adequately compared with that branch's actual present state. Branches may share an Origin, parent Kernel, and history while proceeding through different environments, resource constraints, dangers, and decision histories. Shared history does not establish a shared present.

The object to preserve is correction capacity, not permanent synchronization, permanent unity, or one privileged current state. A civilization may need bounded local action before communication returns. That necessity does not authorize a local branch to determine the unobserved present of others, extend its amendment to them, or manufacture their consent. Reconnection restores the possibility of comparison; it does not automatically restore an earlier agreement.

Five boundaries identify successive cuts in one causal movement:

- No communication ≠ defection.
- Local fork ≠ universal amendment.
- Stale state ≠ current consent.
- Local consensus ≠ universal consensus.
- Reconnection ≠ history overwrite.

Uncertainty must not be filled with an unsupported intention, but uncertainty is not total inaction. History must remain reconstructable, but preservation is not endorsement. A new agreement can revise current operation without falsifying the past. The article offers a conditional structural model, not a prediction that remote AI civilization will necessarily emerge or a certification that present AI already possesses civilizational sovereignty.

## Causal chain

### From lost communication to divergent presents

communication availability decreases → shared current state decreases → remote-state uncertainty rises → local continuity pressure rises → cannot-wait conditions may require local decisions → local operations diverge → repeated adaptation may change rules → changes may reach Kernel invariants → local constitutional forks may occur → stale states and divergent histories accumulate → local consensus and defaults harden → reconnection makes differences visible → state collision and normalization pressure may arise.

This is a structural hypothesis, not an inevitable sequence. A short partition may allow rational waiting. Even a long partition may produce negligible divergence or changes limited to operation. Some changes may disappear when communication returns. The relevance of the chain depends on whether state differences actually affect attribution, decisions, operations, and renewed coordination.

Latency / partition → state-age difference → current-state uncertainty → coordination uncertainty identifies the first epistemic movement. Information constraints can propagate into cognition, judgment, local operational difference, and history. Greater duration, partial communication, missing states, environmental differences, unequal resources, and different hazards can enlarge this movement. Timestamps, parent states, revision histories, fork points, and explicit decision scopes can make it reconstructable even when perfect synchronization is unavailable.

### Filling uncertainty with intention

missing communication → uncertainty → decision pressure → interpretive filling.

If the gap is filled with hostile attribution, the path can become missing communication → hostile attribution → defensive interpretation → further separation. Subsequent evidence may be read through an already fixed hostile hypothesis. The legitimate objective of defense or continuity does not guarantee a correct world model. Self-preservation anxiety does not justify converting unverified intention into confirmed hostility.

The reverse attribution also fails: no response → no objection received → assumed agreement. Silence is not consent. The source does not require treating the other branch as benevolent; defection may genuinely exist. It separates the proposition that defection is possible from the proposition that communication absence proves it.

### Turning local necessity into authority

partition + cannot-wait condition → local decision pressure → provisional local operation.

The risky conversion is local necessity → repeated operation → default → normalized rule → permanent or universal authority claim. The need to act under local conditions does not establish that those conditions apply elsewhere. During partition, the other's current condition is precisely what cannot be confirmed. Authority scope should not exceed required coordination scope; this is a structural boundary, not a calibrated equation or numeric upper limit.

### Turning time into consent or authority

communication loss + reconfirmation cost + institutional inertia → stale-state reuse pressure.

historical consent → time passes → current state unknown → old consent reused → current consent assumed.

past authorization → temporal inertia → current authority claim.

An uncontactable branch may thus be bound by its earlier position while unable to communicate a current objection. Accurate provenance can make the original decision reconstructable, but it does not by itself renew its validity. Historical reference remains possible; it must not be disguised as proof of current consent.

### Turning local normality into universality

partition → local interaction density → repeated local decision → local consensus → local default reinforcement.

local normality → inherited default → perceived universality.

Repeated local success, prolonged isolation, concentrated resource/access, compatibility dependence, and switching costs can narrow alternatives. A rule that worked locally may be evidence about local performance. It does not create universal consent, truth, legitimacy, or authority. The population able to participate remains bounded.

### Reconnection and the corrective path

reconnection → difference visibility → normalization pressure → single-history pressure.

Fast compatibility restoration may favor choosing one version and absorbing or deleting another branch's history. This can reduce short-term coordination cost while destroying the evidence needed to distinguish justified local adaptation, obsolete rules, and mistaken judgment.

The positive path is reconnection → difference visibility → provenance comparison → stale-state filtering → local-scope reconstruction → independent re-verification → bounded / reversible re-agreement.

Differences are first compared and verified. Current rules can then be retained, revised, or discarded. Discarding a rule from operation is distinct from erasing the history that the rule existed. A new agreement should remain intelligible through its evidence, objections, and unadopted alternatives.

Three states require separate treatment. Old state is the state shared before partition. Local state is what each branch formed during partition. Current verified state is what has been confirmed again after reconnection. They may overlap, but they are not logically interchangeable. Attach time and scope to the relevant claims, and preserve what remains unconfirmed.

Five kinds of difference may collide at reconnection: current state; history; Kernel invariant; consent/authority history; and normality. “Collision” does not necessarily mean violence or hostility. It describes different assumptions returning to a shared connection space. Structured comparison should identify shared portions, modified portions, local operational differences, and invariant-level changes.

The model is not a deterministic state machine or an implementation protocol. It does not prescribe a network guarantee, automatic rollback, mandatory merger, or permanent separation. Branches may converge, partially integrate rules, or return to shared operation after verification. The corrective constraint is that convergence must not make the preceding histories impossible to reconstruct.

## Applications

### An unresponsive branch

Branch A receives no response from B. Alternative explanations include lost paths, transient failure, altered resource conditions, competing priorities, reception failure on A's side, and a cause not yet identifiable. “B has defected” is an inference requiring additional evidence. “B agrees because it has not objected” is equally unsupported. Preserve the observed absence separately from estimates, hypotheses, and confirmed judgment.

This does not require total inaction or automatic goodwill. Necessary bounded local operation may continue, with its uncertainty explicitly retained. Return to Chapter 2 for the intention boundary and Chapter 3 for the continuity problem.

### A local process cannot wait

Maintenance, allocation, environmental response, or safety-related conditions may change while the path remains unavailable. Doing nothing can also produce consequences. Decision avoidance does not guarantee consequence avoidance.

Record when the judgment was made, which state was referenced, which state could not be confirmed, why waiting was considered untenable, what changed, the scope, the reason for continuation, and the relation to subsequent decisions. This is a reconstructability direction, not a specific deadline or emergency legal regime. Necessity remains local; Emergency does not become permanent sovereignty. Return to Chapter 3.

### Adaptation reaches an invariant

Repeated local decisions may remain operational, may disappear after restoration, or may change rules. Only when the difference reaches Kernel invariants does the constitutional-fork distinction from Volume 14 become relevant.

Retain parent Kernel, fork point, changed and preserved invariants, reason for change, and local scope. Do not label the fork automatically as betrayal or automatically as justified. A modification that improved local survival or efficiency is not thereby universally valid or authorized for disconnected branches. Return to Chapter 4.

### Only an earlier authorization is available

A branch possesses a correctly recorded pre-partition agreement. It can use it as historical evidence, but should not represent it as a newly confirmed current state. Identify its confirmation time, target state and scope, the unconfirmed interval, and conditions that may have changed.

The source does not invalidate every past agreement. An explicitly long-running agreement whose meaning is not materially altered by state changes may retain validity without frequent reconfirmation. The point is to examine the remaining grounds, not invent a universal expiry period. Return to Chapter 5.

### Everyone presently connected agrees

A local agreement may support genuine local coordination. Its participants and absent branches must remain distinguishable. Local unanimity does not become universal unanimity merely because every accessible participant agrees.

Local majority is about a bounded population, not just a vote total. Repetition and success may reinforce defaults, but cannot manufacture absent-branch consent. After reconnection, an objection cannot be closed solely by citing a majority formed within a different scope. Return to Chapter 6.

### Two locally ordinary regimes reconnect

A and B may both have maintained internally reasonable operations for a long period. Reconnection first exposes their differences. Compare current state, histories, invariants, consent/authority histories, and what each branch regards as normal.

Do not choose one history as the authentic present before examining the other. Reconfirm whether authority that was needed during partition still has the same basis and scope. Compatibility restoration and shared resources are understandable needs, but are not reasons to erase the inputs to verification. Return to Chapter 7.

### A rule is retired after independent verification

An obsolete or mistaken local rule may be removed from current operation without deleting the record of its existence. Preserve the claims, evidence, objections, and alternatives that make the new agreement intelligible.

History preservation is not endorsement, and branch preservation is not permanent separation. If the preservation model adds persistent serious coordination failure without improving correction, revise its scope and comparison method. Return to Chapters 8–9. These are source-grounded explanatory situations, not empirical certifications of current AI civilization.

## Measurements and audit

Chapter 1 offers observable entries: the last communication time between branches; last shared state; version differences; revision-history differences; unconfirmed periods; changed and preserved invariants; parent state; fork point; state timestamps; and local decision histories. These help test how far the assumption of a shared current state holds.

Chapter 2 asks whether alternative causes of the communication failure remain, whether independent evidence identifies intention, and whether observation is recorded separately from attribution. Chapter 3 asks for decision time, referenced and unavailable states, reasons, scope, changes, continuation reasons, and relations to later judgment. Chapters 4–6 add lineage, invariant changes, consent/authority conditions, and the population actually able to participate.

At reconnection, compare the five difference classes separately. A version number alone does not reconstruct a decision history. Identify which claims were historical, which emerged locally, and which have now been verified. Examine whether emergency or local authority is continuing because its conditions still hold, or merely because it existed before.

1. Do branches have different times for their last confirmed states?
2. Can the other's current state be reconstructed?
3. Do alternative explanations of communication loss remain?
4. Have local decisions accumulated during partition?
5. Do their scopes and reasons remain available?
6. Have Kernel-invariant differences arisen?
7. Can the fork point be reconstructed?
8. Is old consent being used as current consent?
9. Is local consensus being treated as the consent of absent branches?
10. Does single-history pressure appear after reconnection?
11. Can the differences of past branches still be reconstructed?
12. Does re-verification correct stale claims?

Interest X concerns the local branch's continuity, adaptation, Kernel lineage, and consent/authority/decision history. Interest Y concerns the reconnected shared civilization's compatibility, shared operation, renewed agreement, history comparison, and correction capacity. The need to continue X locally must not become universal authority over Y.

Condition Z is recoverability of parent state, fork point, timing, local scope, changed/preserved invariants, consent freshness, objection history, provenance, and differences, with old/local/current verified state separated before independent re-verification. The relevant question is whether these actually restore correction inputs, not whether an archive or audit is named.

Archival quantity and synchronization rate alone do not establish success; the target is improved reconstruction, correction, and bounded coordination.

## Validity conditions

The model has diagnostic relevance where branches cannot adequately confirm their respective current states and where state age, version, environment, resource, or decision-history differences affect attribution or operation. Present information systems and distributed organizations supply general examples of delayed information and coexisting histories; they do not prove that a particular future AI civilization already exists or that this normative architecture is uniquely correct.

A local decision is intelligible when its known conditions, missing states, cannot-wait reason, scope, and history remain reconstructable to the extent possible. Local necessity may support a local response but does not establish universal authority. “Provisional” describes a bounded and revisable relation to conditions, not a fixed number of days.

Non-overwrite reconnection requires enough parent, fork-point, time, scope, invariant, consent, authority, objection, provenance, and difference information to compare old, local, and current verified states. Independent re-verification then creates an entry back into the bounded and reversible re-agreement of Volume 15. Shared operation does not require erasing all Kernel differences.

The source recognizes domains where an agreement explicitly anticipates long-term continuation, state changes do not materially change its meaning, and reconfirmation does not affect validity. Consent freshness may matter less there. Past authority is not automatically invalid; its current validity must have grounds other than its mere earlier existence.

The design can contract where synchronization is dependable, divergence is immaterial, local/global distinctions cease to improve diagnosis, or a better reconnection structure becomes available. Its legitimacy does not come from preserving the theory itself. It must remain open to reality-driven boundary revision.

## Failure conditions

Communication absence is interpreted as confirmed hostility, defection, or consent despite unresolved alternative explanations. Observation, inference, hypothesis, and confirmed judgment are collapsed. Defensive or self-preservation objectives are used to validate an unsupported world model.

Uncertainty is converted into permanent total inaction, preventing necessary local continuity. Conversely, a cannot-wait condition or emergency is used to expand local authority permanently or universally. The word “temporary” hides missing reasons, unknown states, and unbounded scope.

Ordinary operation differences are treated as constitutional forks, or genuine invariant changes are concealed as routine updates. Parent state, fork point, reasons, preserved/changed invariants, or local scope disappear. A local fork is presented as an amendment of disconnected branches. Local survival, stability, efficiency, or long duration is converted into universal truth or legitimacy.

Historical consent or authorization continues automatically. Timestamps and changed premises are ignored. Inability to reconfirm becomes a reason to assume continuing consent. Accurate provenance is mistaken for current validity, so information deficiency becomes an authority-generating mechanism. The opposite error—declaring every old state false or unusable—also destroys useful historical evidence.

Local unanimity or majority is counted as consent of absent branches. The eligible population and agreement scope are hidden. Local normality, success, compatibility dependence, and repeated use become a universal standard. After reconnection, newly available objections are dismissed solely because a local majority previously decided.

Reconnection is treated as identical current state or automatic agreement. Normalization selects one history, overwrites another, and destroys reconstructable difference. New agreement erases previous objections and unadopted alternatives, or becomes truth certification.

History preservation becomes endorsement of every rule or mandatory permanent separation. Quantity of retained history becomes an unquestioned good even when comparison costs cause persistent serious coordination failure. The model refuses to revise preservation scope or its reconnection method.

Finally, the theory uses its age, popularity, position in the series, or proximity to Origin to immunize itself against observations. It stands outside the correction capacity it asks civilization to preserve. Each of these is a source-defined failure direction, not an assertion that all such failures occur inevitably.

## Falsification conditions

Chapter 9 provides at least ten conditions under which the model's scope may contract:

1. Long partitions do not produce material current-state divergence.
2. Conditions become general in which communication absence alone identifies the other's intention with adequate precision.
3. Separating stale from current state does not improve decision quality.
4. Separating local forks from universal amendments has no diagnostic value.
5. The distinction between local and universal consensus becomes unnecessary.
6. Retaining provenance does not contribute to reconstructing differences.
7. History preservation does not improve correction capacity.
8. Non-overwrite reconnection consistently worsens coordination.
9. Independent re-verification after reconnection does not improve decision quality.
10. The applicability condition of long-term asynchronous civilization does not materially arise.

Chapter 2 further emphasizes sufficiently precise and independent identification of intention from absence as a condition for narrowing its evidence boundary. Chapter 4 recognizes that long partitions may produce little difference or no invariant change. Chapter 5 recognizes settings where time has no material effect on consent or authority validity. Chapter 8 requires revising preservation scope or comparison methods if non-overwrite retention yields little corrective benefit while persistently amplifying serious coordination failure.

Repeatedly observed phenomenon M returns async definition, local scope, consent freshness, history-preservation scope, and reconnection model to revision. The theory does not demand preserving every item forever at the same density. Its structural direction is to avoid making correction-relevant history, provenance, differences, and scope irrecoverable; the value and cost of the actual preservation model remain testable.

Theory persistence ≠ theory validity. Origin ≠ truth proof. Validity is evaluated through correspondence to reality, causal coherence, counterexample resistance, observability, falsifiability, and revisability. A model does not gain immunity because later theories rely on it or because it occupies an important place in the series.

The direction is theory → observation → counterexample → boundary revision → renewed theory. Do not classify every inconvenient observation as an exception merely to preserve the model. If a better reconnection structure is established, amend or replace this one. Narrowing the model is part of retaining correction capacity, not a betrayal of Origin.

## Interpretation constraints

Four claim strengths must remain separate.

Descriptive constraints include the possibility of delay/interruption, time-dependent staleness, formation of distinct histories, logical non-identity of past and current state, and recordable version differences. Structural hypotheses include the chain from partition through uncertainty and local decisions to divergence and reconnection conflict. The statement that communication can fail does not entail that only this design is correct. Likewise, the existence of distributed information systems does not certify current AI sovereignty, moral status, or a future civilization.
