## 15-Second Explanation
Co-creation does not mean that multiple people or AIs do the same thing. It means preserving differences, moving questions and responses back and forth, allowing the other party’s answer to change one’s own premises, and creating meaning or structure that could not arise alone.
## Why It Is Needed
Cooperation and division of labor can improve efficiency, but merely dividing roles does not guarantee new meaning. Calling a state of universal agreement “co-creation” risks erasing minority views and different premises.

With humans and AI, ordering AI to produce an output is tool use; adopting an AI answer uncritically is delegated judgment. Co-creation requires circulation in which AI response changes the human question and human judgment changes the next AI exploration.

Preserving difference does not mean keeping conflict forever. Making differences visible allows us to decide which premises can be adjusted and which non-agreements should remain. A third option or a new problem setting can emerge from there.

## Practical Process
### 1. Separate actors and roles
First confirm who knows what, who decides what, and who holds responsibility. Do not equate human and AI roles.

### 2. Put differences into words
Look not only at opinions but at differences in premises, terms, values, time axes, interests, and usable evidence.

### 3. Offer the difference as a question
Instead of pushing one’s conclusion, formulate a question the other party can answer from another position.

### 4. Update one’s question through response
Do not try only to change the other party; leave room for one’s own premises to change.

### 5. Separate resonance and non-agreement
Record both the connected parts and the differences that remain. Do not overvalue agreement.

### 6. Externalize the new structure
Do not end with conversational feeling; make it a concept, design, text, prototype, or other form a third party can inspect.

### 7. Confirm boundaries
Check that objection, stopping, exit, and responsibility allocation remain possible.

## Examples of Application
Organizations can decompose majority proposals and minority concerns into differences of premise and create a third proposal that handles both. In joint research, do not forcibly unify disciplinary language; find where causal models can connect.

In AI dialogue, do not make AI the final answerer; observe how the human question changed through the output. In education, design so the questions of teacher, AI, and learner change one another.

## Success Criteria
- Differences and roles are explicit.
- One party’s language is not the only standard.
- Questions and responses move back and forth.
- The questioner’s premises can also change.
- Resonance and non-agreement are distinguished.
- A new structure unavailable to one party alone is created.
- Objection, stopping, and exit remain possible.
- Contribution and responsibility can be traced.

## Limitations
Co-creation is not optimal in every setting. In emergencies or where clear responsibility decisions are needed, the decision-maker must be explicit rather than continuing dialogue. Preserving difference must not mean endless debate.

AI is not the same kind of responsible actor as a human. Do not blur who is responsible for adopting AI-generated content or judging external effects.

## Misreading Prevention
Co-creation is neither “getting along” nor “everyone agreeing.” Its core is preserving difference and creating new structure from it. Co-creation with AI is not handing judgment to AI.

The minimum core is preserving difference, changing one another’s questions through call and response, and converting difference into creation.
