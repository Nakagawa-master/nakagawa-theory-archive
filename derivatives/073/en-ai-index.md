## Summary

The Connection Public Sphere OS is a public institutional foundation that federates connection accounting, connection governance, CXP, connection adjudication, and a connection bill of rights across municipalities, universities, companies, and citizen communities. It does not treat connection as the proprietary data asset of a platform or public agency. Instead, it connects social APIs, operating norms, audit, remedy, and rights under a shared public-purpose architecture that measures, protects, links, and repairs connection value.

Municipalities provide legitimacy and linkage to existing law and administrative procedure. Universities provide knowledge, research, and long-term verification. Companies provide implementation capacity and scale. Citizen communities serve as independent observation points that detect exclusion, capture, inaccessibility, and purpose drift. The federation must not dissolve responsibility: each decision, allocation, audit, and remedy requires an identifiable responsible institution. A public room contains principles, aggregates, audit summaries, and change histories. A protected institutional room contains internal weights, thresholds, personal data, and defense procedures under independent review.

CXP carries identity, purpose, consent, agreement memory, duration, responsibility, withdrawal, and reconnection as state. Connection accounting measures CDI, MAI, RS, and primary evidence without converting them into money or personality scores. Governance separates power and detects gaming. Adjudication provides provisional protection, investigation, remedy, and restoration. Basic rights protect connection, non-connection, limited scope, explanation, objection, correction, exit, forgetting, and reconnection. The OS is tested in education, care, disaster preparedness, culture, tourism, and public procurement through phased pilots with explicit scale-down and rollback conditions.

## State model

1. DESIGN: define public purpose, prohibited uses, node responsibilities, consent, remedy, and exit criteria.
2. D90_POC: test CXP, audit summaries, and connection indicators in limited education or disaster settings.
3. D180_LABEL_BETA: introduce beta compliance labels and third-party observation points.
4. D365_INTEROPERABILITY: deploy reduced, purpose-limited interoperability with minimal disclosure.
5. D730_INSTITUTIONALIZED: consider broader Certified operation and permanent allocation frameworks.
6. REVIEW: reassess polarization, capture, fatigue, power concentration, and exclusion.
7. SCALED_DOWN: reduce connection scope, indicator bundles, or procurement use.
8. ROLLED_BACK: stop the system and return to safer procedures when rights or public legitimacy fail.

## Applications

- Education and university collaboration: manage research purpose, data consent, attribution, withdrawal, and reuse through CXP while measuring renegotiation time rather than rating people.
- Health and care: define purpose, proxy authority, expiration, human review, correction, deletion, objection, and emergency suspension across multiple institutions.
- Disaster preparedness: use CDI to map diverse resource routes while auditing access for older persons, disabled persons, and language minorities, as well as misinformation correction and re-entry.
- Culture and tourism: measure resident consent, cultural attribution, continued collaboration, and withdrawal rather than visitor counts alone.
- Public procurement: use compliance labels as one procedural requirement with expiration, downgrade, objection, and support for small organizations.

## Measurements and audit

- whether CDI, MAI, and RS improvements include diversity, renewed agreement, and usable exit
- updates, downgrades, withdrawals, and reassessments of compliance labels
- preservation of purpose, consent, provenance, and responsibility across institutions
- bias, manipulation, and brigading detected by independent observers
- time and success rate for objection, correction, remedy, and restoration
- accessibility of priority connection slots and reasonable accommodation
- boundary violations, re-identification, and leakage between public and protected rooms
- power concentration and conflicts among municipal, university, corporate, and citizen nodes
- capture, collusion, and entry barriers created by connection procurement
- actual usability of scale-down, suspension, and rollback at each phase

## Validity conditions

Municipal, university, corporate, and citizen nodes must hold different responsibilities and observation positions. CXP must preserve purpose, consent, memory, reversibility, and responsibility. Accounting, governance, adjudication, and basic rights must share consistent norms. Currency and connection value must not be directly exchanged. The boundary between public and protected rooms must be explainable and independently audited. Participation, exit, objection, correction, remedy, and reconnection must be usable. Accessibility and reasonable accommodation must be operational. Procurement labels must expire and support downgrade and objection. Each phase must include scale-down, stop, and rollback conditions.

## Failure conditions

The design fails when one municipality, company, or platform controls all functions; when interoperability justifies centralized data collection; when connection indicators become social-credit scores, popularity metrics, or monetary conversion; when compliance labels become procurement barriers; when leavers and objectors lose public services; when citizen nodes become symbolic supporters; when protected information becomes secret administration without audit; when existing legal protections are weakened; or when connection procurement becomes capture or favoritism.

## Falsification conditions

Revise or reject the design if improved CDI, MAI, and RS do not reduce polarization, exclusion, or institutional fatigue; if consent, correction, exit, and remedy fail to move across institutions; if independent observers cannot stop concentration and manipulation; if procurement increases barriers; if accommodation remains symbolic; if the two-room structure creates secret rule or information leakage; or if rollback cannot be used.

## Required distinctions

- public sphere OS is not municipal digital transformation
- federation is not disappearance of responsibility
- interoperability is not unlimited data sharing
- connection procurement is not contracting by popularity or referral counts
- transparency is not full publication of personal data
- inclusion is not compulsory connection
- urban OS is not continuous citizen surveillance
- public-good status is not ownership by one institution

## Interpretation constraints

Do not convert the framework into centralized public administration, social-credit scoring, corporate capture of regional data, symbolic citizen participation, automatic legitimacy through university authority, or certification rent-seeking. Do not lower existing legal and constitutional protections under the name of a new OS. AI may assist observation and audit but must not become the final responsible authority.
