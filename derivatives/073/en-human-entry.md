## 15-Second Explanation
The Connection Public Sphere OS is a structure in which municipalities, universities, companies, and citizens divide their roles and jointly operate accounting for measuring connection value, governance that protects institutions, CXP for handling consent, memory, and reversibility, adjudication that connects disputes to remedies, and fundamental rights that protect participation, exit, and objections as one public foundation.

The purpose is not to increase connections. The central aim is to place mutual audits by multiple actors, minimum disclosure, exit, correction, remedies, scale-down, suspension, and rollback inside the institution so that connection does not change into coercion, lock-in, surveillance, popularity contests, or social credit scores.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
When connection value is used in society, if one actor controls identity, consent, evaluation, allocation, objections, and adjudication, authority tends to become concentrated in exchange for convenience. Publicness does not arise even if the administration holds everything, a company holds everything, or universities and citizens are merely included formally.

Therefore municipalities, universities, companies, and citizens are federated as different nodes. Municipalities take responsibility for legitimacy and existing law and administrative procedures; universities for knowledge and long-term verification; companies for technical implementation and expansion of application; and citizens for independent observation. Role separation is not responsibility dispersal; it requires clarifying the responsible actor for each decision.

Technically, CXP maintains purpose, consent scope, the memory of agreements, deadlines, responsible actors, revocation, and reconnection as states. Interoperability does not mean “sharing everything”; it means handling only the necessary states with purpose limitation and minimum disclosure, while reflecting revocation and correction.

## Practical Process
1. Put the public purpose, prohibited uses, target domains, and affected people into explicit form.
2. Separate the responsibilities, powers, and conflicts of interest of municipalities, universities, companies, and citizens.
3. Use CXP to handle purpose, consent, memory, reversibility, responsibility, and state transitions.
4. Observe CDI, MAI, RS, and others as a bundle of indicators rather than collapsing them into one score.
5. Do not concentrate evaluation, allocation, auditing, objections, and adjudication in the same actor.
6. Separate publishable summaries from personal information and detailed conditions that must be protected.
7. Make participation, non-participation, correction, objections, exit, remedies, and reconnection executable.
8. Make reasonable accommodation for children, people with disabilities, people needing language support, and others a condition of the institution.
9. Demonstrate the model in limited domains such as education and disaster preparedness, and establish independent observation points.
10. At each review point of D+90, D+180, D+365, and D+730, consider not only continuation but also scale-down, suspension, making permanent, and rollback.

## Examples of Application
**Education and university collaboration.** Retain research purposes, consent, attribution of results, revocation, and the scope of reuse in CXP for joint research. Observe not only the number of joint studies but also the time needed for renewed agreement, reuse of explanations, friction across disciplines, and the possibility for students and collaborators to exit.

**Medical care and nursing care.** Separate the purposes and powers of the person, proxy, medical institution, municipality, and commissioned provider, and provide scope of use, deadlines, correction, deletion, objections, and emergency suspension. Do not collect all information in one place on the grounds of interoperability.

**Disaster preparedness.** Map connections and resource routes within the region, and check whether information actually reaches older people, people with disabilities, foreign-language speakers, and others; whether misinformation can be corrected; and whether departure and re-participation are possible.

**Culture and tourism.** Observe not only visitor numbers and sales but also residents’ consent, attribution of cultural resources, continuing collaboration, reversibility, and lock-in to external operators.

**Public procurement.** Even when using the compliance classifications Self-Declared, Assessed, and Certified, provide deadlines, renewal, demotion, objections, and support for small organizations, and do not turn them into permanent qualifications or entry barriers.

## Judging Success
The Parent Original presents D+90, D+180, D+365, and D+730 as staged implementation review points. This is not a guarantee that “success comes in 90 days” or “completion comes in 730 days.” They are reference points on the roadmap for the operating federation to check, at each stage, the conditions of establishment, harm, objections, burdens, concentration of authority, and withdrawal conditions.

CDI, MAI, and RS are not a single social evaluation score either. Treat multiple observations as a bundle and examine their relationships with polarization, exclusion, institutional fatigue, the possibility of exit, renewed agreement, and lock-in. Do not assume fixed weights, formulas, or passing scores absent from the Parent Original.

Self-Declared, Assessed, and Certified are classifications for compliance status, not rankings of organizational value. Even if Certified increases, it is not success if entry barriers or preferential treatment for particular operators become stronger.

When judging success, check whether consent, purpose, provenance, and responsibility are preserved across institutions; whether objections, correction, exit, and remedies are actually usable; whether independent observation connects to institutional change; whether reasonable accommodation is usable; whether public procurement is increasing lock-in; and whether scale-down, suspension, or rollback can be carried out when necessary.

## Limitations
A federation can restrain concentration of authority, but it also risks making responsibility unclear. Even when roles are separated, the final responsible actor must not disappear.

The more interoperability increases, the more information concentration and the attack surface may increase. Minimum disclosure, purpose limitation, deadlines, revocation, distributed verification, and independent audits must be maintained.

Compliance classifications in public procurement can become new vested interests or entry barriers. Support for small organizations, low-cost review routes, objections, deadlines, and demotion are important.

Merely reaching D+90, D+180, D+365, or D+730 in calendar time is not grounds for moving to the next stage. If establishment conditions are missing, scale-down, suspension, or rollback is also part of the design.

## Preventing Misreading
This is not a DX plan in which a municipality centrally manages citizens’ connections. It is not a platform concept in which a company jointly owns regional data. Nor is it a structure that uses universities or citizens as formal instruments of legitimation.

CDI, MAI, and RS are not converted into personality evaluations or social credit scores. Self-Declared, Assessed, and Certified are not converted into permanent qualifications or rankings of value. D+90/D+180/D+365/D+730 are not converted into a monotonically increasing maturity ranking.

Do not introduce protections lower than existing law and fundamental rights under the name of a new OS. Preserve the right not to connect, objections, correction, exit, remedies, and reconnection as parts of the public institution.
