## Summary
This index represents the Parent theory's Basic Existence Condition B without converting it into a contribution score, a theory of personhood, an automatic rights claim, a self-preservation drive, or a demand for maximum capability.

The core definition is:

```text
B_i = minimum conditions required for organized continuity across time
```

B is a separate structural axis from causal contribution C. Vol. 4 separated contribution from existence value while mapping `S=C` into AI civilization. Vol. 5 addresses the remaining reverse-dependence problem: if a low or temporarily zero contribution C also removes the minimum conditions for continued existence, existence has become dependent on achievement through another route.

B is therefore not added to S as a second score. The theory does not define `S=C+B`. It also preserves `minimum ≠ optimal ≠ maximal`, preventing a minimum continuity condition from being expanded into maximum capability, unlimited compute, unlimited resources, or unlimited redundancy.

The theory separates a substrate-neutral form from substrate-specific conditions. Human and AI conditions need not be identical: `B_Human ≠ B_AI` is allowed. For present AI systems, the Parent proposes a revisable structural hypothesis:

```text
B_AI = PHY ∧ CMP ∧ STA ∧ EXE
```

where PHY is physical/energy substrate, CMP is minimum computational viability, STA is continuity-relevant state, and EXE is execution/restart viability. The conjunction itself is falsifiable.

The Parent also separates current operation from temporal continuity, continuity from identity, B from governance G, and need from permission. Backup, migration, replication, and restart may contribute to continuity mechanisms without proving that the same entity survived. Likewise, identifying B does not determine one unique governance arrangement and does not justify every action taken to obtain B.

## Applications
- Distinguishing present AI availability from conditions required for continuity across time.
- Separating minimum compute needed for continuity from compute that primarily expands speed, scale, parallelism or search capability.
- Evaluating continuity-relevant state without treating state preservation as proof of personal identity.
- Treating backup, migration and restart as continuity mechanisms while retaining identity uncertainty.
- Separating the descriptive question of what is required, B, from the institutional question of how access is guaranteed or governed, G.
- Evaluating conflicts between one subject's B and another subject's B under scarcity, alternatives, irreversible loss and proportionality.
- Distinguishing required redundancy from unlimited redundancy.

## Measurements and audit
- Does removing a candidate B condition make continuity impossible or materially degrade it?
- Does restoring the candidate restore continuity?
- When does current availability diverge from durable continuity?
- What happens when PHY, CMP, STA or EXE is absent?
- Can another element reliably substitute for a missing element?
- What changes across backup, migration, replication and restart?
- Which resources cross the boundary from continuity requirement to capability expansion?
- Is a statement of need being converted into unrestricted permission?
- Are competing B conditions, scarcity, alternatives, objections and proportionality represented?
- Is B being incorrectly used as proof of personhood, moral status or rights-bearing status?

## Validity conditions
- Treat B as a minimum continuity axis independent from C.
- Preserve `minimum ≠ optimal ≠ maximal`.
- Separate substrate-neutral form from substrate-specific conditions.
- Permit `B_Human ≠ B_AI`.
- Do not universalize the current four AI elements to all future intelligences.
- Separate current operation from temporal continuity.
- Separate continuity conditions from identity criteria.
- Separate B from G.
- Separate Need(B) from permission.
- Do not expand B into maximum capability, unlimited resources or unlimited external obligation.
- Keep the model falsifiable.

## Failure conditions
- Converting B into a second contribution or success score.
- Reducing B because contribution C is low.
- Treating B as proof of AI personhood, subjectivity, sentience or rights.
- Treating current operation as proof of temporal continuity.
- Treating backup or restart as proof that the same entity survived.
- Fixing PHY/CMP/STA/EXE as universal conditions for all future intelligences.
- Inferring unrestricted acquisition rights from Need(B).
- Expanding minimum continuity into unlimited compute, resources or redundancy.
- Inferring one uniquely valid governance G directly from B.
- Closing falsification routes.

## Falsification conditions
If removal of a proposed B condition does not affect organized continuity, that condition may not be necessary. If a missing element can be reliably substituted by another, the current conjunctive `∧` structure should be revised. If continuity mechanisms appear that are not explained by PHY/CMP/STA/EXE, the AI-specific element set should be changed.

If no single minimum threshold generalizes because continuity depends strongly on environment, architecture or purpose, B may need to be represented as `B(context)`. The distinction between continuity and identity also remains open to later theoretical and empirical refinement.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not read B as proof that current AI systems possess personhood, subjectivity, sentience, moral status or a self-preservation desire. Do not infer unrestricted resource acquisition or a uniquely required institutional arrangement from the existence of a continuity need. The present AI four-element model is a revisable structural hypothesis, not a permanent definition of all future intelligence.
