## 15-Second Explanation
Core Point Discovery is a method for finding the unique causal fulcrum that moves the KGI, rather than listing many KPIs. It works backward from the KGI through causality, produces no more than three candidates, applies the question “Can this remain unmet even if that condition is satisfied?”, and tests the remaining one or two candidates with a single factor.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Call counts, meeting counts, proposal counts, engagement rates, and similar figures can be used for observation, but the parent original positions them as Sub Metrics rather than the original KPIs. Even when activity volume rises, the numbers alone move if the causality connected to the KGI has been missed.

The core point is not consolidated into one by preference from the start. First, “observe the field before counting”: observe time to agreement, self-running index, and the quality of resistance/silence. Then make the KGI concrete, break down causality backward, and narrow candidates to no more than three.

Every candidate must face a counterexample: “Can the KGI still remain unmet even when this condition is satisfied?” Only the remaining one or two candidates proceed to the next verification. Verification compares prior method A with B’, changing only the core point, and checks whether an effect in the same direction appears for three consecutive cycles.

## Practical Process
**1. Observe the field before counting.** Look at time to agreement, self-running index, and resistance/silence quality; record the subject, point in time, and relationship diagram.

**2. Make the KGI concrete.** Clarify the achieved state rather than using an ambiguous term such as “increase sales.”

**3. Expand causality backward.** From the KGI, break down what must happen for the next condition to be established.

**4. Limit candidates to three.** Keep only points that could move the whole system.

**5. Filter with counterexamples.** Discard candidates under which the KGI can remain unmet even when the candidate is satisfied; narrow to one or two points.

**6. Define the necessary actuation.** Express it as number of actions, pressure, intensity, or as frequency, sequence, or duration.

**7. Verify with a single factor.** Conduct an A/B’ comparison, three-cycle stabilization, freedom test, and steering check.

## Applications
**1. Organization.** Instead of increasing meeting frequency, change only the condition that places a meaning axis at the opening and verify whether time to agreement is shortened.

**2. Education.** Do not track only learning time; examine whether learners articulating their own role causally leads to retention.

**3. Public sphere.** Rather than approval rate, verify whether questions that turn opposing views into a resource lead to voluntary participation and convergence of the issue.

## Success Criteria
When a core-point candidate is satisfied, the causal difference leading to the KGI is reproduced in A/B’.

The effect is confirmed in the same direction for three consecutive cycles, not just once.

Even when acting on the core point, multiple goods remain instead of closing everything into Yes/No, and hidden answers or steering do not increase.

The structure can be reenacted as a structural condition even after a particular person is removed.

## Limitations
The parent original makes the very strong claim that “if the core point is acted on with 100% effectiveness, achievement of the KGI is guaranteed.” At the same time, it provides counterexample filtering, A/B’, three-cycle stabilization, freedom, and steering checks, so core-point identification requires verification.

“100 actions” is an example of quantifying necessary actuation, not a universal necessary count. When it is difficult to express numerically, the original says that quasi-quantitative forms such as frequency, sequence, and duration are acceptable.

## Preventing Misreading
This is not a method saying “choose one and you will always win.” The unique point is retained through counterexamples.

Do not interpret 100% effectiveness as unlimited pressure or commands. The parent original simultaneously requires freedom and non-command.

Do not convert three cycles, three candidates, one or two candidates, six elements, or three questions into scores or success probabilities. Each number is connected to a procedure or composition within the original.
