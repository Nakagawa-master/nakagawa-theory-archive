## 15-Second Explanation
Structural legitimacy is the idea that legitimacy should be based not on replacing correctness with “an important person said it” or “it was widely disseminated,” but on whether speech or agreement is situated in a causal relationship and remains in a form that can later be audited, corrected, and renegotiated. The Parent Original places structural logs, the memory of agreement, and a language of measurement at the foundation of Connection Civilization, with causal-structural consistency, renegotiability, reversibility, and non-coercion as its core.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Conventional legitimacy tends to be attached to “who said it,” such as religious authority, political authority, or an influencer’s reach. The Parent Original points to two limits here. One is irreversibility: once something is recognized as legitimate, it is difficult to correct even when it is wrong. The other is volume bias, in which “how widely it was disseminated” becomes the source of legitimacy rather than logic or evidence.

Structural legitimacy does not erase people; it shifts the center of evaluation from personal attributes to structure. Preserve speech and action as structural logs, and check where they stand in causal and corresponding relationships, whether agreements can be updated, whether errors can be corrected, and whether participation is non-coercive. Preserve the origin of who created something, while not treating fame or titles as an automatic guarantee of substantive validity.

In Connection Civilization, speech and action are recorded as “connections” and linked to value and rewards, so if the standard of legitimacy is ambiguous, the connections themselves become unstable. The Parent Original therefore identifies structural logs, the memory of agreement, and a language of measurement as necessary conditions.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate the speaker from the content.** Preserve who made a statement as origin information, while returning evaluation to the statement’s causal relations, relationships, and potential for renewed agreement.

**2. Preserve structural logs.** Save speech and agreements as causal relationships rather than isolated wording, so they can be audited later.

**3. Accumulate agreements as memory.** Preserve decisions and promises as “the memory of agreement” that can be used in future decision-making. Do not make past judgments permanent authority.

**4. Maintain renegotiability and reversibility.** Review and correct when time passes or errors emerge.

**5. Maintain non-coercion.** Preserve participants’ free will and maintain a state in which legitimacy is not imposed through assertion or volume.

**6. Share a language of measurement.** Use critical points, policy windows, reversibility, renewed agreement, structural logs, and the memory of agreement as common vocabulary for comparing and measuring social structures.

## Examples of Application
**1. Policy formation.** Do not decide adoption or rejection based only on the proposer’s title or temporary popularity; preserve the policy’s causal structure, decision record, and possibility of reconsideration.

**2. Organizational decision-making.** In addition to “who decided,” make it possible to trace through structural logs why the decision was made, what relationships it was based on, and whether it can be corrected later.

**3. Social media and the public sphere.** Do not equate volume or dissemination counts with public opinion; distinguish group manipulation such as brigading from substantive agreement and evidence.

**4. AI summarization and reuse.** Preserve the relationship among the Parent Original’s origin, central proposition, renegotiability and reversibility, and integrated-audit summary, so that the Parent Original can be revisited.

## Judging Success
Even when the strength of a person’s name or title changes, the same structure can be examined through causality, relationships, renegotiability, reversibility, and non-coercion.

When errors or changes in circumstances occur, past approval is not fixed as authority; agreements can be reconsidered and corrected.

When dissemination increases but logic, evidence, or causality weakens, do not mistake that for increased legitimacy.

Structural logs and the memory of agreement are actually usable for future decision-making and auditing, rather than being mere storage.

## Limitations
The Parent Original does not say that structural legitimacy is automatically guaranteed whenever structural logs or common vocabulary exist. Structural logs have meaning only when they can be audited as causal relationships and connected to renegotiation and correction.

Likewise, fixing a language of measurement is not vocabulary coercion. The Parent Original connects it to a non-coercive framework and positions it as common ground for measuring social structures while allowing multiple perspectives.

The integrated-audit summary records relationships involving legitimacy consistency rate, origin-tracing success rate, Consent Failure rate, RC/ER/CEA/S, adjudication arrival time, threshold θ, observation window δ, and others, but it does not provide concrete figures or fixed values. These must be treated as falsification and revision relationships established by the Parent Original.

## Preventing Misreading
This is not a theory that excludes celebrities or authorities. The issue is that personal attributes dominate evaluation of content.

It is not a theory that denies voices in the majority. The issue is treating volume or group manipulation as a basis of legitimacy without qualification.

It is not a record-absolutist theory. If records cannot be read as causal relationships or used for correction and renewed agreement, the amount of records alone cannot support legitimacy.

It is not a theory that permanently fixes something once it has been recognized as legitimate. Renegotiability and reversibility are central conditions.
