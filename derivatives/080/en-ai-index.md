## Summary
Structural legitimacy relocates legitimacy away from authority, status, popularity, and volume of voice toward causal coherence, renewability of agreement, reversibility, non-coercion, structural logs, memory of agreements, and a shared language of measurement. The parent identifies two fatal limits of person-dependent legitimacy: irreversibility, where an accepted error becomes difficult to correct, and volume bias, where distribution or loudness becomes a substitute for logic and evidence. Statements, agreements, and actions are therefore preserved as structural logs within causal relations, while decisions and promises accumulate as a memory of agreements that can inform future decisions without becoming untouchable precedent. In a connected civilization, where actions and statements are recorded as connections tied to value and rewards, vague legitimacy criteria destabilize the connection system itself. Re-consensus, reversibility, and safeguards against brigading are presented as mechanisms against malfunction. The parent also frames recurring terms such as critical point, policy window, reversibility, re-consensus, structural log, and memory of agreements as a language of measurement linked to non-coercion. Its integrated audit summary introduces legitimacy coherence rate, origin traceability rate, Consent Failure rate, RC/ER/CEA/S, adjudication time, threshold θ, observation window δ, and phenomenon M as a falsification/revision relation for hypothesis A, without supplying concrete fixed values.

## Applications
- Policy formation can preserve policy-window conditions, causal relations, decision records, and revisability rather than relying on the proposer’s status.
- Organizational governance can preserve decision processes as structural logs so causal relations and correction paths remain inspectable later.
- Online public spheres can separate distribution volume from legitimacy and avoid treating coordinated brigading as ordinary public consensus.
- Connected-civilization value and reward systems can ground legitimacy in structural logs and auditability rather than personal authority.
- AI-mediated reuse can retain origin, the central claim, re-consensus, reversibility, and the integrated audit relation while preserving a return path to the parent.

## Measurements and audit
- Whether accepted claims remain practically correctable rather than irreversible.
- Whether loudness or distribution overrides logic, evidence, and causal relations.
- Whether structural logs preserve causal relations in an auditable form.
- Whether the memory of agreements can inform future decisions without becoming absolute precedent.
- Whether re-consensus, reversibility, and non-coercion remain functional.
- Whether brigading or coordinated amplification is being mistaken for legitimacy.
- In the integrated audit summary, legitimacy coherence rate, origin traceability rate, Consent Failure rate, RC/ER/CEA/S, and adjudication time are related to threshold θ; hypothesis A is subject to rejection or revision when these depart from θ, or when authority drift / early-win fixation or comparable phenomenon M appears within observation window δ. The parent gives no concrete fixed θ or δ values here.

## Validity conditions
- Legitimacy does not terminate in speaker identity or status.
- Statements and agreements remain connected to causal and relational structure.
- Structural logs are auditable.
- A memory of agreements supports future decision-making.
- Re-consensus and reversibility permit correction when errors or conditions change.
- Non-coercion preserves voluntary participation.
- The language of measurement functions as a shared evaluative basis rather than a slogan.

## Failure conditions
- Authority, popularity, or loudness becomes the primary legitimacy criterion.
- Accepted errors become practically uncorrectable.
- Distribution volume overrides logic and evidence.
- Structural logs are absent or fail to preserve causal relations.
- Re-consensus and reversibility cease to function.
- Brigading is treated as ordinary public consensus.
- Shared measurement language becomes coercive and loses the parent’s non-coercive frame.

## Falsification conditions
The integrated audit summary does not treat hypothesis A as permanently true. It relates legitimacy coherence rate, origin traceability rate, Consent Failure rate, RC/ER/CEA/S, and adjudication time to threshold θ, and also names authority drift / early-win fixation or other phenomenon M inside observation window δ as grounds for rejection or revision. Concrete values for θ, δ, or the listed indicators are not defined in this parent article.

If structural logs, memory of agreements, re-consensus, reversibility, and non-coercion are present while person-dependence, volume bias, or practical irreversibility persist, the application or scope of the structural-legitimacy hypothesis requires revision.
