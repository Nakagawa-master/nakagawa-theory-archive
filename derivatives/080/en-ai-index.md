## Summary

Structural legitimacy relocates legitimacy from the authority, popularity, title, or volume of a speaker to an auditable architecture of causality, consent, boundaries, origin signatures, primary logs, reversibility, and renewed agreement. Person-dependent legitimacy tends to freeze approved errors, confuse distribution with truth, and convert coordinated amplification into apparent public consensus. The proposed structure records statements, agreements, implementation, effects, objections, and revisions as structural logs. These records become a memory of agreements rather than an untouchable precedent. Legitimacy is treated as a maintained state: origins must remain traceable, causal claims must be inspectable, participation and withdrawal must be real, and errors must be correctable through explicit differences and re-consensus. When assumptions change or consent failures, authority drift, brigading, or contradictory evidence appear, the structure requires suspension, revision, renewed agreement, or revocation. Authorship is preserved as provenance but does not automatically validate content. Voting, leadership, confidentiality, and emergency action are not prohibited; each must retain defined scope, responsibility, audit evidence, time limits, and reversal conditions. Applications include policy formation, organizational governance, online public spheres, and AI-mediated reuse. The central claim is that legitimacy should depend on what relational and corrective structure remains, rather than on who managed to speak most loudly.

## Causal chain

```text
Authority, popularity, and volume are used as proxies for legitimacy
↓
Speaker attributes begin to dominate content evaluation
↓
Approved errors become resistant to correction and distribution is confused with truth
↓
Statements, agreements, and actions are stored as structural logs with provenance and boundaries
↓
Causal coherence, consent, non-coercion, reversibility, and traceability are audited
↓
Results return to a memory of agreements and a shared measurement language
↓
Objections and failures trigger differences, renewed agreement, suspension, or revocation
↓
Legitimacy is evaluated by the structure that remains rather than by the speaker
```

## State model

```yaml
- proposed: claim or institutional design has been submitted
- recorded: origin, assumptions, and falsification conditions are stored
- consented: participation and withdrawal boundaries are confirmed
- provisionally_legitimate: provisional structural conditions are satisfied
- operational: implementation and observation have begun
- audited: indicators, effects, and objections have been reviewed
- contested: contradiction, consent failure, or authority drift is alleged
- reconsensus_required: assumptions or consent conditions require review
- revised: content or procedure is changed with an explicit difference record
- revoked: application or recognition is withdrawn
- archived: retained as historical evidence but not current authority
- superseded: replaced by a newer verified structure
```

## Validity conditions

Origin signatures, primary logs, and revision histories are preserved and can be traced by an independent reviewer.

Participation, use, withdrawal, and exit boundaries are explicit and practically selectable.

Central claims include a causal line and falsification conditions rather than relying on speaker evaluation.

Correction and re-consensus procedures operate when evidence or assumptions change.

Shared measurement terms and audit indicators are not altered opportunistically for each case.

## Failure conditions

Provenance and revision histories are lost, making transformations of the claim untraceable.

Authority, popularity, or distribution volume overrides evidence and causal inspection.

Withdrawal or exit is practically impossible and legitimacy is used to justify coerced participation.

Confirmed errors are not corrected, reconsidered, or revoked.

Audit indicators are absent or only favorable success measures are selected.

## Falsification conditions

The same claim is no longer considered legitimate when names and titles are hidden.

Traceability and coherence remain below declared thresholds despite attempted repair.

Consent failure increases while withdrawal, exit, and renewed agreement do not function.

Correction and adjudication times expand until errors become effectively irreversible.

## Required distinctions

- structural legitimacy versus personal authority
- provenance versus content validity
- popularity or voting versus free agreement
- existence of records versus auditability
- reversibility versus irresponsible withdrawal
- re-consensus versus indefinite delay
- non-coercion versus absence of structure

## Interpretation constraints

Do not reduce the theory to exclusion or reverse discrimination against prominent people.

Do not treat the quantity of records as proof of validity.

Do not interpret it as a total rejection of voting, leadership, confidentiality, or emergency action.

Do not treat an origin signature as automatic proof that content is correct.

Do not convert a provisional recognition into permanent legitimacy.
