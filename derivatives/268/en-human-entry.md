## 15-Second Explanation
This is a protective design for responding to acts that damage a transition system without turning it into a mechanism for punishing people or ideas. **It confines the target to limited structural acts such as log tampering, threshold manipulation, metric misuse, and audit obstruction, and handles them briefly, proportionally, reversibly, publicly, with two-stage review and appeals.**

## Why It Is Needed
A strong transition design needs resistance to obstruction, but excessive protection can mutate into social-credit scoring or exclusion of opponents. The Parent fixes NX as “acts only,” a “limited set,” “time-limited,” and “reversible” to avoid both. What must be protected is not institutional authority, but both the transition design’s resistance to hollowing-out by tampering, loopholes, and audit obstruction, and the prevention of that protection itself becoming new irreversible power.

## NX: In Scope and Out of Scope
In scope are limited acts that destroy, distort, misuse, or obstruct structure: log tampering, arbitrary threshold manipulation, using metrics outside their purpose, audit obstruction, and intentionally creating institutional loopholes. Policy criticism, statements of belief, negotiation, satire, withdrawal, and legitimate whistleblowing must not be included in NX. The boundary concerns observable acts and their structural effects, not a person’s attributes, beliefs, or affiliation.

## Practical Process
1. Confirm that the target falls within the limited set of destruction, distortion, misuse, or obstruction.
2. Treat policy criticism, statements of belief, or negotiation as out of scope.
3. Choose the proportional minimum layer from warning → restriction → blocking → isolation.
4. Make the primary judgment through automated audit plus multiple auditors.
5. Accept counterevidence during a cooling window.
6. Make a secondary judgment through a separate audit surface.
7. Diminish or reverse NX through correction, renewed agreement, and recurrence-prevention design.
8. Keep only an action log; do not keep a stigma attached to a person.
9. Audit the auditors’ weights, grounds, and uncertainty as well.

## Why Two-Stage Review and a Cooling Window Are Needed
The protection judgment itself may be wrong. If an irreversible restriction is imposed on the primary judgment alone, NX shifts from institutional protection to power fixation. With counterevidence, a differently composed secondary audit, post-correction decay or reversal, and audit-of-audit, the system becomes not only resistant to obstruction but also able to return from its own misjudgments.

## Application Examples
The Parent gives limited examples including public loophole design, unaudited corporate changes, improper institutional misuse in education, and audit obstruction during AI updates. It is not a theory that generally justifies sanctions against real people or organizations. In application, record the target act, evidence, scope of impact, period, release conditions, and appeal route separately.

## Success Criteria
Success is a state in which misapplication can be reversed, appeals actually succeed, restrictions decay after correction, the system does not become personal evaluation, auditors’ errors can also be rolled back, and obstruction detection improves while renewed agreement and withdrawal function. If only detection rises while appeal success and reversibility fall, it is not success.

## Structural Necessity Expansion
The Parent’s causality entails that the stronger protection becomes, the more the protection side’s **reversibility, accountability, and auditability must also rise at the same time**. Limited target acts, proportionality, time limits, counterevidence, secondary review, decay, and audit-of-audit are not separate add-ons; they mutually constrain one another so NX does not deviate into personal sanctions. In practice, therefore, “what to stop” and “who does what, on what grounds, until when, how it is lifted, and how a misjudgment is reversed” are one design unit.

## Falsification and Revision Conditions
Revise the institutional design if misapplication cannot be reversed; criticism or whistleblowing expands into NX targets; restrictions remain after correction; auditors’ grounds become untraceable; the system turns into personal scoring or a permanent blacklist; or appeals become merely formal.

## Limitations and Public Boundary
Because judging what counts as “hollowing out an institution” is itself a form of power, the limited set, separate audit surface, public abstract, counterevidence, and reversal cannot be removed. Do not directly apply the Parent’s strong future-transition vision as a general sanction rule in present society. The public derivative handles principles, target boundaries, procedural safeguards, and counterevidence conditions; it does not invent unpublished concrete thresholds, individual scores, or internal weights.

## Preventing Misreading
NX ≠ personal punishment; NX ≠ silencing criticism; NX ≠ a permanent blacklist; a cooling window ≠ suppressing counterevidence; audit ≠ infallible authority; reversal ≠ exemption from responsibility. Numerical examples are not transferred unconditionally to other systems.
