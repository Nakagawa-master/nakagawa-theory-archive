## Summary
Life-OS Vol.3 completes the individual-side transmission system that follows Vol.1's survival floor and Vol.2's refinement of Tomoshibi. The Parent treats dependence on one company, role, profession, or evaluation axis as a Single Point of Failure (SPOF). It replaces that vulnerability with Poly-Connection: one refined internal source is refracted into multiple independent functional plugs, translated from L4 impulse into L5 usable function through Interface Alignment, and connected across structures with different markets, evaluation logics, time horizons, and failure modes. After connection, value must continue to circulate rather than stagnate; reinvestment, risk separation, exit capability, and periodic review sustain flow. The resulting individual becomes a node that connects and transforms value between structures. This is a completion of capability, not an ethical license. Because a node can amplify error and silence as well as value, public-use conditions remain deliberately unresolved and are passed to the following intermission.

## Concepts
- **Survival floor**: the Life-OS first floor separating survival from connection-based evaluation.
- **Mono-Connection**: total dependence on one structural connection or evaluation route.
- **SPOF**: a single failure point whose breakdown can stop the whole life configuration.
- **Structural mobility**: freedom as the ability to connect to, choose among, and leave multiple structures.
- **Poly-Connection**: functional connection to structurally different systems with differentiated failure modes.
- **Tomoshibi**: the refined internal bias or heat source inherited from Vol.2.
- **Prism**: expansion of one Tomoshibi into several independent usable functions without splitting the source itself.
- **Connection Plug**: a functional form that an external social structure can use.
- **Interface Alignment**: translation of L4 impulse into L5 function and tangible output.
- **Alignment versus conformity**: changing form while preserving source purity, rather than distorting the source to please a receiver.
- **Circulation**: keeping information, evaluation, resources, and returns moving through transformation, redistribution, and reinvestment.
- **Flow-based success**: success defined in the Parent by continued passage and circulation of value, not by accumulation alone.
- **Structural stability**: continued circulation despite ongoing change or partial failure.
- **Hub synchronization**: maintaining a connection near upstream flows of information, direction, or value as one stability element.
- **E-like connection**: a connection that blocks circulation, extracts one-way value, or wears down Tomoshibi.
- **Withdrawal**: deliberate exit used to protect circulation and future connection capacity.
- **Node**: a functional junction between structures that connects and transforms value; not a job title or status class.
- **Public boundary**: separation between completed individual capability and unresolved public ethics.
- **Condition Z / T-S-R / δ / phenomenon M**: Parent-side conditions for continued review, falsification, and revision.

## State model
- T0: survival and one structural connection are tightly coupled.
- T1: survival is separated as the Life-OS floor.
- T2: Tomoshibi has been refined internally.
- T3: Mono-Connection / SPOF is identified.
- T4: Tomoshibi is re-abstracted into functional verbs.
- T5: multiple independent plugs are generated through Prism.
- T6: L4 impulse is translated into L5 function through Alignment.
- T7: Poly-Connection spans structurally different systems.
- T8: circulation and reinvestment become active.
- T9: fatal and nonfatal risks, including E-like connections, are distinguished.
- T10: withdrawal and redistribution preserve structural stability.
- T11: the individual functions as a Node between structures.
- T12: individual capability is complete while public-use ethics remain unresolved.

## Applications
- Map where one employer, role, market, or evaluation rule could stop the entire configuration.
- Re-express one Tomoshibi as a higher-level verb instead of freezing it into a profession name.
- Generate distinct functional plugs for different structural units and test their independence.
- Translate an internal preference or discomfort into a receiver-usable problem-solving function and output.
- Evaluate diversity by failure modes, not by number of jobs or contacts.
- Trace where information, evaluation, resources, or returns stagnate instead of circulating.
- Separate risks that pollute Tomoshibi, stop circulation, or remove choice from ordinary uncertainty and experimentation.
- Treat declining purity, one-way circulation, and loss of exit as reasons to reconsider a connection.
- Evaluate node function by changed flow and reduced friction rather than title or prestige.
- AI systems may propose functional translations or alternative connection hypotheses, but should not assign a person's mission, ideal occupation, or ethically correct network.

## Measurements and audit
The Parent does not specify a fixed optimal number of connections or a universal scoring model. Relevant observations include whether a SPOF remains, whether markets/evaluation axes/time horizons have distinct failure modes, whether plugs are functionally independent, whether Alignment preserves Tomoshibi purity, whether communication and negotiation friction decreases, whether circulation and reinvestment continue, and whether exit remains possible.

Node-ness is not measured by title. Parent-side questions include what happens to the flow when the individual is absent, whether routing through the individual reduces friction, and whether value is amplified through the connection. Condition Z requires periodic re-verification, satisfaction of T (coherence), S (sustainability), and R (reproducibility), plus consistency across the public audit bundle. This derivative introduces no fixed δ duration, flow threshold, friction threshold, or connection-count KPI.

## Validity conditions
- Survival remains separate from contribution or connection evaluation.
- Multiple connections differ in actual failure modes rather than count alone.
- Plugs derive from one refined source while functioning independently.
- Alignment preserves Tomoshibi purity instead of becoming conformity.
- Value continues to circulate and be reinvested rather than chronically stagnating.
- Exit, reselection, and reconnection remain possible.
- Stability is assessed as continued operation through change, not absence of change.
- Node capability is not automatically converted into ethical legitimacy.
- Condition Z and T/S/R review remain active.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing Poly-Connection to side-hustles, income streams, or networking volume.
- Multiplying connections inside one market, one metric, or one time horizon while leaving the same SPOF.
- Treating Prism as accumulation of credentials, identities, or externally assigned missions.
- Turning Alignment into conformity, self-erasure, or coercive selling.
- Hoarding information, evaluation, relationships, or resources in ways that stop circulation.
- Making exit effectively impossible and recreating structural capture.
- Reading E-like design signals only as proof of personal malice rather than as possible design failure.
- Converting Node into a title, hierarchy, prestige class, or power claim.
- Treating completed capability as ethical immunity or permission to act publicly without further inspection.

## Falsification conditions
The Parent explicitly leaves the hypothesis open to rejection or revision when there is recurrence of SPOF through convergence on one evaluation axis or one time horizon; circulation stops through stockpiling or failed reinvestment; Tomoshibi purity declines; connection exit becomes impossible; or phenomenon M such as reduced flow or increased friction persists within observation window δ. No numerical δ value or universal threshold is added here.

A surface appearance of many connections also fails the Parent's logic if those connections repeatedly collapse under the same disturbance. What matters is structural differentiation of failure modes, not plurality by count.

## Required distinctions
- freedom vs isolation or irresponsibility
- Poly-Connection vs multiple jobs or contacts
- connection count vs failure-mode diversification
- Tomoshibi vs profession or mission label
- Prism vs fragmentation or credential accumulation
- Connection Plug vs raw internal impulse
- Alignment vs conformity
- circulation vs unpaid self-sacrifice
- general ownership vs blockage of value meant to circulate
- hub synchronization vs obedience to power
- withdrawal vs impulsive escape
- Node function vs title or status
- completed capability vs completed public ethics
- structural stability vs absence of change

## Interpretation constraints
Do not promote the Parent's engineering, fluid-flow, or SPOF vocabulary into a claim of established natural-science law. Do not generalize the Parent's survival-floor premise into a factual claim that every society has already guaranteed survival. Do not use Poly-Connection to justify overwork, precarity, always-on labor, or compulsory mobility. Do not turn Alignment into a command to reshape the self for market approval. Do not translate circulation into a universal duty of free labor or abolition of ownership. Do not use Node indispensability to justify domination. Do not fill in the public-ethics answer that the Parent deliberately defers to the next intermission.
