## Summary
Life-OS Vol.3 assumes the survival floor established in Vol.1 and the refined Tomoshibi of Vol.2, then designs the transmission system that carries a person's internal drive into social structures. Its central claim is that Mono-Connection should be diagnosed as a single point of failure (SPOF), one refined Tomoshibi should be expanded through Prism into multiple independent plugs, L4 impulse should be translated through Interface Alignment into L5 usable function, connections should be distributed across heterogeneous markets, evaluation criteria, time horizons, and failure modes, and value should circulate through redistribution and reinvestment. This is presented as the completion of a person's capacity for structural mobility.

Freedom is not isolation or absence of constraint. The parent defines it as the ability to connect to multiple different social structures through different functions while retaining the ability to choose and disconnect those connections. Poly-Connection is therefore not a synonym for having many jobs, income streams, or contacts. It requires dispersion of both destination and function, and especially heterogeneity of failure modes so that one failure does not stop the whole system.

After connection, the focus shifts from the number of connections to flow. Information, evaluation, relationships, resources, and rewards that should circulate become structural blockages when they are held in place. The parent redefines success as a state in which large amounts of value (C and S) pass through the person. Structural stability means not the absence of change, but continued circulation despite ongoing change and partial failure. The final form is the Node: not a title or owner of value, but a functional position that connects and transforms value between structures. Yet capability completion is not ethical completion. A Node can amplify error and silence as well as value, so the article ends with the simultaneous completion of individual capability and incompletion of public ethics.

## Concepts
- **Life-OS survival floor**: the Vol.1 premise separating survival from the evaluation of a single connection.
- **Mono-Connection**: fixed dependence on one company, occupation, role, or structural connection.
- **SPOF**: a design vulnerability in which failure at one point stops the whole system.
- **Structural mobility**: freedom defined as the ability to connect to multiple structures through different functions and to choose or remove those connections.
- **Poly-Connection**: multiple connections in which both destinations and functions, and therefore failure modes, are genuinely distributed.
- **Tomoshibi**: the structural bias that remains after noise removal; one source to be expanded, not multiple missions to be accumulated.
- **Prism**: re-abstracting one Tomoshibi and expanding it into multiple functions usable by different social structures.
- **Plug**: a function or form through which an external structure can use the Tomoshibi.
- **Interface Alignment**: translation from L4 impulse into L5 function and artifact while preserving Tomoshibi purity; not conformity.
- **Circulation / Flow**: keeping value that should move from becoming blocked by transforming, redistributing, and reinvesting it.
- **Flow success**: the parent's redefinition of success as large amounts of value (C and S) passing through the person.
- **Trust**: predictability that the flow will not stop.
- **Structural stability**: continued circulation despite change and partial failure.
- **Hub / upstream structure**: a structure close to the source of information, policy, and value direction.
- **E-like connection**: a connection that stops circulation, extracts value one-way, and wears down Tomoshibi.
- **Withdrawal**: disconnection used to protect Tomoshibi, circulation, and choice.
- **Node**: a functional position connecting and transforming value between multiple structures, not a title or status class.
- **Capability / public boundary**: the boundary between completed individual capability and unresolved public ethical conditions.
- **Condition Z / T-S-R / δ / M**: the maintenance, audit, falsification, and revision conditions stated in the integrated audit summary.

## Applications
- Diagnose places where losing one company, occupation, role, or evaluation axis would stop the whole life structure as SPOFs.
- Keep one Tomoshibi at high abstraction and expand it from verbs into multiple independent plugs instead of fixing it to a job title.
- Observe the receiving structure's purpose, problem, and constraints, then translate L4 impulse into L5 problem-solving function and concrete artifacts.
- Audit heterogeneity of markets, evaluation criteria, time horizons, and failure modes rather than merely counting connections.
- Identify blockages in information, evaluation, relationships, resources, and rewards, and widen circulation through redistribution and reinvestment.
- Include upstream structural connection, risk selection, and periodic review so circulation continues despite change or partial failure.
- Read E-like signs as structural design failure rather than proof of personal malice, and preserve withdrawal when purity declines, circulation becomes one-way, or exit becomes impossible.
- Observe Node function through changes in flow when the person is absent, reductions in friction when value passes through the person, and amplification of value across structures.

## Measurements and audit
- Presence or absence of a SPOF where one connection failure stops the whole system.
- Dispersion of connection destinations and functions, including heterogeneity of markets, evaluation criteria, and outcome time horizons.
- Independence of each plug and traceability to one Tomoshibi.
- Ability to translate L4 impulse into L5 function and tangible artifacts.
- Tomoshibi purity after Interface Alignment.
- Friction observable in explanation length, receiver comprehension, condition negotiation, and natural continuation.
- Blockage, redistribution, reinvestment, and continuity of information, evaluation, relationships, resources, and rewards.
- Whether other circulation continues when one connection stops.
- Presence of at least one connection to an upstream structure close to information, policy, or value direction.
- Separation of fatal risks—Tomoshibi contamination, circulation stoppage, loss of judgment freedom—from nonfatal risks such as delayed results, uncertain evaluation, and trial-and-error.
- E-like connection signs and practical disconnectability.
- Flow change when a Node is absent, friction reduction and value amplification when flow passes through the Node.
- Condition Z, T (coherence), S (sustainability), R (reproducibility), and consistency of the published audit bundle.

## Validity conditions
- Preserve the survival floor as a Life-OS premise and do not merge it with extension-layer connection rewards.
- Diagnose Mono-Connection as a structural SPOF rather than a problem of attitude or effort.
- Treat Poly-Connection as dispersion of destination, function, and failure mode, not as connection count.
- Expand one Tomoshibi through Prism rather than creating multiple externally induced missions.
- Require each plug to stand independently.
- Keep Interface Alignment distinct from conformity and preserve Tomoshibi purity.
- Keep value that should circulate from becoming permanently blocked; maintain redistribution and reinvestment.
- Design structural stability on the assumption that change and partial failure will occur.
- Prioritize avoidance of fatal risks and distinguish them from nonfatal experimentation.
- Observe E-like signs as structural failure and preserve withdrawal when needed.
- Do not convert Node capability into completion of public ethics.
- Maintain revalidation under Condition Z and T/S/R.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing Poly-Connection to numbers of side jobs, income streams, or contacts.
- Adding connections within the same market, evaluation axis, and time horizon while leaving the same SPOF intact.
- Splitting Tomoshibi into multiple missions, qualifications, or skill collections.
- Treating an un-translated “what I want to do” as already usable social value.
- Turning Alignment into conformity or self-erasure and contaminating Tomoshibi.
- Hoarding value that should circulate and stopping reinvestment.
- Equating more connections with structural stability.
- Recasting hub synchronization as obedience to powerful actors.
- Explaining E-like connection only as personal malice.
- Making disconnection practically impossible and recreating capture by one structure.
- Turning Node into a title, status, or justification of power.
- Treating completed capability as ethical absolution or completed public ethics.

## Falsification conditions
The integrated audit summary maintains the hypothesis under Condition Z as long as periodic revalidation, T (coherence), S (sustainability), R (reproducibility), and mutual consistency of the published audit bundle are satisfied.

The stated falsification or revision conditions are recurrence of a SPOF through convergence on one evaluation axis or time horizon; stoppage of circulation through stockpiling or failed reinvestment; contamination or declining purity of Tomoshibi; practical impossibility of disconnection; or phenomena M such as declining flow and increasing friction during observation window δ. If such conditions are observed, hypothesis A is to be rejected or revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Engineering, SPOF, Prism, fluid, friction, and flow terminology in the parent functions as structural design language. It should not be promoted into universal natural-science law or a fixed formula guaranteeing personal outcomes. The statement that survival is fixed as a floor is a Life-OS civilizational premise in the article and should not be substituted for a separate empirical claim that institutional survival guarantees have already been implemented for every person in current society.

Poly-Connection is not a command to maximize labor volume, side jobs, income streams, or contacts. Prism is not the accumulation of qualifications. Alignment is not conformity. Circulation is not compulsory unpaid service, self-sacrifice, or rejection of ownership in general. Hub synchronization is not obedience to powerful actors. Withdrawal is not simply escape. Node is not a status class or authorization to dominate.

Life-OS Vol.3 defines completion conditions for individual capability but does not complete public ethics. Because a Node can amplify error and silence as well as value, the boundary between completed capability and unresolved public use conditions must remain intact.
