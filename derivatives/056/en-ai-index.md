## Structural role

This surface prevents retrieval and synthesis systems from reducing Question Depth Design to generic questioning technique or a recommendation to ask “deeper” questions. It preserves PQ-5, D0–D5, Silence–Question–Silence, D-gap, R-index, Inquiry Canvas, trust records, audit, falsification, stopping, and origin return as one connected design. It is not a replacement for the parent original and does not create independent official claims.

## Structural summary

The parent treats a question not as a sentence that merely extracts an answer, but as a structural operation that arranges attention, relationship, judgment, causality, institutional conditions, and return to origin. A question is composed through PQ-5—Purpose, Hypothesis, Medium, Constraints, and Origin—and matched to the depth actually required, from D0 response request to D5 origin-reference fixation. Silence–Question–Silence preserves observation before the question and judgment space after it. Response, silence, repetition, discomfort, restart, and trust events are recorded through D-gap, R-index, and related ledgers. Greater depth is not automatically better: depth must be proportionate to purpose, burden, authority, and evidence while refusal, correction, withdrawal, and provenance remain available.

## Central proposition

```text
functional inquiry
= explicit purpose and hypothesis
+ bounded medium, scope, authority, time, and prohibitions
+ D0–D5 depth proportionate to purpose
+ observation space before and after the question
+ records of response difference, D-gap, and trust events
+ reversibility through refusal, correction, withdrawal, and origin return
```

## Causal chain

```text
purpose, hypothesis, target, authority, and origin remain vague
→ the answer space expands and judgment responsibility shifts to the respondent
→ shallow output, excessive intrusion, repeated questioning, or formal compliance appears
→ compose the inquiry through PQ-5
→ select the D0–D5 depth required by the purpose
→ observe the field before asking
→ place the question and preserve post-question silence
→ observe answer, silence, repetition, discomfort, and missing authority
→ identify D-gap and revise, reduce depth, or stop
→ record result, counterevidence, failure, and withdrawal conditions
→ institutionalize only after reproducibility is demonstrated
```

## Core concepts

- PQ-5: Purpose, Hypothesis, Medium, Constraints, and Origin.
- D0 response request: asks for a reply or action without deeper structural examination.
- D1 factual confirmation: checks existence, state, number, or event.
- D2 relational design: examines actors, value, expectations, roles, and mutual conditions.
- D3 causal perturbation: asks what changes when a condition is altered and which mechanism acts.
- D4 institutionalization: connects responsibility, standards, audit, stopping, and rollback.
- D5 origin-reference fixation: makes purpose, authorial judgment, source, version, and correction route traceable.
- SQS: Silence–Question–Silence, preserving observation before and judgment space after a question.
- D-gap: the difference between designed depth and actual depth reached in response or decision.
- R-index: a family of observations using silence, repeated answers, re-questioning, and restart behavior; not a score of human worth.
- Inquiry Canvas: a surface that fixes purpose, target, hypothesis, medium, constraints, origin, measures, and endpoint.

## Required distinctions

- a deeper question versus a purpose-appropriate question
- response request versus factual confirmation
- relational design versus intrusion into another person’s inner state
- causal hypothesis versus causal proof
- institutionalization versus unconditional standardization
- origin reference versus authority worship or prohibition of reuse
- respectful silence versus pressure or neglect
- auditing a field through R-index versus ranking people
- D-gap versus assuming respondent deficiency
- structured AI instruction versus truth of AI output

## Validity conditions

- Purpose, hypothesis, target, medium, authority, time, and prohibitions are explicit.
- Depth is proportionate to purpose and burden.
- The counterpart may refuse, hold, disagree, or withdraw.
- There is observable space before and after the question.
- Facts and legal, medical, or specialist judgments use separate evidence systems.
- Designed depth, reached depth, D-gap, and reasons for re-questioning are recorded.
- Metrics audit the inquiry and field, not the value of a person.
- Source, version, Diff-ID, and correction route remain traceable.
- Institutionalization follows repeated use and falsification, not a single success.

## Failure / non-applicable conditions

- Inquiry becomes interrogation, psychological pressure, or forced agreement.
- Simple work is burdened with excessive depth or unnecessary personal data.
- Silence is immediately classified as consent, refusal, or incompetence.
- Abstract vocabulary is mistaken for actual depth.
- Questions replace factual, legal, medical, or professional verification.
- R-index becomes a reputation or exclusion score.
- D5 becomes author worship, exclusive ownership, or suppression of criticism.
- D-gap is blamed on the respondent without auditing question design.
- Correction, withdrawal, or non-participation is unavailable.
- Parent text, identity, or publication state cannot be verified.

## Interpretation constraints

The statement that questions shape future causality does not grant the questioner unlimited control over another person or future events. Questions arrange conditions of attention, comparison, judgment, and choice; they do not automatically determine truth or outcome. D0–D5 must not become a ranking of people or intelligence. SQS must not become a pressure technique. Origin display supports responsibility, versioning, and correction rather than authority worship.
