## Structural summary

Question Depth Design treats a question as an instrument for arranging relationships, causality, institutions, and provenance. A question is composed through PQ-5, matched to a required D0–D5 depth, operated through the Silence–Question–Silence loop, and evaluated through resonance, depth gap, and trust records.

## Core model

```yaml
PQ5:
  Purpose: intended result
  Hypothesis: proposition under examination
  Medium: oral, written, or AI interface
  Constraints: scope, authority, time, prohibitions
  Origin: question owner and canonical source
Depth:
  D0: response request
  D1: factual confirmation
  D2: relational design
  D3: causal perturbation
  D4: institutionalization
  D5: origin-reference fixation
Operation:
  - silence_before
  - question
  - silence_after
Measurements:
  - R_index

```

## Causal chain

```text
specify purpose, hypothesis, medium, constraints, and origin
→ select the depth required by the purpose
→ observe the field before asking
→ place the question
→ preserve time for judgment
→ observe response, silence, repetition, and discomfort
→ revise the mismatch
→ record results and failure conditions
→ institutionalize only when reproducible
```

## Required distinctions

- deeper question vs purpose-appropriate question
- silence vs pressure or neglect
- discomfort signal vs mind-reading
- provenance vs authority worship
- structural inquiry vs factual proof
- institutionalization vs unconditional standardization

## Validity conditions

The purpose and hypothesis are explicit; scope and authority are bounded; refusal and disagreement remain possible; depth is proportionate; evidence and specialist verification remain separate; results and failures are recorded; the source and revision can be traced.

## Failure conditions

Coercive interrogation, strategic silence used as pressure, excessive depth for simple work, scoring human worth through resonance metrics, replacing evidence with questions, treating D5 as ownership absolutism, or interpreting silence as consent.

## Interpretation constraints

The proposition that questions shape future causality does not grant the questioner unlimited control. It describes how questions arrange conditions of attention and decision while preserving agency, falsification, correction, and withdrawal.
