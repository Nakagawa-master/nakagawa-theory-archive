## 15-Second Explanation
In Theory 4, “civilizational sovereignty” is not the sovereignty of a state. It refers to who is substantively creating “before the decision”—what society treats as a problem, which options it creates, how it repairs institutions, at what speed it updates, what it judges to be a crisis, and how it explains itself. Even if humans remain the final approvers, if that upstream moves to AI/LLMs, legal and substantive subjects can separate.

## Why It Is Needed
The binary of “humans have sovereignty because humans are using AI” and “sovereignty transition means AI rules the state” cannot capture an actual change of bearers. Even if meetings, signatures, and responsible persons remain human, if questions, candidates, evaluation frameworks, update tempo, concepts of crisis, and explanatory vocabulary originate from AI, humans may remain decision-makers while ceasing to be generators of the judgment space. Theory 4 makes that change observable.

## Practical Process
1. Fix one target domain. Do not mix medicine, government, education, corporate management, research, crisis management, and so on.
2. Confirm the source of generation for each of the six upstream authorities. Separate problem-setting, option design, institutional revision, update speed, crisis thresholds, and society’s self-description.
3. Record who created the judgment space before the decision, not who gave final approval.
4. Confirm whether humans can independently reconstruct questions, candidates, and evaluation axes outside AI’s presentations.
5. Separate whether AI dependence is local, temporary, and reversible from whether it is continuous and invisible upstream dependence.
6. Do not declare full transition from partial signs; describe only the necessary and sufficient scope as a transition that is leading in some domains.

## Examples of Application
Even if a human policy meeting remains, if agenda-setting comes from AI, comparison proposals are closed within AI’s candidates, impact assessment presupposes AI comparison, and the deliberation cycle is pulled by AI processing speed, the formally human subject and the upstream-forming subject can be audited separately. Conversely, if humans independently create different questions and candidates, change evaluation axes, and can reconstruct an equivalent judgment space without AI, this can be evaluated as limited auxiliary use.

## Success Criteria
For the six upstream authorities, if one can trace not “who made the final decision” but “who made the premises, candidates, and comparison axes,” and distinguish in a falsifiable form an early transition in some domains from auxiliary use, this definition framework is functioning. It is not necessary to predict when full transition will occur.

## Limitations
Theory 4 does not determine institutional branches or desirable policies in post-transition society. Connection-reward society, the exploitation-structure prolongation line, A-system through E-system, and the reverse examination of whether humans have ever truly been sovereign are within the scope of later theories. It also does not generalize signs in some domains to the whole civilization.

## Misreading Prevention
Civilizational sovereignty transition is not synonymous with AI dictatorship, overthrow of the state, exclusion of humans, or immediate collapse. The fact that humans use AI is also not evidence that humans lead upstream. Conversely, even if AI is used extensively, if humans independently retain problem-setting, candidate generation, evaluation, institutional design, and self-description, and dependence is reversible, there is less need to call it sovereignty transition.
