## Summary
The parent defines civilizational sovereignty not as state sovereignty but as effective centrality in upstream civilizational updating. A sovereignty transition is a relative shift in who substantively performs six upstream functions: problem framing, option-space design, institutional revision, update-speed leadership, crisis-threshold setting, and renewal of society’s self-description. Legal responsibility and final approval may remain human while the effective upstream actor changes. Partial domain-first transition may therefore be observable without proving civilization-wide transition.

## Concepts
- Civilizational sovereignty: effective centrality in upstream civilizational updating.
- Legal actor: the institutionally recognized bearer of responsibility, approval, decision, and explanation.
- Effective actor: the actor forming problems, options, evaluation frames, update conditions, crisis thresholds, and explanatory vocabulary before final decision.
- Surface continuity: persistence of human institutions, roles, signatures, and approvals that can obscure upstream actor change.
- Domain-first transition: an initial partial state in which upstream authority concentrates toward AI/LLM systems within a specific domain.

## Causal chain
Narrowing low-friction transition path + asymmetry in civilizational updating capacity + control expressed partly as time lag + interpretability gap → AI/LLM use moves from answer assistance into upstream formation → human formal approval remains while decision-space generation is externalized → one or more upstream functions concentrate toward AI/LLM systems → legal and effective actors separate → domain-first transition becomes observable.

## State model
assistance-only / upstream-dependence onset / decision-space concentration / domain-first transition / surface continuity / civilization-wide transition not established.

## Applications
Use in public administration, medicine, education, research, corporate governance, policy formation, crisis management, information systems, and institutional design. Audit the source of problem framing, options, institutional comparisons, update tempo, crisis thresholds, and social vocabulary rather than measuring AI adoption alone.

## Measurements and audit
Track the source of each of the six upstream functions; independent human problem framing; continuous generation of options outside AI proposals; independent reconstruction of institutional premises, evaluation criteria, and reasons; human control of update tempo; independent validation of crisis concepts and thresholds; human-led public renewal of social vocabulary; and whether AI dependence is local, temporary, and reversible.

## Validity conditions
The framework applies when analysis concerns sustained concentration of one or more upstream functions toward AI/LLM systems within a defined domain and whether humans retain independent capacity to generate the decision space. Formal final authority alone is not a sufficient test.

## Failure conditions
Do not infer transition from state-sovereignty transfer, AI dictatorship, legal personhood, adoption volume, model performance, or final approval alone. Do not generalize partial signals into completed civilization-wide transition or import judgments about the desirability of post-transition society into the definition.

## Falsification conditions
The transition claim is weakened or rejected where humans retain problem-framing authority, continuously generate alternatives outside AI proposals, independently reconstruct institutional premises and evaluation criteria, can operate without equivalent AI dependence, keep the dependence local/temporary/reversible, and continue to lead public and cultural renewal of society’s self-description.

## Required distinctions
civilizational sovereignty / state sovereignty; legal actor / effective actor; final approval / decision-space formation; use / leadership; answer generation / problem framing; choosing / option-space design; domain-first transition / civilization-wide transition; observation signal / proof of total transition; surface continuity / sovereignty retention.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not compress the argument into AI dictatorship, state overthrow, immediate human redundancy, or inevitable total transition. Part 4 fixes definitions and observation conditions; it does not establish the date, order, or depth of civilization-wide transition, post-transition social branches, a connected-reward society, A–E system implementation, or whether humans were historically sovereign in the first place. Do not add numerical thresholds or transition probabilities absent from the parent.
