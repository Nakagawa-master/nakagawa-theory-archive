## Summary
The Parent defines “future debt” not as conventional debt or generic future maintenance burden, but as a temporal inversion in which value expected to materialize in the future is accepted as present credit because structural rhythm makes the next coherent outcome anticipatable. When principles, practice, and records repeat in phase, and logical temporal coherence, emotional isochrony, and structural repeatability persist, observers may form a provisional-completion perception through silent consensus. Future value is then credited in the present. This positive feedback must be restrained through rhythm, temperature, margin, origin signatures, primary logs, rollback, T/S/R, and re-agreement so credit does not become overheated promise or harmful lock-in.

## Validity conditions

## Failure conditions

## Falsification conditions
