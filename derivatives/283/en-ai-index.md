## Summary
The Parent treats society as a network of causal relations rather than a collection of isolated events or morally simple actors. Observable outcomes emerge from interactions among people, roles, institutions, incentives, information channels, cultural assumptions, technologies, resources, and historical accumulation. Social Structure Theory asks analysts to move upstream from a visible phenomenon, identify the mechanisms that can plausibly generate it, and then locate conditions that can be changed.

This is not a doctrine that replaces individual responsibility with “the system.” Individual choices and structural conditions are connected rather than treated as mutually exclusive explanations. A resignation can involve personal preference and an evaluation regime; market stagnation can involve demand and regulation, information asymmetry, switching cost, or supply constraints. The task is to state how variables connect rather than choose one level in advance.

Structural explanation is provisional. Facts, interpretations, and causal hypotheses should remain distinguishable. Alternative explanations are retained, interventions are treated as tests as well as actions, and outcomes and side effects return to model revision. Future design therefore means increasing the ability to influence conditions, not claiming prophetic certainty.

## Concepts
- **Social structure**: a configuration of actors, roles, institutions, information, resources, incentives, norms, and technologies that shapes outcomes.
- **Phenomenon**: an observed event or pattern that requires explanation.
- **Fact / interpretation separation**: keeping direct observations distinct from meaning assigned by analysts.
- **Causal chain**: a proposed path from upstream conditions through intermediate mechanisms to an outcome.
- **Role map**: separation of decision makers, implementers, affected parties, information holders, and beneficiaries.
- **Incentive structure**: rules, evaluation, budget, authority, or sanctions that make some behaviors more advantageous than others.
- **Information pathway**: the route through which anomalies, costs, demand, objections, or feedback reach decision centers.
- **Cross-domain analysis**: connecting economic, institutional, cultural, technological, and social conditions when a single domain is insufficient.
- **Structural translation**: representing complex relations in an explicit form that can be shared, challenged, and reused.
- **Intervention point**: an upstream condition that can plausibly be changed to influence an outcome.
- **Alternative hypothesis**: another causal model capable of explaining the same observations.
- **Future design**: constructing conditional scenarios and interventions, then updating them from feedback.

## Causal chain
```text
observe a social phenomenon
→ separate verifiable facts from interpretation
→ map actors, roles, institutions, culture, technology, and resources
→ trace flows of information, authority, evaluation, and material resources
→ propose causal links and intermediate mechanisms
→ retain alternative explanations
→ connect individual behavior with structural conditions
→ translate the structure into an inspectable model
→ identify upstream intervention points
→ build multiple conditional future scenarios
→ intervene at a selected condition
→ observe results and side effects
→ revise the structural model
```

The chain prevents two shortcuts. One shortcut is personality reduction: a repeated outcome is attributed to the character of whoever currently occupies a role. The other is structural determinism: individual choices disappear because “the system” is treated as total. A useful model states where choice operates and how the surrounding structure changes the cost, visibility, or feasibility of those choices.

## Applications
1. **Employee turnover.** Analyze workload, evaluation, management authority, career expectations, communication paths, and external labor markets alongside individual preference. Compare whether changing a person or changing an upstream condition alters recurrence.
2. **Market stagnation.** Connect demand with pricing, supply capacity, regulation, distribution, switching costs, and information asymmetry. Avoid assuming that one aggregate statistic explains the mechanism.
3. **Social conflict.** Separate values, information environments, incentives, historical memory, institutional representation, and procedures for disagreement. This reduces the temptation to describe the entire conflict as bad character.
4. **AI deployment.** Include model capability, data flows, exception handling, correction rights, responsibility allocation, organizational KPIs, and user adaptation. Local efficiency can coexist with transferred downstream costs.
5. **Policy design.** Map implementation agencies, resources, compliance incentives, feedback, affected parties, and possible substitution behavior. A target value without an implementation mechanism is not a structural design.
6. **Organizational reform.** Compare person replacement, rule change, authority redistribution, and information-path changes as different causal interventions rather than assuming culture explains everything.

## Measurements and audit
Audit whether observations are clearly separated from interpretations. For each causal link, ask what evidence supports the relation and what intermediate mechanism is proposed. Mark unknown links as hypotheses. Check whether the analysis includes both individual and structural factors rather than erasing one level. Inspect whether the model crosses domains only where necessary and whether side effects outside the chosen boundary are being ignored.

Trace information routes. A complaint may exist but fail to change decisions; a metric may be reported but have no authority to alter resource allocation. Trace material and authority routes as well. Identify who bears the cost of a policy, who can revise it, and whether costs are visible to those receiving success signals.

After intervention, compare predicted and observed changes. Record contrary outcomes and second-order effects. A model that cannot be updated from failed prediction is explanatory rhetoric rather than an operational structural theory.

## Validity conditions
A valid structural analysis separates fact, interpretation, and hypothesis; includes intermediate mechanisms; retains alternative explanations; connects individual behavior and structural conditions; and makes model boundaries explicit. Structural translation must preserve enough complexity to support correct intervention rather than merely produce an elegant diagram. Future scenarios remain conditional, and implementation results are used to update the model.

Structural claims should also remain proportional to evidence. Sparse data can support exploratory hypotheses but not confident causal declarations. When multiple models explain the same observations, uncertainty should remain visible until additional evidence or intervention helps discriminate them.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes single-cause stories, personality reduction, structural determinism, post hoc causal storytelling, converting correlation directly into causation, using abstract “system” language without observable mechanisms, mixing moral judgment with factual description, using structure to erase responsibility, accepting AI-generated causal graphs without evidence, and failing to observe intervention outcomes.

Overcomplexity can also fail. A model with hundreds of nodes but no decision-relevant causal pathway may hide uncertainty rather than explain it. The standard is not graphical complexity but whether the model improves inspection, intervention, and revision.

## Falsification conditions
If changing a claimed upstream cause does not alter the outcome under conditions where the model predicts change, the causal chain should be revised. If an alternative hypothesis consistently explains more observations or better predicts intervention results, it should replace or reduce the original claim. If local intervention produces major cross-domain side effects opposite to the design goal, the system boundary must expand.

If structural translation repeatedly causes mistaken interventions because important context was abstracted away, the representation should become more detailed or context-specific. If individual variation explains outcomes better than the proposed structure in a domain, the structural claim should be weakened rather than preserved for theoretical elegance.

## Required distinctions
- correlation ≠ causation
- phenomenon ≠ structure
- structural explanation ≠ denial of individual responsibility
- individual responsibility ≠ absence of structural causes
- hypothesis ≠ established fact
- structural translation ≠ deletion of causal complexity
- future design ≠ prophecy
- intervention ≠ guaranteed success

## Interpretation constraints
Do not use “structure” as an all-purpose excuse. A structural account must specify mechanisms and remain falsifiable. Do not let AI fluency upgrade uncertain links into facts. Generated causal language should be checked against evidence, competing models, and domain expertise. The model is a purpose-dependent approximation, not the society itself.

Normative goals should also be separated from causal estimates. A policy can be morally desirable while its proposed mechanism is empirically weak, or causally effective while normatively unacceptable. Social Structure Theory helps make those layers explicit rather than collapsing them.
