## 15-Second Explanation
This human-oriented summary presents social structure theory — reading causality and designing the future as a method: observe facts, separate interpretation from hypothesis, trace causal structure, identify upstream intervention points, and update the model from results. It preserves responsibility, alternatives, limits, and the possibility that an intervention does not work.

## Why It Is Needed
Surface results cannot be reliably explained by one person, one number, or one forecast. The method keeps facts, roles, institutions, information, resources, assumptions, and values distinct while connecting them in a causal chain.

## Practical Process
1. Record the observed situation and separate fact from evaluation.
2. Identify actors, roles, responsibility, and available evidence.
3. Trace institutions, incentives, information routes, and constraints.
4. State intermediate mechanisms and alternative hypotheses.
5. Choose an upstream intervention and preserve non-fit and non-adoption.
6. Observe results and side effects, then revise the model.

## Examples of Application
It can be used in social problems, forecasting, sales, organizational decisions, education, research, and AI adoption. The common requirement is to avoid premature certainty and to make the path from observation to intervention reconstructible by a third party.

## Success Criteria
- Facts and interpretation are separated.
- Intermediate causal mechanisms are explicit.
- Individual and structural conditions are both handled.
- Alternative explanations remain visible.
- Intervention conditions are identifiable.
- Results and side effects are re-observed.

## Limitations
Social systems and futures are open systems. A structural model is an approximation, not reality itself, and neither a theory nor an AI output automatically verifies facts or determines values.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not turn the method into a personal ranking, a prophecy, a sales manipulation technique, or a transfer of responsibility to AI. Preserve refusal, revision, and withdrawal.
