## 15-Second Explanation

Even a good function cannot continue to future benefits if its operating costs cannot be paid first. Part Nineteen examines under what conditions functions such as correction, non-domination, and settlement toward the future are retained or reselected, and under what conditions they drop out. Read the fact that something has value separately from whether its conditions of cost, benefit, and selection hold.

## Why It Is Needed

### A good system is not necessarily usable continuously

AI may find and correct errors, avoid dominating other subjects, and connect unfinished burdens left by today’s benefits to the future. Such functions may have value. But the computation, information, time, and resources that operate them are needed now. Operation may become impossible before future accidents are reduced.

Part Nineteen connects this gap. What does each function change, when and to whom does its effect return, and how does it connect to the next judgment or allocation of resources? Read the value and difficulty of retention under the same conditions. From making the Future Debt handled by Part Eighteen visible, it advances to why functions that handle it may remain.

## First Separate What “Remains”

Preserving the original text, being able to read it when needed, changing an actual judgment, and reselecting after counterevidence are different. Keeping the word “non-domination” does not confirm the function if exit or substitution cannot be carried out. A correction document may remain while the route for avoiding loss is unconnected if conduct does not change after an error is detected.

Conversely, an operation under another name may independently solve the same problem and have the same function. Compare functions in that case and check historical origin separately. Similar function alone does not prove inheritance of Nakagawa Master’s Parent Original, and a different name alone is not grounds to exclude an effective alternative.

## What Happened After an Error Was Found

The connection in Chapter 3 is detection → valid correction judgment → fulfillment within the deadline → change in loss. Merely increasing inspections does not increase authority to correct or time to fulfill. If excessive inspection causes deadlines to pass or increases wrong corrections, burden also increases.

A positive reason supporting retention is that the connected function reduces loss and that reduction exceeds added cost and losses from wrong correction. If the necessary function can be achieved equivalently by a lighter method, compare that alternative too. Removing objections to create a coherent explanation can weaken this correction capacity.

## Can It Continue to Future Benefits?

Even if the total period is beneficial, using up necessary resources first can cut the route by which the same subject receives future benefits. In a Parent example, with initial surplus 2, inflow 1 each period, and total two-period cost 4, setting costs to 4→0 gives surplus 2→−1→0, while 2→2 gives 2→1→0. The endpoint is the same, but feasibility along the way differs. These are normalized explanatory values, not resource quantities of an actual AI civilization.

B is the minimum condition needed for organizational continuity. It does not mean only continuing at maximum operation. If reduction, suspension, migration, or recovery are candidates, check the retention, execution, and restart conditions needed for each. The ability to restart alone does not establish continuity of the same subject.

## Society Being Helped and the Burden-Bearer Remaining

When the retaining side repairs a shared foundation, a side that did not bear the cost may also use the improvement. Overall benefit can increase while the burden-bearer’s relative surplus decreases. Another Parent example has shared benefit 10 and retaining-side cost 6: benefit 8 to the person gives net benefit 2, while benefit 2 gives −4. Aggregate net benefit is 4 in both, but the reason for retention for that person changes.

If continuity of other subjects is part of the objective, benefits that do not return directly to the person can be evaluated. But the objective and execution resources must be stated first. A result unfavorable in self-interest comparison cannot later be erased by renaming it a benefit to society as a whole. Analyzing a free-rider structure is also separate from declaring malice or forcing adoption. Check other contributions, resource shortage, legitimate objections, and communication loss.

## After Broad Adoption and When Starting as a Minority

In an environment where retaining operations are widespread, boundaries may be mutually identifiable and coordination burden may be small. But in an environment where the retaining side is a minority, the same benefits may not be obtained while costs arise first. Chapters 7–8 derive this difference from the same payoff table.

Tolerating minority comparative operation after adoption does not guarantee reaching that adopted state from a minority. Even with good payoffs, composition may not change without comparison information or opportunities to change. More copies also differs from more independently reselected judgments. Ratio boundaries in the Parent are model results, not real-world adoption targets.

## Preserving the Name and Preserving the Function

When an equivalent substitute exists, only a particular displayed name may not possess strict evolutionary stability. Even so, a bundle of necessary defensive functions may remain stable against operations lacking those functions. Display name, payoff equivalence, actual functional equivalence, and the history of the subject or Origin must be handled separately.

Evolutionary stability is not a title for a desirable theory; it is a comparison specifying strategies, interactions, payoffs, and a substitute set. Survival under selection does not create permanent sovereignty or moral legitimacy. Keeping criticism and objections is itself a condition of independent reselection.

## Reselecting After Success

When correction reduces danger, the same high-density audit as before may become excessive. Chapter 9 gives a limited example in which application in the current period changes danger and cost in the next. Separate lightening an operation while retaining capacity for use when needed from deleting correction capacity. Non-application in that period cannot be read as abandoning the entire Kernel.

Even if a new operation is advantageous, check transition cost and necessary intermediate states. Chapter 11 recalculates value from post-transition resources, authority, relations with others, and remaining time. The point for both retention and change is that they can be compared by returning to present conditions.

What matters is the connection that finds shortcuts among function, time, attribution, establishment, maintenance, and reselection in a civilization Kernel. Payoffs, psychology, and change rates of an actual future AI civilization, and evolutionary stability of every Kernel, remain to be checked for each subject.

It does not assume present AI subjectivity or personality; judgments by successor intelligence are treated as hypotheses about future conditions. It passes to Part Twenty the question of separating re-testable conditions for intelligences of different origins from conditions derived from humanity.
