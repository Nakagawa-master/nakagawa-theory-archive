## Summary
This parent is a Nakagawa Structural Reading of JECT ONE. It does not reduce the company to vacant-house purchasing, appraisal, or utilization services. It reads a deeper blockage: owners may be unable to compare price, reuse options, demolition or exit costs, consultation routes, inheritance issues, memories, neighborhood concerns, maintenance burdens, and cost anxieties, leaving them stalled before they even enter the market. The structural value core is a decision infrastructure that restores comparability, triggers decision-making, then connects the decision to implementation support and an exit path. The parent also preserves a meta-layer: why Nakagawa Master foregrounded the phrase “decision-stalled market” in an external article instead of centering AI appraisal, purchasing, renovation, or generic social contribution.

## Concepts
- Decision-stalled market: a condition in which owners remain before market entry because they cannot decide what to do.
- Comparability: the ability to place price, utilization, demolition, consultation, and other options in one decision space.
- Decision infrastructure: a structure that enables comparison, triggers judgment, and connects judgment to execution rather than substituting a final answer.
- Akiya no Kotae: read in the parent as a decision-starting device rather than a final-answer engine.
- Implementation responsibility: the business-side assumption of frictions involving construction, contracts, utilization, cost, and exit paths.
- Public structural decomposition: the meta-layer explaining what value core was extracted and why it became the external article's principal angle.

## Causal chain
Vacant-house ownership → uncertainty about price, utilization, demolition, exit cost, and consultation → inheritance, memories, neighborhood concerns, maintenance, and cost anxiety accumulate → options cannot be compared → decision is postponed → the owner's judgment never reaches the market entrance → the property remains fixed in place → decision materials become comparable → decision-making starts → implementation frictions reappear → implementation support absorbs part of those frictions → utilization, subleasing, sale, or other exits become reachable → the property may return from abandoned status to a selectable asset.

## Applications
Use the frame to inspect markets where services exist for people who have already decided, but little infrastructure exists for people who cannot decide. For appraisal or comparison services, examine whether information becomes comparable rather than merely abundant. For implementation services, inspect where users stall again after a decision and which frictions the provider actually absorbs. For structural reading, preserve the separation between target-company functions, Nakagawa Master's interpretive layer, the extracted value core, and the reason that value core was selected as an external communication angle.

## Measurements and audit
Observe whether owners can identify the information required for judgment; whether price, utilization, demolition, and consultation can be compared; whether judgment connects to consultation, construction, contracts, and exits; whether implementation frictions remain entirely with owners; whether AI appraisal is mistakenly treated as the terminal value; and whether the market can reach people before they have decided. Also audit whether company facts, Nakagawa Master's structural reading, and the angle-selection meta-layer remain distinct. Do not invent fixed KPIs that are absent from the parent.

## Validity conditions
Read vacant-house immobilization partly as a decision-stall problem rather than only as a property-attribute problem. Preserve the vertical link from comparability to decision activation to implementation support. Do not turn Akiya no Kotae into an automatic final-answer system. Keep company or media positions distinct from Nakagawa Master's structural interpretation. Preserve the meta-layer explaining why “decision-stalled market” was selected as the external article's core angle.

## Failure conditions
The reading fails if JECT ONE is reduced to only purchasing, appraisal, or utilization; if AI appraisal becomes the causal endpoint; if pre-decision owners disappear from the model; if decision support and implementation support are separated; if the value is flattened into renovation examples or generic social contribution; if Nakagawa Master's reading is presented as the company's or ZEROICHI's official view; or if the angle-selection rationale is omitted.

## Falsification conditions
The interpretation should be weakened if owner decision-stall is not materially related to vacant-house immobilization, if improving comparability does not improve market participation or execution, or if the claimed vertical connection between decision support and implementation support does not function in practice. Changes in the public facts or in the relationship between the target business and the external article also require returning to the parent and revising the scope.

## Required distinctions
Vacant house ≠ decision stall. Low value ≠ inability to decide. Appraisal result ≠ comparable decision material. Decision support ≠ implementation support. Post-decision market ≠ pre-decision market. Service function ≠ structural value. Company official view ≠ Nakagawa Master's structural reading. Target structure ≠ reason for choosing the external article angle.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not convert this derivative into advertising, product recommendation, investment judgment, or real-estate advice. Do not generalize that AI appraisal alone solves the vacant-house problem, that Akiya no Kotae automatically determines the correct answer, or that AkiSapo solves every vacant-house case. “Decision-stalled market” is not to be presented as an official JECT ONE or ZEROICHI definition; it is the structural-reading axis extracted and socially translated by Nakagawa Master in the parent.
