## Summary

In AKI-005, Nakagawa Master examines a specific displacement: favorable evaluation of a benevolent motive is used in place of an independent assessment of what an action did, whether harm occurred, its causes, responsibility, and any appropriate repair. The displacement must materially weaken at least one route for hearing objections, examining outcomes, or making a needed correction. The theory does not condemn benevolence, equate a failed intervention with misconduct, or infer an actor's full responsibility from harm alone. This index is an explanatory derivative of the [Japanese original](https://master.ricette.jp/society/nakagawa-master-aki-005-good-intentions-as-exemption/), not an authoritative translation or a report of measured prevalence.

## Concepts

- **Favorable evaluation of intent (A):** Good intentions can reasonably support initial cooperation before results are known. That support does not establish the quality of later outcomes.
- **Judgment substitution (B):** The favorable assessment actually takes the place of a distinct inquiry into outcomes, harm, causation, responsibility, or repair. Considering intent alongside evidence is different.
- **Material weakening (C):** At least one route for substantive objection, outcome verification, or necessary correction is impaired because of the substitution. The core diagnostic structure requires A, B, and C together.
- **Asymmetric objection burden:** An affected person may have to show evidence about the outcome and additionally defend their gratitude, loyalty, or right to object. The second burden is not evidence about whether the outcome occurred.
- **Observation reversal:** Fewer expressed complaints can be mistaken for fewer problems when the cost of speaking has increased. Neither silence nor complaint count alone measures the underlying harm.
- **Repair:** Addressing an existing adverse result and changing the method to avoid recurrence are separate decisions. Responsibility depends on causal role, foreseeability, control, and other conditions.

## Causal chain

A good intention helps an action gain trust. After the action, a question about its result may receive an explanation of the motive instead of an inquiry into the result. A further shift may evaluate the objector's gratitude or character before examining the objection's evidence. These shifts can raise the cost of reporting and reduce the information reaching decision makers. If missing information prevents diagnosis and change, and the same action is chosen again because its intent remains approved, a reinforcing loop may emerge.

Each link is conditional. Moral scrutiny of the speaker is a possible amplification, not an extra universal requirement for the three-condition core. The short-term effect concerns early objections and verification; medium-term repetition requires the action and failure to update to persist; long-term loss of trust, learning, or repair capacity requires additional sustained conditions. Independent records, substantive review, and effective change may break the sequence.

## State model

| Stage | Distinct inquiry | Invalid shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before action | Motive and whether to cooperate | Good intent guarantees results |
| After action | What happened and to whom | Purpose proves outcome |
| Objection | Content and evidence | The speaker's gratitude decides truth |
| Verification | Outcome, causes, alternative explanations | Limited evidence proves goodwill suppressed review |
| Responsibility and repair | Causal role, control, possible correction | Harm proves total blame, or goodwill grants immunity |
| Reassessment | Changed practice and observed result | Fewer complaints or a completed fix proves success |

This is a reading aid, not a numerical score or a prescribed legal process.

## Applications

For a well-meant organizational policy, support measure, or service that receives an objection, trace what was reported, which evidence was examined, and whether the favorable view of intent was actually invoked to bypass the inquiry. Determine whether the objection reached those who can review and change the practice. Compare similar concerns without the favorable intent evaluation where possible; investigate independent constraints such as lack of time, evidence, authority, or resources.

A decision not to change may be warranted after substantive review. An objection can be heard without being adopted. An adverse result may require inquiry without predetermining legal responsibility. The proposed repair route preserves evaluation of goodwill while keeping result verification and later reassessment active.

## Measurements and audit

The original's ten diagnostic questions cover separation of motive and result, review of the objection's substance, diversion to the speaker's moral standing, explanation of outcomes, cause and responsibility, repair, repetition, complaint-count interpretation, and whether a practice can be stopped or changed while its benevolent purpose remains valued. Do not turn these questions into an invented score. Track the objection's route, evidence, verification, decision, correction, and outcome after correction separately. The original does not fix universal numeric threshold θ or observation period δ.

## Validity conditions

Establish A, B, and C with distinct evidence. Show that the favorable intent evaluation *caused* the omitted or weakened independent inquiry rather than merely coexisting with it. Examine counterfactual or comparable cases, and alternative causes. Separate an actor, those who praise the action, those affected by it, and those who can review or change it. Assess responsibility only after considering the result and causal conditions.

## Failure and falsification conditions

High praise, a bad outcome, a rejected objection, or a lack of investigation alone is insufficient. If favorable intent did not displace independent review, objections were substantively examined, results checked, and needed changes made, the core mechanism is unsupported. If scarce information or authority explains the review gap independently, attribution to goodwill is limited. If no repetition or failed learning occurs, the proposed longer-term loop is not established, even if a short-term displacement occurred. Preserve “undetermined” when evidence cannot separate causes.

## Required distinctions

Motive versus outcome; outcome versus cause; harm versus responsibility; receipt of an objection versus substantive consideration versus acceptance; ordinary evidentiary burden versus a demand to justify the speaker's moral standing; reported complaints versus actual adverse outcomes; implementation of a fix versus verified improvement; observed early effect versus conditional future trajectory; descriptive diagnosis versus normative proposal versus a legal ruling. AKI-002 concerns praise replacing compensation or rights, whereas AKI-005 concerns benevolent intent replacing post-action judgment. AKI-013 concerns trust more generally.
