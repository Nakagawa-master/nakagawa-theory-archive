## 15-Second Explanation
Real Estate Market OS Vol.3 is a numerical design that transforms a market centered on appraisals answering “how much can it sell for?” into a market that compares every branch—selling, holding, renting, renovating, rebuilding, and so on—from the present through future events, including differences among individuals, corporations, and other actors, using after-tax proceeds, TCO, required capital, periods, and risks. AI does not set the price; it makes the grounds and branches explainable, while uncertain matters remain uncertain and are connected to specialists.

## Why It Is Needed
In real estate, knowing only the sale price does not produce a rational decision. Once taxes, remaining loan balances, repairs, vacancies, insurance, interest rates, disasters, and regulations are deducted or considered, the amount left in hand and the future burden are different things. Moreover, “sell now,” “hold for several years,” “rent it,” “repair and sell,” and “rebuild” each create a different future structure.

In conventional markets, sellers, buyers, and businesses look at different numbers and premises, making price differences prone to becoming a psychological contest. Vol.3 shares the same branch-simulation board and the same grounds so that price differences can be explained as differences in conditions.

## Practical Process
1. **List the branches.** Define, as needed, selling, holding, renting, renovating, rebuilding, demolition, joint development, gifting, succession, and so on.
2. **Place the time points.** Connect the present, arbitrary years such as three or seven years later, and event points such as inheritance, vacancy, repairs, interest rates, disasters, and regulatory changes.
3. **Distinguish the actors.** Reflect that taxes, exits, and events differ among individuals, asset-management corporations, general corporations, and others.
4. **Convert to after-tax proceeds rather than sale price.** Deduct taxes, costs, remaining debt, holding costs, and investment amounts to see the substantive result of each branch.
5. **Include TCO.** Bundle full-period costs such as insurance, taxes, management, repairs, vacancies, and restoration to original condition.
6. **Use existing price methods as evidence inputs.** Do not replace them with an AI-invented price; add branch information to the grounds of the existing market.
7. **Convert risks into costs and ranges.** Translate disasters, retaining walls, elevation differences, road access, and similar factors into branch-specific construction costs, insurance, selling periods, price differences, and so on.
8. **Do not hide uncertainty.** For an undetermined boundary or missing repair history, do not fill the gap with a low-accuracy coefficient; display that the decision premise is undetermined.
9. **Share the common board.** Let sellers and buyers see the same branches and grounds and verbalize their differences in conditions.
10. **Connect responsibility to specialists.** Link tax, appraisal, boundaries, buildings, rights, finance, and other matters to the responsible specialists.

## Use Cases
**Sell now or hold for five years.** Compare the after-tax proceeds of a current sale with future proceeds over five years, including taxes, insurance, repairs, loans, and value changes.

**Conversion to rental.** Produce after-tax cash flow including vacancies, restoration to original condition, management fees, repairs, and rent decline, not just rental income.

**An old building.** Do not treat its value as zero based only on age; assess it through repair history, equipment renewal, current condition, and unavoidable future costs.

**Terrain and disasters.** Do not end with a label of “danger” for retaining walls, elevation differences, flooding, and similar factors; convert them into required capital, insurance, periods, and exit conditions for each branch.

## Success Criteria
Success is not that “the AI appraisal price was accurate.” It is a state in which the evidence for each branch’s inputs can be traced, sellers and buyers see the same board, price differences can be explained as condition differences, uncertainties are stated, responsibility is connected to specialists, and identical conditions reproduce identical results.

## Limits
- AI estimation is not a substitute for real estate appraisal.
- Do not assert when future events will occur.
- The meaning of risk changes by branch; it is not a uniform score.
- Inheritance is important, but it is not the sole axis for every actor.
- Quantification has limits for neighborhood disputes, psychological defects, individual litigation, and similar matters.
- Fixed θ values and δ periods are not presented as universal values.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read “complete definition” as meaning that AI can perfectly predict the future and prices. What is being completed is the arrangement of the structures, branches, inputs, and responsibilities needed for comparison.

Also, this is not a theory that becoming more correct means adding more numbers. Uncertainties should be shown as uncertainties, and areas that cannot be quantified should have a human responsibility route. The market OS aims not at a high appraisal, but at consensus-building on shared grounds.
