## Summary
Real Estate Market OS Vol.3 defines an Executable Logic that makes “all branches × all timepoints × all subject types” calculable rather than reducing real-estate judgment to one appraisal figure. Each future branch is compared through after-tax cash flow, TCO, required capital, duration, and risk. Existing valuation methods are preserved as source inputs rather than replaced by an opaque AI price. Disaster, terrain, repair, interest-rate, vacancy, and regulatory factors are translated into branch-specific costs and ranges. Seller and buyer share the same decision board so price becomes the result of visible condition differences. AI organizes evidence and calculations but is neither appraiser nor responsible decision subject; unknown and non-quantifiable areas remain explicit and are routed to accountable professionals. The audit principles are Traceability, Safety, and Reproducibility.

## Concepts
- All-Branch Simulation: parallel comparison of sale, holding, leasing, renovation, rebuilding, demolition, joint development, succession, and other branches.
- All Timepoints: connecting the present, arbitrary future years, and event points.
- Event Engine: inserting inheritance, vacancy, major repair, rate changes, disasters, and regulatory changes at selected timepoints.
- All Subject Types: reflecting differences among individuals, asset-management companies, corporations, and other ownership forms.
- After-tax Cash Flow: comparing what remains after tax, fees, debt, and operating costs rather than headline price.
- TCO: total ownership cost across insurance, tax, management, repair, vacancy, restoration, and related items.
- Existing Valuation Methods as Inputs: preserving established valuation approaches as evidence inputs.
- Risk-to-Cost Translation: translating risk into branch-specific capital requirements, operating cost, duration, and exit conditions.
- Probability and Range: representing uncertainty without claiming certainty.
- Shared Decision Board: seller, buyer, and professionals viewing the same basis and branches.
- White-box AI: traceable inputs, assumptions, variables, and calculation rules.
- Responsibility Routing: connecting AI outputs to accountable professionals.
- T/S/R: Traceability / Safety / Reproducibility.

## Applications
- Compare immediate sale with five- or ten-year holding using after-tax proceeds and TCO.
- Model leasing with vacancy, restoration, repairs, rent decline, insurance, and interest costs.
- Include grading, retaining walls, access constraints, and use changes as required capital in renovation or rebuilding branches.
- Translate disaster and terrain information into insurance, repair, rebuilding, sale duration, discount, and financing conditions.
- Compare individual and corporate ownership conditions on the same board.
- Route uncertain boundaries or building conditions to professional confirmation.

## Measurements and audit
- Are all relevant branches defined rather than collapsed into a binary choice?
- Can present, arbitrary future years, and event points be compared?
- Are ownership-subject differences reflected?
- Is comparison based on after-tax CF, TCO, required capital, duration, and risk rather than headline price?
- Are input sources and existing valuation methods traceable?
- Are risks translated into branch-specific costs?
- Is uncertainty represented as ranges and scenarios rather than certainty?
- Can seller and buyer view the same evidence board?
- Are AI-answerable and non-quantifiable areas separated?
- Are unknowns shown as unresolved flags?
- Is there an accountable professional route for each domain?
- Does the same input and rule set reproduce the same result?
- Can negotiation iterations, unexpected-cost rate, post-closing complaint rate, input correction rate, and price-gap width be audited?

## Validity conditions
- Do not make price the objective; center branches and net proceeds.
- Include all branches, timepoints, and subject types.
- Preserve and connect established valuation methods and professional domains.
- Keep inputs, assumptions, variables, and calculation rules traceable.
- Represent uncertainty through scenarios and ranges.
- Do not apply one risk coefficient uniformly across every branch.
- Keep unresolved facts unresolved until investigated.
- Do not make AI the appraiser, decision maker, or responsible party.
- Route responsibility to professionals.
- Maintain a shared decision board.
- Continuously audit T/S/R.

## Failure conditions
- Presenting one appraisal number as a universal answer.
- Allowing AI to claim appraisal authority or responsibility.
- Separating tax, maintenance, repair, vacancy, rates, disaster, or regulation from the decision model.
- Making inheritance the universal axis for all ownership types.
- Ignoring subject-type differences.
- Collapsing risk into one generic danger score.
- Presenting uncertain futures as certain predictions.
- Filling unresolved facts with low-confidence pseudo-values.
- Giving seller and buyer different evidence boards.
- Leaving no accountable person outside the AI output.
- Producing non-reproducible results under identical conditions.

## Falsification conditions
Condition Z audits T=Traceability, S=Safety, and R=Reproducibility. Example indicators include negotiation-round count, unexpected-cost rate, post-closing complaint rate, input-correction rate, and the gap between indicative price and the evidence-based after-tax outcome. If indicators move outside acceptable threshold θ, or phenomena M such as closed inputs or loss of transparency appear during observation window δ, hypothesis A, variable definitions, calculation rules, and responsibility routing must be rejected or revised. The parent does not specify universal fixed θ or δ values.

## Required distinctions
- price ≠ basis
- appraisal figure ≠ after-tax proceeds
- AI estimate ≠ professional real-estate appraisal
- calculability ≠ quantity of formulas
- all branches ≠ sell/hold binary
- all timepoints ≠ extrapolated current price
- inheritance ≠ only event
- individual ownership ≠ all market subjects
- risk display ≠ fear label
- range ≠ certain prediction
- unknown ≠ low-confidence fixed value
- AI explainability ≠ AI responsibility
- shared board ≠ identical conclusion

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not read “complete definition” as perfect prediction of future or price.
- Do not extend AI estimation into legal/professional appraisal authority.
- Do not force non-quantifiable areas into artificial scores.
- Do not reduce disaster or terrain risks to fear messaging or uniform discounts.
- Do not force an inheritance-centered model on all ownership forms.
- Do not reduce after-tax proceeds to pure monetary maximization; duration, risk, responsibility, and conditions remain part of the board.
- Do not treat professional routing as a disclaimer-only mechanism.
