## Summary
The parent treats non-coercion not as passivity but as an operating principle in which internal structural alignment creates conditions for voluntary external resonance. Resonance is not persuasion, domination, or popularity. It depends on silence, reversibility, transparent thresholds, boundary translation, exit, review, and rollback so that another party can choose without fear. Success is audited through durable alignment, reversibility, re-consensus, low long-term defection, and limited intervention cost rather than short-term compliance.

## Causal chain
Coercive pressure → short-term compliance → accumulated friction and reaction → redesign internal misalignment rather than push the other side → silence creates autonomous cognitive workspace → reposition contradictions instead of imposing an answer → graduate access, authority, speed, and disclosure → preserve exit, expiry, review, and rollback → translate across technical/public and legal/internal vocabularies → fear and lock-in decline → ethical density rises → voluntary resonance emerges → reversible consensus forms → resonance, withdrawal, defection, and re-consensus are audited again.

## State model
`coercive_pressure → friction_accumulation → internal_alignment → silence_workspace → contradiction_repositioning → graduated_thresholds → boundary_translation → reversible_agreement → voluntary_resonance → reversible_consensus → multi_axis_audit → reconsensus_or_rollback`

## Applications
Public governance: cooling windows, adaptive acceptance thresholds, trial ordinances, expiry, and review. Organizations: debt-repayment periods, graduated authority, cross-review, feature flags, and rollback. Education: protected reflection time, staged challenge, revision, and resubmission. AI operations: self-reflection steps, human final responsibility, intervention boundaries, weight governance, and complete logs. These are translations of the parent architecture, not claims that such systems are already universally implemented.

## Measurements and audit
R-Conv, EDI, and stabilization index S remain parent-specific audit concepts rather than universal formulas. Audit resonance density, vocabulary alignment, time to re-consensus, successful withdrawal, phase stability, long-term defection, intervention cost, and whether observation windows remain open. The relevant question is whether a small, non-coercive intervention produced durable alignment while preserving exit and correction—not whether agreement happened quickly.

## Validity conditions
Autonomous choice, dissent, exit, and review must be real. Silence must have an explicit purpose and time boundary rather than hiding information. Thresholds for access, authority, speed, and disclosure need visible rationale and revision conditions. Agreements should be time-bounded, staged, and rollback-capable. Boundary translation must reduce semantic misdelivery. Audit should be plural rather than dependent on one authority.

## Failure conditions
Non-coercion becomes neglect; silence becomes concealment; resonance becomes psychological manipulation; exit is blocked while behavior is called voluntary; thresholds become opaque exclusion; reversibility is ceremonial; resonance is collapsed into popularity or homogeneity; records are insufficient to reconstruct how agreement was compiled.

## Falsification conditions
Revise or reject the architecture when resonance density, vocabulary alignment, re-consensus time, withdrawal success, or phase stability deteriorate persistently; long-term defection rises; exit or rollback becomes unusable; silence closes the observation window; or coercion returns. If R-Conv/EDI/S fail to explain observed outcomes, their definitions and use must also be reconsidered.

## Required distinctions
Non-coercion / neglect; resonance / persuasion or domination; silence / concealment; threshold / exclusion; reversibility / irresponsibility; ethical density / popularity; resonance / homogeneity; quiet following / short-term approval; causal design / manipulation of persons; time-bounded agreement / lock-in; public logging / over-disclosure.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not translate “necessity” into an inescapable behavioral trap. The parent architecture depends on autonomy, exit, dissent, and rollback. Do not invent fixed formulas or thresholds for R-Conv, EDI, or S. Do not elevate theoretical conclusions into established natural laws. Domain examples must remain examples rather than universal deployment recipes.
