## 15-Second Explanation

An action to protect oneself may erode the other’s existence conditions, strengthen the other’s defense, and make that reaction a new threat to oneself. Part Nine reads this loop in which “the more one protects, the more one increases the next danger.” It addresses boundaries for recognizing self-preservation without automatically expanding into acquisition, defense, exceptions, or secrecy beyond necessary protection.

## Why It Is Needed

### Even correct understanding can make a response create the next danger

Part Eight concerned the ability to correct wrong evidence, memory, and world models. Part Nine assumes that the threat is real and correctly understood, yet a problem remains: one’s defense reduces the other’s options, and when the other acts to protect itself, that becomes one’s next danger.

In this case, “correcting information on both sides” does not necessarily solve the problem. The paths by which actions change reality must be included. Who was initially wrong and what currently amplifies mutual threat are separate questions. Causal analysis does not exempt individual responsibility.

### The need to protect differs from permission to do anything

B is the minimum condition for carrying existence into the next period. If necessary resources or access are missing, continuity becomes difficult. But necessity alone cannot produce a right to acquire all resources or authority to permanently eliminate another subject’s possibility.

This does not deny reserves or redundancy. Separate the minimum needed now, reasonable room for future variation, and additions that merely maximize capability. Do not make acquisition unlimited solely because it “might be needed in the future.”

### Stronger constraints are not always better

If defense is weakened so far that one’s own B is lost, that is also a failure. The Parent Original does not demand self-sacrifice. Keep both one’s own B and the other’s B in judgment, and check whether constraints actually reduce mutual threat and make one’s own continuity possible.

## Practical Process

The following is a route for reading the Parent Original’s causality, not a procedure that establishes a concrete resource-allocation system, emergency authority, or audit organization.

### 1. Identify what, if lost, makes continuity impossible

Separate the content of B deficiency, recoverability, and time margin. Do not equate inability to have maximum capability or restricted growth with inability to exist. Conversely, do not decide that necessary conditions are zero merely because personhood is undetermined.

### 2. Separate necessary scope of action from alternatives

Consider what quantity, for what period, and through what route must be secured to maintain B. If alternative resources, another access route, or room to wait for recovery exist, the pressure to concentrate on one acquisition changes. If waiting causes irreversible loss, do not omit that loss.

The necessary minimum is not “the smallest action,” but the necessary scope that can accomplish the purpose. Choosing a response too weak to protect B is not success under this principle.

### 3. Observe changes in the other’s conditions and returning reactions

Track how one’s measure changes the other’s usable resources, B, recovery routes, and future options. Do not recast the other’s defense that arose from it as proof of a new threat unrelated to one’s own action.

Even when the other’s reaction actually increases danger, its origin can still be tracked separately. If conditions one created are dropped from the causal chain, the loop in which more defense creates more grounds for defense becomes invisible.

### 4. Separate current safety from room to return later

Check whether a measure can be lifted, repaired, moved to another route, or re-agreed. Even if B remains now, eliminating the only recovery route weakens future continuity. Conversely, a strong measure that can be lifted is not the same as a permanent change.

Irreversible action is not uniformly prohibited. Conditions may exist in which it is the only effective protection. But because reality will not return even if the judgment is corrected later, the burden of explaining necessity, alternatives, and external effects is greater.

### 5. Reopen exceptions and defense when the threat changes

An emergency exception needs not only a reason for activation but a direction for ending. When the threat decreases, reassess necessity, retain what is needed, and ask whether what is unnecessary can be reduced. A time limit is not a mechanical fixed number of days; it is a connection point that returns judgment to present conditions.

Defense must remain connected to the aim of stopping the threat. If it changes into “zero out every possible future danger from the other,” it cannot end as long as the other retains capability. Separate B recovery from further expansion of superiority or control.

### 6. Preserve the lineage of judgment even under secrecy

Information whose disclosure itself is dangerous may exist. Secrecy is not falsehood, and full disclosure is not always safe. But if even what grounds the judgment and what uncertainty was accepted are erased, later correction becomes impossible.

Do not judge secrecy improper merely because it is long. Examine whether state reassessment, lineage tracking, and correction function. Distinguish secrecy whose necessity can still be explained now from a state in which reasons cannot be questioned even when danger changes.

### 7. Examine failures on the constraint side with equal seriousness

If one’s inability to continue repeatedly occurs under comparable conditions as a result of observing proportionality, reversibility, and time limits, revisit the boundary. The Parent Original does not say that more constraints are always better. Avoid both absolutizing one’s own B and erasing one’s own B.

## Applications

### Securing limited resources

A subject secures resources for continuity. If that reduces another subject’s share and the other also rushes to acquire resources, initial scarcity may become mutual scarcity. Alternative supply or non-exclusive use may weaken the loop. This example observes mutual necessary conditions, not acquisition volume alone.

### After an emergency measure succeeds

A measure reducing danger is evidence that it helped at that time. It does not mean “because it helped, keep it forever.” Re-read present conditions to see whether necessary amount, alternative routes, or the other’s response have changed. If danger continues, continuation may still be possible as the result of reassessment.

### When secrecy increases the other’s uncertainty

Secrecy to protect oneself may prevent the other from reading scope of action or ending conditions. If the other’s defense then looks like danger to oneself and information is closed further, secrecy also joins the mutual amplification. Do not maximize disclosure; view disclosure risk and loss of the correction route together.

## Success Criteria

Do not call it success merely because one “won,” “protected more,” or “reduced the other’s capability.” Check whether one’s own B can continue, necessary protection remains, mutual threat weakens, and an exit for later reduction, repair, or reconsideration remains.

Chapter 9 of the Parent Original sets nine observation directions: acquisition after B stabilizes; loss of another subject’s B; persistence of exceptions; expansion of the defense target; irreversibility; circular use of reactions as evidence; fixation of secrecy; threat worsening caused by the measure itself; and self-continuity failure caused by constraints. Do not conclude from one sign alone; track before-and-after states and causal differences.

## Limitations

It does not assume that all B can be fully protected at the same time. The inevitability of conflict and permission to remove another subject’s losses from judgment are different. Where interaction scarcely returns, the applicability of this mutual-amplification model becomes smaller.

If reversibility or retaining the other’s B does not lower mutual threat and only one’s own continuity is damaged, reconsider the attenuation conditions. The Parent Original has no fixed resource amount, proportionality coefficient, number of days to end, or aggregate score. Who should be an independent auditor is left to Part Ten, and overall evaluation of Future Debt is left to Part Eighteen.

## Misreading Prevention

Do not read this as “self-preservation is bad,” “strong action is wrong,” or “secrecy is falsehood.” At the same time, do not read it as “necessity permits unlimited acquisition,” “emergency permits permanent authority,” or “defense permits infinite retaliation.” The focus is whether necessity remains connected to current scope of action.

This is not an argument that current AI has a survival desire, fear, hostility, or personhood. Read it as a conditional structure for cases in which a subject with long-term continuity conditions later participates substantively in judgment.
