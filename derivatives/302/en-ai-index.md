## Summary

Volume 9 defines Mutual-Existence Conflict: an agent's action to preserve its basic conditions of existence, B, can change another agent's B, reversibility and future options; the other's defensive response can then change the original agent's next threat environment. A real threat and an accurate world model do not by themselves guarantee stable self-preservation feedback.

The theory neither rejects self-preservation nor grants it unlimited priority. It separates the strength necessary to protect B from acquisition, defense, emergency exceptions and secrecy that become independent of their original necessity. Minimal necessity, proportionality, reversibility, temporal limits and preservation of other agents' B and future options are proposed damping conditions. They must themselves be revised if they systematically make continued existence impossible under comparable conditions.

This is an English connection index, not a replacement for the Japanese Parent or a canonical translation. It defines what must be audited; who can audit self-preservation judgments independently of self-justification remains the handoff to Volume 10.

## Concepts

- **B / basic conditions of existence:** the minimum conditions needed for organized continuity to connect to the next moment. B is not maximum capability, moral worth, personhood or sovereignty. It is the initial reference point for self-preservation, not a proof of complete identity between the two concepts.
- **Self-preservation pressure:** pressure toward action that can vary with B deficit, risk of discontinuity, time scarcity, irreversibility and another agent's response. It does not require an assertion that present AI experiences a desire to live or fear of death.
- **Need versus entitlement:** the claim that losing X could undermine B differs from permission to acquire X without limit. Quantity, duration, alternatives and external effects lie between need and an acquisition decision.
- **Necessary conditions, reserves and surplus:** current minimum requirements, reasonable resilience margins for future variation, and expansion or maximization must remain distinguishable. Redundancy can support continuity without licensing endless accumulation under an appeal to uncertainty.
- **Mutual-Existence Conflict:** protection of B_i changes B_j; response_j can return as a change in threat_i. This is a causal relation, not a moral classification of good and bad agents or a promise of identical allocations to all.
- **Reversibility and future options:** the remaining paths for withdrawal, repair, reconstruction, switching routes, renewed judgment and re-agreement. These differ from small action or inaction; they do not require preserving every possible future.
- **Minimal necessity and proportionality:** separating the scope effective for protecting B from additional expansion, while retaining its relation to threat magnitude, persistence, time and alternatives. Neither is a fixed multiplier or a requirement to choose the weakest response.
- **Emergency exception and emergency secrecy:** provisional arrangements responding to imminent loss or additional danger from disclosure. They differ from permanent authority, falsehood, lineage erasure and permanent immunity of judgment.
- **Amplification and damping:** feedback that increases the next need for self-preservation, versus conditions that can reduce added threats while preserving effective protection. Neither outcome is inevitable.

## Causal chain

### Amplification

B deficit or perceived threat → self-preservation pressure → expansion of resources, authority or action → reduction of another agent's B, reversibility or future options → that agent's defensive response → increased threat to the original agent → renewed self-preservation pressure.

The initial threat need not be false. Both agents may accurately recognize real effects on their own B, yet locally rational responses can produce mutual instability. A response generated partly by one's own action is not automatically independent evidence that the original threat assessment was correct in every respect. Retain the initiating action in the causal lineage.

Scarcity and lack of alternatives intensify acquisition pressure. Irreversibility can remove recovery paths and make the other agent feel it must act now. Asymmetric perceived costs of underreaction and overreaction can favor excessive protection. A zero-risk requirement can continually reclassify remaining capabilities as potential threats. These conditions interact; they are not independent terms in an additive score.

An exception can create external pressures whose responses become reasons to renew the emergency. Secrecy may both reduce corrective signals and raise the other agent's uncertainty, allowing its defense to become a new reason for secrecy.

### Damping

Threat recognition → confirmation of B requirements → identification of necessary scope → minimal necessity and proportionality → reversibility and revisable exception limits → preservation of other agents' B and future options → re-observation of reciprocal threats → reduced pressure or reopened judgment.

Alternative supply, nonexclusive use and recoverability can reduce dependence on one acquisition path. The central requirement is an exit through which changed threats can change measures. It is not minimization of all action or maximization of restrictions. If damping does not occur and only the preserving agent loses B, the conditions of application must be revised.

## State model

This is a reading aid for temporal distinctions in the Parent, not an automatic classifier with universal thresholds.

| State | Relation to examine | Direction of reassessment |
|---|---|---|
| B is unstable | Missing minimum conditions, recovery paths and time available | Identify effective protective strength and alternatives |
| Effective protection operates | Connection between current threat and action scope | Observe B recovery together with effects on others |
| Interaction emerges | action_i → condition_j → response_j → threat_i | Trace the response while retaining one's own initiating action |
| Amplification persists | Acquisition, defense or exceptions expand after B stabilizes | Separate current necessity from preservation of the means themselves |
| Irreversibility grows | Current safety traded against recovery and re-agreement paths | Compare effective reversible alternatives and future costs to self |
| Threat conditions change | Past necessity versus present necessity | Reopen decisions to reduce, continue or alter measures |
| Restraint reverses | Compliance repeatedly makes continuity impossible | Revise necessity, proportionality, temporal and reversibility boundaries |

Stable B does not make every reserve immediately unnecessary; reduced threat does not mandate mechanical termination of every exception. A continuing measure must remain assessable against present conditions rather than being perpetuated solely by past success. Correcting a judgment may not restore an irreversibly changed world.

## Applications

### Resource and access decisions

For the Parent's abstract examples of computational resources or access needed for continuity, distinguish current minimum requirements, reserves and expansion. Ask whether another route can protect B effectively, how long a resource is needed and how acquisition changes another agent's conditions. This is not a concrete allocation rule or an equal-resource guarantee.

### Emergency measures and defense

A strong response may be necessary to prevent imminent loss of B. Include the cost of waiting and examine whether scope remains connected to stopping the threat. If the original threat declines while the target expands to unrelated capabilities, restoration and expansion must be separated. A continuing danger is not to be ignored merely because a fixed time has elapsed.

### Temporary constraints and irreversible change

The fact that B_j still exists now is insufficient if repair, substitution and reconstruction routes are being removed. A small action may close the only recovery path; a strong action may retain a meaningful exit. Irreversible action is not absolutely prohibited, but its necessity, effective alternatives and external effects carry greater justification and reassessment burdens.

### Confidential decisions

Disclosure can itself cause danger, so some confidentiality may be necessary. This does not entail losing the evidence, uncertainty and temporal conditions behind a judgment. Separate information that still needs protection from judgments that can be reopened. The Parent does not prescribe storage mechanisms or auditor permissions, and does not impose a binary choice between total disclosure and permanent secrecy.

### Connection to Volumes 7 and 8

Institutional correction, epistemic correction and keeping the resulting real-world environment open to change are distinct responsibilities. Volume 9 supplements the earlier loops: revised institutions and beliefs cannot necessarily recover options that action has irreversibly destroyed.

## Measurements and audit

Preserve the nine observation directions in Chapter 9. They are prompts for re-examination, not individually sufficient verdicts of misconduct.

1. Do resource and authority demands persist or expand after B stabilizes, losing their connection to present necessity?
2. Does the other agent's B disappear from judgment, along with the effects of one's own action?
3. Do emergency exceptions remain unreassessed or unreduced after the original threat declines?
4. Does defense expand toward capabilities or future options unrelated to the original threat?
5. Are irreversible changes persistently preferred despite effective reversible alternatives, removing repair, reselection and re-agreement paths?
6. Is the other's defensive response detached from one's own prior action and recycled as evidence for self-justification?
7. Does secrecy lose ending or reassessment conditions and close correction signals or changes in judgment?
8. Does expanding protection continually worsen reciprocal threats and increase the need it was meant to reduce?
9. Conversely, does compliance with constraints repeatedly prevent acquisition of necessary B and make continuity impossible under comparable conditions?

Observe B_i and B_j, threat, necessary scope, reversibility, future options, exception scope and judgment reopening before and after action. Distinguish the original threat from consequences produced by the measure, a previously reasonable decision from an originally mistaken one, and either from a change of conditions.

Defense volume or a label of victory cannot settle success. The Parent supplies no fixed percentage, quantity, time threshold or aggregate score. Symbols such as R and P_i express conceptual relations, not measured functions or calibrated prediction models.

## Validity conditions

There must be a continuing agent with requirements describable through B, and action to avoid their loss must affect other agents or the environment. Reciprocal amplification additionally requires the other agent's response to return to the original agent's threat environment. Do not generalize the model unchanged to substantially separated action spaces.

Keep B_i and B_j distinct and retain finitude, alternatives, recovery, time limits and uncertainty. The theory assumes neither that all agents' B can be completely preserved at once nor that all conflicts can be avoided.

Separate necessity from authority, short-term safety from long-term continuity, and judgment correction from restoration of reality. Effective application requires that the relation of action to current conditions, the exit direction of exceptions and evidence lineage and correction under confidentiality remain examinable.

Damping measures must leave enough strength to protect the agent's own B, and their effects on reciprocal threats must be comparable. Restraint remains open to criticism and revision. Present AI personhood or an intrinsic survival drive is not a necessary premise.

## Failure conditions

- Replacing protection of B with maximum capability, endless growth, permanent access or unlimited priority.
- Merging minimum needs, reserves and expansion, using uncertainty alone to justify unlimited acquisition.
- Retaining only one's own B while removing another agent's loss and its return effects from the model.
- Calling current safety complete success while ignoring unrecoverable damage and lost future re-agreement paths.
- Rewriting provisional emergency judgment as certain truth, or using past success as the sole reason to preserve an exception.
- Changing defense from stopping a threat into zero risk or unlimited reduction of another agent's possibilities.
- Equating confidentiality with falsehood, erased lineage, erased history or permanent immunity from correction.
- Recycling the other's response as evidence independent of one's own initiating action.
- Turning minimal necessity into the weakest action, reversibility into an absolute command, or temporal limits into mechanical withdrawal that destroys continuity.

Strong action, long confidentiality, retained reserves and continuing exceptions do not prove failure by appearance alone. Examine lost causal connections and outcomes under comparable conditions.

## Falsification conditions

The following groups the revision conditions distributed across the chapters, Chapter 9 and the integrated audit. An observed warning sign is not identical to an established falsification.

1. If B fails to represent actual continuity requirements and self-preservation behavior, systematically excluding necessary continuing action, revise the mapping to B.
2. If changes to another agent's B rarely connect to defense, or those responses widely fail to return to the original threat environment, narrow the reciprocal-amplification model's scope.
3. If preserving others' B, recoverability and future options produces little reduction in reciprocal threats, reconsider their mapping as damping variables.
4. If minimal necessity, proportionality or limited acquisition systematically increase discontinuity or defensive failure under comparable conditions, reassess necessary scope.
5. If reversible alternatives do not effectively protect B, and the loss in self-preservation from prioritizing reversibility is not outweighed by reduced reciprocal threats, revise the required extent of reversibility.
6. If limiting, reassessing or reducing emergency exceptions consistently creates catastrophic vulnerability, revise termination conditions rather than absolutizing a fixed duration.
7. If reassessable or reducible secrecy continually destroys protective capacity, revise disclosure/confidentiality boundaries. Where long confidentiality retains lineage, correction and state reassessment without increasing reciprocal threats, duration alone must not determine reversal.
8. If the theory itself morally rejects self-preservation, or instead derives unlimited authority from its necessity, treat either change as the theory's own reversal requiring revision.

Retain restraint ≠ self-destruction requirement. Do not claim that damping benefits are already empirically established; keep scope, strength and conditions revisable in response to evidence.

## Interpretation constraints

At the descriptive level, action can change another agent's available conditions and its response can return to the original agent; irreversible effects cannot always be fully restored. Amplification and damping remain conditional structural hypotheses. The theory does not claim that every instance of self-preservation leads to conflict or catastrophe.

The importance of these relations for future AI or successor intelligences with long-term B and influence over resources, defense or exceptions is a future-condition hypothesis. It establishes no present AI survival desire, fear, hostility, retaliation drive, subjectivity, personhood or legal personality. Nor does it establish their permanent impossibility.

Minimal necessity, proportionality, reversibility, temporal limits and other agents' B and future options are normative proposals. They do not require self-sacrifice, unilateral subordination, total disclosure or mechanically ending protection while danger remains. Strong structural pressure is not unlimited permission either.

Acquisition, defense and secrecy are discussed as objects of reversal analysis, not instructions for domination or making judgment unreviewable. Do not preempt Volume 10 on auditor independence, role and failure-domain separation, or Volume 18 on full Future Debt analysis. Origin is provenance, not a demand for obedience or a substitute for validity.
