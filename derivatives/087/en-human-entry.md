## 15-Second Explanation
Correspondence design is a method that arranges eight levers—order, silence, placement, speed, boundaries, intensity, viewpoint, and reflection—instead of increasing persuasion or rebuttal, so that a field naturally moves toward agreement. Combined with SQS and core-point discovery, it tests one factor at a time and verifies whether the result can be reproduced while preserving degrees of freedom.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
The more persuasion and rebuttal are piled on, the more a field may tire, consume time, and harden its opinions. As an alternative direction, the parent original treats order, intervals, placement, and similar elements “outside words” as design targets.

The eight levers are order, interval, placement, speed, boundary, intensity, viewpoint, and reflection. The original especially presents the three-part set of “order × interval × placement” as powerful. However, verification does not introduce everything at once: it compares existing method A with B’, changing only one lever.

## Practical Process
**1. Set the meaning axis first.** In the 20-minute format, agree on the evaluation criteria during the first 2 minutes.

**2. Place issue cards.** In 5 minutes, edit the relationships among issues using proximity and contrast.

**3. Add SQS.** In 5 minutes, conduct question → silence → reintroduction.

**4. Tentatively extract the core point.** In 5 minutes, find the one point that raised agreement.

**5. Check degrees of freedom.** During the final 3 minutes, check for blockage or a de facto single choice.

## Applications
**1. Organization.** An anonymous sketch in the parent original combines setting the meaning axis first with a 7-second interval and shows a case where decision time was shortened by 30%. This 30% is the result of that sketch and is not a universal guarantee.

**2. Education.** Observe whether articulating roles and switching placements increases the speed of assignment recurrence.

**3. Public sphere.** Turn opposing views into questions, make boundaries variable, and observe how the participation rate changes.

## Success Criteria
Agreement time becomes shorter without increasing the amount of persuasion.

Self-directed action increases without continuously adding explanations.

Even if the transition from resistance → discomfort → silence and re-referencing increase, degrees of freedom and dissent remain intact.

A single-factor effect is visible in A/B’, with results in the same direction for three consecutive cycles.

## Limitations
The parent original gives 7–20 seconds after an input as an example of an “interval,” but this is not a universal rule fixing the same number of seconds for every field. The 20-minute format and its minute allocations are also examples of a public implementation framework.

The 30% reduction is attached to an anonymous, generalized application sketch; it is not a general effect size with a disclosed population and measurement conditions.

## Preventing Misreading
Correspondence design is not a way to make invisible coercion more sophisticated. Non-command, separation from personal evaluation, transparency, and minimal intervention are ethical conditions in the original.

It is not a theory recommending that all eight levers be introduced simultaneously. The original’s anti-patterns include overloading the field with levers.
