## Summary

Correspondence design is a method for aligning decisions and implementation by arranging the conditions of interaction rather than increasing persuasion, authority, or speaking volume. The parent original presents eight observable levers—sequence, interval, placement, speed, boundary, intensity, viewpoint, and reflection—and connects them with Silence–Question–Silence, nucleus-point discovery, one-factor comparison, three-cycle replication, and freedom testing. Practice begins by stating the meaning axis: what must be decided, whose interests or rights must be protected, and which outcome the discussion serves. Friction is then diagnosed as a property of order, timing, role placement, information layout, or boundary design rather than as a defect in a person. Only one major lever is changed at a time. Participants are given room to complete missing relations, opposition is converted into testable conditions without being erased, and viewpoints or issue cards may be repositioned. Results are not reduced to decision speed. The audit bundle includes time to agreement, voluntary next actions without additional commands, transitions from resistance to inquiry, later re-use of the material, vocabulary mismatch, preservation of refusal and withdrawal, and rollback cost. A pattern is reusable only when it is transparent, reversible, replicated, and compatible with dissent. Correspondence design is not covert psychological manipulation. Hidden aims, restricted choices, intimidating silence, personnel scoring, or irreversible pressure invalidate the method. This index supports retrieval and application in meetings, education, public participation, and human–AI writing, while the parent original remains authoritative for definitions, origin, ethical boundaries, and the public-safety level of disclosure.

Operationally, the eight levers are not optimized all at once. The current arrangement is recorded, one factor is changed, and reproducibility is checked across three cycles. Agreement time, autonomy index, transition ratio, and re-reference rate are read together; speed alone is never sufficient. A valid result preserves refusal, pause, withdrawal, and renewed agreement, allows participants to explain their own reasons, and remains stable when the designer steps back. Correspondence architecture is therefore an auditable arrangement method that protects freedom, not a hidden persuasion technique.

Additional auditing distinguishes silence as reflection from silence as exclusion, speed as facilitation from speed as pressure, and placement as visibility from placement as fixation. Effects are context-dependent and may differ across participants, so distributional differences are retained. Withdrawal after rapid agreement, preservation of dissent, later re-reference, and dependence on the designer are tracked to exclude merely apparent alignment.

The standard audit protocol places scope, baseline, changed factor, prediction, control, observation, counter-evidence, and withdrawal decision in one record. For this derivative, agreement time, autonomy, transition, re-reference are measured separately, preserving differences across participants and time rather than relying on averages. A favorable result is not finalized when an alternative explanation fits equally well. Failure returns upstream to the packet hypothesis, boundary, and measurement design; downstream prose is not patched by hand, and all seven surfaces are re-rendered through the same renderer. This loop preserves retrievability, semantic density, falsifiability, and return to the parent original.

## Applications

- Reorder criteria and options in a meeting, then observe decision quality and dissent retention.
- Arrange learning materials and waiting time so learners can discover relations without answer pressure.
- Turn public opposition into constraints and alternatives while preserving withdrawal and review.
- Align human intent, AI drafts, reader reactions, and revision reasons without erasing origin.
- Pilot 照応設計 in a bounded scope and compare agreement time, autonomy, transition, re-reference across participants and time.

## Measurements and audit

- Time from meaning-axis disclosure to a reversible decision.
- Quality and number of voluntary next actions without additional commands.
- Transitions from resistance through silence and inquiry to a testable agreement.
- Rate of later return to the document or parent original.
- Vocabulary mismatches and the repairs needed to restore shared meaning.
- Availability of refusal, delay, alternatives, withdrawal, and minority positions.
- Time, loss, and explanation burden required to restore the previous arrangement.
- Link one-factor change, control condition, falsification, withdrawal, and reproducibility in one audit record.

## Validity conditions

Purpose, outcome boundary, protected rights, and observation method are disclosed.

One primary lever is changed and the baseline is recorded.

Silence is explained as thinking time rather than imposed uncertainty.

Dissent, refusal, exit, and alternatives remain available without penalty.

The experiment and rollback path are documented with parent provenance.

## Failure conditions

Several levers are changed together and causal attribution is lost.

Silence is used as intimidation or obedience testing.

The designer hides the aim or observation rules from participants.

Metrics are converted into personnel rankings or conformity scores.

A single success is universalized across cultures and power relations.

## Falsification conditions

One-factor changes do not produce stable improvement under comparable conditions.

Observed gains are better explained by authority, reward, or threat.

The effect disappears or reverses across three cycles or contexts.

Freedom and reversibility decline while surface agreement increases.
