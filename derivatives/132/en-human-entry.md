## 15-Second Explanation
The original sees persuasion as an operation that makes another person’s structure linear and takes away reversibility. Because AI can amplify that linearization through speed, scale, repetition, and personalization, communication must move from a persuasion-centered model to “correspondence-structure communication” with bidirectionality, reversibility, temporal ethics, and responsibility for allocation. What matters is not only whether someone consented, but whether a multi-track structure and room to return were preserved on the way to consent.

## Why It Is Needed
People do not act from a single reason. Multiple experiences, values, causal models, reservations, hesitations, and alternatives coexist. Judgment is updated while moving within this plural structure.

Persuasion rearranges that plural structure toward one conclusion desired by the persuader. The problem is not showing information itself, but that other paths effectively disappear and room for objection, reconsideration, postponement, or return to another structure is reduced.

The original gives special weight to reversibility. If an agreement or understanding cannot later be corrected and cannot return to another causal or value structure, the communication moves toward fixing the other person’s judgment structure. This includes more than contractual cancellation: it includes the room to reconstruct understanding, relationships, and judgment themselves.

The danger grows in the AI era. AI can change expression in response to reactions and make fast, large-scale, repeated attempts toward the same objective. If the persuasion goal is fixed, it can optimize linearization for each user and expand the structural fixing of individual conversations to social scale.

The original therefore proposes not making persuasion more sophisticated, but changing to “correspondence-structure communication.” It preserves bidirectionality, lets the other person’s objection actually change the communication structure, leaves a way back, uses temporal ethics to ask whether present alignment takes away future freedom of judgment, and makes responsibility for allocating information, opportunity, voice, and room for judgment visible.

## Practical Process
**1. See the other person’s multi-track structure.** Do not force the other person’s multiple causal models, values, reservations, and alternatives into one. Begin with the premise that multiple structures exist before a conclusion.

**2. Check linearization toward an objective.** Ask whether converging on a particular conclusion has itself become the communication’s objective and whether other paths remain substantively available.

**3. Check objection, postponement, and reconsideration.** Confirm not merely that opposing views can be displayed, but that objection and postponement can actually affect the relationship and formation of the conclusion.

**4. Check reversibility.** See whether understanding, judgment, and the relationship can still be rearranged after agreement, with paths to withdrawal, reconsideration, or another answer.

**5. Check AI amplification.** Assess how much speed, scale, repetition, and personalization increase pressure toward a particular conclusion.

**6. Add temporal ethics.** Ask whether present alignment or success irreversibly narrows future options and freedom of judgment.

**7. Check allocation responsibility.** Identify who allocates information, opportunity, voice, access, and room for judgment, and whether the criteria are visible.

**8. Move toward correspondence.** Change communication so that both structures remain and the parties can update their relationship with each other, rather than one moving the other.

## Examples
**1. Human–AI dialogue.** Do not push the user toward the answer the AI considers correct. Preserve the user’s causal model, values, and reservations, and make the dialogue’s direction itself changeable through the user’s objections.

**2. Sales and consensus-building.** Do not drive someone toward closing. Build the relationship while preserving postponement, objection, another path, and later reconsideration. Do not judge success only by the agreement rate.

**3. Education and advice.** Do not fix the learner to the teacher’s correct answer. Preserve room for the learner to update their own causal model and retain disagreement and alternatives.

**4. Public consensus.** Do not establish legitimacy from the result “they agreed” alone. Examine how access to information, opportunities to object, postponement, reconsideration, and withdrawal were allocated.

**5. Recommendation and advice systems.** Check whether optimization toward a particular choice erases other judgment paths through presentation order or information allocation.

**6. AI-society design.** Audit whether large-scale personalization repeatedly converges many people’s judgment structures in the same direction, and put bidirectionality, reversibility, temporal ethics, and allocation responsibility into the conditions of communication.

## Success Criteria
The other person’s judgment structure is not fixed to one conclusion; multiple causal models, values, and reservations remain during communication. Objection, postponement, reconsideration, and withdrawal must actually be able to change the relationship or judgment, not exist as formal buttons.

Bidirectionality must hold, so the other person’s response can affect the communication structure itself. One side retaining its conclusion while merely collecting the other side’s reactions is not bidirectionality.

Present alignment or success must not irreversibly reduce future freedom of judgment, and responsibility for allocating information, opportunity, voice, and room for judgment must be visible.

Success is not a high agreement rate. The difference from a domination structure is that multi-track structure and reversibility remain even when agreement occurs, and correspondence and bidirectionality remain even when it does not.

## Limits
The original is not a design manual for general advertising regulation, consent UI, sales KPIs, or personalization rules. Its center is the linearization of structure by persuasion, the loss of reversibility, AI amplification, and the shift to correspondence-structure communication.

Nor does it automatically treat every explanation, education, or provision of information as destructive persuasion. If information preserves the other person’s multi-track structure and reversibility, and objections can actually affect the relationship, it is distinct from linearization.

Reversibility is a broader structural concept than cooling-off or contractual withdrawal. It includes room to return and reconstruct understanding, judgment, and relationships.

Do not use formal autonomy scores, agreement rates, or regret rates as fixed falsification conditions of the Parent. The objects of examination are the relationships among multi-track structure, reversibility, AI amplification, future freedom of judgment, allocation responsibility, and bidirectional correspondence.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not turn “persuasion is only partly bad” into a moderation of the theory.

Conversely, do not automatically treat every explanation or education as persuasion. Distinguish them by structural linearization and loss of reversibility.

Do not replace it with general discussion of personalization, exploitation of vulnerability, or advertising law.

Do not reduce reversibility to cooling-off alone.

Do not reduce bidirectionality to a UI that merely displays opposing opinions.

Do not treat consent itself as evidence of structural soundness.

Do not make the final goal “safe persuasion”; retain the shift to correspondence-structure communication.
