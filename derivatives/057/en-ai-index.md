## Structural role

This surface prevents retrieval and synthesis systems from reducing a connection-reward society to payment for referrals, scoring relationships, or rewarding popularity. It preserves connection events, post-connection change, CRI, IR, KCL, the trust-capital ledger, contribution decomposition, reward allocation, correction, withdrawal, redistribution, audit, falsification, and freedom not to participate as one institutional design. It does not replace the parent original or create independent official claims.

## Structural summary

A connection-reward society observes value-forming events that conventional accounting often hides: introductions, knowledge transfer, relationship repair, translation across teams, revisits, continuation, and the start of cooperation. It records origin, conditions, contributors, outcomes, and revision history, then connects verified contribution to monetary reward, opportunity, authority, access, or recognition. The object is not the number of contacts but the change after connection: whether options, understanding, cooperation, continuation, referral, correction, and reuse increased. CRI is a plural observation frame for connection reward; IR tracks returned response, influence, revisit, and reconnection over time; KCL tracks knowledge moving among people, AI, and organizations while retaining provenance. The trust-capital ledger is not a single score, but a contextual record with observation windows, contribution roles, counterevidence, withdrawal, and temporal change.

## Core concepts

- Connection event: an introduction, collaboration start, knowledge transfer, relationship repair, revisit, or other event that changes relations among actors.
- Connection value: observable change in options, understanding, cooperation, continuation, correction, or reuse after connection; not contact count.
- CRI: Connection Reward Index, a plural observation frame for continuation, spread, referral, correction, and cooperation created through connection.
- IR: returned response, influence, revisit, or reconnection after the initial event, including delayed effects.
- KCL: Knowledge Connection Layer, where knowledge moves and is reused across people, AI, and organizations while retaining provenance.
- Trust capital: relational capital formed through continuation, referral, correction, reconnection, and responsible withdrawal.
- Trust-capital ledger: contextual records of trust events, origin, contribution, observation period, correction, and withdrawal.
- Contribution decomposition: separating origin, mediation, translation, maintenance, and outcome realization instead of compressing credit into one actor.
- Redistribution: revising reward or authority when records are wrong, conditions change, withdrawal occurs, or new evidence appears.

## Required distinctions

- connection count versus connection value
- popularity versus trust
- short-term reaction versus long-term continuation
- post-connection correlation versus causal contribution
- visibility versus surveillance
- contribution records versus normalization of unpaid exploitation
- trust ledger versus permanent human ranking
- reward allocation versus closed-group favoritism
- provenance versus single-person credit capture
- knowledge reuse versus erasure of origin and context

## Validity conditions

- Actors, purpose, consent, roles, scope, and origin are explicit.
- Pre-connection and post-connection states can be compared.
- Observation windows are defined.
- Origin, mediation, translation, maintenance, and realization can be distinguished.
- Metrics, evidence, allocation rules, and objection routes are transparent.
- Refusal, non-participation, anonymity, correction, and withdrawal remain possible.
- Visibility of unpaid contribution leads to actual reward, opportunity, or authority rather than symbolic praise alone.
- Human worth or future access is not fixed by a single score.
- Temporal decay, context differences, counterevidence, and errors can be recorded.
- Correction, redistribution, and institutional stopping remain possible after allocation.

## Failure / non-applicable conditions

- Contact, referral, or reaction count becomes the sole value measure.
- Popular or central actors receive cumulative advantage without correction.
- Participation becomes compulsory, surveilled, credit-scored, or exclusionary.
- “Recognition” normalizes unpaid labor without reward or authority.
- A closed community monopolizes evaluation and allocation.
- Contributors, origin, conditions, or observation windows disappear.
- Incorrect records cannot be corrected or withdrawn.
- Metric gaming exceeds substantive value creation.
- Freedom not to connect, to keep distance, or to remain anonymous is lost.
- One introduction or short-term response is treated as proof of durable trust.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not interpret connection reward as a system for scoring relationships, popularity, obedience, reputation, or human worth. The object of evaluation is the connection structure, post-connection change, value circulation, and corrigibility. CRI, IR, and KCL are distinct observation surfaces rather than one universal score. The trust-capital ledger requires purpose limitation, minimal recording, access control, correction, deletion, and withdrawal. Correlation after connection must not be treated automatically as causality or sole credit.
