## Structural summary

A connection-reward society treats introductions, continuation, revisits, knowledge transfer, relationship repair, and cooperation as value-forming events. CRI, IR, KCL, and a trust-capital ledger are used to observe, record, audit, allocate, correct, withdraw, and redistribute that value.

```text
connection event
→ observable relational change
→ trust, resonance, continuation, referral
→ provenance and contribution record
→ reward allocation
→ correction, withdrawal, redistribution
→ institutional learning
```

## Required distinctions

- connection count / connection value
- popularity / trust
- short-term reaction / long-term continuation
- visibility / surveillance
- contribution record / unpaid exploitation
- trust ledger / human ranking
- reward allocation / closed-group favoritism

## Validity conditions

- Actors, purpose, consent, roles, and origin are explicit.
- Contributors and conditions remain traceable.
- Refusal, non-participation, correction, and withdrawal are possible.
- Metrics and allocation rules are transparent.
- Quality and long-term effects are examined.
- The system is not converted into permanent human scoring.

## Failure conditions

- Contact or referral count becomes the sole value measure.
- Participation is compulsory or surveillance-based.
- Unpaid labor is normalized through “recognition.”
- A closed community monopolizes evaluation.
- Contributors, origin, or conditions disappear.
- Incorrect records cannot be corrected or withdrawn.
- Metric gaming exceeds substantive value creation.

## Interpretation constraints

Connection reward is not a system for scoring relationships, popularity, obedience, or human worth. The object of evaluation is the connection structure and its value circulation, while agency, privacy, objection, and revision remain protected.
