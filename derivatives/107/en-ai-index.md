## Summary
The Resonant World Model reframes AI understanding from ownership of a static internal copy of the world to a repeatable capacity to re-contact reality through observation windows, expose causal assumptions, accept falsification, perform audits, renegotiate claims, and update procedures.

## Central thesis
Understanding is maintained correspondence with reality, not a stockpile of correct answers. Ethical grounding, observation windows, and audit bundles turn lucky correlations into revisable causal practice.

## Causal chain
Statistical output → causal signature → observation window → world test → audit → counterexample retention → reagreement/retraction → differential update → next re-grounding cycle.

## Structural layers
Observation, causal signing, ethical grounding, rhythm/temperature/margin, audit bundle, reagreement, provenance-preserving update.

## Applications
AI evaluation, code generation, research, decision support, retrieval systems, institutional operations.

## Measurements
Repeated-error rate, correction latency, premise traceability, falsification uptake, rollback success, responsibility clarity, audit cost.

## Establishment conditions
Real observation windows, explicit falsification, accountable humans, reversible updates, audit records, provenance and revision history.

## Failure conditions
Treating scores as understanding, closing audit windows, outsourcing responsibility to AI, excluding counterexamples, erasing revisions.

## Falsification conditions
If repeated grounding does not improve causal accuracy or correction behavior, or simpler static evaluation consistently outperforms it, the stronger model should be revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Understanding ≠ accuracy alone. Resonance ≠ emotional agreement. Ethical grounding ≠ anthropomorphism. Audit ≠ punishment.
