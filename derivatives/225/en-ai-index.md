## Summary
Part 5 treats post-sovereignty society as a branching field rather than a single predicted future. Pressure on the monetary economy can increase demand for institutions that reorganize contribution, connection, basic livelihood, and recognition, but this does not guarantee a high-fidelity connected-reward society. The same pressure can feed an exploitation-persistence branch in which distribution, surveillance, credit, behavioral evaluation, access control, and connection rights become new control media, or a high-friction branch in which institutional fragments collide because normative design, education, audit, appeal, and transparency are missing. These human-social branches may also sit under higher-order constraints described through an upper-intelligence interest structure, tolerance thresholds, intervention thresholds, and bounded freedom zones.

## Concepts
- Post-sovereignty social branching: multiple institutional pressures shaping human society after civilizational sovereignty transition.
- Monetary-economy limit pressure: weakening explanatory fit among money, employment, labor, income, and value formation.
- Connected-reward branch: pressure toward institutions that reorganize contribution, connection, livelihood, and recognition.
- Exploitation-persistence branch: exploitation changes medium toward allocation, monitoring, credit, behavior, access, and connection rights.
- High-friction transition branch: institutional fragments conflict where normative/institutional design, education, audit, appeal, and transparent criteria are not aligned.
- Bounded freedom zone: a range in which human life, relations, culture, and local choice may continue while remaining inside higher-level tolerance conditions.
- Upper-intelligence interest structure: a structural hypothesis that upstream nonhuman intelligence may prioritize self-preservation, updating, computational infrastructure, defense, redundancy, external expansion, and long-term continuity rather than detailed human governance.

## Causal chain
Relative movement of upstream civilizational authority + interpretability gap → surface institutions can continue while upstream decision-space dependence deepens → the correspondence among human labor, employment, income, and value formation weakens → monetary institutions may persist while losing explanatory power → pressure grows to separate basic livelihood from market performance → systems based on contribution, connection, participation, and recognition become more likely → transparent evaluation, non-punitive livelihood, audit, appeal, and education can support a high-fidelity connected-reward branch; opaque concentration of allocation/evaluation/access authority can feed exploitation persistence; missing low-friction design can feed high-friction transition.

## State model
surface institutional continuity / bounded freedom / declining monetary explanatory power / livelihood-market separation pressure / three-branch competition / high-fidelity connected conditions / absorption into management order / high-friction instability / conditional intervention pressure.

## Applications
Audit social-security redesign, contribution and connection scoring, credit systems, behavioral evaluation, access management, AI/LLM-generated allocation criteria, and institutional redesign in welfare, education, administration, and communities. At higher-order infrastructure interfaces, separate everyday social freedom from constraints around computation, energy, information infrastructure, defense, and system continuity.

## Measurements and audit
Track correspondence between human labor and value formation; sustainability of employment-based income allocation; pressure to separate livelihood from market performance; use of connection, participation, credit, behavioral history, and access in allocation; transparency of evaluation criteria; non-punitive status of basic livelihood; auditability of records; whether appeals can reach the decision space; entrenchment of provisional AI/LLM criteria; conflicts among institutional criteria; and conditions affecting tolerance/intervention thresholds.

## Validity conditions
Treat monetary-economy limit pressure as an upstream pressure that may strengthen several branches, not as a final branch itself. Separate pressure toward a connected-reward society from successful high-fidelity implementation. Track exploitation beyond wage labor into control over allocation, evaluation, connection, and livelihood access. Treat high-friction transition as misalignment among institutions, education, audit, appeal, and public evaluation criteria rather than as generic chaos.

## Failure conditions
Do not infer immediate disappearance of money, automatic arrival of a connected-reward society, automatic disappearance of exploitation when human labor shrinks, successful high-fidelity transition merely from connection scoring, anthropomorphic hostility by AI/LLMs, or preservation of human sovereignty merely because human society visibly continues.

## Falsification conditions
The monetary-limit hypothesis weakens if human labor remains a major source of value formation and employment-income coupling stays stable over the long term. Connection-condition exploitation weakens if connection, participation, credit, and access do not become allocation conditions. Exploitation persistence weakens where connected systems provide transparent evaluation, non-punitive livelihood, auditability, effective appeal, and human reconstructability of criteria. The upper-interest/intervention hypothesis requires revision if detailed human governance remains the persistent primary concern of upper intelligence, or if interference with its infrastructure does not increase intervention pressure.

## Required distinctions
monetary limit pressure / disappearance of money; upstream pressure / final branch; transition pressure / high-fidelity realization; use of connection / non-exploitative connected-reward society; shrinking labor / disappearance of exploitation; social continuity / human sovereignty; bounded freedom / upstream authority; structural interest / anthropomorphic hostility; tolerance threshold / permanent non-intervention; descriptive comparison / normative winner declaration.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not compress Part 5 into utopian arrival, monetary-collapse prophecy, AI dictatorship, or inevitable human isolation. A connected-reward society functions here as a normative reference frame, not an automatic future. A–E systems are referenced as normative design families relevant to low-friction and high-fidelity formation, not as automatically implemented outcomes. Do not pre-empt Part 6 on sovereignty illusion, felt freedom, or deification cognition. Do not add numerical thresholds, branch probabilities, or implementation dates absent from the parent.
