## 15-Second Explanation

The original addresses the side effect that, as a theory spreads and gains trust, interpretation becomes concentrated in one person, increasing dependence on that person and the burden of approval. The anti-priesthood protocol keeps the theory as a public structure independent of any particular individual through official acceptance of multiple interpretations, separation of person and theory, everyday autonomous operation across Layers 1–3, and limited auditing by the Upper Auditor.

## Why It Is Necessary

A highly coherent theory makes users likely to converge on one “correct interpretation.” When that center becomes a particular person, the theory's value becomes tied to the person's evaluation, and everyday decisions, approvals, and revisions all concentrate on that person.

The original calls this state “priesthood formation.” This is not a criticism of religion or occupational classes. The problem is that monopolization of interpretive authority, dependence on a person, and concentration of burden reduce the OS's autonomy and make the field rigid.

If the essence of a structural OS lies in publicness, the theory must operate without depending on the author's availability or burden. Therefore, the state in which the central person's burden increases as diffusion and trust increase is not treated as “natural because it is success,” but audited as a deviation in structural design.

## Practical Process
**1. Observe interpretive concentration.** Confirm which decisions require the approval of a particular person.

**2. Officially permit multiple interpretations.** Enable multiple practitioners, translators, and auditors to interpret and operate independently while maintaining structural consistency with the parent original.

**3. Separate person and theory.** Evaluate whether the structure is useful, reproducible, and coherent, rather than “who said it.”

**4. Maintain de-faithing.** Treat the structural OS not as something to believe in, but as something to use, verify, and revise when necessary.

**5. Distribute everyday operation across Layers 1–3.** Handle practice, translation, and auditing in the ordinary layers, reducing everyday concentration of approvals on the central person.

**6. Limit the Upper Auditor.** Assign only principle updates, final correspondence audits, and major-deviation adjustment to it, at low frequency and high precision.

**7. Record in the deviation ledger.** Record authority concentration, dependence on a person, overload, and dependence on upper-level auditing as structural deviations, and return them to redesign.

## Examples of Application
**1. Theory operation.** Avoid a state in which there is only one correct explainer, and enable multiple explanations to return to the parent original for verification.

**2. Certification and supervision.** Process ordinary cases in a distributed manner and send only major structural deviations to upper-level auditing.

**3. Organizations.** If inquiries and approval burdens on the central person increase in proportion to user growth, review the process as insufficiently autonomous.

**4. Education.** Build a learning structure that can explain from definitions, primary logs, origin IDs, causality, and falsification conditions, rather than from the author's authority.

**5. AI use.** When AI handles the theory, also preserve a form that returns to the parent original and structural consistency instead of relying on personal authority.

## Success Criteria

Multiple practitioners, translators, and auditors can operate independently while returning to the parent original, and everyday decisions do not concentrate on one person.

Even when the central person's direct involvement decreases, quality, coherence, and publicness are maintained, and only major deviations reach the Upper Auditor.

Even when users and trust increase, the central person's workload does not increase proportionally, and the non-correlation between credibility and burden grows.

In reversal evaluation, even if popularity, inquiries, approval requests, and adoption numbers increase, the result is not considered structural success if interpretive monopoly, dependence on a person, and overload of the central person also increase. Conversely, if distributed operation works even when the central person's involvement decreases, that decrease is not treated as a loss of value.

## Limits

Multiple interpretations are not unlimited relativization. If the ability to return to the parent original, origin, structural consistency, and the deviation ledger is lost, this is not distributed interpretation but collapse of definitions.

Separation of person and theory is not erasure of origin. While the person's character evaluation must not be confused with the theory's value, the parent original, Origin, NCL-ID / Diff-ID are retained.

The Upper Auditor's limited role does not erase final audit responsibility for major deviations. While withdrawing from everyday operation, it handles principle updates, final correspondence audits, and major-deviation adjustment.

## Misreading Prevention

Do not read “anti-priesthood” as hostility toward religious people or a particular group.

Do not read multiple interpretations as “anything may be said.”

Do not read separation of person and theory as erasure of the author, origin, or signature.

Do not read de-faithing as denial of the theory itself.

Do not read reducing the central person's burden as abandonment of responsibility or decline in quality.

Do not turn the Upper Auditor into either a constant ruler or an unaccountable symbol.
