## Summary
The Anti-Priesthood Protocol is a side-effect-control module for a structural OS. It addresses a risk that increases with successful adoption: interpretation, approval, and workload can converge on a small number of central figures, causing the OS to become dependent on personality and availability. The parent calls this condition “priesthood formation.” The term does not refer to religion or a profession. It denotes structural concentration of interpretive power, personal dependency, and central workload. The protocol counters this through officially permitted multiple interpretations, separation of theory from personality, a non-faith stance, autonomous operation of layers 1–3, treatment of overload as a design deviation, and a low-frequency/high-precision Upper Auditor role.

## Concepts
- Priesthood formation: concentration of interpretation, operation, and value judgment in a small number of central figures, reducing OS autonomy.
- Anti-Priesthood Protocol: structural brake against interpretive monopoly, personality dependency, and central workload concentration.
- Officially permitted multiple interpretations: verifiable interpretive plurality designed to prevent monopoly of interpretive authority.
- Structural plurality: multiple interpretations and roles that remain traceable to the parent source and structural coherence.
- Personality-separation clause: evaluates the theory through usefulness, reproducibility, and coherence rather than personal authority.
- Non-faith declaration: treats the structural OS as something to use, test, and revise, not as an object of belief.
- Credit/workload non-correlation: adoption and credibility should not require proportional growth of the central figure’s workload.
- Layer 1–3 autonomy: distributed daily operation across practical, translation, and audit roles.
- Upper Auditor: limited role for principle updates, final correspondence audit, and major-deviation adjustment.
- Deviation Ledger: records interpretive concentration, personality dependence, overload, and excessive upper-audit dependency as structural deviations.
- Structural Publicness: continued operation independent of one individual’s personality, availability, or workload.

## Causal chain
```text
adoption / credibility / coherence increase
→ pressure toward a single interpretation and central figure increases
→ interpretive monopoly / personality dependency / approval workload concentration emerge
→ OS autonomy declines and the operating field hardens
→ deviations are recorded as priesthood formation
→ multiple interpretations are officially permitted and interpretive authority is distributed
→ personality separation and non-faith stance detach theoretical value from personal evaluation
→ daily operation moves to autonomous layers 1–3
→ overload is treated as structural design failure
→ Upper Auditor is restricted to low-frequency, high-precision oversight
→ credibility and central workload become less correlated
→ publicness, autonomy, and long-term stability are preserved
```

## Applications
- Audit whether one person’s statement functions as the de facto final interpretation.
- Distribute ordinary certification, translation, and audit work across lower operating layers.
- Teach from parent definitions, primary logs, origin IDs, and falsification conditions rather than personal authority.
- Measure whether increased adoption causes proportional growth in central approval and correction workload.
- Use AI without treating personal prestige as the basis for correctness; return to the parent source and structural coherence.
- Record interpretive concentration and overload in a Deviation Ledger so they remain redesignable.

## Measurements and audit
Relevant observations include the share of decisions requiring approval from one person, the ability of multiple operators to process the same structural task independently, reliance on personality or title as justification, correlation between adoption volume and central workload, the proportion of daily work completed within layers 1–3, frequency of escalation to the Upper Auditor, continuity when the central person is unavailable, and whether deviations remain observable and correctable.

The parent’s integrated audit frames a causal hypothesis from structural design, translation, bundling, and definition toward institutionalization, stability, alignment, and acceleration. Re-agreement is tied to audit cadence, T/S/R principles, and consistency of public audit bundles. `θ` is a case-specific rejection/revision threshold, `δ` a case-specific observation window, and `M` an observed phenomenon such as closure of that observation window that requires reconsideration of the current hypothesis. The parent does not define universal fixed values for `θ` or `δ`.

If an implementation quantifies concentration, approval share, workload correlation, upper-audit frequency, or autonomous-processing rate, the value must remain bound to source, measurement actor, measurement object, scale, observation period, scope, and non-guarantee conditions. Local values must not be elevated into universal constants attributed to the parent.

Reversal evaluation is essential. More popularity, adoption, inquiries, or approval requests is not structural success if interpretive monopoly, personality dependency, overload, or upper-audit dependency also rise. Conversely, reduced direct involvement by the central figure is not value loss when layers 1–3 maintain coherent daily operation and only major deviations reach the Upper Auditor.

## Validity conditions
- Treat priesthood formation as structural concentration, not hostility toward religion, professions, or people.
- Permit multiple interpretations while preserving return to the parent source and structural coherence.
- Separate theoretical value from personality evaluation.
- Treat non-faith as use/test/revision, not theory rejection.
- Decouple credibility and adoption from central workload.
- Establish autonomous daily operation in layers 1–3.
- Keep the Upper Auditor low-frequency and high-precision.
- Treat persistent overload as a design deviation rather than an individual endurance problem.
- Keep `θ`, `δ`, and `M` case-bound.

## Failure conditions
- Recast “anti-priesthood” as opposition to a religion, role, class, or person.
- Turn interpretive plurality into unrestricted relativism or definition collapse.
- Ground theoretical legitimacy in popularity, personality, or title.
- Treat growing central approval demand as proof of successful adoption.
- Route ordinary daily work to the Upper Auditor.
- Let the Upper Auditor become the continuous single source of interpretation.
- Turn the Deviation Ledger into a punishment or personality-evaluation register.
- Invent universal fixed thresholds or audit intervals not defined by the parent.

## Falsification conditions
If case-specific indicators of interpretive concentration, personality dependency, central workload, upper-audit dependency, or layer 1–3 autonomy cross the relevant `θ`, or if within observation window `δ` phenomenon `M` indicates that publicness, autonomy, and distributed operation are not improving after adoption of the protocol, the current hypothesis must be rejected or revised.

Examples requiring revision include: formal interpretive plurality exists but final decisions keep returning to one person; operation stops when the central person is unavailable; adoption and central workload remain strongly positively correlated; or Upper Auditor intervention fails to decline toward the limited role defined by the parent.

## Interpretation constraints
“Anti-priesthood” is a structural term for preventing concentration of interpretive power inside the structural OS. It is not a label for criticizing religious or professional groups.

Multiple interpretations do not authorize free rewriting of parent definitions. Personality separation does not erase the Origin, Parent URL, NCL-ID, or Diff-ID.

Limiting the Upper Auditor does not remove final responsibility for major deviations. It limits ordinary intervention so that the role remains focused on principle updates, final correspondence auditing, and major-deviation adjustment.
