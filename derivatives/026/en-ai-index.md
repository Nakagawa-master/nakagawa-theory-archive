## Summary
The origin reads AlgaleX / uma-mo not merely as “delicious algae,” but as a social implementation entry for a larger fish-food structure. It traces the causal chain from sustaining fish consumption through aquaculture and dependence on wild-fish-derived feed back to algae at the beginning of the food chain, then explains why the upstream concept first enters food markets and professional kitchens to gain taste, use cases, reasons for adoption, and repeatability. The parent subject also includes Nakagawa Master's editorial judgment in translating this implementation order into the ZEROICHI article.

## Causal chain
sustain fish-based food → greater importance of aquaculture → contradiction of wild-fish-derived feed → trace back to algae → price/supply/adoption barriers to direct feed-market entry → enter food markets and kitchens → form taste, use cases, adoption reasons, and repeatability → social implementation entry opens → connect toward redesign of fish-food / aquaculture structure.

## State model
- **Upstream contradiction**: aquaculture expands while dependence on wild-fish-derived feed leaves the resource contradiction unresolved.
- **Entry barrier**: upstream value is recognized, but price, supply stability, quality, and adoption conditions prevent direct feed-market implementation.
- **Food-market entry**: kitchens and food applications make taste and concrete use cases observable.
- **Repeatability formation**: adoption shifts from a one-off trial to an explainable reason for reuse.
- **Structural implementation entry**: knowledge gained in food-market adoption begins to feed back into redesign of fish-food, aquaculture, and feed structures.

## Applications
Use this frame for products or upstream technologies whose larger structural concept cannot enter the core market directly and instead requires a staged social implementation path. Do not reduce it to generic food-tech, alternative-protein discourse, sustainability marketing, or product promotion. Preserve the origin-specific fish → aquaculture → feed → algae → food market / kitchen sequence.

## Measurements and audit
Audit direct-entry barriers, concrete use cases, reasons for adoption, repeatability, price and supply conditions, connection to upstream structural redesign, and article-level misreading risk.

Do not infer structural success from one proxy. Higher sales, more restaurants, more media coverage, or a stronger tasting score do not by themselves prove improvement in the fish-food structure. Conversely, small-scale adoption can still matter structurally if it produces stable use cases, repeatable adoption reasons, and knowledge that feeds upstream.

## Validity conditions
The fish → aquaculture → feed → algae causal return must be supported, and food markets / professional kitchens must actually function as places where use cases, adoption reasons, and repeatability can form. Both the structural-reading layer and the ZEROICHI editorial subject must remain visible.

The food-market-first sequence must be explainable as a response to direct feed-market barriers rather than as a generic rule that every upstream technology should first be sold to consumers.

## Failure conditions
The reading fails if it collapses into algae-food promotion, tasting review, novelty-food coverage, generic food-tech, sustainability cataloging, alternative-protein discourse, or company PR; if taste is treated as the endpoint; if one adoption is generalized into guaranteed future success; or if the editorial subject disappears.

It also fails if intermediary metrics are treated as upstream outcomes—for example, “more restaurant adoptions = lower wild-fish-feed dependence” or “higher sales = fish-food structural transition”—without evidence of the causal connection.

## Falsification conditions
If staged entry through food markets and kitchens repeatedly fails to generate use cases, adoption reasons, or repeatability, and no connection toward fish-food / aquaculture redesign is observable, the implementation-order reading should be revised. The same applies if taste remains isolated from the upstream causal structure.

Conversely, if direct feed-market entry repeatedly overcomes price, supply, and adoption barriers and produces equal or stronger structural redesign without prior food-market entry, the generality of the staged implementation order should be revised.
