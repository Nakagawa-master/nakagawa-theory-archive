## 15-Second Explanation
Do not leave sales to the skill of “capable people.” Embed period, amplitude, synchronization, and the sequence observe → translate → design → execute → verify in the organization. This does not erase individuality; it shares the causality and learning that produce results and creates a sales rhythm reproducible after the person in charge changes.

## Why It Is Needed
In person-dependent sales, results, judgment, and customer understanding remain inside an individual, and the causal chain breaks when a person changes or the organization expands. The Parent replaces this with structural rhythm: period is comparable repetition, amplitude is the depth of customer interest and editing density, and synchronization is alignment of the tone across site, materials, demo, meeting, and CS.

It also separates work into observe → translate → design → execute → verify, preventing action from being fixed before observation or execution from coming before translation. A short silence slot is an observation window for unspoken discomfort; RC is the room to return price, scope, and due date by differential units. Because return remains possible, the organization can step in small increments and quickly cycle customer reaction → organizational improvement → re-proposal learning.

## Practical Process
1. Align the period of sales activity into comparable beats.
2. Define amplitude as the depth of observation, hypothesis, editing, and proposal, not heat.
3. Synchronize vocabulary and tempo across site, materials, demo, meeting, and CS.
4. Keep the order observe → translate → design → execute → verify.
5. Add silence slots to meetings and sales talks to stop premature conclusions.
6. Clarify the responsible contact for each beat.
7. Preserve RC for price, scope, due date, and similar items.
8. Return customer reaction as a primary log to organizational learning and connect it to re-proposal.
9. Review the causal path monthly, not only results.

## Applications
At handover, transfer not only a talk script but customer observations, shared vocabulary, responsible contact, next action, and withdrawal conditions. In weekly reviews, compare cases with the same beat and format, and check that the words of site, materials, and meetings have not become separate personalities. Do not finalize a proposal all at once; give it returnable differences.

## Success Criteria
Beats align without becoming uniform, handoff friction falls, and the cycle from vocabulary alignment and phase stability through customer reaction to re-proposal improves. Result causality does not reconcentrate in one individual, and the learning route survives a handover. Treat UCI/REI/UCI-i as introspective trend observations; do not make sales the sole test of rhythm health.

## Limits
“Same day/time,” “30-second introduction,” and “30–60 seconds of silence” in the Parent are examples, not fixed values for every organization. Do not supplement unpublished indicator formulas or thresholds. Sales rhythm is not a technique for taking away customer autonomy; it is design for aligning one's own structural conditions.

## Preventing Misreading
“Eliminating person-dependence” does not mean eliminating humanity, individuality, or discretion. “Synchronization” is alignment of principle and contact points, not a unified script. “Silence” is an observation window, not neglect; “RC” is room for reversible learning, not timidity. Do not repurpose indicators for individual evaluation or external rankings.
