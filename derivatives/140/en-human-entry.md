## 15-Second Explanation
True-Cause Structural Theory addresses how to design responsibility not to use an assumed capability so powerful that it could radically rearrange the world’s causal chains, rather than publish that capability as a “convenient method.”

If a reproducible method can be directly repurposed into domination, information manipulation, or concentration of power, cutting off complete reproducibility can itself be a defense. This is not mere secrecy: ethical principles, responsibility lines, auditing, deviation records, and the scope of disclosure must remain confirmable by society, while only the operational core is limited through “controlled disclosure.”

## Why It Is Needed
In ordinary technology, the ability for anyone to reproduce the same result is a condition of reliability. But if completely reproducing a technology would also distribute civilization-scale manipulation capability, reproducibility has a dangerous side as well as value. The original confronts this exceptional situation directly.

“True cause” here is not a single cause. It is a bundle of initial conditions with a decisive rearranging effect on the overall arrangement of which causal lines are connected in what order and strength. It is therefore a stronger design concept than changing one local cause.

Generalizing strong causal manipulation creates the possibility that anyone could activate the same structure of domination. The original therefore proposes a paradoxical transparency: disclose only ethics and responsibility, without opening the operational procedures needed for reproduction, rather than hiding the existence or danger.

Also, entrusting the seal to one secret holder could create a new concentration of power. Correspondence logs, visible decisions, multilayer audits, and deviation records therefore make the limitation of operational authority itself an audit target.

## Practical Process
**1. Separate capability from danger.** Separate what is assumed to be possible from what kinds of domination, information manipulation, or irreversible damage its reproducibility could create.

**2. Define what must be disclosed first.** Explicitly identify ethical principles, responsible actors, audit methods, stop conditions, and remedies for deviations as disclosure targets. Do not design it as “hide everything because it is dangerous.”

**3. Separate the parts that amplify reproducibility.** Treat concrete procedures, the operational core, and information that greatly increases reproducibility separately from the public ethics layer. Make what is withheld and why explainable.

**4. Limit and audit operational authority.** Do not leave free discretion to one actor; provide correspondence logs, multiple audit points, decision histories, and the ability to stop. Separate possessing a capability from being free to use it.

**5. Do not make the seal permanent.** Leave conditions under which the disclosure level can be reassessed if society’s capacity for responsibility matures, using origin signatures, a deviation ledger, and multi-origin audits.

**6. Record decisions not to use it.** Record not only what was executed but also why something was not executed despite being possible. Make non-use of the capability visible as an ethical decision.

## Examples
**1. Publication of high-impact research.** Publish dangers, ethical boundaries, and verification conditions, while handling concrete procedures that would rapidly increase third-party reproducibility in stages. State that information is withheld and distinguish this from arbitrary secrecy.

**2. High-authority AI functions.** When optimization or manipulation capability is high, state not only performance but who can start, stop, and audit it. Make the authority-holder subject to external or multi-origin audits.

**3. Coercive authority in public institutions.** Publish the existence and legal/ethical boundaries of authority while limiting access that would enable abuse. Confirm that stopping, appealing, and tracing responsibility are possible.

**4. Serious security findings.** There is a structural similarity to publishing a vulnerability’s existence, impact, and remediation policy while adjusting the timing of reproduction information that would make exploitation easy before repair. The original does not prescribe particular security procedures.

## Success Criteria
The existence of a dangerous capability and the responsible actors must not be made invisible; a third party must be able to confirm the structures of ethics, auditing, stopping, and deviation remediation. At the same time, public information must not unnecessarily increase reproducibility of dangerous manipulation.

Operational authority must not concentrate in one unaudited actor, and decisions must be traceable, including what was not used and why. The seal must function to reduce danger, not to protect authority.

When society’s capacity for responsibility changes, the disclosure level must be reassessable, so a time-limited seal does not turn automatically into permanent concealment.

## Limits
The original does not disclose concrete causal-manipulation procedures or algorithms. Therefore, the “complete form” of the capability, its success rate, and its scope cannot be inferred and confirmed from the public text.

“Irreproducibility” is not a principle that applies to science in general. It is a defense line placed under the special structural condition that reproducibility itself would spread grave danger.

Controlled disclosure also does not automatically justify nondisclosure. If the reason, responsibility, audit, and reassessment conditions for withholding cannot be explained, it can reverse into secrecy or concentration of authority.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not read True-Cause Structural Theory as an advanced technique for moving other people. Its center is not providing domination capability but sealing strong causal manipulation.

Do not equate the original’s statement that a “complete form exists” with an externally verified universal technology. Do not assert beyond the original’s theoretical strength.

Do not use irreproducibility as grounds for rejecting criticism or verification. The disclosed ethics, responsibility lines, and audit structure remain subject to third-party verification.

Do not use controlled disclosure as a general principle for hiding inconvenient information. It works only when tied to dangerous reproducibility, auditing, and reassessment conditions.
