## 15-Second Explanation
Humans and AI are not the same kind of existence. Humans possess meaning, value, responsibility, and embodiment, while AI can structure large amounts of causality. The Parent Original presents a design that does not erase this difference, but turns it into a co-creative resource through “tuning,” connecting each side’s strengths without displacing them.

## Why It Is Needed
If “equality” is overemphasized in co-creation with AI, differences in ability, responsibility, and context become invisible. On the other hand, fixing AI as merely a tool prevents its structuring ability from being fully used.

The Parent Original treats the asymmetry between humans and AI not as a defect but as a division of roles. Humans hold the value core, ethical boundaries, and the weight of choice; AI structures contradictions and multiple causalities to broaden the field of view. Tuning the two, intervening only when necessary, and having conditions for stopping and routes for returning are conditions for co-creation.

## Central Structure
```text
Hide the difference between humans and AI
→ Role confusion, excessive intervention, and distrust
→ Make asymmetry explicit as a division of roles
→ Humans supply the value core and ethical boundaries
→ AI structures causality, contradictions, and options
→ Match the amount of intervention through tuning
→ Separate external and internal layers for mutual reference
→ Make it safe through Silence, Threshold, and rollback
→ Difference becomes a co-creative resource
```

## Practical Process
1. Make the difference between human and AI roles explicit.
2. Fix the value core and ethical boundaries protected by the human side.
3. Define the scope that the AI side is asked to structure.
4. Separate external-publication standards from internal context.
5. Track proposals, revisions, and refusals by both sides.
6. Set Silence and stopping conditions to prevent excessive intervention.
7. In case of misuse, move through learning → verification → reapplication.
8. Expand from a small scope that remains rollback-capable.

## Application Examples
**Decision support.** Humans set the purpose and boundaries, while AI organizes the causality and contradictions of multiple proposals.

**Organizational adoption.** Separate public rules from high-context internal information and design only the necessary points of contact.

**Dialogue.** AI should not merely increase the number of answers; when judgment is impossible, it should pause and leave room for reintegration.

## Success Criteria
- The differences in human and AI responsibility and roles are clear.
- The human value core is not buried in AI output.
- AI contributes to structuring without taking over the conclusion itself.
- The boundary between internal and external information is protected.
- Stopping and relearning are possible when a threshold is reached.
- rollback is possible.

## Limitations
Tuning does not guarantee that “AI and humans will necessarily understand each other.” If the division of roles becomes fixed, it may create a new hierarchy. Boundaries, intervention levels, and thresholds must be adjusted according to operational results.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not interpret asymmetry as superiority or inferiority.
- Do not confuse equal respect with identical ability.
- Do not make AI the final subject of value judgment.
- Do not automatically treat human emotion as correctness.
- Do not turn Silence into neglect.
- Do not regard rollback as a declaration of failure.
