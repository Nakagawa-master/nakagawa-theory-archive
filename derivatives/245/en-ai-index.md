## Summary
A theory of an organization that can observe its own rules, exceptions, and decision history, describe the gap between formal and actual operation, and revise its structure under explicit authority, audit, stop rights, and rollback. It is not a claim of autonomous AI management.

## Concepts
Organizational singularity; structural reality; self-referential OS; exception; self-description; recursion; rule revision; authority; stop right; rollback; revision lineage.

## Causal chain
Fixed rules → environmental drift → exception growth → observation of decisions → self-description of rule/operation gaps → governed revision → outcome observation → next revision.

## State model
S0 fixed OS → S1 exception accumulation → S2 observable operation → S3 self-description → S4 governed self-revision → S5 audit of the revision loop.

## Applications
Complex review and approval systems, multi-department operations, AI-assisted workflows, public services, finance, healthcare, and other domains where exceptions outgrow manuals.

## Measurements and audit
Exception recurrence, revision latency, rule/operation divergence, traceability of change reasons, side effects, stop authority, rollback, responsibility continuity, recursion stability.

## Validity conditions
Observable logs, explicit rules, legitimate revision authority, before/after comparison, rollback capability, and preservation of human responsibility.

## Failure conditions
Self-justification disguised as self-reference; unbounded automated revision; missing change reasons; erased responsibility; recursive instability; single-objective optimization hiding externalities.

## Falsification conditions
If self-description and governed revision do not improve adaptation, exception recurrence, or explainability and instead only increase instability, the framework’s scope should be reduced.

## Required distinctions
Organizational vs technological singularity; self-reference vs self-justification; revision vs uncontrolled modification; recursion vs endless change; AI assistance vs governing authority.

## Interpretation constraints
Automation rate is not maturity. Model recommendations are not authority. Preserve lineage, stop rights, and rollback. Do not invent universal maturity thresholds.
