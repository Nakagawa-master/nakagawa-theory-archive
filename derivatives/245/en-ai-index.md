## Summary
Future-Defined Causal Rectification Theory I does not claim that the physical future acts backward on the past. It defines a feasible future state, connected to real constraints, and uses the conditions required for that future as **terminal constraints** against which the present is evaluated. Meaning, time allocation, action order, and organizational or institutional structure are then inspected for mismatch and realigned. The future is therefore not treated as predetermined information but as a revisable design condition used to audit present configuration.

A present-centered local optimum can privilege urgency, inherited institutions, sunk investment, and current metrics while postponing capabilities, time, or structural conditions required later. The theory responds by making the future condition explicit, checking feasibility, exposing future debt, and rectifying four layers: what current activities mean, how time is allocated, which actions continue/stop/move earlier, and which roles, authority, institutions, or information flows must change. If implementation does not increase the feasibility of the defined future, the rectification—and if necessary the future definition itself—must be revised.

## Concepts
- **future definition**: a concrete description of a state to be made real, constrained by physical, institutional, resource, time, and capability conditions.
- **feasibility**: whether the defined future can plausibly be implemented under real constraints.
- **terminal constraint**: a future condition used as an evaluation criterion for present meaning, time, action, and structure.
- **meaning rectification**: reevaluating the role of activities, experience, resources, and relationships relative to future conditions.
- **time rectification**: changing allocation, priority, and sequence rather than following urgency or inertia alone.
- **action rectification**: deciding what to continue, stop, advance, add, or test.
- **structural rectification**: changing roles, authority, institutions, or information flows when individual effort cannot resolve the mismatch.
- **future debt**: constraints and costs pushed into the future by present local optimization or postponement.
- **mismatch**: a gap between the present configuration and conditions required for the future definition.
- **revalidation**: testing implementation against terminal constraints and revising the configuration or future definition.

## Causal chain
Present-centered local optimization → urgency, inherited structures, sunk investment, and current metrics dominate → capabilities, time, and structural conditions needed later are postponed → define a feasible future state → inspect physical, institutional, resource, time, and capability constraints → extract the conditions required for that future as terminal constraints → compare present activities, resources, time allocation, actions, roles, authority, institutions, and information flows with those constraints → expose meaning/time/action/structure mismatches and future debt → reevaluate the meaning of current activities and resources → redesign time allocation and sequence → continue, stop, advance, or add actions → change roles, authority, institutions, or information flows where necessary → implement the new configuration → compare outcomes with the terminal constraints → revise the rectification or future definition when falsified.

## State model
- **present-local-optimum state**: current efficiency, urgency, and institutional inertia dominate judgment.
- **future-definition state**: a future condition is described explicitly.
- **feasibility-audit state**: reality constraints are checked.
- **terminal-constraint state**: future requirements become evaluation criteria for the present.
- **mismatch-visibility state**: gaps and future debt are identified.
- **meaning-rectification state**: present activities and resources are reinterpreted.
- **time-rectification state**: allocation and sequence change.
- **action-rectification state**: continuation, stopping, acceleration, and addition are redesigned.
- **structural-rectification state**: roles, authority, institutions, and information flows change.
- **implementation state**: the reconfiguration is applied.
- **revalidation/update state**: effects on future feasibility are tested and the design is revised.

## Applications
1. **business design**: use future customer value, revenue structure, and organizational capability to reassess current meetings, hiring, development order, and legacy activities.
2. **project design**: go beyond deadline backcasting by deciding what to stop, advance, or validate based on future conditions.
3. **career design**: derive learning, relationship building, work selection, and time allocation from capabilities required by a future role without treating that role as destiny.
4. **institutional design**: use future responsibility, authority, and information requirements to locate present institutional bottlenecks.
5. **organizational operation**: inspect whether current KPI optimization is eroding capabilities needed later and accumulating future debt.
6. **resource reallocation**: reduce currently valuable resources that do not support the future condition and invest in currently underused resources that do.

## Measurements and audit
- Is the future definition concrete enough to test for feasibility?
- Are physical, institutional, resource, time, and capability constraints explicit?
- Can the required terminal constraints be explained?
- Can present mismatches be separated into meaning, time, action, and structure?
- Can future debt be traced to concrete present choices?
- Did rectification actually change reasons for continuing, stopping, advancing, or adding activities?
- Are structural problems returned to roles, authority, institutions, or information flows instead of being reduced to individual effort?
- Can the effect of implementation on future feasibility be evaluated?
- Are inconvenient facts being retained rather than filtered out?
- Can the future definition itself be revised when evidence contradicts it?

## Validity conditions
The future definition must be reality-connected rather than aspirational fantasy, and its feasibility must be inspectable. Terminal constraints must enable a meaningful comparison with the present, reveal concrete mismatches in one or more of the four layers, and produce explainable configuration changes. Those changes must be implemented and tested for contribution to future feasibility. The future definition and terminal constraints must remain revisable under falsifying evidence.

## Failure conditions
The theory fails when the future is treated as prophecy, destiny, already-known information, or a physical cause traveling backward; when the future definition is wish fulfillment detached from constraints; when only confirming evidence is retained; when terminal constraints are made unrevisable; when mere task backcasting is labeled four-layer rectification; when changed meaning is confused with changed historical fact; or when structural mismatch is reduced to “try harder” at the individual level.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the theory's application when the future definition is infeasible under stated constraints, when rectification does not improve the feasibility of the future state, when four-layer changes fail to reduce the identified mismatch, or when an alternative future definition or existing design method consistently explains and improves the situation better. Also reconsider the independent scope of the theory if the use of terminal constraints and four-layer integration produces no observable implementation difference beyond ordinary planning or backcasting.

## Required distinctions
- terminal constraint / fate
- future definition / prediction or prophecy
- backward design / physical retrocausality
- meaning rectification / alteration of historical fact
- time rectification / reversal of physical time
- action rectification / simple task-list backcasting
- structural rectification / increased individual effort
- future debt / remaining tasks
- feasibility / inevitability
- falsifiable design condition / fixed wish

## Interpretation constraints
Do not interpret the theory through quantum causation, manifestation, destiny, information sent from the future, or physical time reversal. A future definition is a revisable design condition, not an outcome guarantee. Do not add unsupported realization probabilities, dates, or thresholds. Do not treat changes in meaning as changes in physical historical fact. Acknowledge overlap with backcasting while preserving the theory's explicit integration of meaning, time, action, and structure.
