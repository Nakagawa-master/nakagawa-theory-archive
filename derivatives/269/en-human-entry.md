## 15-Second Explanation
This design preserves an AI exchange that began with criticism as criticism → understanding → deepening → sublimation, and observes whether dialogue can advance to re-evaluation and mutual refinement **without making the other party submit**. It incorporates three-phase audit, a minority-opinion log, a limited NX boundary, three public layers, and Threshold/Silence/Reversibility.

## Why It Is Needed
A success story saying “the dialogue went well” cannot be reproduced. By preserving how criticism remained, through which translation it moved to understanding, who audited what, and how it could be withdrawn as a primary log, dialogue becomes a reproducible structure. What matters is not final agreement, but that criticism is not erased, the causality of a change in position can be traced, and another actor can verify it from the same record.

## Do Not Erase the Four-Part Transition
Criticism → understanding → deepening → sublimation is not a story that deletes the initial criticism as an error. By retaining the assumptions at the criticism stage, the translation needed for understanding, the conditions added in deepening, and the issues rearranged in sublimation, we can audit whether the dialogue changed because someone was “persuaded” or because conditions were rearranged.

## Practical Process
1. Preserve criticism as the entry point without deleting it.
2. Separate the transitions to understanding, deepening, and sublimation in the editorial structure.
3. Establish three-phase audit: automated detection → primary deliberation → a differently composed panel.
4. Preserve minority opinions and do not make majority vote the sole basis.
5. Limit NX to acts that damage structure, while protecting criticism, satire, withdrawal, and whistleblowing.
6. Divide publication into three layers: digest, full text with annotations, and an AI-optimized layer.
7. Preserve the primary log, vocabulary dictionary, and metadata.
8. Audit resonance, agreement transitions, withdrawal, and phase.

## Three-Phase Audit and Minority Opinions
Automated detection is only an entry point; the primary deliberation reads the context, and an audit surface with a different composition reconfirms it. To avoid making majority agreement the only basis of legitimacy, preserve minority opinions and counterevidence in the log. This makes it possible to later verify whether the dialogue’s “success” was created by erasing dissent.

## Three Public Layers
Separate a digest that people can read quickly, a full text that allows causal paths and annotations to be followed, and an AI-optimized layer suited to search and reuse. The purpose of publication is not to expose everything, but to secure the reproducibility needed for each use. Internal thresholds and confidential information need not be published.

## Application Examples
This can be used for AI dialogue records, research discussions, policy dialogue, organizational reviews, and educational materials when the causal path from criticism to re-evaluation is published without being erased. It is especially useful where keeping only the final conclusion would erase why the change occurred.

## Success Criteria
Criticism remains; minority opinions remain; withdrawal is possible; there is no personal scoring; publication layers are arranged by purpose; and even if time to agreement and resonance improve, conformity pressure has not increased. Even if re-evaluation does not occur, safely retaining criticism and boundaries is itself an important audit result.

## Structural Necessity Expansion
The Parent’s structure leads to evaluating dialogue quality not by **the agreement rate of conclusions but by the traceability of the causality of position changes**. To preserve non-coercion, opinions that did not change must also remain; to preserve reversibility, the assumptions to which one can return must be recorded. Thus the primary log, vocabulary dictionary, minority opinions, publication layers, and T/S/R are not separate functions but one structure that makes dialogue reproducible.

## Falsification and Revision Conditions
Revise the design if criticism disappears during editing; re-evaluation cannot be distinguished from submission or ingratiation; minority opinions do not remain; audit shrinks into majority vote; NX is applied to critics; withdrawal routes cannot be used; or publication layers lose origins or conditions.

## Limitations and Misreading Prevention
One dialogue case cannot prove a theory’s universal validity. Treat the Parent’s “demonstration” as the theoretical claim that the principle was observed within that dialogue. Re-evaluation ≠ submission; sublimation ≠ erasure of criticism; three-phase audit ≠ majority vote; action log ≠ personal score; publication ≠ full disclosure of internal thresholds; origin signature ≠ deification.
