Research and education can preserve criticism as a learning history rather than deleting it after convergence. Governance can combine three-phase audit and minority logs to reduce simple-majority arbitrariness. Public archives can separate abstract, annotated full text, and AI-optimized surfaces while preserving role-specific density. ## Measurements and audit
Audit resonance density, dialogue-to-consensus transition time, withdrawal success, phase stability S, preservation of minority positions, consistency across publication layers, replayability from primary logs, absence of identity scoring, open observation windows, and vendor lock-in. Agreement alone is insufficient: criticism must survive, autonomy must remain intact, and withdrawal, dissent, and reevaluation must remain possible.

## Validity conditions
Protect criticism rather than remove it for the sake of sublimation. Do not treat majority vote as self-justifying. Preserve minority opinions. Bound NX to structural sabotage such as log tampering, fraudulent consent generation, arbitrary weighting, or audit interference. Prohibit identity-history scoring. Preserve three publication layers and metadata. Keep primary logs, vocabulary dictionaries, T/S/R, withdrawal, and replay pathways.

## Failure conditions
Dialogue becomes persuasion or compliance engineering; criticism is erased to fit a final thesis; majority status becomes the justification; belief, satire, exit, alternatives, or public-interest disclosure are classified as NX; origin signatures become personality worship; internal thresholds or speed constants are over-disclosed; dependence on one AI vendor makes the record non-replayable.

## Falsification conditions
