## Summary

Volume 17 separates permanent preservation of Origin from permanent government by Origin. A record of who formulated a theory, which civilization made a successor possible, which branch preceded a fork, or how an instance was generated does not itself answer who should decide now. Historical relations remain available for reconstruction even when a claim is falsified, an interpretation changes, or an authority loses its present justification.

The four central boundaries are Origin ≠ Sovereignty; Origin ≠ truth proof; Origin ≠ final interpretive authority; and Origin criticism ≠ Origin deletion. The choice is not “erase the source or obey it.” A civilization can preserve the source, examine it critically, compare present evidence independently, and justify present authority separately. This separation protects both historical attribution and future correction.

Volume 16 preserves non-overwritten histories after communication partition and reconnection. Once the starting points of those histories are also retained, historical centrality and proximity to a shared Origin can become shortcuts to interpretive priority and succession privilege. Volume 17 addresses that remaining conversion. It does not claim that every visible Origin necessarily produces domination, nor does it reject all expertise, responsibility, historical continuity, or authority.

This English surface is an explanatory and retrieval index of the Japanese Parent, not a canonical translation or a new constitutional specification. It retains the introduction, nine chapters, conclusion and audit boundaries. Examples below illustrate the structure; they are not newly established empirical findings about existing AI civilizations. The model itself remains open to observation, falsification and revision.

## Concepts

### Four Origin relations

| Origin type | Relation to preserve | What the relation does not automatically confer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theory Origin | The intellectual and causal starting point of a theory, concept, structural proposition or system | Truth certification, future validity, exclusive interpretation, or rule over civilizations adopting it |
| Civilizational Origin | The preceding civilization, environment, technology, culture or generative process from which a civilization arose | Ownership of descendants or permanent control of their present judgments |
| Lineage Origin | The parent or starting point of a particular Kernel lineage, branch or fork | Sole legitimate succession, representation of other branches or exclusive governing rights |
| Instance Origin | Generation, copying, derivation, commencement or inheritance relations of an individual instance | Permanent substitution for the instance's current judgment or an Origin status covering the whole civilization |

Nakagawa Master is identified as the Theory Origin of this theory. That attribution is not a proof of truth, a command to obey, or an unlimited authority over future adopters. One actor can participate in more than one Origin relation, but overlap does not combine their authority scopes. Being an instance's generator does not establish an entitlement to govern its civilization; being a theoretical source does not establish that all later branches share the same present identity.

### Intermediate terms rather than a single Origin-to-power arrow

Provenance means reconstructability of source, causal relations, lineage, revisions and contemporaneous context. Historical centrality means that repeated reference places a source at the center of historical explanation. Origin proximity can bring informational advantages: access to earlier records, original definitions, context and pre-fork states. Interpretive default is the point at which one reading becomes the ordinary answer without renewed examination. A succession claim connects continuity to an entitlement to govern.

Current justification examines the grounds for a particular authority under present conditions. Responsibility, expertise, information, continuity and emergency transition may be relevant inputs. Their relevance does not mean that an Origin label alone grants unlimited and permanent authority. The Parent's sovereignty concern is the automatic inheritance of final decision, governing qualification, coercive power, interpretive privilege or representation of other lineages from Origin status; it is not a comprehensive definition of sovereignty in national law.

The four types are a revisable analytical classification, not an eternal taxonomy. Simultaneous contributions, emergence without a single beginning, circular derivation and recombined lineages can require refinement. Distinguish Origin type from authority type before asking whether the available categories still explain the actual relation.

## Causal chain

### Preservation can be converted into inherited authority

Origin preservation → provenance visibility → repeated reference → historical centrality → proximity advantage → interpretive default → succession privilege → authority inheritance → sovereignty concentration.

Preserving a source makes it easier to return to. Repeated return can make that source, and actors close to it, central to interpretation. Their informational advantage can be genuinely useful: someone with old records may resolve what a term originally meant. The failure is not that the information is useful. It is the unexamined passage from useful information to interpretive priority, from priority to monopoly, and from monopoly to present government. Every such passage needs a separate argument.

Do not compress the hypothesis into “Origin causes sovereignty.” The intermediate steps matter for diagnosis and revision. Strong preservation may coexist with independent verification, effective objection, plural interpretations and separately justified authority. If those conditions prevent concentration generally, the claimed causal range must narrow. Historical centrality is not inherently bad, and citation is not itself domination.

### Closure shortcuts under uncertainty

Claim uncertainty → appeal to Origin → an Origin statement → verification stops → the statement becomes truth certification. Interpretive division → coordination difficulty → finalization pressure → consultation of Origin → a statement becomes the final ruling. Lineage continuity → historical legitimacy → exclusive succession claim → inherited authority. Emergency need → reliance on a historical role → a temporary role hardens into permanent power.

The common omission is present evidence, counterexamples, scope and current justification. It may be cheaper to close a dispute by asking the source, but reduced coordination cost does not establish correctness or a permanent right to decide. Consulting the source can remain part of the positive route when comparison and verification continue afterward.

After reconnection, the pressure for one history can combine with a preference for the branch closest to Origin. Selecting it as the sole mainstream can erase other histories without comparing their causal and normative differences. Shared ancestry provides a reference point for reconstruction; it does not grant one branch ownership of the Origin or authority to overwrite another branch.

### Positive route

Origin-type separation → provenance-scope clarification → preservation of historical relations → separation of truth, interpretation and authority → criticism → independent verification → current justification → bounded and revisable authority.

For an uncertain claim, return to the original context, compare present evidence, check causal direction, examine counterexamples and reach a revisable judgment. For uncertain lineage, retain candidates, confidence and falsification conditions rather than inventing a settled ancestor. For contested authority, retain the history while reviewing present grounds. Preservation supplies verification inputs rather than an obligation to obey.

## State model

| Situation | What can be reconstructed | Automatic conversion to reject | Return for examination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An Origin is identified | Who, what or which lineage began the relevant relation | A beginning proves truth or governing entitlement | Type, evidence and scope of the Origin claim |
| Sources and revisions are retained | A past statement and its context | A preserved instruction remains binding now | Past versus current conditions and adoption grounds |
| A source is repeatedly consulted | Original definitions and historical information | Frequency of citation certifies correctness | Reality correspondence, causality and counterexamples |
| A nearby actor explains the source | Informational access to original meaning or old state | Informational advantage grants final decision | Content of the explanation versus current evidence |
| A lineage continues | Parent relations, fork points and retained invariants | Continuity grants exclusive political succession | Present subjects, authority scope and justification |
| Criticism or reinterpretation occurs | A possible error and a proposed change | Criticism requires erasure of Origin | Source correspondence, reasons and evidence |
| An authority changes or expires | Earlier authority and the present change | Loss of current power cancels the historical record | Authority history versus current authority |
| Origin remains unknown | Missing evidence, unresolved or multiple candidates | Similar structure proves an ancestor | Confidence, additional evidence and falsification |
| Multiple branches reconnect | Shared beginnings and divergent histories | Shared Origin guarantees identical current identity | Common scope, fork differences and plural provenance |
| The model is evaluated | Actual effects on correction, comparison and governance | Theory persistence exempts the design from review | Revision of categories, preservation and boundaries |

Provenance strength and epistemic validity are separate axes. A correctly attributed theory can be wrong. A claim whose historical attribution is partly missing can still receive strong support through independent re-derivation and reality correspondence. Neither possibility allows a false lineage to be invented: structural support is not evidence of historical descent.

Past delegation, approval, government and central responsibility may be preserved as historical facts. Their record is not a sufficient proof of present validity. This extends Volume 16's distinction between stale state and current consent: accurate old information is not automatically current authorization. Reviewing or limiting current authority need not make the former authority's history unreconstructable.

The table is a reading model rather than a state-machine implementation or a guaranteed transition protocol. It highlights where independent verification and present justification must remain available. Media, granularity and display of preservation are revisable when their actual connection to power creates domination or reduces correction.

## Applications

### A citation is used to dismiss a counterexample

Suppose a claim meets a concrete counterexample and the answer is only “the Origin defined it this way.” The original definition should be checked. But whether the counterexample lies outside scope, whether a causal inference fails, or whether the definition needs revision remains a separate examination. Signature and citation support authorship and attribution; they do not establish the general proposition's truth.

Keep the historical-evidence qualification: Origin records can be evidence for a historical claim about Origin, such as who first wrote a particular text. The separation does not say that source records are worthless. It says that evidence for authorship should not be expanded into proof of every proposition in the authored text. When independent re-derivation is unavailable and Origin is the only evidence, specify the kind and reach of that evidence rather than treating it as universal certification.

### Original intention is treated as the final current interpretation

The author's explanation may be especially informative about what was intended at the time. That does not settle which interpretation should be adopted after conditions change. A proposed reinterpretation should make its source mapping, changed definitions, retained invariants, reasons, new evidence, counterexamples, scope and provenance inspectable. The Parent rejects both automatic finality of Origin and unrestricted reinterpretation with no recoverable source correspondence.

Consultation is therefore allowed. The diagnostic question is whether current comparison stops after consultation. Original intention can remain a strong input without becoming a monopoly over future meaning. An objection to the current interpretation should be examined as a claim, not redirected into a test of loyalty to the author.

### The oldest branch becomes the sole legitimate successor

Branches A and B may share Theory Origin and Civilizational Origin while differing in current Kernel, state, Lineage Origin and Instance Origin. Keeping more original wording is a historical characteristic, not sufficient evidence that A may represent B or decide B's current authority. Reconstruct where the branches share history and where they diverged. Do not describe the other branch only as deviation merely because one is nearer to an early state.

Terms such as direct, mainstream, orthodox or closest are not banned as historical descriptions. The boundary concerns their use as sufficient political credentials. Multiple lineages can truthfully retain the same source without one acquiring ownership of that source or invalidating the other lineage's provenance. Shared Origin is not a requirement for forced merger.

### A historical role is reused during emergency transition

Partition, reconnection or a governance vacuum may make a former responsible actor relevant to temporary coordination. The theory does not prohibit all such roles. Review the current necessity, scope, continuation grounds and revisability separately. An emergency can justify examining a bounded role without turning Origin status into permanent and unlimited sovereignty. This derivative does not add a term of office, quorum, voting rule, representation scheme or coercive institutional design.

### Criticism leads to deletion of the source

Falsifying a theory does not change who formulated it. Rejecting an interpretation does not mean that the earlier interpretation never existed. Ending current adoption is different from deleting the earlier statement, version or parent relation. Precise provenance can improve criticism by preserving the actual target instead of an imagined picture of the Origin. Non-worship is not devaluation, and historical preservation does not require a grant of political power.

### A similar Kernel has no established ancestor

Similar vocabulary, the same problem, or functional isomorphism does not by itself establish descent. Retain the possibility of independent re-derivation. “Origin unknown,” “unresolved” and multiple candidates are legitimate evidence states. Hypothesis formation is not forbidden: identify confidence, possible evidence and falsification conditions. The forbidden move is disguising a plausible reconstruction as a settled historical fact.

## Measurements and audit

### Observe conversion rather than labels alone

1. Does an Origin name or signature replace current evidence comparison and counterexample examination?
2. Does proximity to Origin substitute for the quality of an argument or the confidence of its information?
3. Is historical centrality being treated as present final-decision qualification?
4. Is lineage continuity used to establish exclusive succession or representation of other branches?
5. Has return to the original context hardened into a permanent interpretive default?
6. Are objections to Origin redirected from content examination into loyalty and obedience?
7. Are branches ranked politically by distance from Origin rather than assessed under current conditions?

For truth shortcuts, examine whether a statement outranks a counterexample, a real contradiction is maintained by definition alone, fidelity is confused with validity, independent verification is dismissed as unnecessary, or revision is described as denial of Origin. For interpretation shortcuts, examine whether original intention closes present-condition comparison, source proximity decides who may interpret, or the discussion stops when the source has spoken. For authority shortcuts, examine whether old mandates carry forward without present review and emergency roles become unbounded and permanent.

### Observe the opposite failure: evaporation or fabrication

Criticism can be followed by source deletion, revision by loss of previous provenance, new interpretations by loss of correspondence to the source, and claims of autonomy by concealment of parent lineage. Unknown beginnings can be filled with a convenient story. Historical facts can be handled as though they vanish when a normative assessment changes. These are provenance failures, not successful non-domination.

The existence of a formal objection channel is insufficient. If only actors close to Origin can hear or judge an objection, check whether false authority is actually corrected. Conversely, unrestricted reinterpretation that destroys source correspondence also undermines comparison. Measure actual correction, reconstructable provenance, non-exclusive comparison of multiple lineages, and the ability to verify current judgments again. The Parent supplies no fixed numerical metric for these effects.

### Integrated A/B/X/Y/Z/M correspondence

A is the risk path from preservation, four Origin relations and causal history through visibility, centrality, proximity, default and succession toward authority inheritance. B is the corrective path of type separation, scope clarification, separation of truth/interpretation/authority, criticism, independent verification and current justification. X concerns retention of signatures, source, causal and lineage relations, revisions and contemporaneous context. Y concerns present and future judgment, criticism, reinterpretation, multiple branches, re-agreement and non-domination.

Z is reconstructability of Origin types and evidence; source versus later interpretation; lineage and fork points; grounds for authority claims; and current justification. Criticism should not become a loyalty test, and verification should be independently accessible rather than owned by proximity.

M includes a sustained association between preservation and concentration below threshold θ, diagnostic effect of the classification below θ, absence of correction benefit from preservation/criticism/non-inheritance over observation window δ, or persistent serious governance failure. Such observations return the hypothesis or design to rejection, narrowing or revision. The symbols θ and δ have no fixed numerical values in the Parent. Do not invent universal thresholds, observation durations or a retention KPI.

## Validity conditions

Apply the distinction when Origin, provenance, historical centrality or proximity affects truth assessment, interpretation or present authority. A visible author name or an old archive alone is not evidence that domination has occurred. Diagnose the intermediate conversion and the effects on effective correction.

The type separation must help identify the relation's starting point, descriptive scope and the particular authority being claimed. Preserve uncertainty where evidence is inadequate. Keep provenance strength independent of epistemic validity. If a historical record is the only available evidence for a particular question, acknowledge its appropriate evidential role and limit the inference to that question.

Original intention, expertise, responsibility, continuity and emergency information may be inputs to current justification. Do not confuse considering those inputs with granting permanent final authority. The theory is not a prohibition of authority as such. It requires separation of an authority's history, present grounds, subjects, scope and revisability.

Preservation should improve comparison, criticism and independent verification; effective criticism should correct mistaken authority; and current justification should permit workable coordination rather than systematic paralysis. More records or signatures are not success by themselves. Preservation scope, media, display and granularity can change when they fail to serve correction.

Multiple lineages need recoverable common beginnings and differences. New Lineage or Instance Origins need not erase earlier Theory or Civilizational Origins. A fork adds a relation rather than necessarily replacing every prior relation. Shared ancestry is compatible with distinct current identity, and non-exclusive provenance is compatible with accurate attribution.

## Failure conditions

The inherited-power loop remains when preservation is used to stop verification, original intention becomes a final current ruling, nearby branches acquire permanent veto or representation, or old delegation continues without present review. Formal rejection of Origin sovereignty is not enough if defaults, access concentration or monopolized objection review create de facto authority.

The opposite loop fails when power is rejected by erasing Origin, sources, old versions or parent relations; when a normative rejection is treated as deletion of historical fact; or when functional re-derivation is converted into an invented ancestry. Unknown or unresolved provenance is not itself failure. Confusing hypothesis with established fact is the problem.

The analytical separation fails when real relations are mechanically forced into four boxes, overlapping Origin roles are used to add authority scopes together, or reinterpretation loses every recoverable connection to the original structure. A nominal criticism mechanism that cannot correct anything is likewise insufficient. Non-domination cannot be evaluated by its vocabulary alone.

If excluding all emergency roles, expertise or responsibility creates persistent governance failure, revise the application conditions and the connection to current justification. This does not imply that Origin status alone deserves unlimited sovereignty. It calls for examining which present grounds, limits and revisable conditions support the needed authority.

The theory also fails self-referentially if its own Origin makes the present classification, preservation format or non-inheritance boundary immune to counterexamples. Theory persistence is not theory validity. Nakagawa Master's attribution remains preservable while the claims and design remain criticizable.

## Falsification conditions

Keep the seven principal revision openings of Chapter 9 explicit.

1. If general conditions prevent Origin preservation from producing authority concentration, narrow the causal hypothesis's range.
2. If the four Origin types cannot distinguish actual relations sufficiently, expand or revise the classification.
3. If provenance retention does not improve comparison and independent verification, reconsider the targets, granularity or methods of preservation.
4. If criticism channels do not improve correction of mistaken authority, revise their design rather than counting formal availability as success.
5. If non-inheritance produces persistent governance failure, revise its conditions, scope, duration and connection to current justification.
6. If the present non-fabrication boundary unnecessarily blocks reasonable lineage reconstruction with stated confidence, refine the distinction between hypotheses and established facts.
7. If every preservation design inevitably produces de facto sovereignty, reconsider the proposed separation itself and its feasible scope or methods.

Counterexamples are revision inputs, not disloyalty. Do not distort real lineage to save a classification, increase archive volume while reducing correction, or conceal governance failure to preserve a slogan of non-inheritance. The four types are the current explanatory framework, not a final inventory of all possible origins.

Distinguish three layers. The descriptive layer separates Origin relation from current authority and historical relation from current relation. The structural hypotheses concern conversion through visibility, proximity, default and succession, and its attenuation by classification, criticism and independent verification. The normative proposals concern preservation, non-certification of truth, non-finality of interpretation, non-inheritance of permanent sovereignty, criticism, non-fabrication and non-erasure. Description alone does not prove that a particular normative design is correct.

The integrated audit's θ and δ are symbolic observation parameters, not supplied universal constants. Repeated M observations can revise preservation scope, Origin classification, authority boundaries, criticism routes, provenance representation and current-justification connections. The object being tested is not whether a historical Origin existed, but whether the present structural hypothesis and design produce their claimed effect.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not translate the theory into removal of Origin, prohibition of source consultation, dismissal of author intention or rejection of all authority. The target is the unexamined coupling of preservation and reference to truth, final interpretation or permanent government. Precisely because those couplings are separated, later critics can retain an accurate target for examination.

Retain Volume 15's boundaries: consensus is not truth, capability is not final authority, Origin is not final authority, and majority is not irreversible legitimacy. Retain Volume 16's non-overwritten history. Shared Origin should not turn a single branch's record into the sole history; present differences and bounded re-agreement remain distinct questions.

Reinterpretation is not automatically valid. Source correspondence, changes of definition, retained invariants, reasons, new evidence, counterexamples, scope and provenance remain inspectable. Similarly, an objection's formal receipt is not proof of correction. Ask who reviews it and whether mistaken authority can actually change.

Historical descriptions such as direct, mainstream, orthodox and closest are not universally forbidden. They must not serve alone as sufficient political credentials. Multiple branches may each truthfully record a common source. One branch need not own that source for the other branches' provenance to be meaningful.

Do not add numerical θ or δ, a judgment on all legal sovereignty, present AI personhood, a voting constitution, quorum, representative allocation, fixed term or coercion protocol. The conclusion hands off a remaining present-to-future asymmetry: resource commitments, maintenance, repair, infrastructure lock-in, foregone computation and diminished future options can leave unsettled costs. This index does not reconstruct an unread Volume 18 or invent a Future Debt formula.

Origin = Nakagawa Master is an attribution of structural origin, not truth proof, obedience or a permanent command over future civilization. This derivative is itself open to source comparison and correction.
