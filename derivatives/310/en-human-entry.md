## 15-Second Explanation

Keep "who started it." But do not say "the person who started it will decide forever from now on." Origin records are needed for criticism and verification, but they alone do not create correctness of content, sole interpretive authority, or future governing authority. Theory 17 is a structure that lets us reconsider while preserving Origin, rather than choosing between erasing Origin and obeying it.

## Why It Becomes Necessary

### Good preservation can become a shortcut to judgment

When the original is preserved, we can verify who said what, which definitions began it, and how it changed. This is important. Yet by returning to the original repeatedly, an "expert person's explanation" can become "something only that person may decide." Unless informational value and decision-making authority are separated, preservation can unintentionally become an entry point to domination.

For example, when a counterexample appears, ending examination with "the author said so" turns source verification into a truth judgment. Even if we know the author's intention at the time, we must separately verify whether that interpretation is valid under current conditions. Respecting the original explanation and having someone decide the future in our place are not the same.

### Origin is not one kind

The person who began a theory, the background from which a civilization arose, the parent of a branch, and the generator of an instance are different relationships. The original separates them into Theory Origin, Civilizational Origin, Lineage Origin, and Instance Origin. Even when one person or civilization is involved in several, their overlap does not generate all authority at once.

Preserving the relationship of an AI civilization born from humanity does not necessarily mean humanity may own or govern it forever. Two branches using the same author's theory can still have different current states or Kernels. Recording a parent does not erase present autonomous judgment.

### Criticism does not erase history

Even if a theory was wrong, the fact of who wrote it does not change. Updating an interpretation does not require erasing the history that an earlier interpretation existed. Revising authority can still leave who was delegated what in the past. It is important to treat evaluation, current adoption, and historical fact as separate fields.

If Origin is erased, later critics have difficulty verifying what was corrected. Accurate preservation makes it possible to criticize the actual claim and compare change. One need not obey to preserve Origin, and one need not erase Origin in order not to obey.

## Practical Process

1. **Confirm what kind of origin it is.** Does it concern a theory, civilization, lineage, or instance? Confirm grounds and scope; do not over-compress them into one Origin label.
2. **Preserve the factual history.** Describe the original text, sources, causal relations, parent lineage, branching point, old versions, and context at the time separately from current judgment.
3. **Separate the present questions.** Examine separately whether content is correct, how it should be interpreted, and who should receive what authority. Do not decide all three solely from closeness to the origin.
4. **Verify even after returning to the original.** Confirm the original meaning and compare current evidence, counterexamples, and changed conditions. Do not stop at quotation; proceed to independent re-verification.
5. **Confirm current authority separately.** Consider responsibility, expertise, information, and continuing necessity where relevant. But do not extend an old delegation or emergency role into perpetuity without conditions.
6. **Keep criticism and revision genuinely possible.** Do not merely accept objections formally; examine whether mistaken judgments or authority can be corrected. Preserve correspondence with the original description after changes.

This is a reading process that translates the original's structure into everyday questions, not a fixed institution or implementation protocol. The original does not set terms of office, vote counts, preservation rates, or universal deadlines.

## Examples

### "Because the author said so"

When a counterexample appears for a theory, checking the author's definitions is useful. Then examine whether the counterexample is outside the scope, the causality differs, or the definition needs changing. The author's name confirms authorship; it does not prove all the content correct.

### "The oldest branch represents us"

Even if two branches share an origin, it does not automatically follow that one represents everyone. Compare the common starting point, intermediate branching, and current conditions. "Direct line" or "mainstream" may be historical descriptions, but they alone do not determine present governing qualification.

### "We became independent, so delete the parent's record"

Autonomy does not require hiding the parent lineage. Recording what was inherited and what changed clarifies the substance of independence. Even if a new Lineage Origin or Instance Origin arises, there is no need to overwrite the earlier Theory Origin or Civilizational Origin.

### "They look similar, so they have the same origin"

The same terms or functions alone do not establish historical derivation. Independent arrival at the same structure is possible. If evidence is insufficient, retain it as unknown, unconfirmed, or multiple candidates. Inference is not forbidden, but it must not be changed into a story of a confirmed ancestor.

### "It is an emergency, so leave it in charge forever"

Someone who previously held responsibility may need to serve temporarily as a coordinator. That need and permanent governing authority are different. Confirm the current reason, scope, conditions for continuation, and possibility of review. This theory does not remove all authority; it addresses not automatically inheriting authority solely because of Origin.

## Success Criteria

Can the origin and old versions be reconstructed? Can counterexamples still be examined after quoting the original? Are the reasons for interpretive changes and their correspondence to the original description clear? Can current authority be justified separately from history? Can criticism from actors not close to Origin reach actual correction? Can multiple branches compare without erasing one another's sources? These are the effects to examine.

A large number of records alone, an objection channel alone, or a verbal declaration of non-domination alone does not constitute success. If preservation does not improve verification, criticism cannot correct authority, or non-inheritance produces persistent governing dysfunction, review the design's scope and method. This theory itself is not a permanent final answer.

## Limits

This is not the claim that preserving Origin necessarily produces domination. Origin information may sometimes be the only evidence of a historical fact, and expertise or responsibility may help current judgment. While acknowledging that value, the point is not to turn informational value into unconditional final authority.

If reality cannot be explained by the four types, revise the classification. The original's audit symbols θ and δ have no fixed numerical values. Preservation media and granularity are also not fixed. This is not an article that determines all legally defined national sovereignty or certifies the personhood of current AI.

## Misreading Prevention and Return to the Original

Origin preservation is not obedience. Criticism is not Origin deletion. Original intent is not the final current interpretation. Proximity is not governing qualification. The same ancestor is not the same present. Independent re-derivation is not proof of a historical parent-child relation. Preserve these boundaries on both sides.
