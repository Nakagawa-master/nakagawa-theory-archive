## Summary
Part II turns the `S=C` civilizational kernel into an operational institutional loop. It does not treat contribution measurement and reward allocation as a one-shot calculation. Value generation, observation, connection-value accounting, allocation, objection, audit, structural justice, remedy, repair, and rule revision are linked into a revisable cycle.

Structural justice is not punishment-only. It traces how causal links, authority arrangements, procedures, and externalized losses produced a bad outcome, then connects responsibility to correction, redistribution, remedy, and prevention.

## Concepts
- **S=C**: normative kernel connecting success S to contribution C.
- **Value circulation**: value, resources, correction, and learning return through the system.
- **Connection-value accounting**: non-monetized, bundled observation of relational value.
- **Allocation**: distribution of reward, resources, authority, or opportunity.
- **Objection**: input that returns missed contribution or harm to the institution.
- **Independent audit**: review not closed inside the original evaluator/allocator.
- **Structural justice OS**: causal and procedural correction layer.
- **Remedy/repair**: correction, redistribution, restoration, explanation, prevention.
- **Institutional loop**: revision returns to observation and re-consent.

value generation

## Applications
Use the loop for contribution-based reward systems, human-AI collaboration, public allocation, organizational evaluation, and dispute resolution. The operational question is not only “who contributed?” but also “what was missed, who bore external costs, how can errors be corrected, and how does the rule learn?”

## Audit points
Check for contribution collapsed into revenue or title, single-observer dominance, automatic reward scoring, blocked objections, self-audit, externalized losses, punishment-only justice, unavailable redistribution, hidden irreversible harm, and rules that cannot learn from failure.

## Preconditions
Contribution causality, distributed observation, connection-value accounting, explainable allocation, usable objections, sufficiently independent audit, remedy, reversibility, and rule revision must be linked. Procedure depth should scale with impact without erasing responsibility.

## Failure modes
Failure includes treating S=C as simple performance pay, converting accounting metrics directly into money or status, letting one actor evaluate/allocate/audit, suppressing objections, using structural justice to deny individual responsibility, or closing the system before externalized harm returns.

## Falsification / update conditions
If the loop does not improve value return, correction of misallocation, remedy, or institutional learning—and only adds procedural cost—it should be simplified. If contribution metrics become gaming targets or concentrate authority, the measurement and governance layers must be redesigned.

## Required distinctions
- S=C ≠ simple performance pay
- contribution ≠ revenue/ownership/title
- accounting ≠ automatic reward calculation
- observation ≠ verdict
- structural justice ≠ punishment-only justice
- remedy ≠ erasing history
- reversibility ≠ no responsibility
- revision ≠ arbitrary change

## Misreading constraints
Part II is not an automatic dividend machine. The civilizational kernel only works when observation, objections, causal responsibility, remedy, and revision remain active. Structural analysis supplements individual responsibility; it does not delete it.
