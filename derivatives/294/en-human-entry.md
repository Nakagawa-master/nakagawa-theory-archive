## 15-Second Explanation
Part II makes the institutional loop for operating the new civilization equation `S=C`: value generation → observation → connection value accounting → allocation → objection → audit → structural justice → remedy and repair → institutional update → re-observation. Measurement and reward do not end the process; errors, externalized losses, objections, remedies, and rule changes return to the loop.

## Why the Loop Is Needed
Every evaluation system makes mistakes: measurable results may dominate, support work may disappear, and burdens may be externalized. Treating one evaluation as final lets errors propagate into rewards, authority, and later evaluations. Reassessment, reallocation, and rule change must therefore be reachable.

## How to Read S=C
`S=C` is not “calculate contribution points and pay that score.” It is a normative core for a society where contribution C connects correctly to success S. Observe who contributed, who observed, what measurement missed, who received the burden, and whether error can be reversed.

## Practical Process
1. Record causal value generation beyond sales.
2. Use multiple observation points.
3. Read connection value as a bundle including consent and reversibility, not a single score.
4. Keep allocation reasons traceable.
5. Receive objections and return omissions or harm to the system.
6. Investigate rules, authority, evaluation, and externalization through structural justice.
7. Provide correction, reallocation, explanation, and recovery.
8. Update indicators, procedures, and authority and reach renewed agreement.

## Examples of Application
Return behind-the-scenes support and downstream burden to organizational evaluation. In AI collaboration include correction work, responsibility, and user impact, not output count alone. In public systems prioritize functioning objection, audit, remedy, and institutional learning over protecting the first allocation.

## Success Criteria
Causality is traceable; observation is distributed; accounting is not an automatic reward score; allocations are explainable; objections reach audit; externalized losses return; errors can be reassessed and reallocated; structural justice connects to repair and prevention; the institution can update and re-agree.

## Limitations
Too many loops can make every decision wait for review. Adjust audit and appeal density to impact. Not every loss can be restored; record irreversible loss and return it to future institutional change.

## Misreading Prevention
Structural justice does not mean nobody is responsible. State individual responsibility where it exists while correcting institutional causes. The minimum core is **operating S=C as a reversible institutional loop including objection, remedy, and update, not as a one-time formula**.
