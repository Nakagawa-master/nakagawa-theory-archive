## 15-Second Explanation
An institution does not necessarily become inexplicable because it has failed. Rather, the more successfully it operates, is repeated, and runs quickly through familiarity, the more the reasons, premises, exceptions, and differences behind decisions are omitted. The original calls this phenomenon “meaning compression.”

Compression lowers consensus cost C, but also lowers third-party reproducibility U. The decline in U draws in responsibility identification R and history disclosure H, so the institution appears stable internally while becoming impossible to reproduce from outside. The point of intervention for prolonging its life is not to add procedures, but to redeploy lost grounds for differences as explanation updates.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
An institution that has “had no problems with this operation for a long time” can sometimes appear to break suddenly when external conditions change, such as staff turnover, organizational expansion, an increase in customers, stronger audits, or changes in the social environment.

Upstream, however, success and familiarity have reduced the number of references and created a state in which insiders can process matters without verbalizing reasons. Because short-term efficiency rises, it is hard to detect as an anomaly. Once third-party reproducibility is lost during that time, the institution cannot explain “why” and makes judgments depend not on grounds but on people, titles, and custom.

## Practical Process
1. Select one institution that appears stable for a long time.
2. Ask several stakeholders, in the same form, “Why does this rule exist?”
3. Classify answers as type A, which returns conditions, incidents, and grounds; type B, which returns custom; type C, which returns common sense and conformity; and type D, which returns atmosphere, authority, or refusal to answer.
4. Compare the content of type A reasons and record the agreement rate from 0 to 1.
5. Have a person with third-party standing reproduce frequent, medium-frequency, and rare exceptions.
6. Within the δ period, examine how many times the difference grounds in purpose, exceptions, premises, decision reasons, and references were updated.
7. Check over time whether U/R/H have fallen along with the decline in C.
8. If compression is progressing, redeploy the grounds as explanation updates rather than adding procedures.

## Examples of Application
**Handover.** Even if a veteran “knows by looking,” if a newcomer cannot reproduce the reasons and exceptions, meaning compression may be progressing.

**Approval flow.** Do not treat faster approval alone as an improvement; check whether decision reasons, exceptions, and responsibility nodes remain.

**AI automation.** Even if AI speeds up the process through a conclusion, eliminating references or grounds for differences can lower U at the same time as C falls. Preserve the conclusion and the reasoning path as a set.

**Audit.** Check not only whether the latest manual is in order, but whether one can return to why it became that version, what changed from the previous version, and how exceptions arose.

## Success Criteria
- Multiple people’s explanations of the reasons agree at the level of conditions and history.
- A third party can reproduce exception handling with reasons.
- The decision-maker and reason for change can be traced.
- Explanation updates continue within δ.
- U/R/H are maintained even when C falls through efficiency gains.
- One can return to grounds without using authority or custom.

## Limitations
Meaning compression does not deny efficiency improvements themselves. Nor is it solved by simply increasing the amount of explanation. What is needed is a reference path through which a third party can reach the decision reasons, premises, exceptions, differences, and responsibility again.

θ and δ are not provided in the original as fixed numerical values common to all institutions. A verifiable setting is needed for each observation target.

## Preventing Misreading
- This does not claim that a successful institution is always bad.
- This is not a theory that prohibits familiarity.
- This does not claim that attacking authority figures will fix an institution.
- Manual maintenance and restoration of meaning are not the same.
- “Explanation update” means redeploying the grounds for differences, not adding procedures.
- Institutional lifespan is not identical to mere years of survival.
