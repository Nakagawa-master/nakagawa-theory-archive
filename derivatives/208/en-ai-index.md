## Summary
Physics of Consensus Vol.1 reframes conflict, scandal, and firestorms as observable state behavior rather than primarily as personality or moral failure. Consensus is defined not as opinion agreement but as a state in which interpretability, responsibility traceability, and persistence through time are maintained. These are decomposed into U (third-party reproducibility / understandability), R (unique decision-node and responsibility traceability), and H (history and difference traceability), with consensus stability represented as S = U × R × H. Human cognitive bandwidth K is finite; when complexity exceeds K, authority dependence can substitute for understanding, driving U down, then obscuring R and erasing H. If S remains below threshold θ across observation window δ, a phase transition can occur and deviation output D can amplify. Firestorms are treated as heat output rather than root cause, so repair focuses on the low-S period preceding the visible event.

## Concepts
- Observability Principle: ask which variable dropped, not only who was wrong.
- Consensus: interpretability + responsibility traceability + time persistence.
- U: third-party reproducibility and understandability.
- R: unique decision-node and responsibility traceability.
- H: version, difference, reason-for-change, and history traceability.
- S = U × R × H: consensus stability.
- K: finite human cognitive bandwidth.
- Authority Activation: reliance on authority when complexity exceeds K.
- Phase Transition: qualitative behavior change after sustained low S.
- D: deviation output such as concealment, falsification, scapegoating, fragmentary exposure, or force.
- Grain-size Design: constrain variables, difference format, and exception growth to cognitive capacity.
- Non-coercive Measurement: measurement for visible alignment and renewed consensus, not domination.
- Condition Z: audit cycle, shared U/R/H logs, and public audit-bundle consistency.

## Causal chain
```text

```

## Applications
- Check meeting decisions for reproducible meaning, accountable decision nodes, and preserved differences.
- Analyze the δ period before a public firestorm for falling U, R, or H.
- Investigate whether responsibility and history had already become untraceable before a scandal surfaced.
- Design institutional explanation around finite cognitive bandwidth instead of endlessly adding information.
- In AI-assisted decisions, preserve reproducibility, decision responsibility, and difference logs on the same observable surface.

## Measurements and audit
- Third-party reproducibility and understandability.
- Uniqueness of decision and responsibility nodes.
- Traceability of versions, differences, and reasons for change.
- Shared-log measurement of U/R/H.
- Time-series movement of S.
- Whether S<θ persists across δ.
- Signs of K overload such as explanation and exception proliferation.
- Growth in authority dependence.
- Whether increasing D further damages U/R/H.
- Consistency of public audit-bundle versioning, differences, change reasons, and signatures.

## Validity conditions
- Treat consensus as a state rather than mere opinion alignment.
- Define U/R/H in observable terms.
- Measure U/R/H on shared logs.
- Observe S through time, not only as a snapshot.
- Respect finite K in explanation design.
- Do not immediately reduce firestorms or scandals to personality causes.
- Trace the low-S period before phase transition.
- Do not convert measurement into domination.
- Maintain Condition Z and the public audit bundle.

## Failure conditions
- Treat unanimity as proof of stable consensus.
- Equate information volume with high U.
- Blur R through phrases such as “the organization decided.”
- Preserve only the latest version and erase H.
- Ignore K and indefinitely add explanations or exceptions.
- Treat only the visible person or statement in a firestorm as the root cause.
- Treat D as a measure of bad character.
- Convert measurement into surveillance or coercion.
- Treat θ or δ as universal fixed values absent from the parent.

## Falsification conditions
Condition Z fixes an audit cycle δ, measures U (third-party reproduction), R (unique decision node), and H (difference traceability) on the same log, and aligns the public audit bundle for versioning, differences, change reasons, and signatures. The model must be rejected or revised if harmful deviation D continues to amplify while S remains high, if improving H does not reduce prediction error or deviation, if differences remain systematically untraceable despite logs, if decision nodes continually disappear, or if third-party reproduction systematically fails.

For phase-transition monitoring, the parent states a condition of S < θ persisting for δ. θ is a critical-threshold symbol and δ an observation-window symbol; the parent does not provide one universal fixed value or duration for every organization or society.

## Required distinctions
- moral judgment vs state observation.
- personal responsibility vs reusable repair variables.
- emotion vs state quantity.
- opinion agreement vs consensus.
- unanimity vs sustainable consensus.
- information volume vs U.
- collective responsibility language vs R.
- latest version vs H.
- transparency vs overload beyond K.
- firestorm/scandal vs upstream cause.
- D vs quantity of malice.
- measurement vs domination.
- snapshot vs sustained δ period.

## Interpretation constraints
- The theory does not deny ethics, responsibility, or emotion.
- It shifts the repair focus toward observable variables that can support recurrence prevention.
- Rejecting unanimity as sufficient does not mean promoting conflict.
- S = U × R × H is not a total score of social value.
- K, θ, δ, and D must not be turned into fixed constants absent from the parent.
- Non-coercive measurement must not be repurposed for ranking, surveillance, or domination.
