## Summary
Real Estate Market OS Vol.4 converts the calculable decision board established in Vol.3 into a board that can be socially guaranteed without leaving responsibility unassigned. AI is confined to a calculation layer for collection, normalization, primary computation, pattern detection, and visualization of condition differences; professionals, finance, and public institutions are connected as responsibility layers. AI may present the conditions of an option but may not command a final choice or perform individualized legal judgment. Professionals are repositioned not as advertisers but as Guarantors who accept authenticity, explainability, and update responsibility within explicitly bounded domains. Outputs are classified as General / Estimate / Conditional, errors are separated into Input / Model / Interpretation / Operational layers, and uncertain or exceptional cases are escalated to humans with context preserved. Trust is produced structurally through role separation, responsibility boundaries, auditing, and update governance, and is continuously audited under Condition Z with T/S/R = Transparency / Separation / Responsibility.

## Concepts
- Calculation vs Guarantee: separation between computational possibility and socially accountable usability.
- AI Calculation Layer: collection, normalization, primary calculation, pattern detection, and comparison visualization.
- Responsibility Layer: humans, professionals, finance, and institutions that can accept responsibility.
- Boundary Fixing: explicit permitted and prohibited zones for AI.
- Guarantor: a professional who accepts authenticity, explainability, and update responsibility within a defined domain.
- Output Strength: General / Estimate / Conditional.
- Four Error Layers: Input / Model / Interpretation / Operational Error.
- Escalation: transfer of uncertain, exceptional, or individualized matters to a responsible human with context intact.
- Financial Connection: placing “economically buyable” and “financeable” reasoning on aligned assumptions without collapsing them into one value.
- Trust Infrastructure: structural trust through ownership, audit, update, and incident decomposition.
- Condition Z: audit cycle, T/S/R, and consistency of the public audit bundle.

## Causal chain
```text

```

## Applications
- Attach assumptions, uncertainty bands, sensitivities, unresolved points, and professional review routes to AI cash-flow outputs.
- Use AI to organize tax, registration, boundary, building, or financing issues while transferring individualized judgments to responsible professionals.
- Place credit limits, debt-service resilience, and required equity beside Market OS economic reasoning.
- When an incident occurs, distinguish data, model, interpretation, and operational causes rather than labeling everything an AI error.
- Connect professionals as accountable reviewers, auditors, update owners, and escalation endpoints rather than advertising slots.

## Measurements and audit
- Are AI outputs visually distinct from commands or guarantees?
- Are General / Estimate / Conditional outputs labeled?
- Do Estimates include uncertainty, error range, and sensitivity?
- Do Conditional outputs include assumptions and recomputation triggers?
- Are permitted and prohibited AI domains traceable?
- Is each professional's responsibility scope and update duty explicit?
- Can the four error layers be separated after an incident?
- Is context preserved during escalation?
- Are finance and public/legal boundaries visible on the decision board?
- Is there multi-party audit rather than single-party control?
- Are logic changes traceable by time, reason, and responsible actor?
- Can incident rate, deviation rate, unexplainable-output rate, rework, and decision time be observed?

## Validity conditions
- Do not make AI the final decision maker or responsible subject.
- Separate computation from guarantee.
- Fix prohibited AI zones before expanding capability.
- Connect professionals to explicitly bounded guarantee domains.
- Display output strength and uncertainty.
- Escalate unresolved, exceptional, and individualized matters.
- Preserve assumptions and context during escalation.
- Do not absorb financial, administrative, or legal authority into private AI.
- Maintain multi-party audit and logic-update audit.
- Continuously verify T/S/R and the public audit bundle.

## Failure conditions
- Using “AI said so” as a guarantee or liability shield.
- AI commanding or conclusively deciding individualized cases.
- AI substituting for regulated professional judgment.
- Treating professionals as advertising banners while leaving guarantee scope vague.
- Reducing responsibility design to disclaimer text.
- Displaying Estimates as confirmed facts.
- Hiding unresolved conditions to force a calculation through.
- Losing context in escalation.
- Conflating economically buyable with financeable.
- Disguising administrative or legal coercive authority as private AI functionality.
- Collapsing all incidents into “AI error.”
- Allowing a single sponsor to control calculation logic.

## Falsification conditions
The parent places an audit cycle, T/S/R = Transparency / Separation / Responsibility, and consistency of the public audit bundle under Condition Z. If indicators such as incident rate, deviation rate, or unexplainable-output rate remain persistently beyond threshold θ, or if phenomena M such as responsibility gaps, amplified misuse, escalation failure, or pressure to manipulate logic appear during observation window δ, the hypotheses, allocations, boundaries, or audit method of the responsibility-distribution architecture become subject to rejection or revision. No universal fixed θ or δ value is presented.

## Required distinctions
- calculable ≠ socially guaranteed
- AI calculation ≠ AI responsibility
- option conditions ≠ decision delegation
- General information ≠ individualized judgment
- Estimate ≠ confirmed value
- Conditional ≠ guarantee
- Guarantor ≠ advertiser
- professional connection ≠ indiscriminate handoff
- distributed responsibility ≠ vague responsibility
- economically buyable ≠ financeable

- private AI support ≠ public/legal coercion
- disclaimer ≠ responsibility architecture

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not generalize the parent's phrase “AI is an irresponsible calculator” into rejection of AI use; it fixes the structural boundary that AI itself cannot bear social/legal responsibility.
- Do not turn Guarantor into an unconditional guarantor of every AI output; coverage must be domain-bounded and explicit.
- Do not reinterpret distributed responsibility as responsibility avoidance.
- Do not convert “trust is a cost reduction” into psychological manipulation for faster closing; it refers to reducing uncertainty, rework, and unexplainable outcomes.
- Do not give sponsor professionals a right to manipulate values; the parent requires multi-party audit and protection against manipulation pressure.
- Do not expand finance/public connection into institutional control by private AI.
- Preserve T/S/R in this parent as Transparency / Separation / Responsibility.
