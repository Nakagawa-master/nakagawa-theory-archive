## Summary
A transmission architecture that treats tacit knowledge not merely as undocumented know-how but as an acquired capacity to sense causal flow. It externalizes that capacity across perception → language → action. The objective is not to copy an expert's answer but to let another person reconstruct judgment in a new situation. Structural rhythm and Threshold / Silence / Reversibility constrain misapplication, overgeneralization, and rigidification.

## Concepts
Tacit knowledge; transmission; inheritance structure; perception; linguistic encoding; action connection; structural rhythm; externalization of embodied knowledge; Threshold; Silence; Reversibility; reconstructability; origin return.

## Causal chain
Experience accumulates tacit knowledge → result-only documentation drops perceptual causality → observation points are extracted → causal relations are encoded in language → language is tied to action conditions → rhythm and exceptions are retained → T/S/R governs boundaries → recipients reconstruct judgment in novel cases → transmission becomes more reproducible and revisable.

## State model
`tacit_experience → perception_extraction → causal_language → action_connection → rhythm_and_exception_preservation → TSR_boundary_control → recipient_reconstruction → reproducible_revisable_transmission`

## Applications
Skill transfer, sales practice, organizational handovers, education, and human-AI co-creation. Preserve observation points, branching conditions, exceptions, stop conditions, and repair paths rather than merely copying successful procedures.

## Measurements and audit
Audit whether recipients can reconstruct judgment on unseen cases, explain causal grounds, detect exceptions, stop appropriately, reduce expert dependency, return to primary records, and improve editing time without degrading reproducibility.

## Validity conditions
Perception, language, and action must remain distinguishable yet connected. Causal direction, exceptions, and structural rhythm must be retained. Complete verbalization must not be assumed. T/S/R and a return path to primary experience or records must exist.

## Failure conditions
Saving conclusions only; flattening tacit knowledge into fixed procedures; equating document volume with transmission quality; deleting exceptions; universalizing local know-how; preserving expert authority instead of transferable structure; making translated documentation irreversible.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the architecture if recipients cannot reconstruct judgments in new cases, misapplication rises, causal direction is repeatedly misunderstood, expert dependency remains, or shorter compilation time produces lower reproducibility.

## Required distinctions
Tacit knowledge ≠ merely undocumented information; perception ≠ conclusion; verbalization ≠ more text; action connection ≠ rote procedure; structural rhythm ≠ personal habit; transmission ≠ preservation of wording; reconstruction ≠ repetition of the same answer.

## Interpretation constraints
This derivative does not claim that all tacit knowledge can be completely verbalized. Do not invent fixed numerical thresholds. Do not convert conditional knowledge into universal law. Silence is not refusal to explain; Reversibility is not avoidance of responsibility.
