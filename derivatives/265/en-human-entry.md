## 15-second explanation
This theory treats the gap between "I understand when I hear the explanation, but cannot do it in the field" as a **problem of transmission structure**, not of the amount of knowledge. Tacit knowledge is not merely a "veteran's intuition"; it is the ability to read causal channels and choose what not to do, which layer to touch, and how far to maintain contradictions as tension. The rhythm is placed into the three layers of **recognition → language → action** and the training process of **observation → imitation → reconstruction**, so that others can regenerate judgment in their own context.

## Why is it needed?
If only results and successful procedures are saved, the differences experts saw beforehand, the timing of non-intervention, layer selection, exceptions, and handling of tension disappear. Documents remain, but judgment becomes impossible when conditions change.

Conversely, if we try to turn all tacit knowledge into fixed procedures, context-dependent knowledge dies. The Parent Source describes tacit knowledge as the three-way combination of **economy of choice, multilayeredness, and tension maintenance**, and says what is needed is not a linear manual but a transmission structure that retains the rhythm while changing form according to the situation.

## Practical process
1. **Recognition layer｜Structure over Story**: Determine "what is different" before what is similar. Organize factors that amplify or buffer differences, and layers that may / must not be touched.
2. **Language layer｜Questions & Silence**: Run the small cycle of "definition → question → silence → hypothesis." Keep questions strong enough to activate thought internally, not to force an answer, and treat silence as compilation time. The Parent Source gives examples such as asynchronous 48–72 hours and 3 minutes in a meeting, but they are not fixed universal values.
3. **Action layer｜Protocols & Reversibility**: Conduct small, staged, reversible trials. Prioritize avoiding fatal damage over proving success, and separate public logs from compilation logs.
4. **Observation → imitation → reconstruction**: Observe the expert's timing of "stopping, delaying, and bridging," imitate the interval rather than the result, and finally redesign the placement of intervals in one's own context.
5. **Prepare forms**: Use dual tables of contents for external / internal use, definition cards with meaning, opposite, scope of application / non-application, and boundary examples, plus silence cards and reversible cards.
6. **Run the procedure**: Proceed in the order diagnosis → translation → experiment → audit.
7. **Audit and revise**: Treat reproducibility rate, compilation time, deviation detection rate, silence ratio, reversibility cost, and learning retention rate as an observation bundle, not a single score.

## Examples of application
### Public policy
Stage participation layers rather than making immediate full disclosure the only answer. Do not connect reactions directly to finalizing policy; create a cooling window and retain a reversible route such as trial ordinance → time-limited enforcement → re-review.

### Corporate operations
Separate public minutes and decision notes, and do not settle judgments too quickly. Examples of rollback design include a silence period focused on debt repayment and reorganization, feature flags, data migration, and contract cancellation.

### Education and training
Make observation → imitation → reconstruction part of the curriculum and place critique → revision → resubmission into the evaluation loop. Create a state in which people can verbalize not only "why it worked" but also "why there are situations where it does not apply."

### Research and development
Hold competing hypotheses in parallel and do not rush to unify them. Share failure conditions, how to return, and learning points, and include bidirectional translation between specialist and general language as a process requirement.

## Success criteria
Success is not the amount of material, but whether the next actor can regenerate judgment under new conditions.

- Can multiple actors using the same form reproduce similar quality?
- Has compilation time from question to parallel hypotheses become excessive?
- Can deviations be detected and stopped at predefined boundaries?
- Is time for silence and asynchronous compilation actually secured?
- Are the time and cost needed for rollback acceptable?
- Are skills and judgment retained after a certain period?
- Can origin signatures, primary logs, vocabulary dictionaries, translation protocols, and public abstracts be cross-checked?

Raising reproducibility alone is a failure if misdelivery increases, dependence on authority remains, or reversibility is lost.

## Limitations
The Parent Source does not claim that all tacit knowledge can be completely put into text. Verbalization is not a copy of embodied knowledge or intuition itself; it is a foothold for others to approach the judgment structure and reconstruct it in their own context.

Do not turn the Parent Source's concrete silence times and operational examples into fixed numbers that must be obeyed everywhere. Structural rhythm is not preservation of personal quirks or charisma. When the setting changes, form may change, but the central rhythm is to begin from differences, compile through questions and silence, try small, and be able to return.

## Preventing misreading
- This is not a theory that fixes tacit knowledge as secret lore or mystery.
- It is not a standardization theory that mass-copies successful procedures as-is.
- It does not recommend "doing nothing"; it designs the conditions and responsibility of non-intervention.
- Silence is compilation time with a stated purpose, period, responsible party, and restart procedure, not concealment.
- Threshold is a boundary for intervention and standardization, not an exclusion criterion.
- Reversibility is not evasion of responsibility; it connects decision logs with rollback.
- An origin signature is not for personality worship, but for tracing provenance and revisability.
- Transmission is not preservation of wording, but **handing over the operation of judgmental responsibility**.
