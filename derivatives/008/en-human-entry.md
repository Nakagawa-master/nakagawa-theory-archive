## 15-Second Explanation
This volume 1 is not a theory that denies effort. In an era when causality was difficult to see, “do a lot first” and “finish it and see the result” had conditional rationality as substitutes for verification. In the present, however, AI, data, logs, prior cases, and similar resources have widened the range of causal candidates that can be investigated before execution. The theory understands that it is structurally difficult to demand only effort volume while omitting purpose, the causal line, conditions of viability, verification conditions, and revision conditions.

Contemporary effort is the act of examining whether a definition of the outcome placed in the future can be established through the current structure, time, resources, actors, and environment; carrying out the necessary execution; and returning the result to the next hypothesis. The question is not “whether to make an effort,” but “effort to establish what, verifying what, and revising where.”

## Why It Becomes Necessary
The phrases “just do it” and “see it through to the end” are not always wrong. In areas where the result cannot be known without actually acting, exploration itself must beinvestment. The original does not erase the conditional rationality of this older effort theory.

The problem arises when observable things have increased, yet they are not observed and only the same old command format remains. Customer hypotheses can be investigated, past logs can be reviewed, competing cases can be compared, causal candidates can be organized with AI, and field responses can be checked quickly. If, despite this, no purpose or causal line is designed and one merely says “the volume is insufficient,” the uncertainty omitted on the planning side descends onto the field.

The field does not simply bear a workload. It also uses its own time, energy, credibility, and opportunities to compensate for the viability checks: “Can this goal really be established from the current structure?”, “What is this method verifying?”, and “What should change if it fails?” What the original problematizes is this transfer of insufficient viability checking.

## Practical Process
**1. Make the future definition explicit.** Place what you want to establish as an outcome as an achieved state, not merely as an amount of activity.

**2. Formulate the causal line as a hypothesis.** Put into words the path by which the action connects to the outcome. It need not be an established fact, but it must be a testable hypothesis.

**3. Confirm the viability conditions.** Confirm conditions that effort alone cannot substitute for, such as time, resources, actors, environment, institutions, and customer conditions.

**4. Decide the verification conditions.** Define what must be observed through execution in order to support, reject, or revise the hypothesis.

**5. Open revision conditions.** When results are poor, examine whether to change the method, hypothesis, allocation, goal, or viability conditions instead of merely increasing effort volume.

**6. Make the necessary exploratoryinvestment.** Where causality is genuinely invisible, execute in order to obtain information. Here, volume and persistence can have rationality.

**7. Return the result to the next step.** Do not end with evaluating the executor; return outcomes, failures, and responses to the next future definition, causal hypothesis, and condition design.

## Examples of Application
**Sales.** Do not deny increasing the number of calls itself. Set the target customers, problem hypothesis, contact point, and response conditions, and make clear what the volume is verifying.

**Production.** Before merely increasing the number of pieces, confirm for whom and what you want to establish, what response you will observe, and what the usage conditions and evaluation axes are.

**New business.** Explore when uncertainty is high. However, define “what must be known to move to the next step” and distinguish unconditional continuation from exploratoryinvestment.

**AI adoption.** Do not treat usage count or generation volume as the outcome. Establish the hypothesis about which business causality is to be improved and what would show that it has been established.

**Organizational management.** When there is a failure, do not close the matter as “insufficient effort”; make the goal, resources, institutions, timing, and hypothesis subject to the same verification.

## Success Criteria
The purpose, causal line, viability conditions, verification conditions, and revision conditions exist in a form that can be confirmed before or during execution.

Execution volume does not become an end in itself, and it is possible to explain “execution to learn what” and “investment to establish what.”

When there is a failure, not only the executor’s actions but also the plan, hypothesis, resources, and conditions are opened as verification targets, and the result returns to the next execution design.

In domains where causality is genuinely opaque, the rationality of exploratoryinvestment is retained; in domains where causality can be observed in advance, observation is not omitted. Maintaining this distinction is close to the structural establishment of effort in this volume.

## Limitations
This theory does not say that all causality can be foreseen in advance. Unknown markets, research, creation, exploration, and other areas remain where one cannot know without trying. In such cases, volume, persistence, and patience can still be means of acquiring information.

Nor is it a theory that guarantees success if verification conditions are prepared. Viability checking is not a guarantee of success; it is a structure for making explicit what is unknown, what will be checked through execution, and where revision will occur.

The original does not define a universal effort-volume KPI, success probability, or numerical threshold for withdrawal. Creating these in this public reading would turn the original meaning—judging the rationality of effort according to conditions—into a different scoring theory.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not turn this into a claim that “there is no need to make an effort.”

Do not turn it into a simple mockery of Showa-era values. Retain the point that older effort theory had conditional rationality in environments with high causal opacity.

Do not turn it into AI omnipotence. AI is one element that expands the observable range of causal candidates; it is not an actor that automatically completes the viability check for a future definition.

Do not turn it into a claim that field responsibility disappears. The theory opens the responsibility for verifying the plan, hypothesis, and condition design at the same time as execution responsibility.

Do not reverse all failure responsibility onto management. The original calls for verifying the execution side and the planning side on the same causal line, not for a one-way reversal of responsibility.
