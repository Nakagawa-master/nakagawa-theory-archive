## Summary
The Connection Bill of Rights is the higher-order charter that binds connection accounting, governance, and protocol to legitimacy. It defines seven basic rights, four duties, participation/exit/reconnection/dispute procedures, and conformity tests grounded in publicity, auditability, and reversibility. Its purpose is to prevent coercion, enclosure, hollow consent, privacy abuse, and concentrated institutional power.

connection access; purpose disclosure; consent and scope; memory and forgetting; reversibility; audit and objection; non-coercion; honesty duty; non-abuse duty; confidentiality duty; structural preservation; conformity test; compliance label.

## Causal chain

## Validity conditions
clear definitions; seven protected rights; four linked duties; explicit procedures; publicity; auditability; reversibility; independent conformity testing.

## Failure conditions
discriminatory access; hidden purpose; blanket consent; refusal to anonymize; punished exit; blocked audit; rights converted into personality scoring; self-certified compliance only.

## Falsification conditions
Revise or reject the charter if coercion, consent failure, blocked exit, privacy abuse, audit denial, or power concentration do not improve after adoption.

## Required distinctions
right ≠ unlimited demand; consent ≠ blanket permission; forgetting ≠ destruction of accountability; reversibility ≠ evasion of responsibility; audit access ≠ full disclosure; non-coercion ≠ absence of duties; charter ≠ platform terms.

## Interpretation constraints
Rights must remain linked to duties, procedures, remedies, and falsification. Do not use the charter as social-credit scoring, exclusion labeling, or a replacement for the parent original.
