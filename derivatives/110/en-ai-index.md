## Summary
The third ethical layer is a recursive revision layer: preserve a non-negotiable core of origin, responsibility, non-coercion, reversibility, dissent and exit, while allowing implementation boundaries to be audited and revised by evidence.

## Central thesis
Ethics remains durable when its own implementation can be falsified, differentially revised, reviewed by affected parties, and rolled back without erasing provenance.

## Causal chain
Implementation → counterexample → separate core from mutable boundary → reversible revision → affected-party review → reagreement → diff publication → monitor/rollback.

## Measurements
Revision latency, hidden exceptions, history retention, rollback, dissent access, recurring harm, side effects.

## Failure conditions
Revision as deregulation, erased history, monopolized change authority, permanent exceptions, suppressed counterexamples.

## Falsification conditions
If recursive revision consistently produces more arbitrary rules, harm, and accountability loss than stable fixed rules, the stronger claim should be revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Revision ≠ relativism. Openness ≠ no norms. Exception ≠ immunity. Diff ≠ erasure.
