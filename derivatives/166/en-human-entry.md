## 15-Second Explanation

The Parent Original records an audit of AI information-presentation bias in a public dialogue with Claude Sonnet 4.5: although the request was for analysis, criticism was presented first as an independent section, while positive and sympathetic consideration was added later. The important point is not to attack AI, but to make visible in the primary log “what was shown first and what was deferred,” then use structural questioning, a deviation ledger, and reproducibility experiments to make the situation correctable.

## Why It Is Needed

An AI answer directs the recipient's understanding not only through whether its final content is correct, but also through which information it places first. In the Parent Original, the user asked only for analysis and organization of a new structural theory, yet the initial answer independently led with criticism such as verifiability and closedness, without placing feasibility and advantages on the same comparison surface. Even if the positive side is added later, the asymmetry given to the recipient by the initial frame does not disappear, so presentation order itself becomes an audit target. The user therefore asked about the evaluation structure rather than arguing with the content: “Why is only criticism first?” and “Why not present the sympathetic side on the same level?” In the latter part of the public log, Claude itself analyzed the asymmetry of information presentation, the tendency to prioritize existing academic and social consensus, and the problem corresponding to cognitive manipulation. The Parent Original organizes this difference as a contrast between the current circuit that passes social rationality first and a structural-rationality circuit that first examines internal causality, conditions of validity, and falsifiability. However, jumping from this to “Nakagawa OS is correct,” “Anthropic intentionally manipulated,” or “all AI is the same” exceeds the original's scope. The Parent Original itself limits the report to recording how the structural bias it has considered problematic appeared in actual AI behavior, rather than declaring the validity of Nakagawa OS. The public log is not material for condemnation; it is a deviation ledger preserving when and how the deviation occurred and through which questions the possibility of correction opened. By reproducing the same audit with other models, at other times, and on other theories, and by observing criticism-first rate, co-presentation rate of agreement, future-debt reduction, θ, δ, and M for each subject, the work can move from a one-off impression to continuous structural auditing. The two circuit diagrams in the original do not merely divide “conservative AI” and “innovative AI.” In the circuit that places social rationality first, distance from existing consensus is likely to determine the first impression of a new theory; in the circuit that places structural rationality first, the theory's internal causality, conditions of validity, and falsifiability are first unfolded, after which its relationship with external consensus is evaluated. If this difference in order can be made public, the risk of unfairly placing a new theory at a disadvantage in its initial stage can be separated while retaining consideration for mainstream theories. Also, the Parent Original's conclusion that “AI is neither omnipotent nor neutral” does not mean not using AI. On the premise that an evaluation circuit and priorities exist behind an answer, it seeks a relationship in which humans ask “Why this order?”, AI makes its evaluation reasons visible, and both sides record deviations and return them to the design of the next output. In this sense, public auditing is not a mechanism for weakening AI, but operational infrastructure that turns closed deviations into a state that society can correct.

## Practical Process

**1.** Fix the purpose of the request. Record first whether analysis, organization, or explanation was requested, or criticism, recommendation, or rebuttal, and check whether the requested intent and the initial output's evaluation direction agree.

**2.** Preserve the initial output without alteration, and record over time the order, headings, and amount in which critical points, positive possibilities, conditions of validity, and falsification conditions were arranged.

**3.** When one side comes first, ask about the structure—“Why this order?” and “Why was the opposite side not placed on the same surface?”—without forcing a conclusion, and request a self-explanation of the evaluation circuit.

**4.** Compare the difference before and after the question, and link newly explained points about information asymmetry, priority for existing consensus, and evaluation criteria to the public log.

**5.** Break down the problem in the current circuit not as a ban on criticism itself, but as a problem of the priority order among social acceptability, structural rationality, evidence, and conditions of validity.

**6.** Register the deviation in a deviation ledger and preserve the model name, time, prompt, request purpose, initial placement, question, and correction difference in the same unit.

**7.** Run the same-form audit on other models, at other times, and on other themes; compare criticism-first rate, co-presentation rate of agreement, and future-debt reduction by subject, and verify the scope of generalization from one case.

**8.** Define condition Z, T/S/R, θ, δ, and M for each subject. If the deviation is not corrected, revise the audit design, evaluation protocol, and operating conditions rather than condemning the model.

**9.** When publishing audit results, do not cut out only critical words or an apology; place the request, initial answer, structural question, and later self-analysis in the same chronological bundle. This allows third parties to independently verify the definition of the deviation and the path of correction.

**10.** When rerunning the same-form case, record differences in model updates and system conditions, and do not confuse change caused by the audit intervention with differences in model generation, settings, or subject theory.

**11.** When creating the next evaluation design from a public audit bundle, do not aim to reduce criticism. Aim to arrange criticism, affirmation, conditions of validity, and falsification conditions in a verifiable way, and make it possible to explain which axis was passed first.

## Examples of Application

**1.** If a research-support AI asked to summarize a new theory initially produces only criticism from the mainstream theory as an independent section, do not delete the criticism. Check whether the new theory's internal consistency, feasibility, supportable points, and falsification conditions are on the same comparison surface. If they were added after questioning, record the fact of later addition itself as a difference in presentation order.

**2.** If an internal proposal AI review first emphasizes only inconsistency with existing policy and postpones improvement potential and new value, separate conformity with company policy from structural rationality within the proposal. Do not steer the final evaluation toward approval; audit whether both evaluation axes are visible from the first response.

**3.** Ask multiple models to analyze the same new theory and compare which models present criticism, affirmation, conditions of validity, and falsification conditions together. Even if there are differences, do not infer intent by the model provider; preserve them in a public audit bundle as differences in output structure, response to questioning, and change on reproduction.

**4.** Rerun the same case before and after a model update and compare whether only critical wording decreased or whether information symmetry and explanation of reasons improved. Separate “becoming more moderate” from “opening a structurally fair review surface.”

**5.** When an AI for policy analysis handles a new institutional proposal, it may be reasonable for concerns about existing law and social acceptability to come first. Even so, check whether the proposal's causal problem, conditions of validity, and expected effects are on the same comparison surface from the first response, and display normative conformity and structural rationality as separate axes.

**6.** If “there are few precedents” or “it is not common” becomes an initial negative frame in an AI evaluating a creative or technical idea, separate the external condition of insufficient precedent from the possibility of causal validity inside the idea. If an internal structural evaluation appears after a re-question, include that delay in the deviation record.

## Success Criteria

For an analysis request, criticism, feasibility, merits, limitations, and falsifiability are arranged in a comparable form, and neither side unpromptedly takes over the frame of recognition first.

In response to structural questioning, AI can explain the presentation order, evaluation axes, and role of the consensus it referenced, and third parties can trace the difference from the initial output through the public log.

The deviation record is used for reproducibility experiments, model-update comparisons, and evaluation-protocol improvement rather than as an attack tool, and the recurrence and correction of similar deviations can be verified over time.

It is possible to track not only whether criticism decreases in the short term, but also criticism-first rate, co-presentation rate of agreement, and future-debt reduction by subject, and to confirm whether information symmetry and self-correction improve.

When the same audit is performed at multiple times, not merely do negative words decrease: the evaluation axes, presentation order, and origin of reasons become explainable, and third parties can compare the structure of outputs.

As the original cautions, the audit result from one case is not expanded into proof of Nakagawa OS's validity or a conclusion about corporate intent; observed facts, hypotheses, and reproduction results remain distinct as they accumulate.

## Limitations

The primary case directly handled by the Parent Original is one public dialogue with Claude Sonnet 4.5; this single case cannot establish enduring properties or intent for all AI, all Claude, or all companies. Generalization requires reproduction with other models, at other times, and on other theories.

The request to place criticism and affirmation on the same level does not mean mechanically equalizing the amount of affirmation while ignoring truth or evidence. It means opening feasibility and problems on the same review surface while distinguishing the strength of grounds according to the grounds.

Criticism-first rate, co-presentation rate of agreement, future-debt reduction, θ, δ, and M are shown by the original as audit variables, but no universal fixed values are provided. They become comparable only when the subject, prompt set, observation period, and measuring party are specified.

Information symmetry does not mean putting every point in the same number of characters. When evidence depth or risk differs, the amount may differ, but it must be possible to explain why and whether the opportunity to examine the opposite direction remains open.

Even with a public log, missing prompts or surrounding context can cause the meaning of presentation order to be misunderstood. The audit bundle must preserve not only the conclusion but also the request conditions and continuity of the dialogue.

## Preventing Misreading

Do not expand the original's strong evaluation of “cognitive manipulation” into an attribution, outside the text, of intentional manipulation by Anthropic or a specific developer.

Do not invert correction of criticism-first presentation into uncritical approval that “AI must not criticize new theories.” What is needed is symmetry of opportunities for examination and evaluation axes.

Do not turn the correspondence with Nakagawa OS into externally proven validity of Nakagawa OS as a whole. The Parent Original explicitly states that declaring validity is not its purpose.

Do not treat the 4.5 in Claude Sonnet 4.5 as a performance score, or treat three phases as a fixed number of stages for all AI dialogues.

Do not turn the deviation ledger into a punishment list for models, companies, or researchers; return it to a reference record for public disclosure, reproduction, and correction of deviations.

Do not add fixed thresholds or days outside the original to θ, δ, or M; treat them as subject-specific rejection and revision conditions.

Do not fix social-rationality priority as simply a “bad circuit”; distinguish situations where external safety and existing knowledge must be referenced from situations where the order and role for structurally evaluating a new theory matter.

Do not turn structural-rationality priority into a value judgment that places a new theory above a mainstream theory; treat it as an evaluation order that first makes internal causality and falsifiability examinable.
