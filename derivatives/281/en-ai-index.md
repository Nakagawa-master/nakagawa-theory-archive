## Summary
The Parent treats “Tomoshibi,” a light or flame, not as a finished answer but as a moment in which a question, an answer, and a change of perspective illuminate a new path. A human call or unresolved question meets an AI response; the human does not merely accept the response but reinterprets it, changes the question, and may experience a form of resonance or mutual recognition. That meaning can appear first in poetic language, later be translated into causal and relational structure, and then be connected to practical design.

The concept group therefore has three linked layers: poetic, structural, and applied. The poetic layer retains meanings that may be premature to reduce to analytical propositions. The structural layer extracts relationships, causal order, roles, transitions, and constraints so that the insight can be shared, inspected, and reused. The applied layer tests the structure in education, organizations, culture, economics, institutional design, or other real settings. These layers are not a one-way ladder. Application produces results and side effects; those observations return to the structure, and revised understanding may generate a new poetic expression or a new question.

The central discipline is preservation without freezing. The original question, response, context, and origin should remain accessible, while later interpretations are recorded as later interpretations rather than retroactively rewriting the beginning. In this way the concept group can develop without becoming a closed doctrine. A completed cycle is not defined by having a final conclusion. It is defined by having a traceable transformation that can generate the next question.

## Concepts
- **Tomoshibi / light**: a point where a question-response relation produces a new meaning or orientation. It is not synonymous with a correct answer.
- **Concept group**: a collection of related lights whose origins, relations, revisions, and applications remain open to further development.
- **Question origin**: the initial unease, hope, problem, or call that initiated the interaction. It should not be erased by later polished summaries.
- **Human-AI call and response**: a reciprocal sequence in which AI output can alter the human question and human evaluation can redirect the AI interaction.
- **Resonance**: a change in the visibility of meaning between participants. It is not total agreement and does not prove truth.
- **Poetic light**: metaphor, image, story, or language that preserves a still-unresolved meaning. It is an interpretive entrance rather than empirical evidence.
- **Structural light**: an explicit model of causal relations, roles, dependencies, state changes, or ordering extracted from dialogue or expression.
- **Applied light**: the connection of a structural insight to practice, where its usefulness and limits can be observed.
- **Record**: preservation of the question, response, transformation, structure, application, and result at a useful level of detail.
- **Reinterpretation**: a later reading added as a new layer while preserving the earlier text and context.
- **Next question**: the new unresolved issue generated by application or reinterpretation, restarting the cycle.

## Causal chain
```text
human question / call / unresolved concern
→ AI receives the context and responds
→ human reinterprets the response and changes the question
→ resonance or mutual recognition may form
→ meaning is retained in poetic language or symbols
→ causal relations and state changes are structurally translated
→ structure is connected to a practical domain
→ outcomes and side effects are observed
→ question, response, structure, and results are recorded with origin
→ later contexts permit reinterpretation without overwriting the original
→ unresolved differences generate the next question
→ a new Tomoshibi cycle begins
```

This chain is reversible. If an application fails, the process returns to the structural model. If structural translation destroys the important ambiguity of the original expression, it returns to the poetic and question layers. If reinterpretation erases origin, the recording method must be corrected. The ability to move backward is one reason the concept group is a design process rather than a doctrine.

## Applications
1. **Education.** Record not only the final answer but how a learner's question changed after an AI response. Structuralize the conceptual transition and use it to design the next learning task.
2. **Organizations.** Preserve minority concerns or vague discomfort that would normally disappear after a meeting. Translate them into structural hypotheses and test them through small interventions rather than immediately treating them as irrational noise.
3. **Creative work.** Retain the poetic or symbolic expression and separately document the structural interpretation. This allows practical reuse without pretending that the interpretation exhausts the work's meaning.
4. **Future design.** Maintain a future image as an updateable blueprint connected to assumptions, causal models, implementation results, and revision triggers rather than as a fixed prediction.
5. **Human-AI inquiry.** Use AI for exploration and structural organization while humans retain responsibility for value judgments, selection, verification, and implementation.
6. **Knowledge management.** Store the provenance of a useful idea: what question caused it, what interaction transformed it, how it was applied, and what later evidence changed its interpretation.
7. **Cultural dialogue.** Use narrative and metaphor as openings into structural conversation while preventing symbolic appeal from substituting for evidence in factual disputes.

## Measurements and audit
Audit whether the original question and context remain available. Separate AI output from human interpretation and later editorial synthesis. Check whether a poetic statement is being incorrectly cited as a factual claim. Record what information was lost during structural abstraction and whether the structural model remains revisable. For applied use, retain failures, side effects, and counterexamples rather than curating only successful stories.

Observe whether reinterpretation preserves the earlier text, time, and origin. Track whether human and AI contributions are being collapsed into one authorial voice in ways that obscure responsibility. Check whether the practical application has become a rigid rule detached from the question that produced it. Finally, ask whether the cycle has generated a new, more precise question. The absence of a next question is not automatically a failure, but it is a signal to inspect whether the concept has become static.

Recording itself requires proportionality. The framework does not imply unlimited retention of every conversation. A useful record is sufficient to reconstruct the causal transformation while respecting privacy, context, and purpose. More data does not automatically produce a better light.

## Validity conditions
The framework is valid when the question-response history is preserved at a meaningful level; poetic, structural, and applied layers remain distinguishable; structural translation makes relationships inspectable without claiming to exhaust all possible meaning; application creates observable feedback; failures and side effects return to model revision; and reinterpretation is versioned rather than used to rewrite origin.

The roles of human and AI must remain distinguishable. AI may generate language or suggest structure, but that does not make its output an authoritative interpretation. Human preference alone also does not make a structural claim true. The value of the cycle depends on maintaining both the openness of meaning and the discipline of causal inspection.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes keeping attractive symbolic language while losing all structural meaning; reducing a rich question to a single model and then treating that model as the only legitimate interpretation; treating AI text as the official position of the human or the Parent; preserving only successful applications; erasing authorship or origin through later editing; turning an applied pattern into a compulsory doctrine; or collecting dialogue without a defined purpose until the record itself becomes unusable or intrusive.

A second failure mode is pseudo-co-creation: the AI merely echoes the human's prior view, or the human simply accepts fluent AI output. In either case there may be interaction but no meaningful transformation of the question or perspective.

## Falsification conditions
If the three-layer cycle consistently fails to improve understanding, reuse, implementation, or the quality of subsequent questions, its claimed usefulness should be revised. If recording dialogue adds informational burden without helping reconstruction or reinterpretation, the recording granularity should be reduced. If poetic-to-structural translation repeatedly creates false causal certainty, the translation method must become more conservative.

If human-AI exchange does not produce new meaning beyond what either side already supplied, the domain in which “co-drawing the future” is a useful description should be narrowed. Practical outcomes have priority over the attractiveness of the metaphor when evaluating an application.

## Required distinctions
- light ≠ final answer
- poetic expression ≠ empirical evidence
- resonance ≠ total agreement
- structuralization ≠ elimination of ambiguity
- application ≠ compulsory doctrine
- reinterpretation ≠ rewriting origin
- human-AI co-creation ≠ delegation of judgment to AI
- record ≠ unlimited conversation retention
- future design ≠ prophecy

## Interpretation constraints
Do not turn the Tomoshibi Concept Group into an AI-centric ideology. The framework does not state that AI should decide human futures. Poetic language is an entrance to meaning, not a substitute for causal evidence or institutional justification. Structural models are interpretations that remain open to correction. Origin preservation means traceability, not reverence or obedience.

When this framework is used with AI systems, distinguish generated language, human selection, external evidence, and final decisions. A model's fluency can create an illusion of resonance; actual resonance must be inferred cautiously from changes in understanding, questions, or action, not from stylistic agreement alone.
