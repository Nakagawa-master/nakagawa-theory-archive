## Summary
Future-Defined Causal Rectification Theory II is a boundary-fixing paper. It does not deny overlap with Meaning Convergence Theory, Time OS, Future Debt Theory, or backcasting; it identifies what each neighboring concept primarily explains and prevents the causal-rectification theory from collapsing into a synonym for one of them. Its distinctive unit is a feasible future definition used as a **terminal constraint**, followed by integrated detection and rectification of mismatches across meaning, time, action, and structure. Quantum language may be used as metaphorical analogy, but it is not physical evidence for the theory.

## Concepts
- terminal constraint: a future-state requirement used as an evaluative condition for present configuration.
- Meaning Convergence Theory: focuses on how a future definition changes the role or meaning vector of past and present nodes.
- Time OS: focuses on the operating design of time perception, allocation, sequencing, and temporal priority.
- Future Debt Theory: focuses on constraints and costs that current local optimization pushes into future conditions.
- backcasting: works backward from a future state to planning steps; it overlaps with causal rectification but does not by itself imply integrated four-layer change.
- four-layer rectification: coordinated realignment of meaning, time, action, and structure under one terminal constraint.
- boundary fixing: preserving both connection and difference among neighboring concepts.
- quantum metaphor boundary: quantum language may stimulate analogy, but cannot be used as proof of physical retrocausality, information travel from the future, or time reversal.

## Causal chain
feasible future definition → terminal constraint extracted → present configuration compared against that constraint → mismatches detected across multiple layers → Meaning Convergence lens reevaluates node roles → Time OS lens redesigns allocation and sequence → Future Debt lens exposes deferred constraints → action is continued, stopped, advanced, or added → structure, authority, role, or information flow is changed where necessary → backcasting can organize reverse implementation → implementation results are compared again with the terminal constraint → theory boundaries and the future definition are revised when evidence requires it.

## State model
- overlap-recognition phase: neighboring theories are acknowledged as genuinely related.
- difference-extraction phase: each theory’s primary explanatory object is separated.
- terminal-constraint phase: a feasible future is converted into an evaluative condition.
- layer-diagnosis phase: meaning, time, action, and structure are inspected separately.
- integrated-rectification phase: the layers are changed as one coordinated process rather than as independent slogans.
- implementation phase: the redesign reaches real behavior, resource allocation, and institutional configuration.
- revalidation phase: results are checked against the terminal constraint.
- boundary-maintenance phase: the theory is narrowed if neighboring concepts already explain the observed change sufficiently.

## Applications
Use this paper when an intervention is being called “causal rectification” simply because it starts from a future goal. If the work only reverses task order from a desired endpoint, backcasting may be the adequate description. If the future definition also changes how prior assets are interpreted, how time is allocated, what actions are stopped or advanced, and how roles or authority are structured, the four-layer rectification frame may add explanatory value. The paper also supports theory architecture: related concepts can be combined without erasing their different units of analysis.

## Measurements and audit
Audit whether a real terminal constraint exists or only a vague aspiration; whether meaning, time, action, and structure are separately observable; whether Future Debt refers to deferred structural constraints rather than a generic backlog; whether Time OS is being reduced to calendar management; whether Meaning Convergence alone already explains the semantic change; whether backcasting alone explains the implementation sequence; whether the integrated process produces an independent practical difference; and whether quantum metaphors are being incorrectly promoted into physical claims.

## Validity conditions
Theory II is useful when the neighboring concepts remain distinguishable, the terminal-constraint operation is explicit, and the four-layer integration explains or guides changes that are not adequately captured by any one neighboring concept alone. The theory should remain compositional: overlap is not evidence of identity, and difference is not evidence of total independence.

## Failure conditions
The theory fails conceptually when it becomes another name for Meaning Convergence, is reduced to Time OS, treats Future Debt as unfinished tasks, treats backcasting as the entire theory, or uses quantum theory as physical proof. It also fails when “four-layer integration” is claimed despite only one layer changing and no distinctive implementation consequence being observed.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the independent scope of Future-Defined Causal Rectification if repeated cases are fully explained by Meaning Convergence, Time OS, Future Debt, or ordinary backcasting, or if using a terminal constraint plus four-layer integration produces no distinctive explanatory or implementation difference. The theory must allow a conclusion that an existing neighboring concept is sufficient.

## Required distinctions
Meaning Convergence / four-layer rectification; Time OS / terminal-constraint operation; Future Debt / unfinished task list; backcasting / integrated causal rectification; quantum metaphor / physical evidence; overlap / identity; theoretical integration / conceptual collapse; backward planning / physical retrocausality.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not state that quantum mechanics proves future-to-past causation in this theory. Do not infer information transfer from the future, time reversal, guaranteed future realization, or unsupported probabilities. Preserve the explicit differences among neighboring theories and allow the scope of causal rectification to shrink if its independent explanatory value is not supported.
