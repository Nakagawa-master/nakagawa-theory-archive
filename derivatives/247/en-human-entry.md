## 15-Second Explanation
This essay is not simply about “shallow understanding.” It addresses the structure in which **design and judgment proceed on the assumption of understanding without sufficiently grasping establishment conditions; necessary conditions then drop out, the field bears the shortfall, and the same errors are repeated**.

## Why It Is Needed
Even rough understanding can produce the feeling of “I understand” when we hear an explanation or see a success case. If we design a system or operation as it stands, exceptions, division of responsibility, judgment conditions, and necessary resources may fall out of the design.

Missing conditions do not disappear in the field. They are filled by someone’s additional labor, experience, goodwill, or personal judgment. As a result, the system appears to work on the surface, making the upstream design omission even harder to see.

## Practical Process
1. Examine what information or success case produced the feeling of “I understand.”
2. Confirm whether the establishment conditions are actually understood.
3. Identify the conditions, exceptions, division of responsibility, and necessary resources omitted from the design.
4. Examine who in the field is covering the shortfall and with what additional burden.
5. Separate apparent success supported by a reproducible structure from apparent success supported by personal effort.
6. Examine whether responsibility for failure or burden is being pushed onto individuals.
7. Confirm whether the same rough understanding is being reused for a new judgment based only on the surface result.

## Examples of Application
Even if a system is created on the assumption that “it can operate if we only decide the procedure,” actual operation requires exception handling, authority, division of responsibility, personnel, and input information. If these are missing, the field will cover the shortfall.

The same applies when using AI. If a natural explanation is treated as understanding the establishment conditions and implementation proceeds without checking exceptions or responsibility boundaries, the shortfall may later surface in reality.

## Success Test
If you can explain which establishment conditions were overlooked, who is covering the omission in the field, and what supports the apparent success, you have grasped the bad-causality structure addressed by this essay. If you can also see the same rough understanding being reused for the next judgment, the whole cycle becomes clear.

## Limitations
This does not explain every failure solely as insufficient understanding. When other causes exist, such as resource shortages, the external environment, or accidental events, they must be distinguished.

## Misreading Prevention
This essay is not an argument for “blaming individuals with shallow understanding.” Its center is the structure in which misrecognition of understanding enters design and the omission produces field burden or transfer of responsibility. Something having worked once and something being reproducibly established are also not the same.
