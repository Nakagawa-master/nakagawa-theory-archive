## Summary

The parent source was written on November 15, 2025 using a November 14 news report as its immediate material. The report was summarized as saying that a review was being considered regarding the “not permitting introduction” element of Japan’s Three Non-Nuclear Principles in the context of revisions to national security documents. The parent does not treat a reported consideration as a finalized policy, and this derivative preserves that temporal and evidentiary boundary.

The parent is not primarily an argument for or against changing the principle. It translates the event into a structural relationship among national value principles, deterrence and operational effectiveness, domestic legitimacy, alliance/international trust, and temporal ethics. The central diagnosis is that a gap can emerge when a value principle and the operational structures expected to realize national security no longer align.

Its simplified causal model is A→B. A represents the structural design of the Three Non-Nuclear Principles, including principle definition, correspondence lines, and temporal-ethical conditions. B represents institutionalized security policy, deterrence structure, international trust, stability, and alignment. The parent argues that instability in A can create structural risk for B, but it also supplies falsification and revision conditions; the relation is not a deterministic one-variable law.

From the perspective of Resonant Structure, a national principle is not stable merely because its wording exists. Value, institutional practice, deterrence effectiveness, legitimacy, and trust must correspond. The parent sees the reported reconsideration as a moment when the value layer of the Three Non-Nuclear Principles and the functional layer of deterrence/alliance practice appear to be under tension. That tension requires redesign whether the principle is maintained or altered.

Temporal Ethics adds the future-liability dimension. A decision that produces immediate security benefit may still export costs into future institutional trust, regional stability, escalation risk, alliance predictability, or domestic legitimacy. Conversely, preserving a principle without addressing changing security conditions may also create future liabilities. Temporal ethics therefore does not predetermine which policy is correct; it requires the future repayment line of each option to be made visible.

The integrated audit identifies a three-layer translation bundle: national principle, deterrence structure, and temporal ethics. Re-agreement refers to Time, Structure, and Resonance (T/S/R), while observations can include security incidents, decline in deterrence confidence, legitimacy indicators, and international evaluation. The source uses threshold θ, observation window δ, and phenomenon M—such as structural distrust or unintended escalation arising from divergence between principle and operation—as a falsification form.

The source does not define universal values for θ or δ, nor a universal metric for deterrence confidence, legitimacy, or international evaluation. Quantitative implementation must specify the measurement actor, object, scale, time window, evidence source, permitted use, and non-guarantee scope. A threshold symbol is not evidence that a fixed threshold has already been established.

The parent’s signature declaration explicitly positions the text as structural observation rather than an ideological campaign. This derivative therefore does not convert the November 2025 structural reading into an assertion about current policy, a judgment about a current office-holder, or partisan advocacy. Later developments must be evaluated separately from the parent’s historical observation.

## Applications
**1. Maintaining the principle.** Audit how deterrence and alliance operation remain credible and which alternative structures handle security concerns.

**2. Changing the principle.** Audit deterrence benefits together with domestic legitimacy, international trust, regional stability, escalation and future liabilities.

**3. Security-document revision.** Design principle definition, exceptions, operating rules, explanation, review cadence and revision conditions as one bundle.

**4. Alliance dialogue.** Make tension between domestic value principles and alliance operational requirements visible rather than suppressing either side.

**5. Public debate.** Identify whether disagreement concerns value, effectiveness, trust or time rather than reducing the debate to personalities or parties.

## Falsification conditions
- observed maintenance/change effects on deterrence, trust, legitimacy and long-term stability consistently differ from the structural hypothesis
- change fails to improve expected deterrence and worsens legitimacy, trust or escalation
- maintenance fails to sustain alternative deterrence and increases future security liabilities
- domestic legitimacy or international trust move persistently contrary to expected correspondence
- phenomenon M appears within observation window δ or grounded indicators cross θ
- alternative institutional designs repeatedly achieve higher long-term stability, reversibility and legitimacy
