## 15-Second Explanation

The parent original uses reports from November 2025 that consideration was being given to reviewing the Three Non-Nuclear Principles' “not allowing introduction” principle. It does not take a position for or against the policy; it examines how the national principle, the effectiveness of deterrence, trust at home and abroad, and future security debt are connected.

The central issue is not a choice between principle and effectiveness. Whether the principle is maintained or changed, the correspondence lines connecting values, institutions, deterrence, trust, and time must be redesigned, and it must be possible to explain publicly what is gained and what debt is left to the future. The parent original concerns reporting and structural evaluation as of November 2025 and must be separated from later policy status or current politics.

## Why It Becomes Necessary

National principles are incorporated over long periods into institutions, diplomacy, and public expectations. Changing one phrase can therefore move the relationships of trust, legitimacy, and alliance operation that the principle has supported, rather than being a mere technical adjustment. Conversely, even if the wording is retained, a large change in operating conditions can accumulate an unexplained gap between principle and effectiveness.

As the security environment changes, the principle's effectiveness can also come into tension with actual deterrence conditions. Making preservation of the principle the sole objective can overlook divergence from real security conditions. Making effectiveness the sole objective can overlook social legitimacy, international trust, future escalation, and institutional-credit debt.

The parent original calls this tension a “collapse of correspondence”: a condition in which values and institutions, principle and deterrence, and present decisions and future debt no longer adequately correspond. “Collapse” does not mean that the state or institution has physically fallen; it means that a previously stable relationship now requires redesign.

Temporal ethics is the axis for including not only present security benefits but also the debts that must be repaid in the future. A policy change may increase deterrence options in the short term while damaging domestic legitimacy or international predictability, creating future debt. Conversely, maintaining the principle may narrow effective security options and create another debt if no alternative structure exists. Temporal ethics does not automatically make either policy correct; it makes both debts and repayment lines visible.

The source material is also reporting. A reported “consideration” is not the same level of evidence as a final government decision, a legal change, or current policy. Preserving this modality is essential to reliability. Later facts cannot be written backward into the parent original dated November 15, 2025.

## Practical Process

**1. Fix the time point.** Confirm that the parent original was written on the 15th using reporting from November 14, 2025, and do not feed later policy conditions back into it. Treat consideration, decision, enactment, and current status as different states.

**2. Separate evidence modalities.** Do not treat a news summary, a primary government document, parliamentary procedure, an official announcement, public opinion, and expert evaluation as equally certain. State what evidence supports each A→B hypothesis.

**3. Define the principle.** Separate which of the Three Non-Nuclear Principles is under discussion and which institutions, operations, and expectations it connects to. Do not merge historical value, institutional wording, and operating custom into the single word “principle.”

**4. Observe effectiveness.** Observe effects on deterrence and alliance operation without separating them from the principle. Do not decide effectiveness from one proxy; clarify who is deterring what and on which time horizon.

**5. Decompose trust.** Treat domestic legitimacy, alliance trust, and international evaluation separately. Citizens, parliament, allies, neighboring states, and the international community are different observers and need not move in the same direction.

**6. Add temporal ethics.** Place present benefits, short-term risks, medium- and long-term regional stability, institutional credit, and escalation potential on a time axis. Check not only what debt is left to the future but also how it will be reviewed and repaid.

**7. Set re-agreement conditions.** Define the observation window, indicators, and conditions under which the maintenance or change hypothesis will be reconsidered. θ, δ, and M express the form, but concrete values need grounds specific to the subject.

**8. Make the structure public.** Make it publicly verifiable how values and effectiveness were connected and what remains unguaranteed, rather than presenting only a policy conclusion. Share the causal lines before seeking agreement with the conclusion.

## Examples of Application

**Argument for maintaining the principle.** Do not stop at “it should be maintained because it is a postwar principle.” Explain how it can be aligned with actual deterrence conditions and alliance operation, and what alternative security structure exists. Put the legitimacy and predictability it provides alongside the risks accepted in effectiveness.

**Argument for changing the principle.** Do not stop at “it is necessary to strengthen deterrence.” Put domestic legitimacy, international trust, regional stability, escalation, and institutional debt left to future generations on the same audit surface. Keep the change hypothesis revisable if the expected deterrence effect is not observed.

**Revision of security documents.** Alongside wording changes, design the definition of the principle, exceptions, public explanation, evaluation cycle, and correction conditions. Keep the revision falsifiable and connected to observation and renewed agreement rather than closing it as a one-time political decision.

**Alliance consultations.** Make visible the tension between alliance operating requirements and domestic principles, including what effectiveness is needed and what value constraints remain. Keep both negotiable as correspondence lines instead of silently subordinating one to the other.

**Public discussion.** Make visible where judgment differs—principle, effectiveness, trust, or temporal ethics—rather than reducing the issue to approval or disapproval of people or parties. Even with the same policy conclusion, different reasons produce different future revision conditions.

## Success Criteria

A structurally successful discussion lets both the maintenance and change sides explain what effectiveness, legitimacy, international trust, and future debt their choice accepts. The relationship between principle and operation is visible and can later be audited and reassessed.

Policy must also remain revisable within an observation window by examining security incidents, deterrence trust, legitimacy, international evaluation, and escalation. Success is not that every prediction is correct; it includes the ability to revise a hypothesis when it is wrong.

Distinguishing reporting, primary material, and inference, while preserving the time boundary, is another condition. When later conditions change, credibility is strengthened by comparing later verification with the parent original's observation rather than reusing the old argument as a current fact.

## Limits

The parent original is a structural translation based on a specific report from November 2025. It does not automatically explain later policy decisions, the current government position, or the current international situation. Names of political figures and descriptions of offices in the parent original are not substitutes for checking current offices.

The incidents, trust, legitimacy, and international evaluation cited by the original have no universal fixed thresholds. θ, δ, and M mark falsifiability; concrete values need separate grounds. Combining indicators into one can erase differences in meaning between observers.

This theory does not decide maintenance or change of the Three Non-Nuclear Principles as a policy recommendation. It is a framework for auditing the correspondence among value, effectiveness, trust, and time. It is not a document for military operations or concrete nuclear-use methods.

## Misreading Prevention

Reported consideration ≠ confirmed policy. Reporting is source material, not a decision state.

The original's November 2025 time point ≠ the current state. Later political and security facts require separate confirmation.

Structural audit ≠ support for or opposition to a policy. Both maintenance and change are examined by the same structural criteria.

Maintaining the principle ≠ automatic safety. Effective conditions and alternatives must be explained.

Changing the principle ≠ automatic stronger deterrence. Legitimacy, trust, and future debt must also be observed.

Deterrence effectiveness ≠ legitimacy. They are different observation axes.

Domestic trust ≠ alliance trust ≠ international evaluation. Separate the actors and meanings.

International evaluation ≠ moral correctness. It is one observation of policy effects.

Collapse of correspondence ≠ collapse of the state. It is a condition requiring redesign of relationships.

θ, δ, and M ≠ fixed values established by the parent original. Specific use needs separate measurement grounds.
