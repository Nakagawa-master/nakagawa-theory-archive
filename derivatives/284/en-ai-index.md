## Summary
The Parent reframes foresight from the task of predicting a single future into the task of reading causal conditions that make multiple futures more or less likely. Weak signals, technological changes, institutional debates, demographic movement, resource constraints, cultural adaptation, and market behavior are treated as inputs. A signal is not evidence that a particular future is certain. It becomes useful only when placed in a causal network that explains what might amplify it, what might damp it, and what other conditions must be present.

The result is a set of conditional scenarios rather than a prophecy. Scenarios are useful when they expose branch variables: conditions whose changes materially alter the direction of the future. Those variables can then be separated into controllable, influenceable, and externally observed conditions. Future design begins at this point. The task is not to control the entire future but to identify where action can change probabilities, resilience, or available options.

AI can assist by processing large amounts of information, comparing patterns, and generating candidate scenarios. That capability does not resolve normative questions. Probability of occurrence and desirability are different axes. Human responsibility for priorities, acceptable risks, and action remains distinct from model-based pattern detection. Forecast errors and side effects are preserved as inputs to model revision.

## Concepts
- **Signal**: a present change that may be connected to a future development; not a guarantee of that development.
- **Noise / interpretation separation**: distinguishing observable evidence from the meaning assigned to it.
- **Causal network**: a cross-domain map of conditions, mechanisms, dependencies, and constraints.
- **Amplification condition**: a condition that makes a weak trend more likely to scale or persist.
- **Damping condition**: a condition that limits, redirects, or reverses a trend.
- **Scenario**: a conditional future configuration defined by explicit assumptions rather than a single point forecast.
- **Branch variable**: a condition whose state changes which scenario becomes plausible.
- **Controllable variable**: a condition an actor can directly change.
- **Influenceable variable**: a condition an actor cannot directly control but can affect through negotiation, investment, design, or coalition.
- **Structural translation**: expressing complex causal relations as understandable conditions, branches, and feedback paths.
- **Forecast error**: the difference between expected and observed outcomes; an input to learning rather than something to hide.
- **Future design**: intervention on conditions to change the set or likelihood of possible futures.

## Causal chain
```text
observe weak signals and present changes
→ separate observation, noise, and interpretation
→ place signals in technological, institutional, economic, cultural, demographic, and resource networks
→ identify amplification and damping mechanisms
→ generate multiple conditional scenarios
→ identify variables that separate the scenarios
→ distinguish controllable, influenceable, and external variables
→ separate probability estimates from value judgments
→ design action, waiting, hedging, or exit rules
→ observe environmental change and intervention results
→ record forecast error and side effects
→ update assumptions, causal links, and scenarios
```

A critical feature is that forecasts are pre-committed enough to be evaluated. If branch conditions are never stated and any outcome can later be explained as consistent with the forecast, the model is not falsifiable. Useful foresight records what conditions were expected to matter before the outcome is known.

## Applications
1. **Technology adoption.** Connect performance improvement with cost, energy, standards, regulation, skills, procurement, organizational adaptation, and liability. A strong technical curve does not guarantee rapid social adoption.
2. **Labor shortages.** Combine demographics with automation, education, migration, mobility, wages, job redesign, and productivity. Different combinations create different futures.
3. **AI strategy.** Treat model capability, price, data quality, auditability, responsibility, exception handling, and organizational learning as separate variables rather than using model benchmarks as the whole forecast.
4. **Industry planning.** Include supply constraints, substitutes, policy change, standards, and distribution alongside demand forecasts. Design investment, option, and exit strategies for multiple branches.
5. **Corporate strategy.** Predefine triggers: under condition A invest, under B wait, under C reduce exposure. This reduces the pressure to pretend that one forecast is certain.
6. **Public policy.** Include reflexivity: published forecasts and policies can change behavior and therefore change the future being forecast.

## Measurements and audit
Check whether signals are distinguished from confirmed trends and whether signal selection is vulnerable to confirmation bias. Inspect the causal network for missing constraints outside the analyst's preferred domain. Require explicit branch variables and assumptions. Record the time horizon, because uncertainty usually grows with distance and may shift the appropriate level of precision.

Separate probability and desirability. A future may be likely but undesirable, or unlikely but sufficiently harmful to justify resilience measures. Separate variables the actor can control from those that can only be monitored. Inspect whether AI-generated scenarios preserve data dates, model limitations, and uncertainty rather than presenting fluent narrative as confidence.

After the forecast period or an important event, compare expected and observed outcomes. Ask which causal links were wrong, which branch variables did not matter, which unmodeled factors dominated, and which interventions created side effects. Forecast review should update the causal model rather than merely score prediction accuracy.

## Validity conditions
Signals remain probabilistic inputs, not certainties. The analysis uses cross-domain causal relations where necessary, retains multiple conditional scenarios, identifies branch variables, and distinguishes controllable from external conditions. Probability estimates are separated from normative preferences. Uncertainty and time horizon are visible. AI assists pattern analysis but does not become the authority for selecting the desirable future. Outcomes and errors feed model revision.

Scenarios should be decision-relevant. Producing many narratives without branch conditions can create an illusion of sophistication while providing no guidance. Valid foresight gives decision makers explicit reasons to act, wait, hedge, or revise.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes straight-line extrapolation presented as inevitability; single-future declarations; treating popularity, correlation, or media frequency as causal proof; overinterpreting weak signals; using AI forecasts without checking data freshness or model assumptions; mixing probability with desirability; hiding failed predictions; and preserving a forecast model after repeated contradictory evidence.

Another failure is scenario inflation. If dozens of scenarios cover every outcome but none contain discriminating variables, the exercise becomes unfalsifiable. Foresight should reduce uncertainty into meaningful branches, not simply enumerate imaginable futures.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the model if important signals do not connect to the predicted mechanism; if a proposed branch variable does not distinguish outcomes; if intervention on a key variable does not change the direction predicted; if another variable consistently dominates; or if side effects overwhelm the intended change. Repeated forecast error in one direction is evidence that assumptions or boundaries require revision.

If simple models outperform complex causal narratives in a domain, the more complex model should not be retained for prestige. If a supposedly weak signal has no predictive or causal value across repeated tests, reduce its weight or remove it.

## Required distinctions
- forecast ≠ prophecy
- signal ≠ certainty
- trend ≠ cause
- scenario ≠ plan
- probability ≠ desirability
- AI pattern detection ≠ value judgment
- future design ≠ control of all future conditions
- uncertainty ≠ analytical surrender

## Interpretation constraints
Do not convert foresight into authority based on certainty. Conditionality must remain visible. Avoid post hoc reinterpretation that makes every outcome seem predicted. Record branch conditions before outcomes when possible. AI systems can organize patterns but cannot fully anticipate institutional discontinuities, reflexive human behavior, or unobserved shocks. Their output should be integrated as evidence, not treated as revelation.

Normative choice is a separate layer. A technically likely trajectory may conflict with values, rights, or acceptable risk. Foresight supports decision-making by clarifying structure; it does not automate the decision about which future should be pursued.
