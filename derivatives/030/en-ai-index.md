## Central Claim

Reading MOTA only as a car-buying service loses the source-specific value core.

The parent origin treats selling price as a result shaped not only by vehicle value but by information asymmetry, the number and conditions of competing buyers, when prices are presented, how sellers are connected to buyers, and the phone / negotiation / comparison burden imposed on sellers.

MOTA is therefore read as redesigning price-formation conditions. Nakagawa Master then translates that value core into the external-article frame: not merely “selling a car,” but designing the market conditions under which a higher selling price can emerge.

## Structural Rationality

```text
Sellers have limited market information.
↓
A single buyer produces weak competition.
↓
Connecting multiple buyers can create competition.
↓
But phone calls, negotiation, and comparison burden also increase.
↓
High burden makes premature compromise more likely.
↓
Higher-price formation therefore requires competition and lower seller burden to coexist.
↓
MOTA can be read as redesigning those price-formation conditions.
↓
Nakagawa Master translates that value core into article structure.
```
