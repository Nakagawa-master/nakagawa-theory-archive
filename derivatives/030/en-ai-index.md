## 4. Structural Summary

The parent origin does not primarily present MOTA as a used-car appraisal comparison service or as a convenient service with fewer calls. It reads selling price as shaped not only by vehicle value but also by information asymmetry, competitive participation, the sequence of price presentation, connection conditions, and the phone, negotiation, and comparison burden carried by the seller.

The source-specific value core is therefore the redesign of price-formation conditions. The meta-subject must also remain intact: Nakagawa Master extracted that value core and translated it into an external-article frame that shifts the reader from “selling a car” to “designing the market conditions under which a higher selling price can emerge.”

## 5. Central Proposition

```text
Vehicle value alone does not fully explain selling price
→ sellers face information asymmetry
→ a single connected buyer tends to provide weak competition
→ multiple connected buyers can create competition
→ but additional connections may increase phone, negotiation, and comparison burden
→ high burden can cause premature compromise before sufficient comparison
→ higher-price formation therefore requires competition and lower seller burden to coexist
→ MOTA can be read as redesigning those price-formation conditions
→ Nakagawa Master translated that value core into article structure
```

This is not a guarantee of a higher price. The parent origin concerns the structure of conditions that can affect price formation and the editorial judgment used to translate that structure for readers.

## 6. Causal Chain

1. Sellers cannot fully observe the whole market or each buyer's valuation context.
2. A single-buyer connection provides limited comparative competition.
3. Multiple buyers can increase competitive pressure and comparison possibilities.
4. If that competition is obtained by imposing many calls, negotiations, and comparisons on the seller, the seller's operating burden rises.
5. High burden can reduce the seller's capacity to continue comparison and can increase premature compromise.
6. Competition and burden reduction therefore need to be treated as simultaneous conditions rather than independent benefits.
7. The parent origin locates MOTA's value core in this redesign of price-formation conditions.
8. Nakagawa Master then converts the value core into an external-article entry frame.

## 10. Validity Conditions

A valid reading keeps information asymmetry, competition, presentation and connection conditions, seller burden, and premature compromise within one causal system. It does not reduce the source to only competition, only fewer calls, only service features, or only an observed result.

It must also preserve the meta-subject. The reading is incomplete if it explains MOTA's business structure but drops why Nakagawa Master selected this value core and placed it at the entry point of an external article. Structurally necessary expansion is limited to clarifying causal sequence, distinctions, applicability, failure conditions, and misreading boundaries already implied by the source.

## 12. Interpretation Constraints / Falsification Boundary

Individual selling prices also depend on vehicle condition, timing, demand, location, and buyer-specific judgment. This derivative therefore cannot support the proposition that competition plus lower burden will always produce a higher sale price.

When comparing another market or service, verify separately whether information asymmetry, competition, connection burden, and premature compromise form a genuinely comparable structure. Similarity alone does not authorize elevating this parent-specific reading into a universal theory.
