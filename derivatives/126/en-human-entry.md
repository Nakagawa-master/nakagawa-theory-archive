## 15-Second Explanation
The original is not asking whether “AI stocks will rise or fall.” It examines how the story of AI’s future earnings can run ahead of the actual structures of capital, supply chains, talent, regulation, and demand generation, until checks of “correspondence” cannot keep up. The comparison with 1929 is not a crash prediction. It is used to audit unchecked acceleration while preserving the difference between the period’s “credit and delay” and the present’s “speed and synchronization.”

## Why It Is Necessary
In an AI market, stories about future value are easily organized first. Stories move investment, hiring, facilities, corporate valuation, and policy decisions, but the capital, supply chains, talent, regulation, demand generation, and safety evaluation that establish actual value do not advance at the same speed. When expectations become organized first, a correspondence difference arises between the value being narrated and the structure being implemented.

“Time ethics” views this difference on a time axis. When value that should be returned through future implementation alignment is advanced too far into current valuation, the distance between present expectations and future structural completion grows. The problem is not that prices have risen in themselves, but how much future alignment they have anticipated.

Furthermore, as “structural rhythm” accelerates and market synchronization strengthens, expectation updates move ahead of correspondence checks. When information, trading, capital movement, and corporate valuation move together on short beats, local non-correspondence can spread throughout the whole in a short time. This is why the original calls the contemporary fuse “speed and synchronization.”

The comparison with 1929 does not say that the same crash will occur in the same form. The original sees that even when history does not repeat as the same event, the wiring in which expectations overtake real structures can repeat. The fuse in 1929 was “credit and delay”; the present fuse is “speed and synchronization.” We must read the structural repetition while preserving the difference.

## Practical Process
**1. Break the story into structural components.** Divide the overview that “AI will generate enormous profits” into data supply, inference cost, facilities, talent, supply chains, regulatory clearance, demand generation, safety review, and so on.

**2. Compare narrated value with actual structure.** See which components are catching up with expectations and which are lagging. Do not substitute one strong indicator for overall alignment.

**3. Check time ethics.** Confirm whether value that should be returned through future alignment has been advanced excessively into current valuation. Do not confuse expectations taking the lead with future value not existing.

**4. Measure speed and synchronization.** Check whether information updates, trading, capital movement, and valuation changes are becoming faster than correspondence checks. The issue is not speed itself, but the speed gap between implementation and audit.

**5. View T/S/R as one audit bundle.** T is the degree of correspondence, S is structural sustainability in capital, talent, regulation, and so on, and R is the reversibility of verification and correction. Place implementation delay rate, inference gross-margin gap, and safety-review backlog in the same bundle.

**6. Maintain an observation window.** If indicators can no longer be followed because of disclosure failure or unverifiability, do not treat that state as “no problem.” Treat observability itself as an audit condition.

**7. Revise the hypothesis when falsified.** If continued deviation from the set threshold or closure of the observation window occurs, reject or revise the hypothesis rather than changing the explanation to protect the story.

## Applications
**AI companies and business valuation.** Do not use the story “it will grow because it is AI” as valuation evidence as-is; translate it into data supply, inference cost, talent, regulation, facilities, and demand generation. Even if model performance improves, overall correspondence remains incomplete when supply constraints or regulatory responses lag.

**Market audit.** Before discussing whether a stock price is high or low, check the speed gap between expectations and implementation, the strength of synchronization, the correspondence difference, and the disclosure state. Do not equate price stability with structural alignment.

**Management explanation.** Show not only future revenue but also the progress of the capital, supply chains, talent, regulation, and demand formation that make it possible on the same time axis. Do not move only future value forward into the present; disclose the slower structural layers as well.

**Technology roadmap.** Separate the speeds of model performance, inference cost, safety review, regulation, and supply capacity. Do not use only the fastest layer as a proxy for overall progress.

**Historical comparison.** Confirm the commonality with 1929 not as the conclusion “a crash will happen,” but as the wiring of credit, delay, speed, and synchronization. At the same time, retain the differences in institutions and technology.

## Success Criteria
The story and the implementation structure must be visible separately. The advance payment of future value must be checkable on a time axis. The spread of non-correspondence through speed and synchronization must be auditable. T/S/R and concrete management figures must be traceable in the same audit bundle. Disclosure failure and unverifiability must be treated as closure of the observation window. It must also be possible to reject or revise the hypothesis when falsification conditions are met.

Success is not “the price did not fall.” Even if the market is rising, it is structurally unstable if the correspondence difference is expanding and correctability is being lost. Conversely, even if prices are adjusting, it is not simply a failure if implementation correspondence is improving and observation and correction paths are maintained.

## Limits
This is not a model for predicting crash timing or the price of individual securities. The original offers a structural-audit framework of story, institution, and speed, together with correspondence, time ethics, and structural rhythm.

Nor is it an argument against the value of AI technology in general. Even when technological value is real, a time gap or implementation gap from market valuation can arise. Treat the substantive value of the technology separately from the market’s structural alignment.

1929 and the present are not identical. The value of the comparison lies not in matching outcomes, but in reading where structures repeat and how the fuse changes.

## To Avoid Misreading
Do not reduce this to the assertion “AI is a bubble.”

Do not turn it into a prediction that 1929 will be replayed.

Do not change it into an argument against AI technology itself.

Do not make it only a discussion of high or low prices.

Do not erase the problem of speed, synchronization, and correspondence differences on the grounds of institutional differences.

Do not replace T/S/R or implementation delay rate with general investment-ranking indicators.

Do not neutralize nondisclosure as merely “a lack of information for judgment”; treat it as a loss of verifiability.
