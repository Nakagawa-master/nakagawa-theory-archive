## Summary
The central issue is not simply whether AI equities are overvalued, nor a prediction that 1929 will repeat. The parent asks how quickly a market narrative can move ahead of institutional and implementation reality, causing correspondence between stated future value and actual structural conditions to fall behind. Future AI earnings are a narrated value; invested capital, supply chains, talent, regulation, demand formation, equipment, standardization, safety evaluation, data supply, inference cost, and regulatory clearance are slower structural conditions that must support that value.

Correspondence is the relation between the narrated value and those real conditions. A gap appears when the story becomes coherent before the slower implementation layers. Temporal Ethics concerns the timing of value: returns that should be realized only after future structural coherence may be advanced into present valuation. The question is therefore not whether expectations are permitted, but whether present valuation is compressing the time required for implementation and verification.

Structural Rhythm concerns the pace of information, trading, valuation changes, capital movement, and synchronization. When market synchronization accelerates faster than implementation and correspondence checking, local non-correspondence can propagate before it is tested. The parent’s comparison with 1929 is structural rather than deterministic: the 1929 fuse is described as credit and delay, whereas the contemporary fuse is speed and synchronization. The difference must remain visible while the recurring wiring is examined.

The corrective sequence is to decompose the earnings narrative into structural components, extend the repayment horizon of expected value, and keep public audit active. T measures correspondence consistency, S structural sustainability, and R the reversibility of verification and correction. Implementation delay rate, inference gross-margin gap, safety-review backlog, disclosure quality, verifiability, and the openness of the observation window belong to the same audit bundle. Persistent threshold deviation or closure of the observation window requires rejection or revision of the hypothesis.

## Applications
- Decompose expected AI earnings into data supply, inference cost, equipment, talent, supply-chain capacity, regulatory clearance, and demand formation.
- Compare narrated value with the pace of capital deployment, supply-chain readiness, talent availability, regulatory adaptation, and demand realization.
- Audit whether future coherence is being over-advanced into present valuation through Temporal Ethics.
- Observe speed and synchronization as Structural-Rhythm variables rather than treating trading velocity as a neutral background condition.
- Separate model-performance improvement from slower layers such as standardization, safety evaluation, regulation, and infrastructure capacity.
- Preserve T/S/R, implementation delay, inference margin, safety-review backlog, disclosure quality, and observation-window status in one audit bundle.
- Use the 1929 comparison to compare causal wiring—credit/delay versus speed/synchronization—rather than to predict an identical crash.
- Treat disclosure failure or unverifiability as a loss of the observation window, not as evidence that no structural problem exists.

## Measurements and audit
- **T: correspondence consistency.** Measures how well narrated future value maps to the real implementation structure that must support it.
- **S: structural sustainability.** Examines whether capital, talent, supply capacity, regulation, and demand formation can persist rather than merely spike temporarily.
- **R: verification/correction reversibility.** Examines whether the hypothesis can be retested, corrected, and withdrawn when evidence changes.
- **Implementation delay rate.** Compares expected implementation timing with actual progress.
- **Inference gross-margin gap.** Compares the expected economic structure with actual inference cost and realized margins.
- **Safety-review backlog.** Compares deployment speed with the pace of safety assessment and regulatory review.
- **Correspondence gap over time.** Tracks whether the distance between narrative and implementation is closing or widening across audit cycles.
- **Disclosure failure.** Records whether necessary structural components are observable.
- **Unverifiability.** Records whether claims and management metrics can be independently rechecked.
- **Observation window.** Records whether the conditions needed for continuing validation remain open.

No one metric is sufficient. A lower implementation-delay rate does not establish structural alignment if inference margins, safety-review backlog, or disclosure quality deteriorate. Conversely, deterioration in one metric does not automatically invalidate the whole structure if the cause is visible, correction remains possible, and R is intact. The audit unit is the relation among the metrics, not a single ranking score.

## Validity conditions
- Audit causal wiring rather than reducing the question to whether prices are high or low.
- Separate narrative from implementation structure and test Correspondence.
- Distinguish technological value from market valuation.
- Treat advancement of future value as a Temporal-Ethics problem.
- Observe speed and synchronization as the contemporary fuse.
- Preserve the distinction between 1929 credit/delay and current speed/synchronization.
- Decompose expected AI earnings into structural components.
- Read T/S/R together with the concrete management metrics.
- Treat disclosure failure and unverifiability as observation-window problems.
- Reject or revise the hypothesis when its falsification conditions are met.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing the parent to a simple claim that “AI is a bubble.”
- Turning the 1929 comparison into a deterministic crash prediction.
- Treating narrative as equivalent to implementation structure.
- Using AI’s real technological value to erase a correspondence gap in market valuation.
- Advancing future value into present valuation without limit.
- Ignoring propagation of non-correspondence through speed and synchronization.
- Treating stable prices as proof of structural alignment.
- Treating a KPI-only display as a sufficient causal audit.
- Preserving a hypothesis despite disclosure failure or unverifiability.
- Changing audit conditions after threshold deviation merely to protect the narrative.

## Falsification conditions
Following the parent’s integrated audit logic, the hypothesis must be rejected or revised when implementation delay rate, inference gross-margin gap, safety-review backlog, or other configured source-grounded indicators persistently cross their defined thresholds. Closure of the observation window through disclosure failure or unverifiability also removes a necessary condition for maintaining the hypothesis.

Falsification is not determined by market direction alone. A rising market can coexist with deteriorating correspondence, sustainability, and reversibility and therefore still require revision. A falling market can coexist with improving implementation correspondence and an open correction loop and therefore is not, by itself, proof that the structural hypothesis failed. The decisive object is the relation among narrated value, implementation, time, synchronization, observability, and correctability.

Do not turn the parent into a general denial of AI’s technological value. Do not convert the 1929 comparison into a certain prediction of the same crash. Conversely, do not erase the warning about structural recurrence, speed, and synchronization merely because institutions differ. Do not treat market price as a substitute for Correspondence. Do not turn T/S/R into generic investment scores, and do not detach implementation delay, inference gross-margin gap, or safety-review backlog from the parent’s own audit logic.
