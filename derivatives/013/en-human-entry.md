## 15-Second Explanation
This origin is not the general claim that “sales should avoid pushiness.” It treats contact, interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, consent maturity, and closing demand as different stages. When closing is demanded even though the other party is not yet in a state to judge independently, the sales contact changes from an entry point for consultation into something to defend against, and the consultation capacity of the market as a whole is lost.

## Why It Is Needed
Inquiries, document requests, initial meetings, conversations, and light interest are important contact points for sales. But they are not purchase intent. Whether the other party sufficiently understands the problem, recognizes the need, connects value to their own situation, and can judge cost, timing, and implementation conditions is a separate question.

When an organization converts contact points early into “prospects,” “probability,” or “planned revenue,” sales representatives are pulled toward making internal reporting work rather than arranging the other party’s judgment conditions. Pressure then arises—“When will you decide?” or “Why has this not been decided?”—even though the other party is still at the entry point of consideration.

From this experience, customers learn that “if I consult, I will be sold to” and “if I show interest, I will be pressed.” As a result, they begin to avoid inquiries and consultation themselves, narrowing even the entry point that connects them to information and proposals they genuinely need. The origin treats this loss on the market side as damage to “consultation capacity.”

## Practical Process
**1. Treat contact as contact.** Do not immediately convert inquiries, document requests, meetings, or conversations into purchase intent.

**2. Separate the stages.** Distinguish interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, and consent maturity.

**3. Prepare judgment conditions.** Advance toward a state in which the other party can compare need, value, cost, timing, implementation conditions, and alternatives independently.

**4. Check the position of the closing demand.** Place closing confirmation after consent maturity. If it comes earlier, check whether it has reversed into pressure.

**5. See organizational pressure.** Check whether prospect probability, planned revenue, sales meetings, and evaluation systems are advancing ahead of the customer’s formation of intent.

**6. See the customer’s freedom.** Confirm whether deferral, refusal, reconsideration, and additional questions are actually possible.

**7. See market-side learning.** Check whether defensive learning—“if I consult, I will be sold to”—has arisen and whether inquiries and information disclosure are declining.

## Examples of Application
**B2B sales.** Fixing the sales timing at the initial meeting and prioritizing closing over customer understanding in order to protect that number.

**High-involvement consumer goods.** Treating a document request or consultation as purchase intent and demanding a contract decision before cost, alternatives, and implementation conditions are organized.

**SaaS.** Treating a demo or trial as implementation intent and moving to a closing demand before internal operating conditions or the decision-maker have been determined.

**The market as a whole.** Early closing is repeated until sales contacts themselves are avoided from the consultation stage before purchase.

## Success Criteria
Contact and purchase intent are separated. Interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, judgment conditions, and consent maturity are visible as stages. It is important that the other party can independently judge need, value, cost, timing, alternatives, and implementation conditions, while refusal, deferral, and reconsideration remain possible.

Closing confirmation is placed after consent maturity, and internal prospect management does not overwrite the customer’s formation of intent. The sales representative’s attention can return to customer understanding rather than staying only on internal reporting. Further, if consultation and information disclosure are maintained on the market side, the bad causality addressed by AKI-012 weakens.

## Limitations
AKI-012 does not reject sales in general. It is not a theory that makes closing itself, revenue responsibility, KPIs, or prospect management themselves bad. The problem is that they erase stage differences in intent formation and convert contact points or interest into purchase intent too early.

Consent maturity is not an automatic determination of legal consent or contractual effect. This theory alone does not settle a legal evaluation.

Nor does the origin define universal conversion rates, consent maturity levels, consultation-capacity rates, success probabilities, or numerical thresholds. Therefore, this public reading does not invent numbers to automate judgment.

## Misreading Prevention
This is not a claim to “never close.” Closing confirmation after consent maturity can serve as confirmation of a decision.

Nor is it a claim to “wait for the customer to do everything.” Sales can support problem organization, value explanation, and formation of judgment conditions. The important point is not to skip the other party’s decision stage for internal convenience.

Nor is it a character judgment that “the sales representative is bad.” It also examines higher-level structures—prospect management, evaluation systems, sales meetings, and fixed revenue timing—that draw the representative’s attention toward internal reporting.
