## Summary
AKI-012 does not reject sales, closing, revenue responsibility, pipeline management, forecasting, or KPIs as such. It analyzes a specific bad causality: a contact or expression of interest is treated as purchase intent or mature consent before the other party has enough conditions to judge.

The parent keeps contact, interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, value connection, implementation conditions, consent maturity, and closing confirmation distinct. The central question is not whether closing occurs, but where the closing request is placed in the causal order.

A sales organization may legitimately classify opportunities and forecast revenue. The failure begins when those internal representations advance faster than the customer's actual decision state. An inquiry, document request, demonstration, trial, introductory meeting, or expression of curiosity can become an expected probability or closing date before the customer has established the problem, need, alternatives, cost, timing, implementation conditions, and freedom to choose.

When this happens repeatedly, internal reporting pressure can outrun customer understanding. The customer can learn that inquiry itself invites pressure and may begin to avoid asking, comparing, disclosing uncertainty, or contacting a provider before being ready to buy. The theory therefore extends beyond one unpleasant interaction to the market's consultation capacity: whether people who have not yet decided to buy can still safely ask, compare, defer, refuse, and leave.

## Applications
**Sales meetings.** Separate the customer's actual decision stage from internal revenue expectation before asking for a closing date.

**CRM and pipeline design.** Do not automatically equate an inquiry, download, demonstration, trial, or meeting with purchase intent.

**Consultation channels.** Preserve inquiry, comparison, uncertainty disclosure, deferral, and refusal as legitimate states even when no purchase decision exists.

**High-involvement purchases.** Inspect whether closing pressure arrives before the buyer has enough information and implementation context to judge freely.

**AI sales support.** Lead scoring, summaries, next-best-action systems, and generated follow-ups can intensify the substitution if behavioral signals are converted into assumed consent. AI output should remain a prediction for internal management rather than an authority over the customer's decision state.

**Evaluation systems.** Inspect whether compensation, forecast accuracy, meeting routines, and managerial questioning reward customer understanding or reward premature conversion of early contact into a closing promise.

**Post-contract review.** A completed deal does not by itself prove that the earlier consent sequence was structurally sound if major recognition gaps later appear.

## Measurements and audit
The parent does not define a universal conversion threshold, consent score, closing probability, consultation-capacity percentage, optimum KPI, or numerical maturity threshold. No such number is created here.

Observation should instead preserve the same structural distinctions on both sides of the hypothesis:
- Is contact distinguishable from purchase intent?
- Is interest distinguishable from consent?
- Are need, value, alternatives, cost, timing, and implementation conditions available before closing is demanded?
- Is internal pipeline probability visibly separate from consent maturity?
- Can the customer refuse, defer, compare, ask more questions, or leave?
- Does the salesperson retain attention for customer understanding, or does internal reporting dominate the interaction?
- Do inquiries or disclosures decline after repeated early-closing experiences?
- Does market consultation capacity remain available to people who have not yet decided to buy?
- Does lower consultation trigger more contact volume, stronger probability demands, or earlier closing, thereby reinforcing the loop?

## Validity conditions
- Contact or interest is treated as if it were purchase intent or mature consent.
- Internal pipeline state advances faster than the customer's judgment state.
- Problem understanding, need confirmation, judgment conditions, value connection, or implementation conditions remain immature.
- Forecast, evaluation, or meeting pressure gives operational priority to closing before customer understanding.
- Closing demand appears before the other party can freely judge, defer, compare, refuse, or choose.
- Repeated interaction produces defensive learning or reduced consultation.
- The organization compensates for lower consultation by increasing the same pressure that helped produce it.

## Failure conditions
- Contact, interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, and consent maturity remain distinct.
- Internal forecasting does not substitute for the customer's actual decision state.
- The customer has enough information and implementation context to judge freely.
- Closing confirmation is placed after consent maturity rather than used to manufacture it.
- Refusal, deferral, comparison, and additional questions remain practically available.
- Repeated observation shows no defensive learning, trust erosion, or consultation loss associated with the alleged sequence.

The interpretation also fails if it becomes a criticism of all sales, all closing, all forecasting, all KPIs, or all fast decisions. Those are not the parent claim.

## Falsification conditions
The parent origin's integrated audit summary states: if purchase intent, consideration intent, problem, necessity, decision conditions, and implementation conditions are mutually confirmed and closing requests occur only after value connection and consent maturation, the hypothesis is rejected or revised.

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not reject sales, closing, revenue responsibility, forecasting, or KPIs as such.
- Do not infer purchase intent from contact alone.
- Do not equate interest with consent.
- Do not equate internal forecast confidence with customer decision maturity.
- Do not classify a fast sale as structurally invalid merely because it is fast.
- Do not classify a slow sale as structurally sound merely because it is slow.
- Do not reduce the causality to salesperson morality; inspect evaluation, forecast, meeting, and pipeline structures.
- Do not automate legal consent or contract-law conclusions from this theory.
- Do not create a universal consent score, closing threshold, probability, consultation percentage, or ethics score absent from the parent.
- Do not use a successful conversion as automatic proof that free judgment was preserved.
- Preserve the market-level consultation-capacity consequence and the compensating-pressure feedback loop.
