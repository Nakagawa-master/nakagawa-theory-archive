## Summary
AKI-012 does not reject sales, closing, revenue responsibility, pipeline management, or KPIs as such. It analyzes the bad causality produced when a mere contact or expression of interest is substituted for purchase intent or mature consent, so closing pressure is introduced before the other party has enough conditions to judge. The parent keeps contact, interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, value connection, implementation conditions, consent maturity, and closing confirmation distinct.

The distinction is causal rather than moral. A sales organization may legitimately classify opportunities, forecast revenue, and ask for decisions. The problem begins when those internal representations outrun the customer's actual decision state. A first inquiry, document request, demonstration, introductory meeting, or expression of curiosity can be converted inside the organization into an expected probability or closing date before the customer has established the problem, need, alternatives, cost, timing, implementation conditions, and freedom to choose. Internal certainty then becomes a substitute for external consent maturity.

When these stages are collapsed, the salesperson is structurally pulled toward internal reporting before customer understanding is mature. Questions such as “when will this close?” or “why has this not closed?” can become operational priorities while the customer's judgment conditions remain incomplete. The source therefore locates part of the causality upstream of the individual salesperson, in forecast structures, evaluation systems, meeting routines, and pipeline pressure that reward premature conversion of contact into expected revenue.

The customer can then learn that inquiry itself invites pressure. A person who only wanted to understand a problem, compare options, or disclose uncertainty may begin to withhold questions because speaking to sales is experienced as entering a closing sequence. Repetition can transform the sales contact from a consultation entrance into a defensive object. The source's scope therefore extends beyond one unpleasant interaction to the market's consultation capacity: whether people who have not yet decided to buy can still safely ask, disclose, compare, defer, and leave.

The theory distinguishes closing confirmation after mature consent from consentless closing demand before mature consent. Speed itself is not the criterion. A fast decision can be structurally sound if judgment conditions and free choice are already present. A slow process can still be coercive if internal forecast pressure substitutes for the customer's own decision process. The relevant question is where closing demand sits in the causal order.

If consultation capacity declines, organizations may compensate by increasing contact volume, activity targets, probability pressure, and earlier closing. That compensation can intensify the original cause: more people experience early contact as sales pressure, consultation becomes scarcer, and the organization receives even less honest problem disclosure. AKI-012 isolates this self-reinforcing sequence rather than reducing the issue to etiquette, individual personality, or a general slogan of “customer first.”

## Applications
**Sales meetings.** Separate the customer's actual decision stage from internal revenue expectation before asking for a closing date. A forecast may exist, but it must not be treated as evidence that need, value, implementation conditions, and consent have matured.

**CRM and pipeline design.** Prevent an inquiry, content download, demonstration, trial, or meeting from automatically becoming purchase intent. Internal classifications should remain visibly different from the customer's own decision state.

**Inside sales and consultation channels.** Preserve inquiry and problem disclosure as legitimate outcomes even when no purchase decision exists. A person should be able to ask, compare, disclose uncertainty, defer, or leave without the initial contact being retrospectively interpreted as promised revenue.

**High-involvement purchases.** Where cost, timing, alternatives, internal approval, or implementation consequences matter, inspect whether closing pressure arrives before the buyer has enough conditions to judge. The theory does not require slowness; it requires causal order.

**AI sales support.** Lead scoring, next-best-action systems, summaries, and generated follow-ups can intensify the substitution if behavioral signals are converted into assumed consent. AI output must remain a prediction about internal management, not an authority over the customer's decision state.

**Evaluation systems and sales meetings.** Inspect whether compensation, forecast accuracy, review rituals, and managerial questioning reward customer understanding or reward premature conversion of early contact into a closing promise. The source places causal responsibility partly in these upstream structures rather than solely in the salesperson's temperament.

**Post-contract review.** If a deal closes but the customer later reveals major recognition gaps about need, value, conditions, timing, or implementation, the single conversion cannot by itself be treated as proof that the earlier consent sequence was sound.

**Market-level observation.** Track whether people become less willing to ask questions, disclose problems, compare alternatives, or contact providers before they are ready to buy. The distinctive source concern is the erosion of this consultation layer, not merely one deal's outcome.

## Measurements and audit
The parent does not define a universal conversion threshold, consent score, closing probability, consultation-capacity percentage, optimum KPI, or numerical maturity threshold. No such number is created here. Reversal-evaluation remains source-bounded by comparing the same structural variables on both sides of the hypothesized sequence.

- Is contact distinguishable from purchase intent?
- Is interest distinguishable from consent?
- Is problem recognition distinguishable from implementation intent?
- Are need, value, alternatives, cost, timing, and implementation conditions available to the customer before closing is demanded?
- Is internal pipeline probability visibly separate from consent maturity?
- Does an internal closing date change the customer's actual decision conditions, or merely management's expectation?
- Can the customer refuse, defer, compare, ask more questions, or leave without the relationship being treated as failed compliance?
- Does the salesperson retain attention for customer understanding, or does internal reporting dominate the interaction?
- Do inquiries or disclosures decline after repeated early-closing experiences?
- Do customers describe sales contact as a pressure entrance rather than a consultation entrance?
- Do post-contract recognition gaps increase when closing precedes mature judgment conditions?
- Does market consultation capacity remain available to people who have not yet decided to buy?
- Does organizational compensation for lower consultation take the form of more contact volume, stronger probability demands, or earlier closing, thereby reinforcing the causal loop?

These observations are not a derivative-created legal test or universal sales-performance KPI. If a source-provided number is ever used, it must remain attached to its source, measurement actor, measurement object, source modality, permitted use scope, and non-guarantee scope. A number cannot travel alone as proof of consent or proof of the theory.

## Validity conditions
- Contact or interest is being treated as if it were purchase intent or mature consent.
- Internal pipeline state advances faster than the customer's judgment state.
- Problem understanding, need confirmation, judgment conditions, value connection, or implementation conditions remain immature.
- Forecast, evaluation, or meeting pressure gives operational priority to closing before customer understanding.
- Closing demand is placed before the other party can freely judge, defer, compare, refuse, or choose.
- The customer learns that inquiry or disclosure invites pressure.
- Defensive avoidance or reduced consultation follows the repeated sequence.
- The market loses some capacity for pre-purchase consultation and honest problem disclosure.
- The organization responds to reduced consultation by increasing the same pressure that helped produce it.

## Failure conditions
- Contact, interest, problem recognition, consideration intent, purchase intent, judgment conditions, and consent maturity remain distinct.
- An internal forecast does not substitute for the customer's actual decision state.
- The customer has enough information and implementation context to judge freely.
- Closing confirmation is placed after consent maturity rather than used to manufacture it.
- Refusal, deferral, comparison, and additional questions remain practically available.
- Forecast and evaluation systems do not force the salesperson to sacrifice customer understanding for internal reporting.
- Sales contact remains a usable consultation entrance for people who are not ready to buy.
- Repeated observation shows no defensive learning, trust erosion, or market-level consultation loss associated with the alleged sequence.

The interpretation also fails if it becomes a criticism of all sales, all closing, all forecasting, all KPIs, or all fast decisions. Those are not the parent claim.

## Falsification conditions
The parent origin's integrated audit summary states that the applicability or causal connection of AKI-012 weakens and becomes subject to revision if, even with early closing, customers' free judgment is preserved, defensive avoidance of contact does not increase, post-contract recognition gaps do not worsen, and sales trust and the market's consultability are not continuously impaired across broad observation. This derivative index does not invent a separate falsification theory.

- Do not classify a slow sale as structurally sound merely because it is slow.
- Do not reduce the causality to salesperson morality; inspect evaluation, forecast, meeting, and pipeline structures.
- Do not automate legal consent or contract-law conclusions from this theory.
- Do not create a universal consent score, closing threshold, probability, consultation percentage, or ethics score absent from the parent.
- Do not use a successful conversion as automatic proof that free judgment was preserved.
- Do not weaken the origin into generic “customer-first sales” advice.
