## 1. Summary
Connection Value Accounting records the effect of relationship structures on future decision and collaboration friction as “connection value.” It observes connection density, memory of agreement, reversibility, and related aspects without reducing persons to popularity or a single credit score, then links evidenced connection-based contribution to social allocation.

## 2. Concepts
- connection value: capacity of relationship structure to reduce future decision/collaboration friction.
- connection value accounting: social accounting that records connection-based value and links it to allocation.
- connection density: thickness of connection including overlap, continuity, and effective function rather than raw count.
- memory of agreement: reusable traces of decisions, promises, and collaboration.
- reversibility: ability to correct, change, or leave a connection or agreement.
- multi-aspect evaluation: preserving multiple relational aspects rather than one popularity/credit number.
- connection reward: linking value and contribution generated through connection to social distribution.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
transaction price alone leaves relational value partly invisible
→ connection affects future collaboration and decision friction
→ record relationship history and agreements
→ observe density, agreement memory, and reversibility together
→ make connection value visible as an accounting object
→ record concrete contribution
→ connect it to social allocation
→ value circulates into future connection and collaboration
```

## 5. Applications
- continuing collaboration: record whether agreement history reduces restart friction.
- referral/bridging: test whether introductions lead to actual agreement, collaboration, or value creation rather than count introductions alone.
- inter-organizational work: observe whether relationship history supports later joint decisions.
- communities: record mutual connections, reusable agreement, and correction/exit possibility rather than participation count alone.
- social allocation: keep allocation evidence traceable to concrete relations and contributions instead of personality scores.

More connections, records, or distributed value do not indicate higher connection value if collaboration friction does not decrease, agreement cannot be reused, or exit becomes harder. A small number of effective, reusable, reversible connections is not low-value merely because the count is small.

## 7. Validity conditions
- Subjects, purpose, and history of a connection are traceable.
- Contact count remains distinct from connection value.
- Agreement memory materially supports later judgment or collaboration.
- Correction, change, and exit remain possible.
- Allocation evidence returns to specific connection/contribution.
- Persons are not reduced to popularity or a single credit number.
- Accounting remains distinct from governance mechanisms.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Replacing connection value with follower/contact/business-card count.
- Ranking the whole person through one social-credit or personality score.
- Turning agreement history into uncorrectable, non-exitable permanent records.
- Treating difficult-to-exit relationships as higher value.
- Reducing connection reward to direct pricing or a replacement currency.
- Allocation cannot be traced back to concrete relations and contribution.
- Importing adjacent governance mechanisms as OD067’s parent core.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- High connection density does not reduce future decision/collaboration friction.
- More agreement memory is not reused in subsequent judgment or collaboration.
- Reversibility makes the proposed allocation system impossible to sustain.
- Recording repeatedly converts into popularity competition or personality scoring.
- Allocations cannot be traced to the actual relation/contribution that produced value.
- Existing accounting methods can represent the same relational value sufficiently and reproducibly without an additional connection-value accounting layer.

## 10. Required distinctions
- connection value / contact or follower count
- connection density / size of personal network
- memory of agreement / permanent fixation
- reversibility / long-term lock-in
- connection value accounting / personality or social-credit scoring
- connection reward / simple pricing of relationships
- multi-aspect evaluation / one composite score
- accounting / governance
- record / surveillance
- allocation / popularity vote
