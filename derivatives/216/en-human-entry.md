## 15-Second Explanation
The “surveillance” in this original is not limited to cameras or censorship. It is an operating principle that makes deviation costly through observation, recording, evaluation, and later reference, narrowing the range of people’s actions before anyone orders them to do so.

Fear makes departure dangerous, benefit rationalizes remaining, and surveillance suppresses deviation. When the three work in sequence, order can continue for a long time even in a society where consent has weakened.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Fear and benefit alone still leave people able to object, depart from norms, and move to another community. Because directly coercing everyone every time is impractical, durable civilization uses the temporal structure of “being watched,” “being recorded,” and “being evaluated later.”

As a result, people anticipate what they will be disadvantaged for saying, where they should remain silent, and whom it will cost them to connect with. Self-discipline arises without direct orders, lowering the cost of maintaining order.

## Practical Process
1. **Identify the object of observation.** Confirm what has become visible.
2. **Check recordability.** See whether observation becomes a history that can be referenced later.
3. **Check evaluation linkage.** Confirm whether records connect to appraisal, reputation, allocation, or affiliation decisions.
4. **Check the cost of deviation.** See what disadvantages can be expected from objection, movement, connection, or speech.
5. **Check self-discipline.** See whether behavior has narrowed even without orders.
6. **Separate stated purpose from actual function.** Do not judge by stated purposes such as safety, efficiency, or transparency alone.
7. **Check linkage with fear and benefit.** Confirm whether departure, remaining, and suppression of deviation have become integrated.
8. **Check falsifiability.** Verify whether objection or movement is actually possible without disadvantage.

## Examples of Application
### Workplace
When speech and relationships connect to an ongoing evaluation history, and people refrain from objection for fear of disadvantages in future appraisals or case allocation, do not judge freedom only by whether explicit orders exist.

### Digital Space
If self-censorship occurs in anticipation that posts, reviews, visibility, or a history of backlash will be referenced later, examine the connection between observation and evaluation.

### Institutional Operation
Even records made for safety or transparency should be examined for whether they make objection or movement excessively costly.

### Social Observation
Even where direct violence or censorship is limited, if self-discipline is strong and the range of behavior has narrowed, separately verify whether the order is consensual.

## Success Criteria
- Observation, recording, evaluation, and later reference can be grasped separately.
- The strength of surveillance is not judged by information volume alone.
- The relationship between the cost of deviation and self-discipline can be observed.
- The absence of direct coercion is not equated with freedom.
- Stated purposes such as safety, efficiency, and transparency can be separated from actual control functions.
- The sequential relationship among fear, benefit, and surveillance is retained.
- Legitimate observation and recording can be distinguished from surveillance as a substitute for consent.

## Limitations
Observation, recording, and evaluation are not inherently bad. They may be necessary for safety, accountability, quality control, and learning. To judge something as a surveillance main organ, it is necessary to see whether these raise the cost of deviation, narrow the range of objection, movement, and connection, and make self-discipline function as a substitute for coercion.

It is also necessary not to explain all of durable civilization through surveillance alone, but to return to its relationship with fear, benefit, myth, and inertia.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not limit surveillance to cameras or state censorship.
- Do not regard every record as surveillance.
- Do not determine surveillance intensity by the amount of information alone.
- Do not regard self-discipline as voluntary consent.
- Do not treat limited direct coercion as proof of freedom.
- Do not justify it only by the stated purposes of safety or transparency.
- Do not explain durable civilization through surveillance alone.
