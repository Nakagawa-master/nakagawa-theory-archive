## Summary
The parent defines surveillance as the third primary engine of endurance civilization. Surveillance here is not limited to cameras or censorship. It is a structural operating principle in which observation, recording, evaluation, and later re-reference raise the cost of deviation and cause people to narrow speech, association, dissent, and mobility before receiving an explicit command. Fear makes exit dangerous, profit makes remaining rational, and surveillance suppresses deviation. In series, the three can maintain order even after consensus has weakened.

## Concepts
- Third primary engine: surveillance as the deviation-suppression engine after fear and profit.
- Observation: behavior becomes visible.
- Recording: observation becomes a retrievable history.
- Evaluation: records connect to appraisal, allocation, membership, or other consequences.
- Re-reference: past behavior can be recalled in future judgment.
- Cost of deviation: anticipated disadvantage linked to dissent, movement, association, or speech.
- Self-discipline: preemptive narrowing of behavior without direct command.
- Coercion substitution: maintaining order without repeatedly using overt violence.

## Causal chain
Consensus, trust, and meaning weaken → fear raises the cost of exit → profit rationalizes remaining → deviation still remains possible → observation, recording, evaluation, and re-reference become routine → deviation can connect to future disadvantage → people anticipate evaluation and self-adjust → order can be maintained with less overt coercion → the control function becomes less visible under labels such as safety, efficiency, or transparency → surveillance operates as the third primary engine of endurance civilization.

## Applications
- Workplaces: examine whether speech and behavior records connect to appraisal or allocation in ways that suppress dissent.
- Digital spaces: examine self-censorship caused by anticipated review, visibility shifts, controversy, or persistent posting history.
- Institutional operations: test whether records justified by safety, efficiency, or transparency make dissent or movement excessively costly.
- Social analysis: do not equate low overt violence with consensus; identify what produces self-discipline.

## Measurements and audit
- What is observed, what is stored, and who can re-reference it?
- Which evaluations, allocations, or membership decisions use the records?
- What concrete disadvantages can follow dissent, movement, association, or speech?
- Does self-censorship occur without explicit commands?
- Can the stated purposes of safety, efficiency, or transparency be separated from the actual deviation-control function?
- Does surveillance substitute for overt coercion?
- Is it linked in series with fear and profit?
- Can dissent and movement occur in practice without disadvantage?

## Validity conditions
- Order still needs to be maintained after consensus weakens.
- Fear and profit leave residual possibilities of deviation.
- Behavior is observable, recorded, and connectable to later evaluation.
- Deviation or dissent is expected to create future disadvantage.
- That expectation generates self-discipline without direct command.

## Failure conditions
- Treating all observation or recording as the surveillance engine.
- Restricting surveillance to cameras or state censorship.
- Measuring surveillance only by volume of collected information.
- Treating low overt violence as proof of consensus.
- Accepting safety, efficiency, or transparency labels without examining actual function.
- Separating surveillance from fear and profit.

## Falsification conditions
If observation, recording, and evaluation are transparent and limited in purpose and scope; dissent, movement, and opposition can in practice become action without disadvantage; past records do not automatically connect to arbitrary exclusion or allocation; participants can explain institutional meaning and legitimacy; and institutional revision functions, the classification of surveillance as a deviation-suppression primary engine should be revised. Its scope should also be reconsidered if reducing surveillance-like observation does not destabilize order because meaning, trust, and consensus continue to sustain participation.

## Required distinctions
- Surveillance ≠ observation in general.
- Recording ≠ domination by itself.
- Evaluation ≠ proof of deviation suppression.
- Safety purpose ≠ automatic legitimacy.
- Efficiency ≠ consensus.
- Self-discipline ≠ voluntary consent.
- Absence of overt coercion ≠ freedom.
- Visibility ≠ legitimacy.

## Interpretation constraints
This theory does not condemn observation, recording, or evaluation themselves. Legitimate records may be necessary for safety, accountability, quality control, and learning. The relevant question is whether these mechanisms are operating as a substitute for consensus by raising deviation costs, narrowing dissent or mobility, and making self-discipline function as indirect coercion. The parent sets no fixed numerical thresholds, so independent counts, scores, percentages, or durations must not be added as official conditions.
