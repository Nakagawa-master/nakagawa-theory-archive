## Summary
Market price, demand, and atmosphere can emerge as provisional synchronization through repeated observation of purchase, waiting, exit, and other behavior without explicit agreement. Silence is not consent; exit capacity, information asymmetry, and delayed reactions must remain auditable.
## Concepts
silent consensus; market synchronization; price; demand; expectations; observation; participation; exit; waiting; information asymmetry; reversibility; provisional equilibrium.
## Causal chain
signals observed -> expectations inferred -> behavior updated -> responses repeated -> local synchronization -> tacit equilibrium perceived -> imitation reinforces it -> hidden reactions audited -> equilibrium revised when falsified.
## State model
`signals_observed -> expectations_inferred -> responses_repeat -> tacit_equilibrium -> hidden_reactions_checked -> reversibility_preserved -> consensus_retested`
## Applications
Audit pricing changes, product launches, labor markets, and organizational consensus through retention, exit, waiting, substitution, and behavior rather than speech alone.
## Measurements and audit
Price response, demand, retention, exit, revisit, substitution, inventory, inquiries, reaction delay, exit capacity, and observation bias.
## Validity conditions
Multiple actors observable; time axis retained; participation separated from exit; external shocks separated; silence remains multi-interpretable; reselection remains possible.
## Failure conditions
silence=consent; price synchronization=value agreement; inability to exit=support; one KPI=collective preference; temporary equilibrium=permanent consensus.
## Falsification conditions
Revise when exit, substitution, or dissatisfaction rises despite apparent synchronization, hidden groups reverse the interpretation, or the model fails after external shocks are controlled.
## Required distinctions
silence/consent; price synchronization/value agreement; nonparticipation/support; waiting/indifference; observation/preference; equilibrium/permanence.
## Interpretation constraints
Do not use this theory to justify collusion, manipulation, or coercive conformity. Do not invent unpublished thresholds. Never convert absence of speech directly into collective consent.
