## Summary
NAOS is a consensus-formation operating system for national-scale decisions, not a system for strengthening centralized state control. It treats major national problems as failures of accountable explanation and consensus rather than merely shortages of budget or capability. Assets, liabilities, future obligations, risks, benefits, time, stakeholders, and responsibility are represented in a common Transaction Unit. Optimization is defined as minimizing regret by maximizing explainability rather than maximizing efficiency or a single national-wealth metric. For international portability, the consensus Core is separated from country-specific Plugins. T/S/R is Transparency / Selectability / Responsibility. Audit Bundles, a Deviation Ledger, stakeholder access, reason-giving, appeals, and limits on over-collection and purpose drift are legitimacy conditions.

## Concepts
- NAOS: National Asset Optimization System / consensus-formation OS.
- Consensus Failure: absence of accountable explanation enabling informed agreement.
- Transaction Unit: stakeholder, object, time, risk, burden, benefit, and responsibility in one unit.
- Optimization = Regret Minimization through explainability.
- Core: invariant consensus architecture.
- Plugin: country-specific institutional layer.
- Transparency: comparable disclosure of value, future cost, risk, time, responsibility.
- Selectability: meaningful options before coercion.
- Responsibility: separated AI / professional / stakeholder responsibility.
- Tension Equilibrium: management of conflict without erasing it.
- Deviation Defense: trace and repair structural drift.
- Audit Bundle / Deviation Ledger: auditable re-consent surfaces.

## Causal chain
```text
state is treated as one decision-maker
→ actual multi-actor responsibility remains distributed
→ accountability becomes unclear
→ deferral, fragmentation, and local optimization persist
→ redefine root cause as consensus failure
→ represent options in Transaction Units
→ redefine optimization as regret minimization
→ separate Core from Plugins
→ place Transparency / Selectability / Responsibility before coercion
→ preserve tension equilibrium and deviation defense
→ audit through Audit Bundles, Deviation Ledger, and rights procedures
→ revise NAOS when explainability, selectability, or responsibility deteriorate
```

## Applications
- Compare urban contraction alternatives using future cost, benefit, timing, burden, and responsibility.
- Treat vacant housing as a re-contracting problem across inheritance, rights, tax, repair, demolition, emotion, and neighborhood risk.
- Compare disaster recovery, relocation, compensation, and future obligations in one explanatory form.
- Express tax/benefit, infrastructure, land protection, and other national choices in Transaction Units rather than slogans.
- Keep the consensus Core invariant while connecting local registration, tax, judiciary, privacy, and finance through Plugins.

## Measurements and audit
- Are alternatives comparable through numbers, time, and responsibility?
- Does coercion precede understanding and choice?
- Are AI / professional / stakeholder responsibilities separate?
- Has a country-specific Plugin altered the Core?
- Are unexplained exceptions, deferral, or fragmented optimization increasing?
- Is the Audit Bundle consistent with T/S/R?
- Does the Deviation Ledger preserve structural drift records?
- Do stakeholders have access, reasons, and appeal procedures?
- Are over-collection and purpose drift prevented?

## Validity conditions
- Explainability and choice must precede coercion.
- Transaction Units include time, risk, burden, benefit, and responsibility.
- Core and Plugin remain separated.
- Transparency / Selectability / Responsibility hold simultaneously.
- AI, professional, and stakeholder responsibilities remain separated.
- Audit Bundles and Deviation Ledger remain available.
- Stakeholder access, reason-giving, and appeals remain available.
- Over-collection and purpose drift are prohibited by default.

## Failure conditions
- Reduction to government digitization or smart-city management.
- Centering surveillance, enforcement, or attribute labeling.
- Using national wealth or efficiency as a single objective function.
- Coercion before understandable alternatives.
- Allowing local institutions to mutate the consensus Core.
- Blurring AI, professional, and stakeholder responsibility.
- One-way transparency, black-box decisions, or unavailable appeals.

## Falsification conditions
NAOS must remain auditable and self-correctable. A publishable Audit Bundle consistent with T/S/R is required. If explainability collapses, coercion increasingly precedes understanding, or responsibility becomes persistently ambiguous, the NAOS hypothesis must be rejected or revised. Drift of the Core into Plugins, repurposing toward surveillance or national-wealth maximization, or loss of stakeholder access, reason-giving, or appeals also constitutes deviation requiring correction.

## Required distinctions
- national optimization vs state control.
- consensus formation vs efficiency maximization.
- regret minimization vs national-wealth maximization.
- explainability vs disclosure alone.
- Transaction Unit vs single policy metric.
- Core vs Plugin.
- Transparency vs one-way surveillance.
- Selectability vs coercion.
- Responsibility vs responsibility diffusion.
- Audit vs moral condemnation.

## Interpretation constraints
- NAOS is not a device to strengthen state power.
- Government DX, smart-city control, surveillance, enforcement, and attribute labeling are not its goals.
- A globally portable standard does not mean one global legal system.
- National wealth or efficiency must not become the single objective function.
- Consensus formation does not mean suppressing disagreement until everyone agrees.
- Transparency requires stakeholder access to the same explanatory format.
