## 15-Second Explanation
The original reads bear damage, the weakening of hunting associations, and ecosystem change not as separate problems, but as a collapse of correspondence among society, ecology, and ethics. It positions making bear meat consumption common not as simply turning bears into a resource, but as redesigning a coexistence structure that reconnects the broken lines of correspondence among people, bears, mountains, and time.

## Why Eating Bear Meat Becomes a “Coexistence Structure”
The original begins with more than bear appearances and damage. It treats insufficient forest yields, dependence on garbage and crops near human settlements, the convergence of bear ranges and human living areas, the aging and underpayment of hunting associations, and the gaps among government, residents, and hunters as one problem.

From the perspective of causality, it reads bear damage as a gap between “mountain time” and “human time.” The rhythms of supply, demand, and safety have stopped fitting, and that mismatch is reflected as damage.

From the perspective of correspondence, making bear meat consumption common is not simply expanding consumption. It connects the “conversion of life” to culture, institutions, and the economy, and redesigns the meaning of culling within social structure instead of leaving it ethically isolated.

Temporal ethics does not process ecosystems only through present economic rationality. To avoid ecosystem destruction becoming future debt, it places design conditions on the side of bears, mountains, and time, and seeks to align capture and local management with the “breathing of the mountain.”

Bear meat is therefore not merely food culture or a regional economic measure. It is a “structural ethical act” that repositions the four beings—human, bear, mountain, and time—within one resonant structure. The original describes a turn from solutions for humans alone toward “harmonious redistribution across all existence.”

The important point is that increasing demand for bear meat is not itself the objective. Even if demand arises, it does not become a coexistence structure unless it corresponds with bear populations, ecosystems, capture pressure, local safety, and the continuity of hunting associations. The original treats demand creation and ecosystem conservation as two layers, and builds in revision of the hypothesis itself if successful use turns into failed conservation.

## Relationship Among the Four
**People.** They are residents who suffer damage, while also designing institutions, markets, capture, and consumption. Maximizing human safety alone externalizes the conditions of mountains and bears.

**Bears.** They are returned to the structure not merely as causes of damage, but as beings that act within ecosystems and are affected by dependence on settlements and changing habitat conditions.

**Mountains.** They are not a backdrop to bears, but a structural element with yields, habitat, seasonal variation, and ecological time. The question is whether the “breathing of the mountain” fits human management cycles.

**Time.** It connects present damage control with future ecosystem debt. Even if damage falls in the short term, coexistence cannot be declared if over-capture or local extinction risk is sent into the future.

## Reading Procedure
**1. Do not look only at surface damage.** Lay out the mountain, living-area, hunting-association, administrative, and ecosystem gaps behind bear damage.

**2. Look for time gaps.** Examine “mountain time” and “human time,” and the mismatch among supply, demand, and safety.

**3. Read eating bear meat as conversion of life.** See it not as simple consumption, but as an act that reconnects culling, culture, institutions, and the economy.

**4. Add temporal ethics.** Put not only present human convenience but also the conditions of bears, mountains, and the future into the design.

**5. Establish connection responsibility.** State who connects ecosystems and institutions, and under what conditions they operate.

**6. Avoid excessive resource conversion.** Treat creation of bear-meat demand and ecosystem conservation as two layers so demand does not destroy the conservation structure.

**7. Reposition the four.** Return people, bears, mountains, and time to one resonant structure.

**8. Examine multiple indicators together.** Do not decide success from bear-damage counts, bear populations, or distribution volume alone; confirm mutual correspondence.

**9. Check reversal conditions.** If expanded use reverses into over-capture, poaching, or local extinction risk, revise rather than preserve a success story.

## Examples
**1. Local institutions.** See bear damage, hunting-association compensation, municipal operation, and ecosystem management as one connected structure rather than separately.

**2. Bear-meat distribution.** Do not make sales the sole objective. Treat distribution as a design that connects conversion of life to social structure, and audit its two-layer relationship with conservation.

**3. Ecosystem management.** Do not set capture and management conditions only for human convenience; include sustainability conditions on the side of bears, mountains, and time.

**4. Continuity of hunting associations.** Read the gap of requesting capture while leaving aging workers and insufficient compensation unaddressed as a connection failure between local safety and ecosystem management.

**5. Government, residents, and hunters.** Do not concentrate responsibility in one party; clarify the connection conditions for information, judgment, capture, distribution, and conservation.

## How to Read the Audit
A decline in bear-damage counts matters, but it alone does not mean coexistence has been achieved. If bear-population indicators simultaneously enter a danger zone or excessive culling and poaching increase, the decline may have been obtained through another structural destruction.

Conversely, even if bear-population conservation succeeds, coexistence is not established if local safety collapses, hunting associations cannot continue, and the gap between government and residents widens. The original’s audit direction is to make bear-damage counts, hunting-association continuity, bear-meat distribution volume, and bear-population indicators correspond with one another, and to examine alignment among annual/three-year cycles and Time / Structure / Resonance.

Numbers are therefore observation lines for discovering structural reversals, not standalone achievement targets. If demand creation erodes conservation, reduced damage accompanies a population crisis, or expanded distribution induces poaching, the credibility of the original rests on rejecting or revising the hypothesis, including making bear meat common, rather than maintaining it.

## Success Criteria
Bear damage, hunting associations, ecosystems, and institutions are understood as one structural line.

Bear meat operates not as simple commodification, but as a circuit for converting life and restoring correspondence.

Demand creation and ecosystem conservation remain as two layers, without one success reversing into destruction of the other.

Bear-damage counts, hunting-association continuity, bear-meat distribution, and bear populations correspond with one another, and over-capture, poaching, or local extinction risk does not cancel coexistence.

People, bears, mountains, and time are repositioned as a resonant structure without increasing future debt.

## Limits
The original does not advocate unlimited expansion of demand for bear meat. Local auditing explicitly treats demand creation and ecosystem conservation as a two-layer structure and avoids directing society toward excessive resource conversion.

It also contains falsification conditions requiring rejection or revision if excessive culling, poaching, or local extinction risk is observed. Coexistence is not a fixed answer; it includes auditing and renewed agreement.

This does not mean eating bear meat alone automatically resolves every problem of forest conditions, hunting-association institutions, administrative operation, or the approach of living areas. Making bear meat common is presented as a structural line that reconnects these elements, not as a universal measure that makes other elements unnecessary.

## Preventing Misreading
Do not reduce eating bear meat to a regional-promotion or waste-reduction policy.

Do not replace the original with general wildlife management about secondary use after capture.

Conversely, do not turn it into a claim that bear-meat demand should simply be expanded.

Do not treat reduced damage alone as successful coexistence.

Do not treat population conservation alone as successful coexistence.

Do not drop the original structure of people, bears, mountains, and time; correspondence, temporal ethics, and connection responsibility.
