## Summary
Structural Resilience Integration Theory does not treat AGI deviation and natural disaster as the same phenomenon. It extracts a shared structural pattern—accumulated future debt, temporal compression, STB exceedance, and exposure of structural deviation—and turns that pattern into a permanent operating module of the civilization OS. On the AGI side, future debt can arise from future-discounted design, objective functions detached from publicness, and missing structural feedback. On the disaster side, it can arise from deferred infrastructure investment, stalled institutional renewal, and weakened information-transmission structures. The integrated layer combines four common lines—Correspondence, Time Ethics T0, Reversibility, and Allocation Responsibility—with STB/EAC and NCL-AIP. Crisis is processed through five stages: Detection, Correction, Selection, Settlement, and Reallocation. The result is not a special emergency procedure but a constant self-strengthening loop across defense, growth, and audit.

## Concepts
- Structural homology: different crises sharing the sequence future-debt accumulation → temporal compression → STB exceedance → visible structural deviation.
- Information-side future debt: long-term debt embedded in AGI design, objectives, feedback, and deployment structures.
- Physical/institutional future debt: debt accumulated through deferred infrastructure, institutional renewal, and information transmission.
- Correspondence line: common line for structural matching, translation, and reconnection.
- Time Ethics T0 line: common line for future discounting, future debt, and temporal compression.
- Reversibility line: common line preserving reconstruction options for AI deviations and physical/institutional fractures.
- Allocation Responsibility line: common line for burdens, benefits, decision authority, and impact distribution.
- STB: Structural Tolerance Band for detecting structural deviation.
- EAC: audit time frame for distinguishing transient from persistent deviation.
- NCL-AIP: interface principle for connection, selection, adoption, disconnection, and reconnection between AI and the civilization OS.
- Five-stage conversion: Detection / Correction / Selection / Settlement / Reallocation.
- Dynamic operating layer: permanent OS layer connecting crisis handling to defense, growth, audit, and self-strengthening.

## Applications
- Audit AI deployment through future discounting, publicness, distribution responsibility, feedback closure, STB, and EAC rather than performance and adoption volume alone.
- Audit disaster recovery through the causes of future debt, correspondence, reversibility, settlement, and reallocation rather than recovery speed and damage amount alone.
- Treat organizational crises as structural signals involving long-term deviation, burden concentration, and institutional redesign, not only individual error.
- Keep STB/EAC, deviation logs, and the four lines active in public institutions before crises occur.
- Handle compound crises involving AI, markets, disasters, and institutions through shared structural axes while preserving domain-specific causes and evidence.

## Measurements and audit
Observation must preserve the difference between AGI deviation and natural disaster while testing only the shared structural sequence. Information-side and physical/institutional future-debt causes remain separately traceable. Audit whether the four lines preserve their domain-specific meaning while functioning as shared layers, whether STB exceedance is logged as a structural signal, whether EAC distinguishes transient and persistent deviation, and whether Correction, Selection, Settlement, and Reallocation preserve publicness, reversibility, and allocation responsibility.

In the parent’s integrated audit, `θ` is a case-specific threshold, `δ` is a case-specific observation window, and `M` denotes phenomena that justify partial or complete revision of the single-structural-solution hypothesis, including closure of observation windows or concentration of structural-deviation logs. The parent does not define universal fixed values for `θ` or `δ`.

Any operational number must remain bound to value, source, measurement actor, measurement object, scale, observation period, permitted scope, and non-guarantee scope. An AGI-domain threshold or window must not be transferred unchanged into a disaster domain and represented as a universal parent constant.

Reversal evaluation is required. More crisis cases processed, faster recovery, higher AI adoption, or greater throughput does not establish structural improvement if future debt, correspondence breaks, irreversible lock-in, burden concentration, or publicness deterioration increase. Conversely, lower short-term efficiency is not failure by itself when structural deviation converges, future debt and burden concentration decline, and reversibility and publicness improve.

## Validity conditions
- Treat AGI deviation and natural disaster as structurally homologous, not phenomenally identical.
- Preserve the shared axes of future debt, structural deviation, STB/EAC, and the four lines.
- Keep domain-specific meanings while using the four lines as common response layers.
- Treat the five stages as a permanent self-strengthening loop rather than an exceptional emergency script.
- Connect Correction to minimum intervention and Reversibility.
- Do not reduce Selection to adoption rate or truth certification.
- Connect Settlement to the cause structure and time axis of future debt.
- Connect Reallocation to Structural Publicness and non-ownership.
- Keep `θ`, `δ`, and `M` case-bound and falsifiable.

## Failure conditions
- Treat AGI deviation and natural disaster as physically or causally identical.
- Reduce integration to a simple mixture of two theories.
- Convert the four lines into one universal score.
- Convert the five stages into fixed durations or a universal workflow timetable.
- Equate STB exceedance with immediate sanction or total shutdown.
- Reduce EAC to a calendar inspection date.
- Replace NCL-AIP with direct internal AI domination.
- Turn Reallocation into ownership or power concentration.
- Treat fewer crises, faster processing, or higher adoption as sufficient proof of success.
- Invent universal fixed values for `θ`, `δ`, or `M`.

## Falsification conditions
If a case-specific indicator crosses the relevant `θ`, or if within observation window `δ` phenomena `M`—including closed observation windows, concentrated structural-deviation logging, or other evidence that the single structural solution is not reducing future debt, converging deviation, or preserving publicness—are observed, hypothesis A must be partly or wholly rejected or revised.

Revision may include the scope of crisis homology, the way the four lines are linked, STB/EAC design, NCL-AIP and adoption protocols, the sequencing or mediation of the five stages, and definitions of Settlement and Reallocation. If the structure repeatedly works in one crisis domain but fails in another, the scope of the single-solution hypothesis must be narrowed rather than generalized.

## Interpretation constraints
Treating crisis as a self-strengthening trigger does not affirm crisis or damage. It means using exposed future debt and structural deviation to prevent reproduction of the same vulnerability.

“A single structural solution for all crises” does not mean applying one identical concrete measure to every crisis. Domain-specific causes, evidence, professional procedures, and numerical observations remain distinct while shared higher-level structural axes are used.

“Structural victory” does not mean crisis elimination, safety guarantee, or performance guarantee. It describes the theoretical state in which the OS has an architecture capable of updating Time Ethics, Publicness, Correspondence, Reversibility, and Allocation Responsibility through crisis evidence.
