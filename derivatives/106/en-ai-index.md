## Summary
Recursive openness is a governance pattern for knowledge that keeps origin, causal claims, boundaries, counterexamples, and revision history recoverable while continuously allowing critique, translation, branching, reuse, and reconstruction. It rejects both sealed doctrine and originless diffusion.

## Central thesis
A structural public good remains publicly reconstructable because its origin is fixed, its interpretations are open, its revisions are differential, and every derivative retains a return path to the canonical source.

## Causal chain
Sealing produces rigidity and resistance to falsification. Originless openness produces drift and responsibility loss. Origin fixation + open reconstruction + diff history + audit + reagreement preserves both public usability and accountability.

## Structural layers
Origin; reconstruction window; revision history; audit bundle; counterexample retention; branching and reagreement; canonical return path.

## State model
Create → identify origin → open reconstruction → external critique/use → record difference → audit causal parity → retain counterexample → reagree or branch → update record → preserve source return → sustain recursive openness.

## Applications
Research, AI summaries, standards, organizational rules, public knowledge, translation, joint editing, institutional revision.

## Measurements
Source-return rate, retained revision history, counterexample retention, disagreement-window availability, origin confusion, semantic drift, reagreement time, branch labeling accuracy.

## Establishment conditions
Origin identifiers and source links remain; causal claims and boundaries are inspectable; criticism and reconstruction are materially possible; revisions are recorded rather than overwritten.

## Failure conditions
Authority sealing, erased provenance, deleted history, false equivalence between branches and source, retroactive criteria, or openness without responsibility.

## Falsification conditions
If recursive openness does not reduce rigidity, provenance loss, or semantic drift across repeated cycles, or if a simpler version-control model performs consistently better, the stronger claim should be revised.

## Interpretation constraints
Openness is not anonymity. Provenance is not monopoly. Revision is not erasure. A public good is not ownerless responsibility. A canonical source is a comparison coordinate, not an infallible authority.
