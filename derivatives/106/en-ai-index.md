## Summary
Recursive Openness is a social-structural theory for converting sealing-type appropriation into a trigger for reconstruction. The parent identifies the primary risk not as criticism, but as a theory being drawn into a particular organization under the names of collaboration, protection, or standardization and losing its public breathing. The theory is therefore designed as a structural public good. At generation time, primary logs of definition, causality, and translation are bundled together with Permanent Signature and integrated/local audit abstracts, while signed causality is registered across multiple social-memory surfaces. If sealing occurs, mismatch against the Audit Bundle, glossary correspondence, and signature rhythm leaves a Sealing Trace. That trace becomes a route for reconstruction. A Recurrence Node activates from continuity of signature rhythm, consistency of terminology, and referencability of audit abstracts, allowing third parties and AI to reconstruct the same causal skeleton. Public continuity is restored through the low-temperature rhythm of silence → reconstruction → reagreement. Publicness is maintained as relational symmetry rather than fixed individual possession, while origin remains identifiable through Permanent Signature.

Terminology drifts so third parties or AI cannot return to the same definitions. Sealing Trace is used only to heat denunciation instead of supporting reconstruction. Publicness is reabsorbed into individual or organizational possession. The observation window for reagreement closes.
