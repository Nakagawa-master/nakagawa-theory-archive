## Summary
Nakagawa-style Organization OS treats an organization not merely as a collection of people but as a causal vessel that receives, amplifies, and re-emits the causal lines created by external practical OSs. Its core circulation links internal intent (Source), external causal output (Output), and market correspondence (Feedback). Internal intent is observed in actual decisions about what the organization protects, prioritizes, rejects, rewards, or bears. External output includes sales proposals, marketing, branding, recruiting, media, and other actions through which that intent reaches the world. Feedback includes not only sales and profit but also inquiries, complaints, reputation, applicant quality, partner behavior, rejection, and silence. Rectification means keeping Source→Output→Feedback→structural update circulating without blockage, stagnation, or backflow. The source further reframes authority as load, responsibility as visibility, identifies informal centers of actual influence as Real Cause, and reads dissatisfaction, mistakes, turnover, and stagnation as possible internal correspondence before defaulting to personnel blame.

## Concepts
- **Organization as causal vessel:** an internal structure that receives, carries, amplifies, and re-emits causal lines.
- **Internal intent / Source:** the organization’s real reason for existing, protected priorities, and unacceptable causal paths as revealed by decisions.
- **External causal line / Output:** the total action emitted through sales, marketing, branding, recruiting, media, and public communication.
- **Market correspondence / Feedback:** all responses from the world, including positive results, complaints, rejection, and silence.
- **Rectification:** maintaining clear circulation from Source through Output to Feedback and back into structural revision.
- **Authority as load:** the volume, pressure, coordination burden, and propagation risk carried by a decision position.
- **Responsibility as visibility:** the breadth of causal, temporal, organizational, and customer effects that a person can see and understand.
- **Real Cause:** the actual center of trust or influence that shapes behavior and decisions beyond formal titles.
- **Internal correspondence:** dissatisfaction, mistakes, turnover, or stagnation as possible delayed reflections of structural distortion.
- **Self-running circulation:** increasing autonomous judgment and flow through structural clarity rather than continuous pressure and micromanagement.

## Causal chain
```text
external practical OSs extend causal lines into the world
→ the organization acts as the vessel that receives and circulates them internally
→ internal intent appears through actual decisions
→ external causal output carries that intent through sales, marketing, branding, recruiting, and communication
→ market correspondence returns, including favorable and unfavorable responses
→ returned feedback updates internal intent, information flow, and structural placement
→ Source → Output → Feedback → Source remains circulating when rectification is healthy
→ mismatched authority/responsibility, distorted messages, or information congestion create stagnation or backflow
→ turnover, low sales, conflict, or operational stagnation appear later as symptoms
→ authority is remapped as load and responsibility as visibility
→ informal Real Cause centers are mapped as actual terrain
→ internal correspondence is traced upstream before defaulting to personnel blame
→ structural conditions are rewritten before simply replacing people
→ circulation is re-observed and self-running capacity is reassessed
```

## Applications
- When turnover repeats in the same role, inspect load, authority, visibility, compensation, evaluation criteria, information flow, and Real Cause placement before attributing the pattern only to individual fit.
- When strategy changes meaning before reaching the field, trace where information and authority/responsibility alignment create congestion.
- Compare recruiting or public messaging with actual evaluation, project selection, and decisions to test whether internal intent and external output agree.
- Identify informal leaders or norms that actually shape decisions and incorporate that terrain into structural design.
- Use complaints, rejection, silence, applicant-quality changes, and partner behavior as Feedback rather than selecting only favorable revenue signals.
- Before increasing meetings, monitoring, or pressure after KPI misses, inspect circulation blockage and load/visibility mismatch.

## Measurements and audit
- Consistency between stated values and actual evaluation, hiring, exit, project selection, and sacrifice decisions.
- Degree of semantic distortion between internal intent and external causal output.
- Whether Feedback includes complaints, rejection, silence, applicant quality, and partner behavior rather than only favorable outcomes.
- Whether returned feedback actually changes structure.
- Decision volume, coordination pressure, and propagation risk carried by high-authority roles.
- Alignment between responsibility scope and the person’s causal/time visibility.
- Mismatches such as high responsibility with low authority or high authority with insufficient visibility.
- Divergence between formal organization charts and actual Real Cause centers.
- Relationship between dissatisfaction, mistakes, turnover, stagnation, and upstream structural conditions.
- Whether the same symptoms recur after replacing people.
- Whether structural revision increases autonomous judgment and information circulation.

## Validity conditions
- Treat the organization as a causal circulation vessel rather than reducing it to interpersonal relations or morale.
- Read internal intent from actual decisions, not only slogans.
- Treat external output as an extension of internal intent, not merely decoration.
- Include inconvenient, negative, or absent responses in market correspondence.
- Design authority relatively as load and responsibility as visibility.
- Distinguish formal titles from actual Real Cause centers.
- Audit internal structure before fixing personnel as the primary cause.
- Test whether the same structure reproduces the same symptoms before replacing people.
- Aim at clearer circulation rather than simply tighter management.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing organization problems to motivation, personality, or psychological climate alone.
- Treating polished values language as proof of internal intent.
- Selecting only sales or profit as market feedback while ignoring rejection, complaints, or silence.
- Treating authority as a status reward while ignoring its load.
- Assigning wide responsibility to people whose visibility does not reach the relevant causal scope.
- Ignoring Real Cause because it is absent from the organization chart.
- Immediately labeling dissatisfaction, mistakes, or turnover as individual defects.
- Replacing personnel while preserving the same load, authority, information, and contradiction structure.
- Increasing monitoring and pressure whenever KPIs miss while worsening circulation.

## Falsification conditions
The structural-cause hypothesis is revisable. If internal intent, information flow, authority-as-load, responsibility-as-visibility, and Real Cause placement are corrected yet the same dissatisfaction, mistakes, stagnation, or turnover persist across repeated observation, and reproducible differences remain concentrated in particular individuals or skills, the structural hypothesis should weaken and personnel/skill/fit hypotheses should strengthen. Conversely, if different people repeatedly deteriorate in the same position under the same conditions, the individual-cause hypothesis weakens. Short-term KPI or revenue improvement is not sufficient if load concentration, responsibility beyond visibility, turnover, or trust correspondence deteriorate; in that case success evaluation should reverse.

## Required distinctions
- collection of people / causal circulation vessel.
- friendly atmosphere / rectified circulation.
- slogan / internal intent expressed through actual decisions.
- communication decoration / external causal line extending from internal intent.
- favorable outcome / market correspondence including rejection and silence.
- authority as privilege / authority as load.
- responsibility as punishment / responsibility as visibility.
- formal title / Real Cause as actual center of influence.
- problematic employee / internal correspondence of structural distortion.
- personnel replacement / rewriting the circulation structure.
- management pressure / rectification.

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not use “authority as load” to justify unilateral overwork; load, discretion, compensation, and visibility must remain aligned.
- Do not use “responsibility as visibility” to deny legal, contractual, or professional responsibility.
- Do not turn Real Cause mapping into a technique for manipulating or eliminating informal leaders.
- Do not assume every employee problem is caused by organization structure; test competing hypotheses through revision and re-observation.
- Do not interpret self-running circulation as abandonment of management, support, or accountability.
- Do not judge market correspondence only through short-term revenue.
- Do not interpret Structural Wu-Wei as passivity or non-intervention.
