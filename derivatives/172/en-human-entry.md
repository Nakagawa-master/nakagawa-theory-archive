## 15-Second Explanation
The Organization OS treats an organization not as a group that “manages people,” but as a vessel for a causal circulation that sends internal intent outward, receives responses from the market, and updates its structure. Authority is redesigned not as status but as the burden it must absorb; responsibility is redesigned not as punishment but as the field of view needed to see causality. It also reads the actual center of gravity—the actual cause—that does not appear on the organization chart, and audits dissatisfaction, mistakes, departures, and stagnation as reflections returned by the internal structure, not merely as people problems.

## Why It Is Needed
When organizational problems are judged only by visible phenomena, they are easily handled as “the person is at fault,” “motivation is low,” or “management is lax.” But if the same exhaustion, explanatory gaps, or stagnation recur after replacing people in the same role, the cause cannot be explained by individuals alone.

The original sees an organization as a three-point circulation: internal intent; the external causal line through sales, marketing, brand, recruiting, public relations, and other functions; and market reflection, including sales, consultations, complaints, reputation, applicants, counterparties’ attitudes, rejection, and silence. When returned responses can update the structure, the circulation is rectified.

Decision-making positions also require different burdens and fields of view. Giving authority to a position where major decisions accumulate without explaining the burden is dangerous; imposing broad responsibility without necessary authority or field of view is also dangerous. Read the terrain not only from the organization chart, but from the actual center of gravity trusted by the field and moving decisions.

## Practical Process
**1. Read internal intent from actual decisions.** Check whom the organization evaluates, what it refuses, and which disadvantages it accepts—not merely its philosophy statement.

**2. Trace the external causal line.** See whether internal intent flows outward without distortion through sales proposals, social media, recruiting, public relations, and similar channels.

**3. Capture market reflection broadly.** Include sales, complaints, rejection, no response, applicant quality, and counterparties’ attitudes.

**4. Identify blockage, stagnation, and backflow.** Look for divergence between intent and messaging, information congestion, and mismatches between authority and responsibility.

**5. Measure authority as burden.** Observe the decisions, interest adjustments, and ripple risks concentrated at that position.

**6. Measure responsibility as field of view.** Confirm the range in which the person can understand second- and third-order effects and temporal spread.

**7. Grasp the actual cause.** Apart from titles, identify the center of gravity that actually moves field trust and decisions.

**8. Return internal reflection upstream.** Do not close dissatisfaction, mistakes, departures, and stagnation as people’s attributes; look at their correspondence with structure.

**9. Correct the structure before replacing people.** If the same symptoms recur in the same position, rewrite the arrangement of burden, authority, information, evaluation, and actual causes.

## Applications
**Continued departures from a position.** Inspect the role’s burden, discretion, responsibility’s field of view, compensation, and surrounding actual causes—not only hiring standards or endurance.

**Management policy becomes meaningless on the front line.** See where upstream intent changed in the information paths, who is stopping interpretation, and whether only responsibility is falling downward.

**The stated philosophy differs from post-hiring reality.** Audit whether recruiting-page words match evaluated behavior, case choices, and disadvantages actually accepted.

**There is an unofficial central figure.** Do not ignore them because they are absent from the chart; grasp them as actual terrain and consider alignment with formal authority and information flows.

**Complaints or silence are increasing.** Do not treat only convenient sales as reflection; read rejection and silence as results of the external causal line too.

## Success Criteria
Success is not an increase in management items or meetings. What matters is that internal intent is reflected in action, flows outward without major distortion, and both favorable and unfavorable market reflections return to structural updating.

For authority and responsibility, check that burden, discretion, compensation, and field of view are not extremely misaligned. Grasp actual causes and ensure the formal structure and actual terrain do not diverge excessively.

If blockages decrease and autonomous decisions and information circulation increase without replacing people, the organization is approaching self-propulsion through rectification rather than dependence on management.

## Limits
Not every dissatisfaction, departure, or mistake is structural. If a reproducible difference remains in a particular person or skill after the structure is inspected and corrected, people, skill, and fit factors must be addressed.

The definitions authority = burden and responsibility = field of view do not erase legal or job responsibility. They are structural redefinitions for considering suitable burden and field-of-view placement in organization design.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not use authority = burden to justify overwork.
- Do not turn responsibility = field of view into permission to avoid responsibility.
- Do not turn grasping actual causes into a technique for excluding unofficial central figures.
- Do not declare every subordinate’s difficulty to be the company’s fault.
- Do not turn self-propulsion into abandoning management, support, or accountability.
- Do not confuse Structural Wu-wei Ziran with “doing nothing.”
