## Summary
The parent article identifies a civilizational structural mistranslation: voluntary family affection has been converted directly into continuing obligations of caregiving, childcare, and housework. When “love,” “devotion,” and “kindness” are narrated while the actual structure consists of unpaid labor, time erosion, employment exit, income loss, poverty, and end-of-life burden, Correspondence fails. Temporal Ethics also fails when continuing care time is consumed as immediate service without a mechanism that returns that contribution through future compensation, social credit, security, or the possibility of social re-entry.

The proposed transition is not rejection of love or abolition of family. To protect affection, the parent separates it from institutional responsibility and retranslates care as **social right / labor**. Public benefits, evaluation, leave rights, and related institutional forms then carry the burden that private affection had been forced to absorb. This is described as a move toward **institutional love** and a recursively corresponding society, restoring alignment among affection, obligation, time, and system.

## Applications
1. Audit family-care structures by testing whether the language of “family duty” hides unpaid labor, time loss, income loss, or future opportunity costs.
2. Audit care time through Temporal Ethics: continuing care contribution should have a future return line rather than disappear as immediate service.
3. Translate care from voluntary emotion into social right / labor and connect it to public benefits, evaluation, and leave rights.
4. Protect family relationships by removing coercive institutional duty from affection and returning love to a voluntary relation.

## Measurements and audit
The parent defines A as translating affection into institutional language and redefining care from voluntary feeling into social right / labor. B is the institutionalization, stabilization, and alignment of that translation through public benefits, evaluation, and leave rights under Correspondence and Temporal Ethics.

Periodic audit points include intra-family burden, employment exit rates, and care-recipient QOL. T/S/R are used as T = degree of Correspondence, S = institutional sustainability, and R = recursion, including the caregiver’s possibility of social re-entry. Grounds, allocation, and revision processes remain visible through the public audit bundle.

The parent also gives reversal conditions. If unpaid-labor concentration or mental withdrawal exceeds threshold θ, or observation window δ closes so affected voices are no longer visible, hypothesis A becomes subject to revision or rejection. These numbers are not detached generic KPIs; they evaluate whether the parent’s A→B transition, Correspondence, and Temporal Ethics are actually functioning.
