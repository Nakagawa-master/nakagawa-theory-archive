## 1. Summary
Connection Governance Design Theory addresses how systems that measure, evaluate, and allocate connection value can become popularity contests, insider scoring, capture, surveillance, or concentrated authority. It treats public purpose, auditable procedure, non-concentrated interests, and reversibility as legitimacy conditions. Measurement, judgment, allocation, and objections are separated; composite indicators, primary evidence, independent audit, public/protected information boundaries, objection, exit, correction, reconnection, and rollback make institutional self-correction possible.

## 2. Concepts
- Public purpose / prohibited use: the declared purpose of the institution and uses that are not allowed.
- Legitimacy: public purpose, auditable procedure, non-concentrated interests, and reversibility.
- What / Who / How / When: separation of audit object, auditor, method, and review timing.
- CDI / MAI / RS / CRI / KQI: a bundle of indicators for different structural aspects of connection governance.
- Moving targets: keeping evaluation logic revisable to reduce gaming of a permanently fixed scoring rule.
- Reverse incentives: reducing rewards for capture, voice dominance, or superficial connection inflation.
- Disturbance tests: testing the system with disagreement, contradiction, missing data, or exit requests.
- Public / protected separation: combining verifiability with necessary information protection.
- Reversibility: operational objection, correction, exit, reconnection, arbitration, and rollback.

## 3. Causal chain
```text
connection value becomes measurable and allocable
→ benefits and authority gather around indicators and allocation
→ participants optimize scoring rather than cooperation
→ public purpose diverges from actual institutional behavior
→ legitimacy conditions and authority separation are introduced
→ composite indicators, primary evidence, and multiple auditors detect distortion
→ public and protected information are separated
→ objection, correction, exit, reconnection, and rollback are made operational
→ change history returns the institution toward its public purpose
```

```yaml
  authority_separation:
    measurement: separated

  qualitative_evidence: available

  reversible_remedies: available
  change_history: traceable
  states:
    - PROPOSED
    - PILOT
    - ACTIVE
    - REVIEW
    - CORRECTED
    - ROLLED_BACK
```
The state set distinguishes institutional operating and correction states; it is not a maturity score for people or organizations.

## 5. Applications
- Connection-value accounting: separate evaluation, allocation, and audit, and compare rising scores with exhaustion or capture.
- Research and civic collaboration: observe renegotiation, dissent handling, continuity, central-person dependency, exit, and re-entry rather than attendance alone.
- AI-assisted institutions: use AI for anomaly detection, comparison, and summarization while retaining human responsibility and review.
- Communities: do not equate voice, posts, or referrals with trust; audit retaliation against objectors and leavers.

Reversible evaluation means higher scores or participation do not count as success when exhaustion, silence, exclusion, capture, authority concentration, or exit cost worsen. Fewer objections or exits are not improvement when the relevant channels have simply become harder to use.

## 7. Validity conditions
- Public purpose, prohibited uses, and affected parties are explicit.
- Measurement, judgment, allocation, and objection handling are separated.
- Composite indicators and qualitative primary evidence are used together.
- Independent audit works in practice.
- Objection, correction, exit, deletion, reconnection, arbitration, and rollback are operational.
- Public and protected information boundaries are explainable.
- Human responsibility and remedy remain present when AI assistance is used.

## 8. Failure conditions
- Connection count, popularity, or voice is used as a single allocation score.
- One actor controls purpose, measurement, judgment, allocation, and objections.
- Secrecy is used to avoid explaining decisions, audits, or change history.
- Audit produces reports but no institutional correction.
- Objectors or leavers are penalized.
- Personal or defensive information is overexposed in the name of transparency.
- AI output substitutes for accountable human judgment.

## 9. Falsification conditions
- Continued audit does not reduce authority concentration, capture, exhaustion, silence, or exclusion.
- Exit, correction, arbitration, and rollback remain symbolic rather than usable.
- Composite indicators still produce optimization toward one narrow behavior.
- The same failures recur without traceable institutional change.
- Public/protected separation produces secret rule or information leakage.
- After the 30/90/180-day staged checks, legitimacy, reversibility, and self-correction still do not function materially.

## 10. Required distinctions
- governance / stronger management
- audit / continuous surveillance
- transparency / total disclosure
- protected weights and thresholds / unaccountable secret rule
- strong connection / inability to exit
- fewer objections / institutional health
- AI assistance / AI government
- legitimacy / majority approval alone
- indicator bundle / one composite score
- institutional stability / refusal to change

## 11. Interpretation constraints
Do not convert this theory into social-credit scoring, centralized ranking, permanent surveillance, secret rule, or total-disclosure ideology. Do not merge CDI/MAI/RS/CRI/KQI into a personality score. Do not convert 30/90/180 days into universal guaranteed deadlines. Low objection or exit counts must not be treated as health without checking accessibility and retaliation.
