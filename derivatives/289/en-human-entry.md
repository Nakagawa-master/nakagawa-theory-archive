## 15-Second Explanation
Part Five treats a proposal not as “the final persuasion” but as an editing process that orders the customer’s current state, problem, future, and decision conditions so they can re-explain them internally. The basic order is What → Why now / Why us → How much.

## Why It Is Needed
When a proposal becomes a collection of product information, the customer must reconstruct what connects to their problem. Even if the contact understands it, a decision stops if they cannot explain the reason to managers, procurement, or the field.

Keep the customer’s own words, state first what the proposal satisfies, explain why now and why this route under conditions, and present price without severing it from value. Translate it into purpose fit, workload, and recoverability so it can be re-explained inside the customer’s organization.

## Practical Process
1. Organize the current state, problem, and future in the customer’s vocabulary.
2. **What**: show what the proposal satisfies.
3. Place continuity from the present to value realization.
4. **Why now**: show the rational reason to address it now.
5. **Why us**: show the fit after comparing alternatives.
6. State implementation conditions, risks, and non-fit.
7. **How much**: make price and contract conditions transparent.
8. Translate it for approval into purpose fit, workload, and recoverability.
9. Confirm that the customer can re-explain it internally.

## Examples of Application
In proposal materials, do not list product functions from the first page; state purpose and current state in the customer’s words. In price comparisons, put implementation workload and risks on the same comparison surface.

For approval cases, make materials the contact can explain without the salesperson. If there is non-fit, state it openly, including conditional fit or stopping the proposal.

## Success Criteria
- The customer’s vocabulary remains.
- What is clear as value.
- Why now is not fear-based.
- Why us is not denial of competitors.
- Alternatives and non-fit are visible.
- Price remains connected to value and conditions.
- The contact can re-explain it internally.
- Assumptions can be tested after contracting.

## Limitations
Price should not be hidden until the end in every proposal. If the customer needs price information early, show it transparently. More information does not necessarily improve proposal quality.

## Misreading Prevention
The order of understanding is not psychological manipulation. Translating for approval is not a tactic for conquering the customer’s organization. The purpose is to create a state in which the customer can re-explain their own reasons and conditions.
