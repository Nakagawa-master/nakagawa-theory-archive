## Summary
Part Five treats a proposal as a structural crystallization of understanding rather than the final moment of persuasion. By the time a proposal is created, initial contact and dialogue should already have produced customer-confirmed information about present state, problem, causal constraints, desired future, preferences, and decision conditions. The proposal preserves that language and places it into an order that the customer can understand and, in organizational settings, re-explain internally.

The Parent organizes the proposal around the sequence `What → Why now / Why us → How much`. “What” means what condition or future the proposal is intended to fulfill, not merely a feature list. “Why now” explains the decision-relevant timing without manufacturing fear or artificial urgency. “Why us” explains fit between customer conditions and the proposed path rather than relying on competitor denigration. “How much” presents price and contract conditions in the same causal context as value, implementation burden, and alternatives.

For B2B decisions, the proposal must survive the absence of the salesperson. Internal approval translation converts the structure into purpose fit, operational workload, recoverability or return, risk, responsibility, and implementation conditions. The quality test is whether the customer's organization can reproduce the reason for the proposal, including its limitations and alternatives.

## Concepts
- **Proposal as structure**: a reproducible representation of customer-confirmed value, causality, constraints, and decision conditions.
- **Customer language**: the customer's own descriptions of present state, problem, and future, retained rather than overwritten by sales language.
- **What**: the value or unmet condition the proposal is intended to address.
- **Continuity**: the path from present state through implementation and transition to realized value.
- **Why now**: a grounded reason that the issue is decision-relevant now, not artificial urgency.
- **Why us**: the fit between customer requirements and the proposed capability or path.
- **How much**: price, term, and contractual conditions presented transparently in context.
- **Alternatives**: relevant other paths, including delay or no action, kept visible enough for meaningful comparison.
- **Mismatch**: known conditions the proposal does not satisfy.
- **Internal approval translation**: converting the proposal into purpose fit, workload, recoverability, risk, and other terms needed for organizational decision-making.
- **Re-explainability**: the ability of the customer to reproduce the proposal's rationale without relying on salesperson performance.

## Causal chain
```text
customer language and decision structure confirmed in prior dialogue
→ map present state, problem, causal friction, and desired future
→ present What: the value to be fulfilled
→ show continuity from present state to value realization
→ explain Why now using verified conditions
→ explain Why us through fit and alternatives
→ disclose implementation requirements, risk, and mismatch
→ present How much with value and conditions visible
→ translate into purpose fit, workload, recoverability, and internal risk
→ customer can re-explain the rationale inside the organization
→ objections and comparison remain possible
→ decision emerges from structured understanding
→ proposal assumptions remain auditable after implementation
```

The sequence is not a rigid theatrical script. If price is a gating condition, hiding it until late can damage trust. The structural requirement is that price, value, and conditions remain connected enough for the customer to make an informed comparison.

## Applications
1. **Proposal decks.** Lead with the customer's confirmed situation and the value to be created, then show the causal path, timing, fit, implementation conditions, and price. Slide count is secondary to reproducibility.
2. **Internal approval.** Provide language the champion can use with management, procurement, finance, and operations. Separate purpose fit, workload, return assumptions, and risk rather than forcing one benefit statement to serve every stakeholder.
3. **Price comparison.** Compare total implementation conditions, not only sticker price. Include migration effort, operational burden, support scope, and known gaps.
4. **Mismatch.** State what is out of scope or conditional. Hiding mismatch to secure signature converts a proposal into a future trust defect.
5. **AI proposal generation.** Use conversation data to draft, but check for invented customer problems, altered customer language, fabricated urgency, unsupported differentiation, and omitted objections.
6. **Multi-stakeholder decisions.** Preserve differences in criteria between users, approvers, finance, and security rather than letting one contact's preference stand in for the organization.

## Measurements and audit
Audit whether customer language survives into the proposal. Check that the causal path from present state to desired future has been confirmed and includes implementation steps. Review What to ensure it is a value statement rather than a product catalogue. Review Why now for artificial deadlines or exaggerated harm. Review Why us for evidence of actual fit rather than generic superiority claims.

Check whether alternatives, limitations, and mismatch are visible. Inspect whether price is understandable in relation to value, workload, term, and scope. Measure cognitive load: more slides and more data are not automatically higher quality. Test internal re-explanation by asking whether a customer stakeholder can state purpose fit, workload, recoverability, major risk, and why the option is being considered.

After implementation, return to proposal assumptions. Compare promised value, expected workload, timeline, and return conditions with actual experience. A proposal that cannot be audited after contract is structurally weak even if it closed successfully.

## Validity conditions
A valid proposal preserves customer language and confirmed causality, presents value and conditions in a decision-relevant order, keeps alternatives and mismatch visible, makes price transparent in context, and supports internal re-explanation. Why now must be grounded rather than fear-based; Why us must be fit-based rather than competitor-denial. Internal approval support must help the customer explain evidence rather than manipulate organizational procedure.

The sequence can adapt to context. Early price disclosure is valid when it is necessary for efficient qualification. The standard is informed comparison, not adherence to a ritual order.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes overwriting customer language, feature-list proposals detached from need, artificial urgency, unsupported uniqueness claims, competitor denigration, hidden alternatives, hidden mismatch, using price timing as psychological leverage, overwhelming the customer with information, creating proposals that only the salesperson can explain, and using AI to invent facts or suppress objections.

A proposal also fails when it optimizes approval while hiding implementation burden from the teams that will bear it. Internal translation must include affected operational perspectives, not only executive value language.

## Falsification conditions
If the What/Why/How ordering and internal translation do not improve comprehension, re-explanation, implementation fit, or post-contract value—and instead increase delay or information burden—the sequence and granularity should be revised. If early price disclosure consistently improves decisions without reducing value understanding, it should be used.

If proposal automation increases conversion while also increasing mismatch, implementation surprise, or churn, the automation is optimizing the wrong outcome and must be revised.

## Required distinctions
- proposal ≠ persuasion
- What ≠ feature list
- Why now ≠ fear or deadline pressure
- Why us ≠ competitor denial
- information order ≠ psychological manipulation
- internal approval support ≠ organizational exploitation
- contextual price presentation ≠ price concealment
- re-explainability ≠ memorizing a sales script
- decision ≠ persuasion trophy

## Interpretation constraints
Do not treat the sequence as a universal script. Do not deliberately withhold price when the customer needs it to decide whether deeper evaluation is worthwhile. Do not convert internal approval translation into tactics for bypassing dissenting stakeholders or governance. AI-generated proposal statements remain drafts until linked to verified customer evidence.

The aim is to reduce the customer's work of reconstructing the causal case, not to reduce their ability to disagree. A proposal should make refusal and comparison more informed as well as make acceptance more informed.
