## Summary
The Parent defines a public minimum core for connection-value accounting. Its purpose is not to convert relationships, collaboration, or human worth into a price or a single score. It creates a measurement language that keeps multiple dimensions visible: non-monetization, metric bundles, reversibility, distributed observation, and revisable targets.

The public metric bundle includes CDI, MAI, RS, CRI, and KQI. Structural records include MemoryObject, ConsentToken, ReversibilityFlag, and ObservationNote. Audit summaries separate purpose, target, method, result, changes, limitations, and reproducibility clues. Public API examples such as `/v0/summary`, `/v0/observations`, and `/v0/memory` are bounded so PII and internal weights do not become public by default.

## Concepts
- **Non-monetization**: do not collapse connection value directly into money.
- **Metric bundle**: read several metrics together instead of producing a final ranking score.
- **Reversibility priority**: correction and rollback matter more than maximizing a score.
- **Distributed observation**: no single observer should monopolize the definition of value.
- **Moving target**: metrics remain revisable when they become gaming targets.
- **CDI**: Connection Density Index.
- **MAI**: Mutual Agreement Interval.
- **RS**: Reversibility Score.
- **CRI**: Consistency of Review & Inspection.
- **KQI**: Qualitative Impact Quotient.
- **MemoryObject**: a traceable unit of connection memory.
- **ConsentToken**: a structured record of consent conditions.
- **ReversibilityFlag**: the current rollback/correction status.
- **ObservationNote**: a record of observer, method, and context.

## Causal chain

## Applications
The standard can support co-creation projects, human-AI collaboration, inter-organizational programs, communities, audit interfaces, and institutional evaluation. It prevents meeting counts, usage counts, revenue, or popularity from standing in for the entire value of a connection.

## Audit points
Audit whether a composite score is becoming a ranking; whether metrics are directly tied to money, status, or rights; whether consent can be withdrawn; whether observers are concentrated; whether methods and limitations are recorded; whether public APIs leak PII or internal weights; and whether metric optimization is diverging from real-world value.

## Preconditions
Start from the evaluation purpose, choose the smallest useful metric bundle, record consent and reversibility, distribute observation, document the audit method and limitations, maintain a public/private information boundary, and keep metric definitions revisable.

## Failure modes
Failure includes treating CDI as human worth, turning the bundle into a universal score, using ConsentToken as permanent consent, turning MemoryObject into surveillance, hiding irreversibility, monopolizing observation, exposing sensitive/internal scoring details, and leaving gamed metrics permanently fixed.

## Falsification / update conditions
If the bundle does not explain meaningful changes, RS diverges from actual rollback capability, MAI does not track meaningful agreement, or distributed observation reduces rather than improves auditability, definitions must change. If public API exposure increases privacy risk or gaming without improving accountability, the exposure boundary should contract.

## Required distinctions
- accounting ≠ monetization
- bundle ≠ ranking
- connection value ≠ human worth
- consent token ≠ permanent consent
- observation ≠ surveillance
- transparency ≠ total disclosure
- moving target ≠ arbitrary rule changes

## Misreading constraints
Do not use this standard as a social credit system, popularity score, or automatic compensation engine. Measurement exists to preserve inspectability and correction, not to let one authority own and price all relational value. Privacy and anti-gaming boundaries remain part of accountability.
