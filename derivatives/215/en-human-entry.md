## 15-Second Explanation
The “profit” in this original is not proof of prosperity or success. It is prolongation cost distributed to keep people in a broken institution, an adhesive that makes them calculate, “The institution may be bad, but I too will be in trouble if it breaks now.”

If fear creates “it is dangerous to leave,” profit creates “it is still better to remain.” By working in series, the two can keep a civilization whose consensus has weakened operating quietly for a long time.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
An order based only on fear can make people difficult to escape, but it accumulates exhaustion and resistance. Long-term operation requires not only making people stay, but also making remaining feel reasonably beneficial.

Institutions therefore distribute benefits, stability, protection, recognition, priority, and established positions. Even when people know an institution’s defects, they may think that if it collapses their work, status, transactions, and lives will also be shaken, making it difficult to turn criticism into action.

The important point is not that dissatisfaction disappears. Dissatisfaction and dependence coexist, and silence and retention continue. Because this outward form looks like support or stability, do not infer consensus from institutional use or cooperation alone.

## Practical Process
1. **Separate types of profit.** See whether it is legitimate compensation for value creation or prolongation compensation for keeping an institution from breaking.
2. **Separate support from retention.** Confirm whether people trust the institution or remain because leaving would cause loss.
3. **Identify the benefit structure.** Map benefits, protection, stability, recognition, priority, and established positions.
4. **Examine whether criticism becomes action.** See whether dissatisfaction is voiced but action stops in anticipation of harm to self-interest.
5. **Examine denial and dependence together.** Confirm whether “it should be changed” and “it would be a problem if it broke” coexist.
6. **Examine the cause of silence.** Separate agreement from self-restraint caused by dependence on benefits.
7. **Question the imitation of stability.** Do not regard the outward form of an institution being maintained and reproduced as support.
8. **Observe when profit dries up.** See whether fear, resistance, and withdrawal reappear when distribution shrinks.

## Examples of Application
### Organizations
Even when employees are dissatisfied with management policy, if they do not push objections through because they may lose compensation, promotion, benefits, titles, or access to important projects, do not equate retention with support.

### Institutions
Even when users understand an institution’s defects, if they cooperate in maintaining it to avoid losing benefits, qualifications, protection, or priority, examine whether the profit has changed into an adhesive for prolongation.

### Markets and Industries
Even when problems in an existing structure are shared, see whether transaction relationships, subsidies, customers, regulatory advantages, or positions in the supply chain are stopping reform action.

### Social Observation
Do not judge support merely because many people use an institution and cooperate in maintaining it. Observe together the benefits of staying and the losses of leaving.

## Success Criteria
- Legitimate compensation for results can be distinguished from prolongation compensation.
- Retention can be distinguished from support.
- A structure in which dissatisfaction and dependence coexist can be captured.
- Silence is not mistaken for agreement.
- It can be observed how profit weakens the conversion of criticism into action.
- Surface stability can be separated from consensus.
- Fear and profit can be understood as sequential primary mechanisms rather than opposites.
- Profit itself is not declared evil; the conditions of its transformation can be observed.

## Limitations
Profit can properly be a necessary circuit as legitimate compensation for value creation or participation. Its mere existence must not be used to judge it the second primary mechanism of endurance civilization.

What must be observed is whether profit has changed from “something received because value was created” into “something received to keep a broken order from breaking.” Do not explain all of endurance civilization through profit alone; return to the original while including its relation to fear, surveillance, myth, and inertia.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not regard profit itself as evil.
- Do not judge beneficiaries as morally fallen people.
- Do not reduce profit to a simple sweetener or bribe.
- Do not regard institutional use as institutional support.
- Do not regard silence as agreement.
- Do not assume that dissatisfaction means immediate withdrawal.
- Do not regard fear and profit as opposites.
- Do not explain the entire endurance-civilization system through profit alone.
