## Summary
The parent defines profit as the second primary engine of endurance civilization. Here profit does not mean prosperity, success, or legitimate reward for value creation as such. It is a longevity cost distributed by an order that has lost consensus but still needs people to remain inside it. If fear says “leaving is dangerous,” profit says “staying is still better.” The two operate in series. Profit does not erase dissatisfaction; it allows dissatisfaction and dependence to coexist, rationalizes silence and remaining, and can make decay or corruption look like support and stability.

## Concepts
- Longevity profit: distribution that functions to keep a damaged order from collapsing rather than reward value creation.
- Profit as adhesive: benefit that weakens reasons to leave rather than producing genuine belief in the institution.
- Fear-profit series: fear raises exit cost while profit raises the apparent rationality of remaining.
- Coexistence of rejection and dependence: “this cannot continue” and “I will suffer if it collapses now” can coexist.
- Purchased silence: benefits weaken the will to carry dissent through to action without necessarily eliminating recognition of the problem.
- Beneficiary conversion: receiving institutional benefit can turn criticism into a possible injury to one's own interest.
- Stability camouflage: continued use, cooperation, and reproduction make an institution appear supported even when remaining is calculation-driven.
- Second primary engine: profit translates the rough pressure of fear into quieter daily operation.

## Causal chain
Consensus, meaning, and trust weaken → fear makes exit dangerous and prevents stopping → fear alone accumulates resistance, fatigue, and friction → long-term operation requires remaining to look rational → benefits, protection, stability, priority, recognition, vested position, or livelihood advantages are distributed → people can know institutional defects while calculating that immediate collapse would also harm them → criticism connects to possible self-interest loss → dissatisfaction remains but becomes less likely to turn into action → silence, remaining, and institutional reproduction continue → the institution appears supported and stable → decay becomes less visible → profit functions as adhesive and the second primary engine of endurance civilization.

## Applications
- Organizations: test whether compensation, promotion, benefits, titles, or assignments inhibit dissent from becoming action.
- Institutions: examine whether benefits, credentials, protection, or priority make continued participation rational despite recognized defects.
- Markets and industries: observe whether transactions, subsidies, customer access, regulatory advantage, or supply-chain position inhibit reform.
- Social analysis: separate institutional use and cooperation from genuine support by examining benefits of remaining and losses of exit.

## Measurements and audit
- Is profit reward for value creation or longevity payment for noncollapse?
- Do participants remain from belief or because collapse would damage their interests?
- Which benefits, protections, stability, recognition, priorities, or vested positions support remaining?
- Is criticism prevented from becoming action by anticipated self-interest loss?
- Do “this must change” and “collapse now would hurt me” coexist?
- Is silence consent or benefit-dependent restraint?
- Does distribution obscure institutional pain or corruption?
- Are use, cooperation, and reproduction belief-driven or calculation-driven?
- When benefits shrink, do fear, resistance, or exit reappear?
- Can legitimate participation reward be separated from longevity adhesive by domain?

## Validity conditions
- Fear alone generates enough wear that long-term operation needs rationalization of remaining.
- Benefits remain even when participants recognize institutional defects or decay.
- Institutional collapse or exit is tied to loss of self-interest, livelihood, status, or transactions.
- Dissatisfaction persists while dissent action weakens and silence, remaining, and reproduction continue.
- Profit functions less as proof of prosperity and more as adhesive cost for choosing noncollapse.

## Failure conditions
- Treating all profit or reward as a corruption mechanism.
- Reducing profit to a simple carrot or moral bribery.
- Classifying beneficiaries as corrupt and providers as bribers based on character judgment alone.
- Equating use or cooperation with belief or support.
- Assuming dissatisfaction necessarily produces immediate exit.
- Treating fear and profit as opposites rather than serial engines.
- Failing to distinguish legitimate value-creation reward from longevity reward.

## Falsification conditions
If a case classified as dominated by the profit engine shows transparent correspondence between distribution and value creation, free conversion of dissent into action even when benefits may be lost, no automatic reversal of criticism into self-interest injury, participant ability to explain institutional meaning, legitimacy, and future value independently of remaining benefits, and actual institutional revision, the longevity-adhesive classification should be revised. The model's scope should also be reconsidered if reducing benefits does not weaken cooperation or expose fear because meaning, trust, and consensus continue to sustain participation.

## Required distinctions
- Profit ≠ proof of prosperity.
- Legitimate performance reward ≠ longevity reward.
- Remaining ≠ support.
- Silence ≠ consent.
- Continued use ≠ institutional belief.
- Dissatisfaction ≠ immediate exit.
- Benefit ≠ legitimacy.
- “Staying pays” ≠ “the institution is right.”
- Fear and profit ≠ opposites.
- Second primary engine ≠ the complete endurance-civilization system.

## Interpretation constraints
This theory does not condemn profit itself or judge beneficiaries and providers as persons. Profit can be a legitimate circuit supporting value creation and participation. The relevant question is whether, inside an order that has lost consensus, profit has shifted from reward for value creation to adhesive cost for noncollapse. The parent provides no fixed quantitative threshold, so independent amounts, percentages, scores, or durations should not be invented as official conditions.
