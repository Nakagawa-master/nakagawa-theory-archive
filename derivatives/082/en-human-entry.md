## 15-Second Explanation
Connected Value Accounting (CVA) is an approach that does not determine value with a single number such as sales or PV, but brings bundle metrics, structural logs, and the memory of agreement together into one ledger structure centered on connection, agreement, and reversibility. It does not score people; it records through what causality connection, counterevidence, repair, adjudication, and re-agreement occurred, so that legitimacy can be read back later.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Single metrics are easy to understand and speed up decision-making. However, when one number such as follower count, PV, subscriber count, GDP, or stock price monopolizes legitimacy or value, the act of raising that number easily becomes the goal. That is because short-term revenue or volume of voices can push aside other values such as consent, reparability, and the continuity of relationships.

In response, the parent original first shows the need to measure across multiple axes with bundle metrics, and not discard that measurement after use but preserve it in a ledger. Connected Value Accounting understands the unit of value as a combination of three elements: **connection × agreement × reversibility**. This does not mean producing a single score with a formula; it is a structure for not losing the three elements simultaneously when reading value.

The ledger incorporates structural logs, the memory of agreement, the audit abstract, bundle metrics, two-layer agreement, and person decoupling. The basic recording unit is a “connection event,” and new connections, cross-references, presentation of counterevidence, repair, adjudication, and re-agreement are preserved chronologically. The result is made readable as bundle cross-sections for each event, cumulative profiles, and flags for re-agreement windows.

## Practical Process
**1. Identify connection events.** Use what happened—not the person itself—as the recording unit: new connections, cross-references, counterevidence, repair, adjudication, re-agreement, and similar events.

**2. Preserve it in structural logs.** Save the causality of connection, agreement, counterevidence, repair, and adjudication chronologically so that the decision path can be traced later.

**3. Record the impact on bundle metrics.** Preserve impacts on reversibility, re-agreeability, connection independence, and so on in bundle form instead of flattening them into a single composite score.

**4. Connect to the memory of agreement.** Preserve repeatedly referenced agreements not as fleeting impressions, but as a history usable in future decision-making.

**5. Add an audit abstract.** Using the parent original’s short 3–8-line reading window, summarize causality, relationships, counterevidence, and re-agreeability, with a return path to the primary logs.

**6. Keep a re-agreement window open.** Continue rereading legitimacy after a decision, and maintain a state in which the structure as a whole can be rolled back and re-agreed if a malfunction occurs.

## Examples of Application
**1. Media articles and videos.** In Phase 1 of the parent original, a stage is shown for attaching a “simple CVA form.” Reading structural logs, bundle cross-sections, abstracts, and the need for re-agreement alongside PV makes it possible to separate popularity from structural value.

**2. Organizational decisions.** Phase 2 envisions a trial as an accountability tool. Not only the conclusion of a decision, but also which connections, agreements, counterevidence, and repairs it passed through can be read later.

**3. The public sphere, including municipalities and citizen alliances.** Phase 3 shows operation on a deliberative platform. This runs quantitative agreement that prioritizes speed in parallel with qualitative agreement that confirms legitimacy.

**4. Repairing malfunctions.** When judgment is distorted by popularity bias or short-term mobilization, separate the emotional channel from the structural channel, identify which connection event in the ledger produced the distortion, and roll it back.

## Success Criteria
Do not call it a success merely because one number rose; be able to explain whether connection, agreement, and reversibility are maintained simultaneously.

Be able to return from connection events to structural logs, the memory of agreement, and the audit abstract, and reconstruct the causality of past decisions.

Do not flatten bundle metrics into a composite score; read event-specific bundle cross-sections and cumulative profiles separately.

When a malfunction is found, roll back the structure, not only the decision result, and reach re-agreement.

## Limitations
CVA is not a mechanism for converting every value into one precise number. Rather, it preserves the multi-axis form in order to avoid distortion caused by unification.

There is also a boundary to what is disclosed. The parent original discloses concepts, axis names, recording units, how outputs are read, and abstracts, while keeping weights, thresholds, composition algorithms, and audit specifications private. The reasons are prevention of gaming, person decoupling, and safety.

Personal information is also outside the scope. The parent original states that person dependence is excluded and only connection structures are handled.

## Misreading Prevention
Connected Value Accounting is not a social-credit score that quantifies people or citizens. The recording targets are structures and connection events, not personal attributes.

It is not a proposal to abolish money immediately and replace it with another currency. It is an accounting framework for recognizing, measuring, and disclosing relational value across multiple axes—value that is difficult to capture through money alone.

`connection × agreement × reversibility` is not a formula for calculating a fixed score. It indicates a structural relationship among the three elements.

Speed of decision-making alone is not success. Even if quantitative agreement is fast, if qualitative agreement or the re-agreement window is lost, the CVA structure has not been established.
