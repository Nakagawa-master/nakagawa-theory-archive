## Summary

Connected Value Accounting (CVA) is a ledger architecture for preserving relational and structural value that cannot be represented safely by a single monetary, popularity, traffic, or approval metric. It turns multi-axis observations from bundled indicators into a traceable history linked to structural logs, memory of agreement, audit summaries, evidence, and revision records. The accounting unit is not a person's worth or an organization's reputation. It is a connection event: a new relation, mutual reference, falsification, repair, adjudication, withdrawal, or re-agreement. Each event changes several independent axes such as reversibility, re-agreement capacity, connection independence, evidence traceability, and auditability. Those changes remain visible as a profile, including missing axes, tensions, and changes over time; they are not collapsed into one score or ranking. Quantitative consensus may support timely provisional decisions, while qualitative consensus audits causality, consent boundaries, minority impact, legitimacy, and repair conditions. Public information includes axis definitions, accounting units, reading guidance, audit summaries, and review triggers. Weights, thresholds, aggregation logic, and case-specific audit methods may be protected against gaming, but they still require independent oversight, conflict disclosure, change history, and an appeal route. CVA is valuable because it lets later decision makers reconstruct what happened, why it was accepted, what evidence was used, how harms were corrected, and when a new agreement is required. It must not be interpreted as a social-credit score, a personality ranking, automated governance, a replacement for all monetary accounting, or a system that treats more connections as inherently better.

## Causal chain

```text
Complex relational value is compressed into money, traffic, approval, or another single metric.
↓
Optimizing the visible number becomes the goal, allowing short-term revenue, popularity, and volume to override consent and repairability.
↓
Bundled indicators separate reversibility, re-agreement capacity, connection independence, auditability, and other structurally different axes.
↓
The observed profile is linked to structural logs, memory of agreement, audit summaries, evidence, and revision history.
↓
Connection events such as falsification, repair, adjudication, and re-agreement are recorded as changes across the bundle rather than one score.
↓
Fast quantitative consensus and legitimacy-oriented qualitative consensus operate in parallel with explicit review windows.
↓
When gaming, consent failure, rigidity, or evidence conflict appears, the system records responsibility and performs repair, rollback, or re-agreement.
```

## State model

```yaml
- proposed: a connection event or decision is introduced
- identified: scope, parties, origin, and consent boundary are identified
- consented: participation, use, exit, and withdrawal conditions are checked
- logged: premises, evidence, causality, and responsibility are preserved
- bundled: effects across independent value axes are recorded
- summarized: an audit summary provides a readable window into the ledger
- operational: the record informs an actual organizational or public decision
- monitored: side effects, missing evidence, and gaming are observed
- contested: an objection challenges evidence, definitions, or boundaries
- reconsensus_required: changed conditions require a new agreement
- repaired: a revision, rollback, or corrective action is recorded
- audited: the profile is checked against primary logs and outcomes
```

## Applications

- Organizational decisions combine financial results with consent scope, workload, reversibility, repair time, and documented objections.
- Media evaluation separates traffic and virality from provenance, correction capacity, falsifiability, and public-sphere effects.
- Public policy keeps provisional decision speed while recording minority impact, review deadlines, rollback conditions, and re-agreement windows.
- AI-assisted decisions record origin fidelity, causal fidelity, consent boundaries, correction time, and reproducibility across model changes.

## Measurements and audit

- Primary-evidence linkage rate for every material ledger entry.
- Re-agreement completion rate after objections or changed premises.
- Time from detected failure to stop, repair, notification, and closure.
- Consent Failure rate across connection events.
- Missing-axis rate and the duration of unresolved evidence gaps.
- Detected gaming, authority drift, and popularity substitution events.
- RC, ER, CEA, S, Rollback Cost, measurement error, and recognition delay as an audit bundle.

## Validity conditions

The unit of account is a verifiable connection event, not personal worth.

Every material axis links to evidence, consent records, or revision history.

Profiles, missing axes, and tensions remain visible instead of being reduced to one score.

Protected thresholds remain subject to independent audit and change control.

Provisional decisions include deadlines, stop conditions, and later legitimacy review.

Objection, repair, rollback, and re-agreement paths operate in practice.

## Failure conditions

Multiple axes are ultimately compressed into one ranking or reward number.

Popularity, title, or affiliation substitutes for structural evidence.

Entries rely on retrospective opinion without primary logs or consent boundaries.

Weights and definitions change to serve an interested party without recorded reasons.

Rollback and re-agreement exist only in documentation and cannot be executed.

Existing financial or traffic KPIs continue to dominate while CVA becomes decorative.

## Falsification conditions

Single-metric dependence and gaming increase after CVA adoption.

Evidence linkage and audit-summary consistency fail to improve after repair attempts.

Re-agreement time and Rollback Cost grow until decisions are chronically blocked.

Removing names and affiliations materially reverses judgments based on the same evidence.

The system is repurposed for rights restrictions or personality ranking and cannot be corrected.

## Required distinctions

- CVA is not a total replacement for monetary accounting.
- Structural event accounting is not a score of personal worth.
- A bundle profile is not a weighted composite score.
- Public transparency is not publication of every gameable threshold.
- Decision speed and legitimacy audit are separate functions.
- Rollback is not abandonment of responsibility.
- Having records is not the same as having auditable, searchable provenance.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not use CVA as a social-credit or personality-ranking system.

Do not treat more connections or more log entries as inherently valuable.

Do not collapse the bundle into a single league table.

Do not use protected methods to escape independent oversight.

Do not delegate final responsibility and appeal handling to an AI model.
