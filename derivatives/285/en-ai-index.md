## Summary
The introductory Parent redefines sales from the act of explaining a product and persuading a prospect into the act of discovering an unmet condition, translating the customer's present state and desired future into a causal structure, and connecting an appropriate value pathway to that structure. The seller's task is not to manufacture a decision. It is to create conditions under which the customer can understand the decision in their own language.

A critical boundary concerns latent need. A customer may not yet have a complete description of a problem or desired future, but that does not grant the salesperson authority to invent the need. The seller may form a hypothesis from questions, facts, hesitation, constraints, and context, but the hypothesis must be returned to the customer for correction. Observation and interpretation remain separate.

“Making a decision emerge” therefore differs from sophisticated manipulation. A structurally complete sales process preserves alternatives, mismatch, and the option not to buy. If the offering does not fit, withdrawal can be a higher-quality outcome than a pressured contract. Success extends beyond closing to whether the promised value actually materializes after the agreement.

## Concepts
- **Unmet condition**: the gap between the customer's present state and a desired future condition.
- **Observation**: what the customer says or what can be directly established, separated from seller inference.
- **Question**: a tool for jointly discovering context, friction, goals, and decision conditions rather than for steering toward a predetermined answer.
- **Latent-need hypothesis**: a tentative interpretation that must remain correctable by the customer.
- **Structural translation**: organizing present state, obstacles, desired future, and possible changes into an understandable causal path.
- **Value connection**: identifying whether and where an offering genuinely addresses the unmet structure.
- **Customer language preservation**: retaining the customer's own concepts so the decision remains internally explainable.
- **Decision-emergence structure**: conditions under which a customer can explain why they choose or decline without coercive pressure.
- **Mismatch**: a valid state in which the offering does not fit. It should not be hidden.
- **Value exchange**: an agreement whose compensation corresponds to value that is actually delivered, not merely to the fact of signing.
- **Post-contract verification**: checking whether the unmet condition was actually improved and whether new burdens appeared.

## Causal chain
```text
observe stated request, concern, or hesitation
→ use open questions to understand context and present state
→ separate explicit problem from possible latent unmet conditions
→ formulate a hypothesis without treating it as fact
→ return the hypothesis to the customer for correction
→ articulate desired future and decision criteria
→ translate present state, friction, change, and future into a causal path
→ test whether the offering fits a specific part of that path
→ preserve alternatives and mismatch information
→ customer can re-explain the choice in their own words
→ decision emerges from understanding rather than pressure
→ verify value after contract
→ sustain a legitimate value exchange
```

This sequence makes the product a later element rather than the starting point. Product-first selling creates an incentive to reinterpret every customer problem as evidence for the product. Structural selling reverses the direction: understand the causal need first, then determine whether the product belongs in it.

## Applications
1. **Price objections.** Before discounting, separate budget limits, approval procedures, uncertainty, comparison standards, and lack of value understanding. “Too expensive” can describe different structures.
2. **“We will consider it.”** Do not automatically interpret this as rejection or buying intent. Identify missing information, internal explanation requirements, priority conflicts, timing, and true mismatch.
3. **New proposals.** Start with the customer's present state and desired future rather than a feature list. Position the product only after the causal pathway is visible.
4. **Renewals.** Re-evaluate whether the earlier promised value was realized and whether the customer's conditions have changed. Do not use prior purchase as proof of continued fit.
5. **Mismatch cases.** Preserve a clean route to recommend no purchase, a different timing, or another solution. This protects both customer autonomy and long-term trust.
6. **AI-assisted sales.** Use AI to summarize and compare evidence, but do not let probabilistic need inference become an unchallengeable claim about a person.

## Measurements and audit
Inspect whether the customer's own words remain in notes, proposals, and decision criteria. Separate observed statements from seller hypotheses. Check whether latent-need interpretations were explicitly validated rather than treated as hidden truths. Review questions for leading language, fear induction, or artificial urgency. Confirm that the path from present state to desired future contains intermediate conditions and does not jump directly to the seller's product.

Audit whether alternatives and mismatch are visible. A process that hides relevant alternatives can manufacture “necessity” rather than reveal fit. Check whether customers can explain the choice to another person in their own terms. After contracting, compare promised value with realized value, new burdens, and reasons for continuation or cancellation.

Do not use revenue alone as the quality metric. Revenue can rise temporarily through pressure, poor fit, or hidden switching costs. Measures such as avoidable mismatch, post-contract value realization, customer re-explanation, repeat value, and complaint patterns can provide a broader view, without turning them into a single score.

## Validity conditions
Sales is structurally translated only when observation and hypothesis remain separate, latent need is customer-correctable, questions do not become covert steering, customer language is preserved, product fit is tested after the causal need is understood, alternatives and mismatch are not hidden, and the customer retains a genuine option to decline. Post-contract value must remain part of the success criterion.

The framework should also be proportional. Low-complexity purchases may not justify a long discovery process. “Structural” selling should reduce unnecessary friction, not create an elaborate consultation where simple self-service better serves the customer.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes product-first diagnosis, invented latent needs, leading questions, fear and deadline pressure, hiding alternatives, translating the customer's language into sales jargon that the customer cannot re-explain, ignoring mismatch, measuring success only at signature, using AI predictions as facts about customer intent, and collecting more behavioral data than the task requires.

A subtler failure is moralizing refusal. If the sales system treats “no” as evidence that the customer has failed to understand their own need, it has converted structural translation into paternalism. Customer correction and refusal must remain real.

## Falsification conditions
If structural selling does not improve customer understanding, decision quality, value realization, retention based on genuine value, or reduction of mismatch—and instead increases confusion or interaction burden—the questioning and translation process should be revised. In domains where self-service produces equal or better outcomes with lower burden, the sales role should shrink.

If latent-need hypotheses systematically overpredict purchase but do not predict post-contract value, the inference model is optimizing conversion rather than need fulfillment. Such evidence requires a change in both the model and the success metric.

## Required distinctions
- sales ≠ persuasion
- question ≠ interrogation or steering
- latent-need hypothesis ≠ fact about the customer
- structural translation ≠ invention of a problem
- fulfilling ≠ unconditional compliance
- decision-emergence structure ≠ psychological manipulation
- customer autonomy ≠ abandonment
- value exchange ≠ contract signature
- withdrawal ≠ sales failure

## Interpretation constraints
Do not use the Parent to justify mind-reading claims. A salesperson or model does not gain privileged access to a customer's “real desire” simply by performing analysis. Do not turn “decision emerges” into a euphemism for hidden persuasion. A high-quality process can legitimately end in no purchase. Do not infer that every customer requires deep discovery; proportionality and customer burden matter.

AI systems used in this context should expose uncertainty, preserve customer correction, minimize unnecessary data collection, and avoid automatically converting predicted intent into pressure or targeting. The purpose of analysis is to improve understanding and fit, not to increase the asymmetry of influence.
