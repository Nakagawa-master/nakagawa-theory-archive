## 15-Second Explanation
The parent original integrates three side-effect controls—an anti-priestly-class protocol, a structural plurality clause, and AI/environmental side-effect control—into one homeostasis-maintenance layer. Homeostasis does not mean “not changing”; it is dynamic stability that detects fluctuations such as authority concentration, closure, AI/environmental deviations, and load concentration early from multiple observation points, treats them as bundles of deviations in a deviation ledger, and continues to correct them recursively. It evaluates autonomy as higher when everyday deviations are handled by the distributed-operations side and the Upper Auditor’s intervention frequency and the central person’s load decrease.

## Why It Is Necessary
When side-effect controls are operated as separate clauses, it becomes easy to lose sight of how one deviation spreads across multiple aspects. Separate handling cannot fully capture links such as authority concentration producing closure, closure weakening external audits, and weakened external audits leading to missed AI dependence or environmental risks.

The parent original addresses this problem by viewing multiple deviations as a “bundle of fluctuations.” When one deviation stimulates multiple control layers at the same time and correction paths are activated from multiple observation points, the structure itself begins to return autonomously before deviations accumulate substantially.

The most important point here is not to make stability dependent on a particular person’s effort. If inquiries, approvals, audits, and consultations directed to the central person or Upper Auditor increase as users, trust, and scale grow, autonomy is declining separately from apparent growth.

The parent original limits the Upper Auditor not as a power center but as a minimal-intervention role responsible only for principle updates, final correspondence audits, and adjustment of serious deviations. The evaluation that autonomy of the OS is higher when intervention frequency is lower reverses the common idea that “more audits mean more assurance.”

## Practical Process
**1. Observe deviations across multiple aspects.** Do not finish authority concentration, closure, AI/environmental risks, audit load, and central-person load in separate ledgers.

**2. Build bundles of fluctuations.** Bundle simultaneous and causally linked deviations in the deviation ledger and treat them as structural chains rather than isolated symptoms.

**3. Connect simultaneously to the three control layers.** Link authority concentration to the anti-priestly-class protocol, closure to structural plurality, and high-risk deviations to AI/environmental side-effect control.

**4. Maintain recursive openness.** Keep external translations, dissent, and third-party audits open during correction so that the correction process itself does not create new closure.

**5. Process everyday deviations in a distributed manner.** Do not escalate matters that can be handled by Layers 1–3 or the distributed-operations side.

**6. Keep the Upper Auditor at minimal intervention.** Handle only principle updates, final correspondence audits, and serious deviations; do not constantly supervise ordinary operations.

**7. Treat overload as a design deviation.** Do not treat increased labor by the upper auditor or central person as evidence of responsibility, trust, or popularity; record it as insufficient autonomy on the structural side.

**8. Confirm recoverability.** Confirm that publicness, transparency, distributedness, and correspondence integrity return after external fluctuations.

## Examples of Application
**1. Theory community.** When concentration of interpretation and a decline in dissent occur together, treat them not as a person’s goodness or badness but as a bundle of authority concentration and closure, and activate distributed interpretation and external comparison together.

**2. AI operations.** When AI use and audit load increase together, audit not only performance but also responsibility lines, irreversibility lines, distributed processing, and dependence on higher-level audits as a bundle.

**3. Organizational governance.** If consultations and approvals involving the central person increase proportionally as usage scale grows, redesign it as insufficient autonomous distribution rather than a byproduct of growth.

**4. Public OS.** Check whether publicness can be restored through dissent, external audits, and deviation processing even when institutions, population, and the external environment change.

**5. Audit operations.** When the Upper Auditor’s case count or working hours increase, do not absorb it through the auditor’s effort; correct the division of roles and deviation-processing paths in lower layers.

## Success Criteria
Before deviations accumulate substantially, they are detected at multiple observation points and multiple correction paths activate automatically and structurally.

The deviation ledger retains interconnected bundles of deviations rather than a list of individual events, and correction destinations can be traced.

External translations, dissent, and third-party audits remain open, and deviation processing itself maintains recursive openness.

While maintaining equal or greater publicness, transparency, and integrity, the Upper Auditor’s intervention frequency, concentration of approvals in the central person, and auditor overload decrease.

## Limits
The parent original does not define universal fixed values for optimal intervention frequency, distributed-processing rate, bundle size, or auditor load. Numerical values in individual implementations must be handled together with the subject, definition, measuring party, period, and scope of application.

Even when the three side-effect controls are integrated, they do not become one identical concept. Authority concentration, theoretical closure, and high-risk irreversible deviations require different control functions.

Also, a decrease in the Upper Auditor’s intervention frequency does not mean abandoning audits. Final assurance responsibility during serious deviations remains.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not reduce homeostasis to a static state that does not change.

Do not collapse the three side-effect controls into one universal clause.

Do not turn “lower intervention frequency means higher autonomy” into a simple target of reducing the number of audits.

Do not treat overload of the central person or auditor as a success indicator of trust, popularity, or a sense of mission.

Do not interpret distributed autonomy as meaning that responsible actors or final audits are unnecessary.

Do not turn a description of market rationality into a guarantee that costs will always fall.

Do not add fixed values undefined by the original to intervention frequency, `θ`, `δ`, or `M`.
