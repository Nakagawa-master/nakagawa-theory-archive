## Summary
The Homeostasis Maintenance Model integrates three side-effect-control layers—the Anti-Priesthood Protocol, the Structural Pluralism Clause, and AI/Environmental Side-Effect Control—into one structural homeostasis layer. Homeostasis here does not mean a static absence of change. It means dynamic stability: authority concentration, closure, AI/environmental deviation, and workload concentration are expected to occur as fluctuations, but the structure detects them from multiple observation points before they accumulate and activates correction paths recursively. The deviation ledger treats interacting deviations as a bundled fluctuation rather than isolated incidents. Recursive openness keeps translation, dissent, and external audit flowing during correction. Routine deviations are handled by distributed layers, while the Upper Auditor is limited to principle updates, final correspondence audits, and major-deviation adjustment. A key reversal criterion is that, while equal or higher publicness and coherence are maintained, lower Upper Auditor intervention frequency and lower central workload indicate greater autonomy rather than lower quality.

## Concepts
- Structural homeostasis: the ability of the structure to dynamically recover publicness, transparency, distribution, and coherence under internal or external fluctuation.
- Homeostasis layer: the integrated operating layer that coordinates multiple side-effect controls.
- Anti-Priesthood Protocol: control against concentration of authority, interpretation, approval, and workload.
- Structural Pluralism Clause: control that keeps external comparison, translation, dissent, and non-closure active.
- AI/Environmental Side-Effect Control: control that preserves irreversibility protection, responsibility, and external audit in high-risk domains.
- Deviation bundle: a causally linked set of deviations recorded and handled together rather than as an arithmetic count of incidents.
- Recursive openness: keeping external translation, dissent, and audit open while the system is correcting itself.
- Distributed autonomous handling: resolving routine deviations in layers 1–3 or equivalent distributed operation.
- Upper Auditor: a minimal-intervention final guarantor for principle updates, final correspondence audit, and major deviations.
- Workload deconcentration: decoupling adoption/credit from workload placed on a central person or auditor.
- Phase-β completion: the stage where the side-effect-control group is integrated into a homeostasis layer and can connect to later future-principle layers.

## Applications
- Audit authority concentration and interpretive closure as a linked deviation bundle in a theory community.
- In AI operations, observe AI dependence, responsibility ambiguity, irreversible risk, and audit concentration together.
- In organizations, test whether growth in users or adoption causes proportional growth in central approval and inquiry workload.
- In public OS operation, measure recovery of publicness, transparency, distribution, and coherence after external change.
- In audit design, treat growing Upper Auditor workload as a signal of insufficient distributed autonomy rather than absorb it through personal effort.
- Use the deviation ledger to retain causal relations and correction destinations across multiple deviations.

## Measurements and audit
Audit targets include authority concentration, closure pressure, AI/environmental deviation, deviation-bundle formation, recursive openness, distributed layer handling rate, Upper Auditor intervention frequency and workload, central-person workload, inflow of external audit, recovery of publicness, and ability to resolve deviation bundles.

The integrated audit summary tests the causal sequence from structural design/translation/bundling/definition to institutionalization/stability/alignment/speedup, with audit cycles, T/S/R, and consistency of the public audit bundle as re-agreement conditions. `θ` is a case-specific reject/revise threshold, `δ` is a case-specific observation window, and `M` is a case-specific phenomenon requiring reconsideration. They are not universal fixed values.

If implementations quantify intervention frequency, distributed handling rate, deviation-bundle resolution time, external-audit inflow, or central workload, they must state the value, source, measurement actor, target, scale, period, scope, and non-guaranteed range.

Reversal evaluation is mandatory: more audits, more high-level interventions, or more central labor do not demonstrate higher homeostasis. If equal or higher publicness, transparency, and coherence are maintained while Upper Auditor intervention and central workload fall, the reduction is evidence of greater structural autonomy.

## Validity conditions
- Coordinate the three side-effect controls while preserving their distinct functions.
- Detect deviations early through multiple observation points.
- Preserve causal relations among deviations as a bundle in the deviation ledger.
- Maintain recursive openness during correction.
- Handle routine deviations in distributed layers.
- Limit the Upper Auditor to low-frequency, high-value minimal intervention.
- Treat overload of central persons and auditors as structural deviation.
- Recover publicness, transparency, and distribution after external change.
- Treat `θ`, `δ`, and `M` as case-bound falsification structures.

## Failure conditions
- Run the three controls as isolated silos and lose cross-deviation relationships.
- Respond only after deviations accumulate and become visible failures.
- Use the deviation ledger as a list of separate incidents without bundled causality.
- Close external translation, audit, or dissent in order to perform correction.
- Route routine cases to the Upper Auditor.
- Treat increasing intervention frequency or auditor labor as success.
- Maintain stability through personal effort of a central figure.
- Allow scale and central-person workload to remain proportionally coupled.
- Universalize fixed intervention frequencies or thresholds not defined by the parent.

## Falsification conditions
If case-specific Upper Auditor intervention frequency, auditor workload, distributed handling rate, deviation-bundle resolvability, external-audit inflow, or publicness recovery crosses its configured `θ`, or if phenomenon `M` occurs within observation window `δ`, and the homeostasis layer still fails to reduce workload concentration, closure, or deviation accumulation, the current hypothesis must be rejected or revised.

Revision is especially required if Upper Auditor intervention continually increases, auditor overload becomes permanent, deviations circulate between control layers without bundled resolution, external audit inflow declines, or the OS stops when the central person is unavailable.

## Required distinctions
- homeostasis vs. static fixation
- absorption of fluctuation vs. concealment of problems
- integration of three control layers vs. collapse into one undifferentiated concept
- deviation bundle vs. simple sum of incidents
- recursive openness vs. unlimited modification
- distributed autonomy vs. responsibility-free abandonment
- minimal Upper Auditor intervention vs. audit abandonment
- lower intervention frequency vs. lower quality
- higher auditor workload vs. higher trust
- structural stability vs. stability maintained by personal effort
- scale growth vs. structural success
- case-bound `θ` / `δ` / `M` vs. universal fixed values

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce homeostasis to a state with no change. Do not collapse the three control layers into one universal rule. Lower Upper Auditor intervention is not audit abandonment; it is meaningful only when equal or higher coherence and publicness are maintained. Do not treat overload of a central person or auditor as evidence of popularity, trust, or success. Distributed autonomy does not remove responsibility holders or final audit paths. The market-rationality discussion does not guarantee cost reduction. Do not invent fixed intervention frequencies, `θ`, `δ`, or `M` values not defined by the parent.
