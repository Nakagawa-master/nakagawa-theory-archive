## Summary
Emotional extraction occurs when resonance, response, care, or intimacy becomes economically valuable in a way that pushes people beyond voluntary participation. If reactions determine reward or visibility, emotional expression can become a practical obligation. When the correspondence line to the originating question, pain, creation, or care disappears, platforms or central actors can capture benefits while origin actors carry emotional cost. A safer structure preserves continuing consent, provenance, benefit return, the right not to respond, exit, and the ability to stop evaluation. High engagement alone is not success. Audit emotional-labor time, self-censorship, dependence, reward concentration, and whether silence or exit creates penalties. AI may flag patterns but must not diagnose a person's emotions or consent automatically.

## Validity conditions
- Emotional provision remains voluntary.
- Provenance is retained.
- Benefit return is visible.
- Non-response and exit are not punished.
- Intimacy does not automatically create rights.
- Extraction signals are audited.

## Failure conditions
- Non-responders are treated as lower value.
- Empathy is treated as a free resource.
- Benefits do not return to origin actors.
- Exit is punished.
- Intimacy becomes a purchasable entitlement.
- Performed emotion is rewarded without limits.

## Falsification conditions
- Consent and provenance do not reduce emotional burden.
- Benefit return does not improve voluntary resonance.
- Non-response rights consistently destroy necessary cooperation.
- Lower evaluation does not reduce dependence or self-censorship.
- Simpler labor contracts protect against extraction better.

## Required distinctions
- resonance / emotional labor

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not make resonance a response duty.
- Do not treat care as free.
- Do not sell intimacy as entitlement.
- Do not penalize silence or exit.
- Do not turn provenance into exclusive ownership.
- Do not use as individual diagnosis.
