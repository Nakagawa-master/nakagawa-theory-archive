## 15-Second Explanation
What this Parent publicly disassembles is not “a service for digitally storing repair materials.” Repair issues continue over the long term, while boards and responsible persons change over short periods. It is a structure for inheriting the reasons for decisions, comparisons, pending matters, and next-review conditions lost in that time gap in a form that the next actors can reconstruct. The value core selected by Nakagawa Master is **repair-decision continuity infrastructure**.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Large-scale repairs and long-term repair plans have time horizons of several years to more than ten years, while directors and officers of management associations change on shorter cycles. Even if materials are stored, the next board must repeat the same investigation and discussion from the beginning unless it can recover why an option was adopted, why another was rejected, what remains undecided, and what conditions would trigger reconsideration.

The Parent reads this break not merely as insufficient document management, but as a mismatch between the time scale of long-lived assets and that of short-term decision-makers. Therefore, what is preserved is not document volume but the context needed to reproduce a decision.

**Why this value core was selected.** Reading Renoplat only as “repair-management DX,” “document sharing,” or “history management” explains the convenience of digitization, but not why a change of board stops repair decisions. Nakagawa Master values **the ability to reconstruct decision reasons, rejection reasons, undecided matters, and next-review conditions across generational change**. This core distinguishes blindly fixing past decisions from inheriting their premises.

**Why this core was foregrounded in the external article.** Simply telling external readers “a repair-management tool” would hide the recurring structural problem in condominium management: “we do not know what the previous board was thinking.” Nakagawa Master therefore made the time design of inheriting decisions across board changes the main axis rather than feature introduction. This is a social translation extracted by the Parent from the target facts, not MRC’s or an external medium’s official theory.

## Practical Process
```text
Clarify the issue in the repair decision
→ Record the adopted and rejected options and the reasons
→ Link supporting materials to decision issues
→ State undecided matters explicitly
→ Record conditions for reconsideration next time
→ Make who made which decision and when traceable
→ Let the next board reconstruct the decision context
→ Revalidate only conditions that have changed as a priority
→ Return the new decision to the history
```

## Application Example
**1. Large-scale repair.** If construction is deferred, retain reasons such as price, deterioration, funding, and relation to other works, rather than recording only “deferred.”

**2. Rejected options.** Record why alternatives were not selected, not only the adopted option, so the next actors can reproduce the comparison.

**3. Undecided matters.** Structure items awaiting investigation, funding conditions, or institutional changes as undecided and state the next conditions.

**4. Other long-term governance.** The same type of condition can be examined in neighborhood associations, committees, and nonprofit organizations where the issue outlives the person in charge.

## Success Criterion
- The next actor can reconstruct past decisions with reasons.
- Rejected options and their reasons remain, not only adopted options.
- Undecided matters and next conditions are clear.
- Supporting materials are linked to decision issues.
- Unnecessary repetition of the same investigation and discussion decreases.
- Past decisions are not fixed; they can be revalidated when conditions change.
- New decisions return to the history.

## Limitations
Even if records exist, decision continuity does not hold unless the next board uses them. It also backfires if it turns past decisions into authority and treats them as unchangeable “precedent.”

Decision continuity does not prevent reconfirming the latest building condition, prices, institutions, or funding conditions. What is inherited is not the absolute nature of the conclusion, but the context that led to it.

## Misreading Prevention
- Do not confuse MRC / Renoplat’s public facts with Nakagawa Master’s structural reading.
- Do not equate document storage with decision continuity.
- Do not treat decision continuity as fixing past decisions.
- Do not treat “repair-decision continuity infrastructure” as the company’s official definition.
- Do not omit why this value core was selected in the external article and return to a mere management-tool introduction.
