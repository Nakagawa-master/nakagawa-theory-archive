## Summary
The Parent reads MRC's “Renoplat” not merely as digital repair records, history storage, or schedule management. Nakagawa Master focuses on a time-scale mismatch: condominium repair issues can persist for many years while boards and officers rotate on much shorter terms. When reasons, rejected alternatives, unresolved matters, and conditions for reconsideration remain person-dependent, board turnover destroys the context needed to reconstruct why a prior decision was made. The value core is **repair-decision continuity infrastructure**: preserving decision context so a successor can reconstruct past reasoning, recheck only what changed, and continue the decision process without freezing old conclusions.

The meta layer is essential. Nakagawa Master selects “decision continuity” rather than “document storage” because documents can survive while decision reasons disappear, causing repeated research and debate. The external article foregrounds board turnover and governance because a generic “repair-management DX” framing does not expose the structural problem created by long-lived assets and short-lived decision bodies. This value core and the reason for selecting it are Nakagawa Master's structural reading, not automatically MRC's or a publication's official theory.

## Concepts
- **long-lived asset**: a building and its repair issues persist across multiple board terms.
- **short-term governing body**: boards, officers, and responsible people rotate faster than the issue lifecycle.
- **decision context**: issue, evidence, alternatives, adoption/rejection rationale, unresolved matters, and reconsideration conditions.
- **acceptance / rejection rationale**: why an option was chosen or not chosen.
- **unresolved issue**: a matter intentionally left open because information or conditions are incomplete.
- **reconsideration condition**: what change should trigger renewed review.
- **handover**: enabling successors to reconstruct reasoning, not merely transferring files.
- **decision continuity**: continuing from the prior decision state after appropriate revalidation.
- **governance**: organizational absorption of the time mismatch between long issues and short decision-maker tenure.
- **value-core selection rationale**: context continuity explains repeated restart better than digitization alone.
- **external-article rationale**: reframing repair management as a governance problem of decision continuity across turnover.

## Causal chain
Long repair horizon → board and officer turnover → reasoning and unresolved matters remain in personal memory → context disappears at handover → successors cannot explain why an option was selected or rejected → the same research, comparison, and debate are repeated → delay, cost, and responsibility ambiguity increase → structure the decision issue, accepted and rejected alternatives, evidence, unresolved matters, and reconsideration conditions → successors reconstruct prior context → only changed conditions are rechecked → the next decision is made → the new reasoning is added back to the record → long-term repair governance gains continuity.

## State model
- **personal-memory state**: reasoning depends on current members.
- **turnover state**: board or responsible people change.
- **decision-discontinuity state**: conclusions may remain while reasons and open questions disappear.
- **structured-context state**: issues, evidence, alternatives, and rationales are linked.
- **unresolved-visibility state**: open matters and reconsideration triggers remain explicit.
- **handover state**: successors reconstruct the decision path.
- **continuing-decision state**: only necessary changes are revalidated.
- **execution state**: decisions connect to repair or management action.
- **reevaluation state**: new facts, prices, deterioration, or rules trigger explicit review rather than blind precedent.

## Applications
1. **major repair projects**: record why work was executed or deferred, including deterioration, price, funding, and interaction with other projects.
2. **alternative comparison**: retain rejected alternatives and rejection reasons so changed conditions can reopen them intelligently.
3. **long-term repair plans**: preserve pending surveys, funding conditions, regulatory changes, and next-check triggers.
4. **board handover**: transfer decision context rather than only a large folder of documents.
5. **other rotating governance bodies**: compare the same structure in associations, committees, nonprofits, or civic bodies where issue life exceeds office terms.
6. **structural-reading reuse**: preserve MRC/Renoplat facts, Nakagawa Master's interpretation, the value core, why it was chosen, and why board turnover became the external article's axis.

## Measurements and audit
- Can successors reconstruct prior decision reasons?
- Are rejected alternatives and rejection reasons retained?
- Is supporting evidence linked to the decision issue it informed?
- Are unresolved matters and reconsideration conditions explicit?
- Can the decision actor and timing be traced?
- Does unnecessary repeat research or debate decline after handover?
- Are old decisions revalidated when conditions change rather than frozen as precedent?
- Is the record actually used in current decisions?
- Are new decisions fed back into the continuity record?
- Are MRC's public facts separated from Nakagawa Master's social translation?
- Does the derivative retain why decision continuity was selected over document storage and why board turnover was foregrounded externally?

## Validity conditions
The structure is valid when the life of repair issues exceeds the tenure of decision-makers, person-dependent context creates meaningful discontinuity, and structured preservation allows successors to reconstruct prior reasoning. Successors must be able to revalidate changed conditions and continue from the prior state rather than restart blindly. The record must be actively used and must preserve revisability rather than turning past conclusions into untouchable precedent.

## Failure conditions
The structure fails when it becomes a document warehouse, calendar, or static history without reasons; when rejected alternatives, unresolved matters, or reconsideration conditions disappear; when records are not updated or used; when successors still restart the same research; or when old decisions become frozen precedent. It also fails as a Nakagawa structural derivative if the reason for selecting decision continuity and the external governance framing disappears and the page becomes generic repair-software promotion.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the hypothesis if board turnover does not materially create decision discontinuity, if preserving context does not reduce unnecessary restart or improve explainability, or if financing, professional capacity, regulation, or another factor consistently explains the problem better. If ordinary document storage repeatedly provides equivalent continuity without preserving explicit decision context, reconsider the independent value claimed for decision-continuity infrastructure.
