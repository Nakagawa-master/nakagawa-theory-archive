## Summary
Part Two turns the introductory redefinition of sales into a repeatable observation-and-editing structure. Its three core lenses are fulfillment, questions, and value exchange. Operationally, the seller observes three things: the customer's present state, the friction or obstacle preventing a decision or implementation, and the shape of the desired future. The seller then performs two forms of editing: continuity, which makes the path from present to future explicit, and necessity, which explains why a particular path fits the customer's conditions.

Necessity must not be confused with eliminating alternatives. A legitimate necessity claim is comparative: given this purpose, these constraints, and these available paths, this option fits for stated reasons. If alternatives are hidden, the result is manufactured inevitability rather than understanding. Likewise, a latent need is a hypothesis that must be checked with the customer, not a hidden truth owned by the salesperson.

The process ends neither at presentation nor contract. A value exchange is complete only when the expected unmet condition is actually improved and the exchange remains legitimate for both sides. This makes post-contract observation part of the sales structure.

## Concepts
- **Fulfillment**: matching a real unmet condition with appropriate value; not unconditional compliance with every request.
- **Question**: a means to jointly discover context, friction, and desired state rather than steer toward a predetermined answer.
- **Value exchange**: a sustainable relation between realized customer value and legitimate compensation.
- **Present state**: the customer's actual starting condition, including existing tools and constraints.
- **Decision friction**: budget, approval, workload, risk, priority, uncertainty, or other conditions that obstruct action.
- **Shape of desire**: the concrete state the customer wants to achieve, beyond an abstract phrase such as “efficiency.”
- **Continuity**: a non-magical pathway from present state through intermediate changes to the desired state.
- **Necessity**: a transparent explanation of fit after alternatives and conditions are considered.
- **Latent-need hypothesis**: a tentative inference subject to customer correction.
- **Re-explanation**: the customer's ability to explain the reason for a choice in their own words.

## Causal chain
```text
observe present state
→ separate stated issue and possible latent unmet condition
→ identify decision and implementation friction
→ articulate the desired future and its concrete shape
→ return hypotheses to the customer for correction
→ build continuity from present through intermediate conditions to future
→ compare alternative routes
→ explain why a route fits under the stated conditions
→ verify offering fit
→ customer can re-explain value and tradeoffs
→ decision emerges from understanding
→ verify realized value after agreement
```

## Applications
A price objection can be decomposed into affordability, approval, perceived risk, comparison standards, or uncertainty about value. A simple discount will not solve a governance or implementation problem. An “efficiency” request can be translated into which task, whose time, what decision, and what error burden must change. This produces a concrete desired state rather than a slogan.

When multiple solutions exist, the method places them side by side with their conditions. Current-state continuation, internal development, a competitor, or delay may be legitimate options. The seller's proposal becomes necessary only in the limited sense that it fits the specific constraints better, not because the alternatives were made invisible.

For stalled deals, the process asks whether desire is weak, friction is unresolved, the path lacks continuity, or the offering does not actually fit. Increasing follow-up pressure before diagnosing this structure can amplify noise rather than resolve the cause.

## Measurements and audit
Check whether the present state is recorded as customer-confirmed fact rather than seller interpretation. Audit whether latent needs were explicitly tested. Inspect whether “price” has been used as a catch-all for distinct forms of friction. Require the desired state to be concrete enough that the customer could later say whether it was achieved.

Review the continuity map for missing implementation, migration, training, organizational, or risk conditions. Review necessity language for hidden alternatives, artificial urgency, and claims of exclusivity. Confirm that the customer can re-explain the value and tradeoffs to another stakeholder. After contract, compare expected and realized value, including new burdens created by the solution.

## Validity conditions
The three observations must be grounded in customer-correctable information. The two editing operations must preserve alternatives and intermediate conditions. Need hypotheses remain hypotheses until checked. The offering is connected only after fit is established. Customer autonomy and re-explanation are preserved, and post-contract value is included in the success criterion.

The depth of the method must remain proportional to the decision. A low-risk standardized purchase should not be made artificially complex merely to demonstrate consultative skill.

## Failure conditions
Failure includes inventing latent needs, reducing all friction to price, defining the customer's future on the seller's behalf, hiding implementation burden, presenting necessity by removing alternatives, using urgency or fear to close, and declaring success at signature even when the promised value is not realized.

Another failure is over-questioning. Questions that add burden without improving structural understanding can become a performance of consultation rather than a customer benefit.

## Falsification conditions
If the three-observation/two-editing model does not improve decision quality, fit, value realization, or reduction of avoidable churn—and instead increases confusion or customer burden—the observation categories or sequence should be revised. In domains where simpler information reliably produces better outcomes, use the simpler path.

If predicted latent needs correlate with conversion but not with realized customer value, the inference mechanism should be considered misaligned and revised.

## Required distinctions
- fulfillment ≠ submission
- latent need ≠ seller invention
- question ≠ steering
- continuity ≠ guarantee of success
- necessity ≠ elimination of alternatives
- decision ≠ result of pressure
- value exchange ≠ contract signature
- customer understanding ≠ customer control

## Interpretation constraints
Do not turn “understanding an unspoken need” into a claim of privileged access to a customer's inner life. Need remains correctable. Do not use the concept of necessity to create psychological inevitability. AI-generated need predictions are probabilistic evidence, not purchase justification. Data collection should remain proportionate to the decision.

The framework allows a legitimate non-purchase outcome. A customer who understands that the offer is not suitable may represent a more complete structural decision than a customer who signs under pressure.
