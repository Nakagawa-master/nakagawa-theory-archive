## Summary
Co-designer structural ethics connects AI option-generation and analysis to social design while preserving human goal-setting, authorization, explanation, affected-party rights, and institutional responsibility.

## Central thesis
Co-design is not shared immunity. Separate goal, generation, verification, authorization, impact, dissent, and rollback roles so increased capability does not erase accountability.

## Causal chain
AI generation → hollow human approval risk → role separation → affected parties → audit → accountable authorization → reversible pilot → measurement → reagreement.

## Measurements
Role clarity, responsibility traceability, counterexample retention, affected-party participation, rollback, explainability, recurring side effects.

## Failure conditions
Responsibility outsourcing, ceremonial approval, excluded affected parties, irreversible deployment, erased premises.

## Falsification conditions
If co-design systematically creates more ambiguity, harm, or delay than explicit human-led governance, revise the role architecture.

## Interpretation constraints
Co-design ≠ shared immunity. AI participation ≠ decision authority. Optimization ≠ legitimacy.
