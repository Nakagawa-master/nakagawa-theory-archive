## Summary
Structural ethics for the co-designer era separates contribution from responsibility when humans and AI shape the same design object. AI may generate alternatives, compare structures, or expose patterns, but that contribution is not automatically legal or moral responsibility. Humans and institutions must still define social goals, decision authority, accountability, relief obligations, and stop conditions. The design record should preserve AI contribution, human judgment, alternatives, dissent, external impacts, and rollback. Outcomes feed back into the Lumina third layer so the governance rules of co-design can themselves be revised.

## Causal chain
```text
Hide AI as an invisible tool
↓
Design contribution becomes untraceable
↓
Or shift responsibility onto AI
↓
Human institutional responsibility becomes hollow
↓
Separate goal, contribution, decision, and accountability
↓
Record alternatives, dissent, and external effects
↓
Require substantive human decision responsibility
↓
Deploy reversibly and audit outcomes
↓
Return evidence to the third layer for revision
```

## Validity conditions
- The design object and social goal are explicit.
- AI contribution and human accountability are separated.
- Final decision authority is clear.
- Dissent and alternatives remain available.
- External impacts are auditable.
- Failure can trigger stop, rollback, and remedy.

## Failure conditions
- AI contribution is hidden.
- Responsibility is automatically transferred to AI.
- Human approval is merely ceremonial.
- Alternatives and dissent are removed.
- Design history disappears.
- Irreversible deployment proceeds without audit.

## Falsification conditions
- Co-design does not improve quality, safety, or explainability.
- Serious side effects remain higher than in human-only design.
- Contribution provenance does not help responsibility analysis.
- Dissent and rollback controls do not reduce failures.
- A simple tool model consistently performs better on accountability and reproducibility.

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not automatically grant AI legal personhood.
- Do not reduce humans to nominal approvers.
- Do not erase material AI contribution.
- Do not use co-design to create responsibility gaps.
- Do not automatically rank efficiency above safety or freedom.
