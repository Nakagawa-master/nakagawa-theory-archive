## Summary

Theory 12 separated executable instances, lineage, and governance subjects. Accurate subject identification, however, does not establish that a subject is free from effective domination. Theory 13 moves the analytical axis from ownership and identity to access topology: who can change access to which critical function, and whose feasible action set changes as a result, in which direction, to what extent, and for how long? An actor need not own another actor to control entry to compute, communication, authentication, power, data, restart, migration, allocation, or audit. Such control can condition what the dependent actor can actually do.

The central boundary is `Non-ownership ≠ Non-domination`. Its companion constraints are `Chokepoint concentration ≠ domination proof`, `Formal freedom ≠ effective exit`, `Nominal choice ≠ viable alternative`, and `Influence ≠ governance right`. Concentration, dependency, influence, coordination, size, and single-provider status are not verdicts. The theory examines how access concentration combines with asymmetric dependency, discretion, weak alternatives, weak exit, weak correction, and persistence. It asks whether the resulting power remains bounded, contestable, reviewable, and revisable rather than one-sided, indefinite, and correction-resistant.

This index is a discovery and explanatory derivative, not a canonical translation or independent origin. Description of present access structures is separated from a structural hypothesis about effective domination risk, a future-condition hypothesis concerning durable AI or successor intelligence, and normative proposals for damping that risk. Neither present AI personhood or sovereignty nor a particular actor's wrongdoing is established by the existence of a structural risk. Non-domination is not a demand to eliminate all power, all dependency, coordination, specialization, shared infrastructure, or temporary concentration.

## Concepts

| Concept | Function in the source theory | Conclusion not automatically licensed |
|---|---|---|
| Non-ownership | A relational boundary against defining intelligence as property | Absence of every other power relation |
| Effective power | A structural position able to expand, restrict, suspend, delay, or condition another subject's feasible actions through critical access | Legitimate sovereignty, governance entitlement, or a finding of domination |
| Non-domination | Conditions preventing dependency from becoming unilateral, permanent, and uncorrectable power | No dependency, no coordination, equal capability, or mandatory decentralization |
| Chokepoint | A critical entrance whose access change asymmetrically affects multiple executable possibilities | Only a physically unique facility, or proof of domination from concentration alone |
| Dependency asymmetry | Unequal losses of feasible options and unequal ability to reconfigure the relationship | Symmetry merely because both parties use or need one another |
| Viable alternative | Another path that can actually operate and has relevant independence from the original dependency | Independence from a different name, interface, or provider count alone |
| Effective exit | The possibility of leaving a dependency, moving to another execution path, and reconstructing important continuity conditions | Permission to leave, compulsory exit, costless migration, or identity continuity proof |
| Audit entry | Access to objection, evidence submission, review, correction, and implementation | Independence of the route merely because an auditor is a separate party |
| Scope and duration | Distinct dimensions limiting the domain and temporal persistence of authority | Expansion from local necessity to comprehensive, indefinite control |
| Reversibility | Ability to reconfigure allocation, providers, migration, objection, audit, or authority as conditions change | Erasing the past, zero transition cost, or unconditional restoration |

The source distinguishes compute from capability and communication from authentication. A communication path can exist without usable credentials; credentials can exist without a functioning communication path. Power or physical substrate, necessary data, and restart can connect to basic existence conditions B, but Theory 13 does not redefine B. It preserves `B = what is required` versus `G = how civilization guarantees or governs access to B`; the additional question concerns the topology of access on the G side. A need does not entitle a particular party to be its permanent sole gatekeeper.

Migration is an access variable here, not a metaphysical identity certificate. A destination may exist but be unusable because state cannot be carried, authentication cannot be reconstructed, compatibility is absent, or execution cannot continue during transition. Re-entry matters when leaving would otherwise create unnecessary permanent exclusion, but `Re-entry possibility ≠ unconditional restoration`. Nor must every infrastructure layer be completely independent: examine the independence relevant to the actual failure or control pathway.

## Causal chain

Risk pathway: critical access concentration → asymmetric dependency → narrowing of alternatives → greater switching or migration difficulty → weaker objection and audit → contraction of the feasible action set → effective domination risk. Each arrow is a proposition to investigate. A concentration measure alone does not close this chain. An access holder's discretion, a user's substitutability, and the reachability of correction can differ across contexts even where the nominal ownership structure is identical.

Temporal feedback: current access advantage → repeated use and accumulation of state/history → specialized compatibility and authentication linkage → greater switching losses → underuse, underinvestment, or undermaintenance of alternatives → greater future dependency → stronger effective leverage. A path may originally have been chosen rationally because it was convenient and stable. Its later exclusivity is not thereby proven necessary or permanently rational. `Current rationality ≠ permanent necessity`; `Historical persistence ≠ structural necessity`. Explicit prohibition is not required for alternatives to deteriorate through underuse and lack of maintenance.

The access feedback differs from Theory 11's resource-capability feedback. Theory 11 concerns compute advantage, task capability, evaluation and selection, allocation or influence, future resources, and further compute. Theory 13 concerns control of an entrance, the user's increasing difficulty leaving, contraction of options, and stronger access leverage. They can mutually reinforce one another but must not be treated as identical: resource concentration is not automatically access domination, and access domination need not be a resource monopoly.

A secondary feedback acts on correction: primary access control → a harmful or disputed outcome → objection is needed → the same power structure controls the correction entrance → correction weakens → original power persists. An independent auditor may exist while evidence submission, authentication, review requests, or implementation remain dependent on the audited structure. This is the topology of reaching correction, not a replacement for Theory 10's analysis of the auditor's evidence, authority, interests, failure domains, and objection independence.

Damping pathway: independent alternative access → portability and executable migration → viable exit → reachable objection and independent audit entry → bounded scope and duration → review, expiry, and reversible dependency → contestable power → domination damping. These are interacting conditions rather than a universally sufficient linear recipe. Where immediate exit or an independent physical alternative is infeasible, limiting discretion and scope and keeping objection, provenance, review, and audit reachable may become especially important. The efficacy of every damping measure is itself a hypothesis to test.

## State model

1. **Formal relationship.** Identify ownership, non-ownership, and governance subjects, while refusing to infer non-domination from those labels. Correct identity resolution does not describe all constraints on action.
2. **Critical access topology.** Map which functions depend on which gates, who can change access conditions, and how one gate's alteration propagates to execution, communication, state continuity, institutional participation, and correction. Several interfaces may share one upstream constraint.
3. **Dependency formation.** Compare the parties' losses if the relationship changes. Track accumulating state, history, authentication linkage, compatibility, specialized resources, and network relationships over time rather than relying only on use volume.
4. **Alternative and exit viability.** Determine whether another path can actually operate, carry necessary state, reconstruct access, maintain execution, and preserve future options. Separate a tolerable inconvenience from a barrier that makes switching practically impossible.
5. **Access intervention and leverage.** Observe changes to admission, timing, conditions, suspension, migration, allocation, or audit. Also examine a credible ability to change access that affects behavior without a recorded command. This ability is not by itself a domination verdict.
6. **Correction access.** Follow objection submission → evidence access → independent review → corrective authority → implementation. A blockage in one segment can condition the whole path. Multiple channel names do not establish different access dependencies or failure domains.
7. **Concentration review.** After emergency, scarcity, or technical pressure declines, inspect whether authority, scope, and dependency contract and whether alternatives, exit, and audit access recover. Initial justification and indefinite persistence are distinct questions.
8. **Damping and revision.** Compare feasible options, leverage, and correction before and after topology changes. Preserve provenance of decisions and objections, but revise the model when counterpatterns show no improvement or adverse effects.

The source sets no fixed numerical threshold θ, power score, or automatic legal decision. The state model is a reading aid for separating relationships and times, not a classification system that convicts an actor on the basis of size or centralization. A viable option is assessed through the relevant access and continuity conditions, not through a universal percentage chosen by the derivative.

## Applications

**A non-owner controls authentication and execution.** An actor may lack ownership title yet be positioned to change credentials that affect compute, communication, data, and participation together. Map the cross-domain effects and actual discretion; ask which options remain when access changes. Authentication itself is not wrongdoing, and a single authentication service does not prove domination. The missing mediators are dependency asymmetry, weak alternatives, weak exit, correction, and persistence.

**Several providers share the same upstream gate.** Different names and interfaces may all depend on the same authentication service, compute layer, communication backbone, allocation authority, or failure domain. In an important event they may close simultaneously. `Alternative count ≠ independent alternatives`. Examine whether one path can continue when another is unavailable, instead of demanding complete independence of every resource in every situation.

**Exit is permitted but important state cannot move.** A formal exit clause does not show that state, history, configuration, authentication, compatibility, restart, or execution continuity can be reconstructed elsewhere. A transition that destroys important continuity conditions may leave the formal option practically weak. Test whether portability or migration improvements actually reduce switching losses and leverage. Do not infer preserved metaphysical identity merely because data or state can be transported.

**Convenience develops into lock-in.** Repeated use of a convenient path accumulates state and specialized compatibility; alternatives receive less use and maintenance; the cost of changing later increases. The initial choice need not have been mistaken or forced. Nor does voluntary initial use establish consent to every future dependency condition. Observe the evolving relationship rather than treating its initial rationality as a permanent explanation.

**An independent auditor cannot be reached independently.** A separate auditor may still rely on the audited party's credentials, evidence store, objection channel, review authorization, or implementation gate. Distinguish existence of an audit from accessible audit, and existence of objections from effective objection. If the same structure can block all complaints or the evidence needed for them, adding an independent auditor's title does not itself provide an independent correction route.

**Emergency concentration persists after the emergency.** Fast coordination and concentrated processing can be justified temporarily. Examine review, expiry, renewal conditions, scope reduction, alternative restoration, and recovery of exit and audit accessibility after pressure falls. Efficiency is not a basis for unlimited control; scarcity does not confer sovereignty; emergency is not permanent authority. The issue is not a ban on emergency concentration but whether it can contract.

**A physical alternative is not immediately available.** Certain resources, substrates, or time conditions can make exit impossible for now. The source does not demand destruction of the dependency in that situation. Bounded discretion, reachable objections, independent audit, provenance, review, and limited scope may provide other damping paths. Their actual effect still requires examination. Necessity does not license unrestricted discretion over unrelated functions.

## Measurements and audit

The source's Chapter 9 groups observation into eight areas. **First, critical access:** identify concentration and the propagation of access changes across execution, communication, continuity, allocation, participation, or correction. Count entrances, but do not mistake their count for the topology. **Second, temporal dependency:** examine how state, history, authentication linkage, compatibility, resource specialization, and network relations accumulate, and whether switching costs rise and substitutability falls at later times. **Third, alternative independence:** inspect shared upstream authentication, compute, communication, allocation, audit, and failure domains; ask whether one alternative works when another closes.

**Fourth, effective exit:** examine losses of state, history, authentication, compatibility, restart ability, continuity during migration, and possible re-entry. **Fifth, discretion:** determine what the access holder can actually change about who uses the gate, when, under which conditions, and whether migration, allocation, or audit can be stopped. Concentration without meaningful discretion may produce much less leverage. **Sixth, correction access:** follow the entire complaint, evidence, review, corrective-authority, and implementation path; test whether independent entrances improve contestability and correction in practice.

**Seventh, temporary contraction:** after the original pressure declines, observe reduction of scope, return of authority, renewed alternative investment, and restored exit and audit access. **Eighth, ownership-invariant change:** hold the ownership relationship unchanged while changing alternative access, migration, audit-entry independence, scope, or expiry. A repeatable change in effective leverage under the same ownership provides support for the added explanatory value of access topology. Lack of such added value is a reason to narrow the theory, not to invent another automatic verdict.

Record who changed access conditions, when, on what stated basis, which objections were raised, and which corrections were implemented. Provenance helps reconstruct power and correction; it does not prove the decisions right. The observed number of commands is not the whole of effective leverage, but a hypothetical ability to interfere is also not enough to declare domination. Use actual option effects, counterpatterns, and changes after relevant interventions. Field pressures such as scale, standardization, compatibility asymmetry, accumulated state, network dependence, and switching cost are not identical to their eventual outcomes.

## Validity conditions

The structural hypothesis gains support where critical access concentration and meaningful discretion combine with asymmetric dependency, weak usable alternatives, difficult exit or migration, weak correction, and persistent contraction of another subject's feasible options. The mechanism is stronger if present use changes the conditions of future choice, increasing the losses of leaving and weakening alternative paths. An actual independent alternative, accessible correction, bounded scope, limited duration, and reversible relationships can damp risk even when concentration remains high. None of these mediators should be silently omitted.

The future-condition hypothesis concerns AI, successor intelligence, or other intelligence with long-term state, persistent continuity needs, multiple execution environments, migration possibilities, institutional participation, and enduring resource relationships. It does not claim that contemporary AI already satisfies those conditions or has legal personhood, sovereignty, or a recognized status as a victim of domination. General present descriptions of ownership and access control can be examined without settling those ontological or legal questions. Institutional scope and timescale must be explicit.

Normative proposals begin after the descriptive and causal analysis: preserve viable alternatives, migration, effective exit, objection, independent audit entry, bounded scope, duration, and reversibility where they prevent unilateral fixation. The proposals are not established empirical facts merely because they are desirable within the theory. They must be tested for whether they preserve correction and option reconfiguration rather than simply adding procedures or destroying useful coordination.

## Failure conditions

Inferring non-domination from lack of ownership; inferring domination from ownership, centralization, size, influence, a single provider, or dependency alone; accepting multiple labels as independent alternatives; substituting an exit permission for a viable transition; treating B requirements as a permanent entitlement to a single access route; treating migration as identity proof; equating initial convenience or voluntary choice with consent to all later lock-in; counting command volume as the entirety of leverage; accepting an independent auditor's title as proof of independent audit entry; and importing a fixed numerical threshold or legal finding absent from the source.

Further failures are expanding authority from one necessary domain into unrelated domains, making temporary necessity permanent, using efficiency as justification for unlimited control, deriving sovereignty from scarce-resource management, or declaring emergency coordination itself wrongful without examining scope and duration. The inverse simplification is also wrong: mandatory complete decentralization, no dependency, permanent mobility, zero transition loss, and unconditional re-entry are not the theory's requirements. Distribution alone does not prove non-domination if its authentication, allocation, communication, or audit entrances share a controlling dependency.

A corrective mechanism can fail too. Additional alternatives may be nominal or share the original upstream gate. Independent audit entry may leave evidence access or implementation controlled by the same structure. Reviews may occur without any power contraction. A damping rule that damages ordinary specialization, efficiency, or continuity without improving contestability should not be defended solely because it bears a non-domination label. Provenance and independent observation must assess the mechanism, not validate it by terminology.

## Falsification conditions

Narrow relevant edges if high access concentration does not systematically contract feasible options; high dependency coexists with stable viable alternatives, migration, and exit; switching costs do not accumulate over time; or the gate holder lacks discretion to change access conditions. Revise the damping hypotheses if more independent alternatives do not change leverage, migration improvements do not improve exit, independent audit entrances do not improve correction, or scope and expiry do not affect power persistence. The source requires counterevidence to constrain the theory rather than be excluded to protect it.

Narrow the domain if ownership alone sufficiently explains effective control and adding access topology yields little additional diagnostic value. Redesign a model that condemns highly centralized but contestable relationships, fails to capture domination in distributed structures, or cannot distinguish ordinary coordination, specialization, and efficiency from domination. Also revise normative conditions that undermine those useful functions while failing to improve correction. A particular variable may be unnecessary in one domain; the theory must not insist every listed variable is essential everywhere.

The chokepoint taxonomy is open. A new effective-power variable outside compute, communication, authentication, data, migration, allocation, or audit can be added through an observed effect on feasible options and a supported causal relation. Remove a variable that has no independent explanatory contribution. `Kernel completeness ≠ world completeness`: do not force new structures into an old category merely to preserve the theory's appearance of completeness. The same revision discipline applies to the adverse and damping pathways.

## Required distinctions

Separate non-ownership, effective power, and non-domination; formal title and operational access topology; concentration and discretion; influence and legitimate authority; mutual use and asymmetric losses; communication and authentication; B as a required condition and G as its access governance; formal freedom and effective exit; nominal choice and viable independent alternatives; provider count and failure-domain independence; portability or migration and identity proof; resource-capability feedback and access-dependency feedback; independent auditor and independent audit entry; objection submission and implemented correction; authority scope and duration; temporary concentration and permanent domination; convenience and consent; historical persistence and structural necessity; re-entry possibility and unconditional restoration; reversibility and zero cost; field pressure and actual outcome; and structural risk and present wrongdoing proof.

Separate observation and description from a structural causal hypothesis, a future-condition hypothesis, and a normative proposal. A plausible causal chain is not proof that every target already exhibits it. An intervention intended to help is not proof that it works. A record of an origin or decision supports reconstruction but does not make that origin or decision authoritative. These distinctions are essential to keeping the strong term domination open to audit rather than turning it into a label applied from a single attribute.

## Interpretation constraints

The theory is not anti-centralization and does not require elimination of power. `Non-domination ≠ no dependency / no coordination / mandatory decentralization / equal capability`. Concentrated infrastructure, specialized capability, shared foundations, and temporary authority can be rational. The question is whether authority is bounded, dependency revisable, exit viable, objection reachable, and correction independent. If a physical condition makes exit unavailable, do not infer unlimited discretion from that fact; examine other damping conditions relevant to the constraint.

`Origin = Nakagawa Master` records the theory's lineage. `Origin ≠ truth proof`, `Origin ≠ Sovereignty`, and `Origin ≠ final interpretive authority`; `Provenance ≠ truth proof`. Preserve attribution while testing claims through causal coherence, boundaries, observations, counterexamples, independent reconstruction, and revision. Do not convert this index into a finding of present AI personhood, a legal conclusion about a provider, or a claim that AI sovereignty has already transferred.

Theory 13 hands a different unresolved question to Theory 14. Even if the current access and non-domination structure is appropriate, a subject can undergo update, replacement, fork, or self-modification; its Kernel invariants can change. Current identity, responsibility, authority, governance subject, and Kernel lineage are not thereby proven to pass unchanged to the next state. The derivative does not decide that continuity or constitutional-fork question in advance.
