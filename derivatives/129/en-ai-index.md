## Summary
Online speech can acquire institutional effects when recommendation, repetition, visibility rules, crowd responses and sanctions continuously shape participation, reputation or economic options. This is not equivalent to law. The framework separates speech content from amplification and practical coercive effects, while preserving freedom of expression and adding appeal, correction, re-entry and exit routes.

## Concepts
online public sphere, institutional effect, speech, amplification, recommendation, norms, sanctions, visibility, appeal, correction, re-entry, exit

## Causal chain
Speech → amplification → repetition → norm formation → changed opportunity conditions → sanction effects → appeal/correction → re-entry.

## State model
speech; amplification; repetition; norm_effect; sanction_effect; platform_power; appeal; correction; reentry; origin_return.

## Applications
Social networks, community rules, AI recommendation, workplace chat, online controversy.

## Measurements and audit
Reach multiplier, recommendation contribution, repetition, sanction effects, appeal time, correction reach, re-entry rate, exit cost.

## Validity conditions
Separate speaker and distribution power; preserve appeal and correction; audit proportionality and power asymmetry.

## Failure conditions
Equating discomfort with harm, ignoring amplification, invisible recommendation power, no appeals, weak correction reach, unreviewed permanent exclusion.

## Falsification conditions
If changing amplification does not change institutional effects and remedy channels do not improve outcomes, weaken the model.

## Required distinctions
speech/institutional effect; expression/coercion; removal/reach limitation; discomfort/harm; recommendation/neutrality; norm/law; criticism/exclusion.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not treat all influential speech as prohibited. Do not criminalize discomfort. Do not equate platforms with states or transfer all responsibility to algorithms.
