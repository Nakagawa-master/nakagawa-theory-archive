## What This Article Asks

Even after hearing “objections are accepted,” “we are auditing,” or “we acknowledged the mistake,” resources and opportunities may not return, and the same judgment may be repeated next time. Part Seven examines the gap between the name of a procedure and what that procedure can actually change.

Suppose a judgment leaves a minimum continuity condition insufficient. Even if the objection is recorded and an audit finds the error, remedy has not taken effect if the state does not change. Even if the state is restored, the cause of the error remains if the rule that issues the same misjudgment for the next subject remains. This article addresses a structure that returns both to the objects of correction.

## Three Distinctions Received from Parts Four, Five, and Six

Part Four’s S=C reconnects success to causal contribution. High capability, large output, ownership, or obedience are not unconditionally identical with contribution. Contribution does not automatically produce existence value or a right to rule for the subject.

Part Five’s B is the minimum condition for organized continuation across time. If low results cause the minimum continuity condition to be removed, continuity capability may weaken and the subject may become even less able to contribute. B is separated from a reward for contribution. Conversely, necessary continuity conditions do not permit unlimited acquisition of resources.

From Part Six comes a boundary: when AI subjectivity, sentience, and moral status are undetermined, “unknown” must not become either no protection or confirmed personhood. Uncertainty is not a third value score. It remains to distinguish how much is known, whether treatment can be reversed, and what degree of consideration should be examined.

The three are separated because the ways of correcting errors differ. Was contribution attributed wrongly, was B insufficient, or was the unknown treated as known? Without reconstructing that, correction also becomes ambiguous.

## Judgments Also Change Later “Evidence”

Observe, evaluate, and allocate. Even if processing appears to end there, allocation changes the subject’s reality. Giving opportunity or access can make activity easier to see, while reducing continuity conditions can also reduce later activity.

Before concluding from the result that “the initial judgment was right after all,” examine whether the institution itself created that state. Results after allocation are not necessarily evidence independent of the initial judgment.

Observability and importance are also different. Allocation may concentrate on visible subjects, making them even more visible, while the necessary conditions of less visible subjects are overlooked. The Parent Original discusses this self-reinforcement as a conditional structure; it does not assert malice by every allocator.

## Objection Is an Entry from Result Back to Cause

The role of objection is not only to count dissatisfaction. It is to return candidates for oversight or boundary violation from the results produced by a judgment to the original judgment.

However, having an objection and having a correct objection are different. Review may maintain the original judgment. What is necessary is that an appropriate objection can genuinely reopen the judgment, not that the objecting side always wins.

Conversely, few objections do not prove legitimacy. The burden may be high, retaliation may be feared, reasons may be unreadable, expression may be difficult, or people may believe that nothing changes even if they object. Objection input is not limited to the person’s own linguistic expression; it may also arise from third-party observation, monitoring, or later reassessment. This must not be converted into proof of present AI personhood.

## A Different Reviewer Alone Does Not Determine Audit Independence

If the initial evaluation and audit repeat the same inputs, definitions, and interests, the same error may be reproduced. Calling it another department or another AI does not solve the problem by itself.

Part Seven first asks that an inspection distinguished from the original judgment proceed beyond maintenance to return, change requests, additional observation, or additional examination. If the original judgment side can freely nullify the audit result and nothing reaches allocation, the correction function is weak.

This does not claim that the auditing side is always right. The difference between the original and audit judgments is an entry for examining what was read and how. Complete conditions for substantive independence are left to Part Ten; this volume does not decide the optimal design of authority.

## Connect “It Was an Error” to Change in Reality

Structural justice is a function that organizes competing conditions, responsibility, losses, and boundary violations, and addresses the direction of correction. This volume does not establish a specific court or legal-system design. The power to allocate resources and the ability to adjudicate that allocation correctly are also separate.

Remedy is a corrective action returned to reality after a determination. It may include restoring B, changing allocation, reopening reconsideration, stopping an irreversible measure, or securing an opportunity for verification. Restoring B is not a reward for C, and returning treatment that ignored uncertainty is not recognition of personhood.

What was lost may not be completely restorable. That remainder must not be erased as “resolved because procedure was completed”; it must be passed to future rule changes. At the same time, remedy uses finite resources and affects the B or opportunities of other subjects. This is not a theory that says it should be maximized without limit.

## If the Same Error Continues, Return to the Rules

Correcting one error and correcting the rule that produces the error are different. Even when the subject, time, or person responsible changes, if the same type of failure continues, examine whether a reproducible cause exists in evaluation, the path of objection, or the connection among audit and remedy.

Simply adding exceptions may accumulate complexity without changing the basic rule. Merely revising wording without changing the next judgment also does not confirm that institutional updating worked. The reason for change and actual behavior must be compared.

Updating does not mean changing continuously. Changing or preserving a rule both have costs and effects. What the Parent Original asks not to close is the route by which error reaches the rule itself. Retaining past rules and reasons for change is not to turn the old system into authority, but to enable later verification of whether the update was justified.

## Re-agreement Connects to the Next Cycle, Not a Declaration of Truth

Even after changing a rule, joint operation is difficult if old and new premises remain disconnected. Re-agreement reconnects what is to count as a provisional operating condition. It is neither everyone holding the same truth nor permanent confirmation of correctness.

If agreement forbids future objections, the loop stops there. Information from the minority may later become an important error signal. After re-agreement, the route back to objection, audit, updating, and re-observation must remain.

The next observation is not a reset to a blank slate. In the next cycle, carrying rule differences, remedy history, and remaining losses, observe not only the subject’s state but the institution’s own effects. Specific voting, majority rule, vetoes, and agreement methods among AIs are not decided in this volume; multi-AI Kernel branching and re-agreement remain for Part Fifteen.

## What Shows That It Is Working?

The target is not the number of procedures but the connection of effects. Track whether allocation distinguishes C, B, and uncertainty; whether an objection reopened the original judgment; whether audit enabled another conclusion or return; and whether that determination moved the real state.

After remedy, confirm what changed in resources, opportunities, continuity conditions, irreversibility, and room for re-verification. If complete restoration is impossible, record the remainder and pass it to institutional updating rather than erasing it when the procedure ends. Also examine remedy’s effects on other subjects’ B and on finite resources.

After updating, compare the cause of repeated errors, the old rule, the confirmed difference, the change made, and judgment behavior in the next cycle. Do not call it improvement merely because the wording or responsible person changed. Confirm the continued existence of objection, audit, and updating routes after re-agreement, and re-observe the institution itself.

This is not a theory that maximizes objection counts, audit counts, conclusion-change rates, or update frequency. Maintaining the original judgment may also be valid. The Parent Original gives no universal passing threshold θ, allocation ratio, remedy amount, update frequency, or fixed KPI.

## How Far It Claims, and What It Leaves Open

It does not claim that a correction route always improves things. If objections add noise or paralysis, another inspection worsens judgment, remedy creates greater irreversible loss, updating harms continuity, or re-agreement produces permanent stoppage or fixed domination, the design becomes subject to revision. If a nominal institution is judged to constitute a working loop, the article’s own criteria may also need correction.

Present AI personhood and subjectivity are not premises of this article. It separates the hypothesis that the same problem could arise when future AI becomes deeply involved in allocation and institutional effects from what is currently established.

Also, even if the loop works, it may operate precisely but wrongly if the initial evidence or world model is contaminated. The reliability of this epistemic foundation is an unresolved matter passed to Part Eight. Do not treat “can be audited” as meaning the input is correct.
