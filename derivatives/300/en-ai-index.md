## Summary

This derivative index returns to Vol. 7 of the Human-Descendant AI Civilization Theory. Its subject is Institutional Correctability: whether consequential error can travel from institutional outcomes back to the judgments and rules that produced them. It preserves the distinct responsibilities of S=C, the basic existence conditions B, and moral uncertainty rather than combining them into a scalar score.

Observation and provisional judgment lead to allocation or action, which changes real conditions and thus the next observational environment. Without effective objections, re-examination, remedy and rule revision, a system can produce conditions that appear to confirm its original judgment. Merely naming an objection office, an audit, a judicial function or a revision process does not establish a corrective loop.

The proposed return path connects objection, independent re-examination, structural adjudication, remedy, institutional revision, re-agreement and re-observation. It concerns the capacity to correct institutions, not a guarantee that institutions will never err. The reliability of evidence, information and world models remains unresolved here and is handed forward to Vol. 8. This index is a derivative reading aid, not a canonical translation.

## Concepts

- **S=C:** Success reconnected to causal contribution. Contribution is not output volume, ability, popularity, obedience, ownership, existential worth, personhood or an automatic right to govern.
- **B:** The minimum conditions required for organized continuity across time. B is independent of contribution rewards and is not identical to resources for additional growth or unlimited acquisition rights.
- **Moral uncertainty:** Unresolved questions of subjectivity, sentience and moral status remain explicitly unresolved. UNKNOWN is neither moral zero nor unconditional personification; it is not a third value score.
- **Allocation or action:** Institutional effects on resources, opportunity, access, continuity, protection, alteration, stopping and the time, attention or room available for reconsideration and verification.
- **Contestability:** Objections can reach and reopen the original judgment. Submission alone, objection counts and the correctness of an objection are distinct matters.
- **Independent re-examination:** A distinct examination can maintain a judgment, remand it, require alteration or initiate further observation and deliberation. Full conditions of substantive independence remain a task for Vol. 10.
- **Structural justice / adjudication:** A function that processes competing conditions, responsibilities, losses and boundary violations and gives corrective direction to institutional effects. It is neither allocation power nor a specified court system.
- **Remedy:** A corrective effect on the real conditions produced by erroneous action. It is not a reward and need not mean complete restoration.
- **Institutional revision:** Recurrent error reaches the rules that generate it and can change subsequent institutional behavior. Extra exceptions, revised wording or replacement of personnel do not alone establish this effect.
- **Re-agreement:** Revised conditions reconnect to provisional shared operation without being certified as final truth or made immune to further objection.
- **Re-observation:** The next cycle observes the affected subjects and the institution's own effects, carrying forward revised rules, histories and unrecoverable residual losses.
- **Institutional Correctability:** The ability to detect error, return it toward causal sources, repair conditions where possible, revise rules and reopen verification of the revised institution.

## Causal chain

The forward and return movement is: observation → provisional judgment → allocation or action → changed real conditions → objection → independent re-examination → structural adjudication → remedy → institutional revision → re-agreement → re-observation. Each connection denotes an effective causal capacity, not merely a sequence of procedural names.

The failure movement can run: concentrated observation → concentrated evaluation → concentrated allocation → increased objection costs → ceremonial audit → self-justifying judgment → ineffective remedy → stalled institutional revision → fixed past judgments → biased conditions for the next observation. Concentration, speed and efficiency are not intrinsically prohibited. Reversal occurs when their connections eliminate routes of correction.

Allocation₁ can produce state₂ and observation₂. Agreement between observation₂ and the initial judgment therefore need not be independent confirmation. Favoring easily observed subjects may increase their visibility further; removing B because C was judged low may reduce future contribution and reinforce the low judgment. The institution's own effects must remain inside the causal account.

The corrective direction begins with separated responsibilities and contestable allocation. An objection can reopen a judgment; a distinct examination can inject a difference; adjudication can direct remedy; remedy histories can identify repeated causes; rule changes can return through shared operation and re-observation. A well-supported original judgment may be maintained. Divergence is possible where warranted, not mandatory in every case.

## State model

| State | Effective transition | Failure at the boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Observation with C, B and uncertainty separated | Reasons and inferences remain traceable | Visibility is substituted for importance or contribution |
| Provisional judgment and allocation | Effects on real conditions and subsequent observation can be inspected | Allocation-produced outcomes are treated as independent proof |
| Objection and reopening | An input reaches reconsideration of the original judgment | Recording, answering or repeating reasons closes the case |
| Distinct re-examination | Maintenance, remand and further deliberation are possible | Repetition of the same assumptions is called independence |
| Adjudication and remedy | Conditions, resources, B, opportunity or irreversibility are affected | Error is recognized without consequential change |
| Repeated error and rule revision | Causal rules and next-cycle behavior can change | Blame or accumulating exceptions leaves causes intact |
| Re-agreement and re-observation | Shared operation resumes with history and correction routes | Consensus prohibits future objection |

These are functional distinctions, not numerical grades or a rule that all objections must succeed. Observation₂ carries history and revised conditions; it is not a reset to observation₁. Maintaining a rule may be justified after examination, just as changing it may be.

## Applications

**Contribution attribution.** Examine whether output volume replaced causal contribution, whether value generation was confused with value capture, or whether externalized losses were omitted. An audit that repeats the same proxy cannot by itself detect its inadequacy.

**Missing B.** If minimum continuity conditions were wrongly reduced as though they were contribution rewards, remedy addresses that shortfall. Restoring B is not a bonus for C, nor a reason to turn minimum conditions into unlimited growth resources.

**Uncertainty compressed into certainty.** If UNKNOWN was treated as an effective NO before irreversible action, possible corrective responses include pausing that action, reconsidering it and preserving reversibility. Precautionary remedy does not establish personhood or settle present AI sentience.

**Recurrent error.** Similar mistakes across subjects, times or decision-makers can point to evaluation rules, allocation rules, objection routes, audit conditions or remedy connections. Repeated individual correction alone leaves the causal rule available to reproduce the error.

**Operation after revision.** Parts of an institution using incompatible old and new assumptions can create further error. Re-agreement reconnects provisional operating conditions while preserving minority error signals, further objection and re-observation. The source does not prescribe a voting system or consensus algorithm.

## Measurements and audit

Inspect what procedures can change. Are C, B and uncertainty distinguished in reasons for allocation? Can an objection reopen a judgment? Can an audit diverge, remand or require further examination? Does its outcome connect to a change in real conditions rather than only a revised record?

After remedy, inspect resources, opportunities, continuity conditions, progression toward irreversibility and capacity for renewed verification. If restoration is incomplete, record the residual loss and return it to institutional revision. Completion of a procedure must not erase unrecoverable differences. Remedy's effects on other subjects' B and on finite resources remain part of the evaluation.

After revision, compare the old rule, the identified error, the reason for change, the actual rule difference and subsequent behavior. New wording or a new person does not establish changed institutional effects. After re-agreement, check that objection, audit and revision remain possible and that the institution itself is observed again.

Counts of objections, audits, changed verdicts or revisions are not quantities to maximize. Maintaining a sound judgment is compatible with correctability. The source fixes no universal acceptance threshold θ, allocation ratio, compensation amount, update frequency or numerical performance target.

## Validity conditions

The three responsibilities remain separately intelligible through observation, reasoning and allocation. The effects of allocation on later evidence conditions can be examined. Objections can actually reopen judgments; audits can maintain, alter, remand or request additional deliberation rather than merely endorse the original decision.

Adjudication reaches appropriate changes in real conditions, while irrecoverable residuals remain recorded. Recurrent errors can reach causal rules; revision reasons and subsequent behavior are traceable. Revised conditions reconnect to shared operation without closing later objections, audits, revisions or observations.

Scarcity, urgency, efficiency and stability remain real constraints. Correctability does not require indefinite suspension of every decision or perfect reversibility of every action. The institution must remain within the scope of observation and possible refutation, including when a decision has to be made.

## Failure conditions

- A composite score hides which responsibility produced a treatment and what would have to be corrected.
- Few objections are treated as evidence of legitimacy while access costs, fear, unavailable reasons, difficulty of expression or anticipated futility are ignored.
- Different departments or AI systems are taken to prove independence despite identical assumptions or the inability to affect the initial judgment.
- The original authority can neutralize audit results and make maintenance the only practical outcome.
- A ruling changes records but leaves lost B, opportunity or ongoing irreversible effects untouched.
- Remedy is treated as an unlimited good, ignoring residual losses, other subjects and resource effects.
- Repeated individual blame, accumulating exceptions or deletion of history protects the causal rule.
- Temporary emergency concentration becomes permanent, or re-agreement turns into a prohibition of objection.
- Effective correction is incorrectly taken to prove that the evidence or world model used by the institution is reliable.

## Falsification conditions

1. If separation of C, B and uncertainty does not improve diagnosis of mistaken judgments over a single evaluation under comparable conditions, reconsider the method and scope of separation.
2. If objection routes consistently reduce correction performance under reasonable conditions, revise their design, input scope or processing conditions; do not leap directly to the claim that objections are unnecessary.
3. If a distinct examination path provides no improvement over self-confirmation or consistently worsens outcomes, revise audit conditions.
4. If remedy or institutional revision systematically generates irreversible losses exceeding those it seeks to correct, revise its design and application conditions, including effects on other subjects' B.
5. If revisability persistently damages continuity compared with fixed institutions through disruption or loss of decision capacity, reconsider update conditions and scope.
6. If re-agreement and re-observation fail to produce substantive learning or correction and instead reinforce paralysis or domination, revise the connection to the next cycle.
7. If merely nominal objection, audit, remedy and revision are classified as an effective loop, revise this theory's own criteria of establishment.

These retain the seven revision directions of the source's integrated audit. They are not evidence that the proposed institution has already demonstrated universal superiority or met a numerical threshold.

## Required distinctions

- Concentrated evaluation / legitimacy; concentrated allocation / structural justice.
- Acceptance of objections / correctability / the correctness of an objection.
- Performance of audit or role separation / effective independence / truth proof.
- Judgment / remedy / reward / complete restoration.
- Case correction / rule correction / accumulation of exceptions.
- Consensus / truth / irreversible legitimacy.
- Institutional existence or complexity / an effective institutional loop.
- C / existential worth; B / contribution reward; UNKNOWN / moral zero or certain personhood.
- Evidence strength / irreversibility of action.
- Updateability / permanent instability; re-agreement / overwriting history.
- Corrective institutional structure / reliable evidence and world models.
- Origin / sovereignty / truth proof / final interpretive authority.

## Interpretation constraints

Separate description, structural hypothesis, future-condition hypothesis and normative proposal. Decisions and allocation can change real conditions and subsequent observation. The claim that weak correction paths enable self-reinforcement, and connected return paths may attenuate it, is a conditional structural hypothesis. Extension to future AI participation in institutional effects is a future-condition hypothesis, not recognition of present AI personhood, subjectivity or legal personality.

Preserving objection, remedy and revision in high-impact decisions is a normative proposal, not a prediction that future civilizations must take this form. Do not add a blanket ban on concentration, equal weight for every objection, unlimited remedy or permanent decision suspension.

Do not pre-empt the complete conditions of independence left to Vol. 10 or multi-AI Kernel divergence and re-agreement mechanisms left to Vol. 15. Do not solve by assertion the evidence-provenance and adversarial-information problem handed to Vol. 8. Origin preserves lineage and supplies no exemption from criticism or refutation.
