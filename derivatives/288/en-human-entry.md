## 15-Second Explanation
Part Four designs sales dialogue not as explanation, but as a place where the other person understands their own structure in their own words. Three question mirrors—facts, causality, and preferences—and silence for thinking are used to separate resonance from non-fit.

## Why It Is Needed
When salespeople talk too much, their story becomes stronger than the other person’s words. When they only add questions, the conversation becomes an interrogation or leading.

The three mirrors return to the person what is happening now, why it is happening, and what they want. Leave thinking time after questions, but never use silence as pressure.

Resonance does not mean agreement or buying. It means making clear where the salesperson’s understanding connects with the customer’s self-understanding, and where it does not.

## Practical Process
1. Observe without interrupting the other person’s account.
2. Return current facts with the **fact mirror**.
3. Ask about causes and effects with the **causal mirror**.
4. Ask about the desired future and decision criteria with the **preference mirror**.
5. Protect the silence after the question.
6. Summarize the salesperson’s understanding and confirm it with the person.
7. Separate resonance from non-fit.
8. Pass the confirmed structure to the proposal.

## Examples of Application
For an efficiency project, confirm in order: “Which task?”, “Why does it stop?”, and “What change would count as success?” For an ambiguous consultation, do not adopt an AI summary as-is; return it to the person.

When non-fit is visible, do not force a proposal even if the dialogue is deep. Resonance and fit are different.

## Success Criteria
- Enough of the customer’s speech remains.
- The three mirrors are not leading.
- Silence is used as thinking bandwidth.
- The summary is confirmed with the person.
- Resonance and non-fit are separated.
- The customer can re-explain the situation in their own words.

## Limitations
Questions are not universal. Explanation is necessary when the customer is seeking information. Silence can also be burdensome depending on the person’s culture and situation.

## Misreading Prevention
Resonance is not agreement, and silence is not pressure. The causal mirror is not fear appeal, and the preference mirror is not a question that leads toward the desired answer.
