## 15-Second Explanation
Part Six separates sales “trust” from being liked. Trust arises when the other person can think, question, object, defer, and re-explain internally in their own words, while the salesperson’s promises, explanations, and communications remain consistently predictable.

It uses layers of questions, the fact/causal/preference mirrors, a vessel for silence, approval translation, leading by stepping back, and exit design. The aim is not to perform trust to sell, but to build a structure that protects the other person’s self-determination.

## Why It Is Needed
Likeability—getting along, being pleasant, replying quickly, or enjoying a meal—easily gets mixed with trust. Likeability can shift when a contact changes or a problem appears. Long relationships need predictable verification and a structure in which the other person remains the decision-maker.

If the salesperson explains too well, the customer may agree in the moment but fail to reconstruct the reason in their own words. If the explanation collapses when taken internally, agreement depended on the salesperson. Part Six does not call that trust.

## Three Mirrors of Trust
**Fact mirror:** confirm what is happening in the person’s own words, using observable numbers, events, and existing procedures.

**Causal mirror:** confirm why the person thinks it happened. Causes may be hypotheses, not facts, so treat them as the person’s interpretation without asserting them.

**Preference mirror:** confirm what the person wants to protect or change and which conditions come first. Do not rewrite the salesperson’s desired goal as the customer’s preference.

Separating the three prevents the salesperson’s shortcut: “the current state is bad → this is the cause → therefore this product is needed.”

## Vessel for Silence
After placing a question, leave time to think. If the salesperson immediately fills in the answer, the customer’s thinking is replaced by the salesperson’s words.

Silence is not a psychological technique. If the person seems uneasy, say, “Let’s take a moment to think.” Pressing for an answer through looks or silence is pressure, not a vessel for silence.

## Practical Process
### 1. Confirm permission for dialogue
Explain what you want to confirm and preserve the choice not to answer.

### 2. Separate fact, cause, and preference
Do not quickly compress the person’s words into one story; confirm them through the three mirrors.

### 3. Do not fill the space after a question
Leave thinking time and do not substitute the salesperson’s hypothesis for the person’s answer.

### 4. Confirm self-re-explanation
Ask whether the person can state the situation and explain it internally in their own words.

### 5. Make the salesperson predictable
State promises, contact timing, unknowns, and change conditions, and keep conduct stable.

### 6. Support approval translation
Organize purpose fit, workload, recoverability, risk, and implementation conditions for internal explanation.

### 7. Make continuation or exit possible
When there is non-fit, make passing explicit; do not make relationship continuation a contract condition.

## Examples of Application
In a new meeting, separate customer facts, causal hypotheses, and priority conditions before explaining the product. With existing customers, do not assume a long relationship proves trust; check whether changes and objections can be raised.

In B2B proposals, “I think it is good” is not enough. Check whether purpose fit, workload, and recoverability can be explained to managers, procurement, and the field.

In non-fit cases, leaving reasons and conditions for possible reconnection may protect long-term trust better than pursuing aggressively.

## Success Criteria
- The customer can explain reasons in their own words.
- Objection, deferral, and passing do not damage the relationship.
- Promises, explanations, communications, and change conditions are predictable.
- Fact, cause, and preference are not mixed.
- Purpose fit, workload, and recoverability can be re-explained internally.
- Proposal reasons and reservations remain even when the salesperson is absent.
- Exit is possible when there is non-fit.
- The structure carrying trust survives a contact change.

## Limitations
More questions do not automatically create trust. Too many become interrogation; too much silence creates anxiety. The right interval depends on culture, role, and time constraints.

Even a good trust structure does not make an ill-fitting product contractable. Trust is not an excuse covering non-fit; it is a condition for saying non-fit safely.

## Misreading Prevention
“Lead by stepping back” is not a tactic: do not delay replies deliberately, create scarcity, or make the other person anxious. It means not taking away their conclusion after providing decision material.

“Exit design” is not a technique for screening and discarding prospects. It protects both boundaries and the possibility of future reconnection without forcing an ill-fitting relationship.

The minimum core is **treating trust not as likeability, but as a reproducible relationship structure including self-explanation, predictability, objection, and exit**.
