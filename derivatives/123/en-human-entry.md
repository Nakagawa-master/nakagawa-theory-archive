## 15-Second Explanation
When “It is unpleasant, so I want it banned immediately” directly summons penal power, law approaches becoming a device that reflects collective feeling rather than actual harm. The original does not ignore discomfort, disgust, or fear. Before handing them to public authority, it places two lines—Correspondence and Temporal Ethics—and asks whether the danger being described corresponds to actual harm, and whether a beat for verification, deliberation, and proportionality has been secured before punishment.

## Why This Matters
Criminal punishment is one of the strongest interventions the state makes into an individual’s life. Before considering punishment, the legal starting point referenced by the parent original checks whether another person’s rights or interests—the legally protected interest—are actually infringed, or whether there is a serious risk of infringement, and whether harm cannot be prevented by means other than punishment. Many people feeling “disgusted” or “morally unable to accept” something is not by itself an independent reason for punishment.

In an information society, feelings spread faster than deliberation or verification. “I dislike it” becomes “It is dangerous,” and “I am afraid” becomes “It should be a crime,” while institutions are pulled along by that speed. The core question is how to separate this immediacy of feeling from public coercive power once, then return it to an actual structure.

This does not exclude discomfort or fear as worthless emotions. Feelings can signal friction, anxiety, or unease somewhere in society. But whether that ignition point may connect directly to punishment is a separate question: the story told by feeling must be compared with the actual structure of harm or danger.

## Two Filters
### 1. Correspondential Verification
Correspondence checks whether “the danger being described” corresponds to “actual structural harm.” If “I am uncomfortable” becomes “It is dangerous,” feeling and structure do not correspond unless that danger is connected to an actual infringement of a legally protected interest or a serious risk of infringement.

Without this line, law becomes a mirror of majority feeling instead of an institution that organizes actual harm. The original distinguishes discomfort from danger, moral disgust from infringement of a legally protected interest, and symbolic hurt from structural harm, and asks whether a story of feeling is overwriting reality.

### 2. Temporal Calibration
Temporal Ethics is not simply “think carefully.” It is an institutional beat that prevents public authority from being connected directly when anger or fear is strongest.

The original requires time to ask whether an act will recur, whether harm will accumulate, and whether alternatives to punishment have truly been exhausted. Its theory-signature section places Temporal Calibration as a sustainable rhythm that makes verification, deliberation, and proportionality possible before punishment.

Delay is not inherently good. The needed work is to verify causality, confirm the substance of harm, compare intervention strength, and examine long-term institutional coherence—not merely wait for emotion to weaken.

## Delayed Justice Architecture
The parent original calls the structure integrating Correspondential Verification and Temporal Calibration **Delayed Justice Architecture**.

It does not mean that slower justice is always better. It embeds verifiable delay between feeling and punishment to prevent a short circuit. Before converting social feeling into coercive power, it checks actual harm, danger, recurrence, accumulation, alternatives, proportionality, and sustained coherence.

The original calls the short circuit in which public feeling directly activates punitive action without verification **ethical short-circuiting**. In a digital society, anger, disgust, and fear spread faster than legal reason, making this a major risk of contemporary governance.

## Points for Practice
**1. Criminalization demands.** When someone says “That is disgusting” or “I want it gone,” first check for an actual infringement of a legally protected interest or a serious danger. Do not equate feeling’s strength with danger’s size.

**2. Outrage and legislation.** When social media or reporting rapidly amplifies feeling, do not convert diffusion speed directly into regulatory need; check whether room for verification remains.

**3. Moral disgust.** Even where many find an act unpleasant, separately check whether structural harm exists that requires penal power. The size of moral disgust alone is not a reason for punishment.

**4. Alternatives.** Even when harm is confirmed, ask whether it truly cannot be prevented by means other than punishment. Separate immediate emotional satisfaction from structurally necessary intervention.

**5. Rechecking existing institutions.** At fixed points, check how often discomfort has become a direct reason for regulation or enforcement and whether room for verification is shrinking. Ask whether the institution is becoming a proxy for crowd feeling.

## Success Criteria
If the original’s structure works, social feeling is received rather than ignored, but does not directly lead to punishment. The described danger corresponds to actual structural harm, and a temporal beat remains for checking recurrence, accumulation, alternatives, and proportionality before punishment.

The integrated audit summary defines A as translating “Please ban it because it is unpleasant” into institutional language and bundling and defining it through Correspondence and Temporal Ethics. B institutionalizes, stabilizes, and aligns public intervention around structural necessity and sustained coherence rather than emotional reaction.

Re-agreement conditions are an audit cycle, T/S/R, and a public audit bundle. T is Correspondence, S is structural sustainability, and R is recursion. The grounds for intervention must be explainable externally, and the institution must be revisable in light of operational results.

## Falsification / Revision
If phenomenon M is confirmed—where the frequency with which “discomfort” is accepted as a direct basis for legislation or enforcement exceeds threshold `θ`, and the observation window `δ` for verification closes—the original rejects or revises hypothesis A: “emotional demands can be structurally attenuated and channelled.”

One demand based on discomfort is not failure. The issue is sustained institutional bypassing of Correspondence and Temporal Ethics, increasing direct conversion of immediate demands into public coercive power, and the resulting impossibility of verification. Because the parent original fixes no concrete values for `θ` or `δ`, this page adds no arbitrary percentage or period.

## Limitations
This is neither a text treating discomfort as worthless nor a categorical rejection of criminalization. Nor is it merely the legal maxim that criminal law is a last resort. Its center is an ethical infrastructure embedding Correspondence and Temporal Ethics between feeling and public authority.

Delayed Justice Architecture does not specify a waiting period or sunset system. It is structural delay that makes verification, deliberation, and proportionality possible, handling the time beat needed by each subject.

## Misreading Prevention
Do not turn this into a theory that ignores discomfort. Feeling can be an ignition point, but it is not a sufficient condition for punishment.

Do not turn this into a theory that categorically rejects criminalization. It does not exclude intervention where actual infringement or serious danger exists and cannot be prevented by other means.

Do not replace the original’s center with a general “alternative-means check,” “minority chilling KPI,” “AI does not make legal judgments,” or “legal consultation disclaimer.”

Preserve Correspondence, Temporal Ethics, Correspondential Verification, Temporal Calibration, Delayed Justice Architecture, ethical short-circuiting, T/S/R, `θ`, and `δ` as in the original.
