## Structural role
The theory decomposes expectations, concerns, role assumptions, and condition awareness formed at first contact into six layers: existence, meaning, conditions, evidence, confirmation, and memory. It prevents impressions from becoming implicit consent and makes continued decisions reproducible.

## Central proposition
```text
contact
→ first impression and provisional expectations
→ disclose conditions, responsibilities, and evidence
→ confirm understanding in the other party's own words
→ separate agreement, non-agreement, and hold
→ preserve a jointly rereadable record
→ reconfirm, revise, or withdraw at the next touchpoint
→ repeatable consensus memory
```

## Core concepts
- first impression as a provisional consensus signal
- consensus memory
- existence / meaning / condition / evidence / confirmation / memory layers
- explicit non-agreement and hold states
- revision and withdrawal
- protection under power asymmetry
- joint rereadability

## Required distinctions
- impression design vs impression manipulation
- consensus memory vs convenient memory fixation
- limited agreement vs comprehensive consent
- silence vs consent
- reassurance vs suspension of judgment
- record vs surveillance
- continuation vs lock-in
- reconfirmation vs forced convergence

## Validity conditions
Questions, refusal, hold, and correction must remain free. Costs, deadlines, responsibilities, disadvantages, non-applicable conditions, and withdrawal paths must be visible. First-impression hypotheses must not be treated as facts. Records must be jointly rereadable. Power asymmetry requires cooling time or third-party confirmation.

## Failure / non-applicable conditions
Failure includes consent obtained through charm, authority, fear, or haste; treating silence or nodding as consent; delayed disclosure of conditions; using records to bind or blame; inconsistent explanations across touchpoints; no withdrawal path; and repeated failure to reproduce understanding after reconfirmation.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce this theory to sales closing, psychological manipulation, implicit consent, or responsibility shifting. Do not reuse records for ranking or surveillance. Do not convert one-time consent into permanent consent.
