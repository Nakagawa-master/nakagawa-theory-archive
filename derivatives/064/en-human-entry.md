## 15-Second Explanation
This approach does not end a first impression with “It felt good” or “No one objected.” It turns it into a memory that can later be jointly checked: what was understood, what was agreed to in a limited way, what was deferred or refused, and when it can be corrected or withdrawn.

## Why It Is Necessary
At first contact, many assumptions arise about expectations, anxiety, roles, responsibility, and conditions. When those assumptions are treated as facts or agreement, gaps arise: “I did not understand it that way,” “I do not remember agreeing,” or “I was not told that condition.”

Consensus design prioritizes separating assumptions from facts, disclosing conditions and what remains undecided, and confirming understanding in the other party’s own words, rather than making the first impression look good.

## Six Layers
- **Existence**: Who handles what, from which position and with what responsibility.
- **Meaning**: Whether important terms and purposes are understood with the same meaning.
- **Conditions**: Cost, deadline, role, non-applicability, risk, disadvantage, and withdrawal conditions.
- **Evidence**: Materials, histories, actions, and primary logs that connect statements with reality.
- **Confirmation**: Whether the other party can express understanding, questions, refusal, and deferral in their own words.
- **Memory**: Whether agreement, non-agreement, deferral, change, and the next confirmation point remain available for rereading.

The six layers are not evaluation scores. They are a classification for checking where understanding or agreement has diverged.

## Process of Execution
```text
Contact
→ Observe first impressions and assumptions
→ Make purpose, role, and responsibility explicit
→ Disclose conditions, disadvantages, non-applicability, and routes for withdrawal
→ Confirm understanding in the other party’s own words
→ Separate agreement, non-agreement, and deferral
→ Set the next confirmation point
→ Correct or withdraw if necessary
→ A memory of agreement that can be reread jointly
```

## Examples
### Business Meeting
Do not turn “I am interested” into an intention to contract. Separate and record what interests the person and what the budget, approval, comparison, and deferral conditions are.

### Recruitment and Collaboration
Separate a favorable impression from role fit, and confirm results, authority, support, evaluation, compensation, and withdrawal conditions.

### Meetings
Do not treat the absence of objections as agreement. Separate agreement, non-opposition, deferral, unconfirmed status, and opposition.

### AI Collaboration
Separate AI proposals, human judgment, approval, execution, and audit, and do not convert fluent generated text into human approval.

## How to Measure
One can observe reproducibility of understanding, condition mismatches, visibility of deferral and refusal, reconfirmation, attribution of responsibility, explanations across touchpoints, mistaken agreement, complaints, and rework.

However, numbers showing faster agreement, less deferral, or fewer complaints alone do not indicate success. If questions and refusals have become harder to express, the design has instead deteriorated.

## Preventing Misreadings
**This is not a theory for manipulating first impressions.** It is not a way to stop judgment through likability, authority, fear, or rushing.

**Silence and nodding are not agreement.** Treat them as unconfirmed.

**Records are not for binding people.** They are kept for joint confirmation, correction, and withdrawal.

**One act of agreement is not permanent.** Reconfirm when conditions change.

## When There Is a Power Imbalance
When the difference in position is large, a formal “yes” may not express free will. Protect free non-agreement by providing cooling-off time, third-party confirmation, anonymous questions, and a prohibition on disadvantage for refusal.

## Boundaries and Limitations
- In life-threatening or safety emergencies, an immediate decision may be necessary, but examine the grounds and responsibility afterward.
- For consent in legal, medical, employment, and other contexts, prioritize applicable laws, ethics, and duties of explanation.
- Do not use records for surveillance, personnel ranking, or shifting responsibility.
- Do not turn reconfirmation into pressure to reach the same conclusion.
- In relationships where withdrawal or correction is substantially impossible, do not regard formal agreement as free will.
