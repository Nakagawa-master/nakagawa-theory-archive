## Summary
Kyoken is a closed local order that appears when a society has lost the capacity to rebuild whole-society consensus. Instead of collapsing immediately into disorder, the society contracts into smaller spheres where vocabulary, interests, norms, enemies, memories, and meanings remain mutually intelligible. Within these spheres, fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia are redeployed at local scale. Local order can therefore persist even while shared reality and whole-society coordination deteriorate.

The core is not physical isolation but a self-justifying loop. Internal evaluations are supported by internal vocabulary; vocabulary is reinforced by internal experience; experience is interpreted through internal evaluation. This lowers the cost of local stability, but it also reduces semantic compatibility with outsiders, weakens common responsibility and common institutions, and erodes the capacity for whole-society redesign. Kyoken keeps civilization moving locally without repairing it globally, while inter-sphere friction, resource conflict, responsibility gaps, and semantic incommensurability accumulate toward the next phase transition.

## Applications
- Examine whether departments or organizations use internally coherent vocabularies that cannot be translated across boundaries.
- Distinguish substantive disagreement from cases where affiliation vocabulary is judged before the argument itself.
- Examine whether recommendation and rapid-response systems reinforce local vocabulary, emotion, and enemy/friend structures.
- Audit whether measures that improve local stability reduce external explainability, appeal channels, shared responsibility, or inter-sphere translation.
- Track whether resource rivalry, responsibility externalization, and semantic incompatibility are accumulating toward a critical transition.

## Measurements and audit
- Availability of shared vocabulary, explanation, objection handling, and re-consensus routes across the whole.
- Degree to which the scale of order formation has contracted from whole society to local spheres.
- Gap between internal intelligibility and external explainability.
- Whether external objections can trigger revision or are treated mainly as noise/interference.
- Local bundling of fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia.
- Whether internal failures are systematically attributed to outsiders or external conditions.
- Simultaneous change in local stability and whole-society reconnection/redesign capacity.
- Whether recommendation, repetition, and rapid reaction prioritize affiliation confirmation over verification.
- Accumulation of inter-sphere friction, resource conflict, responsibility gaps, and semantic incommensurability.

## Validity conditions
- Whole-society consensus and reconnection capacity are degraded.
- Maintaining shared vocabulary and justification across the whole has become costly.
- Sharing a narrower vocabulary, interests, norms, and enemy/friend model is cheaper and faster locally.
- Meaning, evaluation, responsibility, benefit allocation, and surveillance norms circulate internally.
- Fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia are redeployed for local order maintenance.
- Routes that translate external objections and standards back into internal revision become thin.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing kyoken to generic polarization or information bias.
- Equating kyoken with community, friendship groups, or interest groups.
- Treating closure as physical isolation only.
- Assuming kyoken must be centrally designed by malicious actors.
- Equating local stability with whole-society repair or regeneration.
- Confusing responsibility evaporation with externalization of responsibility.
- Replacing the digital argument with generic echo-chamber language.

## Falsification conditions
A kyoken-centered explanation weakens when local spheres remain explainable to materially different outsiders, incorporate external objections into revision, and can rebuild shared vocabulary, responsibility, and institutions. The model also requires revision if stronger local order reliably increases inter-sphere translation, shared responsibility, and re-consensus instead of reducing whole-society reconnection capacity. Do not add fixed numerical thresholds not present in the parent.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not turn kyoken into a moral claim that closed groups are simply bad. The parent treats closure as a structural self-preservation response that can emerge when whole-society connection capacity has degraded. “What works internally” must not be reframed as conscious lying by members. Do not label specific real-world groups as kyoken without separate evidence, and do not invent a kyoken score, fixed KPI, threshold, or duration.
