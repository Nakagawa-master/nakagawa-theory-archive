## 15-Second Explanation
A shared sphere is a local order in which, when society as a whole can no longer remake consensus, people shrink into a narrow sphere where they can share mutually intelligible vocabulary, gains and losses, norms, and sides of friend and enemy instead of falling into disorder, and circulate legitimacy only within it.

By relocating fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia inside the sphere, a shared sphere can create local stability at low cost. But that stability does not advance reconnection or redesign of society as a whole. Rather, it thins shared vocabulary, shared responsibility, and shared institutions, weakening the capacity for a full restart.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
When society as a whole can no longer maintain one shared reality, the cost of creating an “explanation understood by everyone” rises. Using an explanation that works only within a narrow range is cheaper, faster, and stable for the time being than connecting heterogeneous positions.

Thus, in endurance civilization, order does not disappear; the unit of order formation becomes smaller. People retreat not to society as a whole, but to spheres where their vocabulary works, their pain is understood, friend and enemy are clear, and profit and norms can be read.

The problem with a shared sphere is not that “closed-off people are bad.” It is that society’s overall capacity for connection has weakened so far that even local order is difficult to maintain without closing off. Because shared spheres can arise naturally as adaptations to a degraded environment, moral arguments alone lose sight of the structure.

## Practical Process
**1. Examine overall connection capacity.** Confirm whether shared vocabulary and explanations connecting different positions, as well as routes for handling objections and remaking consensus, are functioning.

**2. Examine the unit of order formation.** See whether order has closed within a department, group, network, or sphere rather than society as a whole.

**3. Examine the self-justifying cycle.** See whether evaluations inside the sphere are supported by its vocabulary, that vocabulary is reinforced by experiences inside the sphere, and legitimacy circulates without external verification.

**4. Examine the bundle of five elements.** Confirm whether fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia have been bundled for maintaining local order.

**5. Examine vocabulary compatibility.** See whether words with the same form fail to carry meaning outside the sphere, and whether external objections become noise unless translated into the sphere’s vocabulary.

**6. Examine the flow of responsibility.** See whether internal failures are not reconsidered as problems of internal design, but are sent one-way toward outsiders, external conditions, or interference.

**7. Examine local stability and full-restart capacity together.** Confirm whether, while stability inside the sphere increases, society’s overall ability to translate, share responsibility, and remake consensus is weakening.

## Examples of Application
**Organizations.** When the same vocabulary is used with different meanings by each department and objections from another department are turned into noise as “not understanding the field,” examine whether local ordering is present.

**Public debate.** See whether “which side’s vocabulary was used” is evaluated before the content of the other person’s claim, and whether rebuttal is converted into confirmation of affiliation.

**Digital spaces.** See whether recommendation and rapid reaction repeat existing vocabulary, emotion, and friend-enemy distinctions, prioritizing immediate declarations of affiliation over verification.

**Institutional design.** See whether measures that increase local operating efficiency or stability reduce external explainability, objection handling, or translation between spheres.

## Success Criteria
- The existence of local order and society-wide consensus-forming capacity can be observed separately.
- Applicability inside the sphere and universal explainability can be distinguished.
- The sphere’s self-justifying cycle and external verifiability can be compared.
- The local relocation of fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia can be confirmed as a bundle.
- Evaporation of responsibility and externalization of responsibility outside the sphere can be distinguished.
- The effect of local stability on society-wide reconnection capacity can be evaluated at the same time.

## Limitations
A shared sphere is not a name for closed groups in general, communities in general, or groups of companions in general. It addresses a local-order structure in which internal legitimacy is maintained only through circulation within the sphere and connections of meaning, evaluation, and responsibility with the outside become thin.

Nor can merely seeing the same opinions in a digital environment be called a shared sphere. The original focuses not only on information bias, but on localization of a meaning space that includes the operating environment of vocabulary, recognition, friend and enemy, responsibility, and reaction speed.

## Preventing Misreading
- Do not reduce a shared sphere to mere “division” or an “echo chamber.”
- Do not equate it with a community, mutual aid, or a group of companions.
- Do not judge closure only by physical entry and exit.
- Do not assume that members inside the sphere know that something is false.
- Do not equate local stability with repair or regeneration of society as a whole.
- Do not apply assertions absent from the original to a particular real-world group.
