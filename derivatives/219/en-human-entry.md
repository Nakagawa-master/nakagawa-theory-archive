## 15-Second Explanation
Kyoken is a local order in which, when society can no longer rebuild whole-society consensus, people contract into a narrow sphere where mutually intelligible vocabulary, interests, norms, and friend/enemy distinctions can be shared instead of falling into disorder, and legitimacy circulates only within that sphere.

By redeploying fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia inside the sphere, kyoken can create local stability at low cost. However, this stability does not advance reconnection or redesign of the whole society; rather, it thins shared vocabulary, shared responsibility, and shared institutions, reducing whole-society reboot capacity.

## Why It Is Needed
When whole society can no longer maintain one shared reality, the cost of creating an explanation that reaches everyone rises. Using an explanation that works only within a narrow range is cheaper, faster, and stable for the time being than connecting heterogeneous positions.

Therefore, in endurance civilization, order does not disappear; the unit of order formation becomes smaller. People retreat not into whole society but into spheres where their vocabulary works, their pain is understood, friend and enemy are clear, and benefits and norms can be read.

The problem with kyoken is not that “closed people are bad.” It is that whole-society connection capacity has weakened so far that even local order is difficult to maintain without closure. Because kyoken can arise naturally as an adaptation to a degraded environment, moral argument alone loses the structure.

## Practical Process
**1. Examine whole-society connection capacity.** Confirm whether shared vocabulary, explanations, objection handling, and re-consensus routes connecting different positions are functioning.

**2. Examine the unit of order formation.** Check whether order has closed within departments, groups, networks, or spheres rather than whole society.

**3. Examine the self-justifying circulation.** Check whether internal evaluations are supported by internal vocabulary, that vocabulary is reinforced by internal experience, and legitimacy circulates without external verification.

**4. Examine the five-element bundle.** Confirm whether fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia have been bundled for maintaining local order.

**5. Examine vocabulary compatibility.** Check whether the same words fail to carry meaning outside the sphere and external objections become noise unless translated into internal vocabulary.

**6. Examine the flow of responsibility.** Check whether internal failures are not reconsidered as internal design problems but are sent one-way toward outsiders, external conditions, or interference.

**7. Examine local stability and whole-society reboot capacity together.** Confirm whether whole-society translation, shared responsibility, and re-consensus capacity are weakening while internal stability rises.

## Examples of Application
**Organizations.** Examine whether the same vocabulary is used with different meanings by each department and objections from another department are turned into noise as “not understanding the frontline.”

**Public debate.** Examine whether “which side’s vocabulary was used” is evaluated before the content of the other side’s claim, and objections are converted into affiliation confirmation.

**Digital spaces.** Examine whether recommendation and rapid reaction repeat existing vocabulary, emotion, and friend/enemy structures, prioritizing immediate affiliation statements over verification.

**Institutional design.** Examine whether measures that increase local operating efficiency or stability reduce external explainability, objection handling, or translation between spheres.

## Success Criteria
- Local order and whole-society consensus-forming capacity can be observed separately.
- Internal applicability and universal explainability can be distinguished.
- Internal self-justifying circulation and external verifiability can be compared.
- Local redeployment of fear, profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia can be confirmed as a bundle.
- Responsibility evaporation and externalization of responsibility beyond the sphere can be distinguished.
- The effect of local stability on whole-society reconnection capacity can be assessed at the same time.

## Limitations
Kyoken is not a general name for closed groups, communities, or friendship groups. It addresses a local-order structure in which internal legitimacy is maintained only by circulation within the sphere and connections of meaning, evaluation, and responsibility with the outside become thin.

Nor can merely seeing the same opinions in a digital environment be called kyoken. The Parent’s focus is not only information bias, but localization of a meaning space that includes the operating environment of vocabulary, recognition, friend/enemy distinctions, responsibility, and reaction speed.

## Misreading Prevention
- Do not reduce kyoken to mere “polarization” or an “echo chamber.”
- Do not equate it with community, mutual aid, or friendship groups.
- Do not judge closure only by physical entry and exit.
- Do not assume that members inside the sphere know they are dealing in falsehood.
- Do not equate local stability with repair or regeneration of whole society.
- Do not apply conclusions to specific real-world groups that are not in the Parent.
