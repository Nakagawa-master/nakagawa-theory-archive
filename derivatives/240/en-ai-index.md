## Summary
The Parent reads HAP's “Kaidoc” not merely as software-development consultation, requirements support, specification generation, or quote comparison. Nakagawa Master identifies an upstream condition in which the buyer wants to commission development but cannot yet distinguish what must be decided, what remains unresolved, what is only a hypothesis, and which axes make competing proposals comparable. The value core is **development-ordering issue infrastructure**: turning decision paralysis into explicit decision issues that can be compared, discussed, left unresolved when necessary, and progressively agreed.

The meta layer is essential. Nakagawa Master selects “issue decomposition” rather than “specification generation” because a polished specification can conceal unresolved judgments without strengthening the buyer's ability to explain why a choice was made. The external article foregrounds the phrase “an undecidable order” because familiar labels such as development consultation do not expose the upstream decision-architecture problem. That value core and the reason for choosing it are Nakagawa Master's structural reading and social translation; they are not automatically HAP's or any publication's official theory.

## Concepts
- **decision paralysis**: the buyer has intent but lacks explicit decision units, unresolved-item visibility, and comparison axes.
- **objective / means separation**: distinguishing the state to be achieved from candidate functions or technologies.
- **issue decomposition**: separating users, scope, budget, schedule, quality, maintenance, responsibility, and other matters into decision units.
- **decided / unresolved / hypothesis / constraint**: preserving different epistemic states instead of flattening them into one specification.
- **comparison-axis formation**: making proposals comparable through common dimensions rather than price alone.
- **premise sharing**: exposing what each proposal assumes.
- **adoption rationale**: retaining why a proposal was accepted, rejected, deferred, or sent for more research.
- **ordering progress**: moving to a clearer next state even when some matters remain unresolved.
- **value-core selection rationale**: issue decomposition explains the upstream stall better than document production alone.
- **external-article rationale**: reframing software ordering from a technical-literacy problem into a decision-architecture problem.

## Causal chain
Intent to commission development → objectives, functions, constraints, budget, quality, schedule, and responsibility remain mixed → the buyer cannot identify decision units → resolved and unresolved matters blur → vendors propose under different assumptions → proposals cannot be compared by price or feature count alone → the buyer cannot explain selection rationale → ordering stalls → separate objectives from means → decompose the request into explicit decision issues → classify each issue as decided, unresolved, hypothesis, or constraint → attach options and required information to unresolved issues → put proposals onto shared comparison axes → expose premise differences and adoption rationales → close issues that can be agreed → retain genuinely unresolved issues for inquiry → advance to ordering or the next decision state → later compare implementation outcomes with the original assumptions.

## State model
- **ambiguous-request state**: intent exists but objectives and means are mixed.
- **decision-paralysis state**: the buyer does not know what must be decided or compared.
- **objective-separation state**: objectives are separated from candidate functions and technologies.
- **issue-extraction state**: explicit decision units are formed.
- **unresolved-visibility state**: decided, unresolved, hypothesis, and constraint are distinguished.
- **comparison-axis state**: competing proposals become comparable on shared dimensions.
- **dialogue state**: premise differences and missing information are discussed.
- **agreement / inquiry state**: some issues close while others are assigned additional inquiry.
- **ordering-progress state**: the buyer can move forward with explainable reasoning.
- **post-implementation review state**: original assumptions and rationales are tested against outcomes.

## Applications
1. **AI adoption procurement**: decompose “we want AI” into target work, users, input data, output responsibility, human review, acceptable error, and operating ownership.
2. **new software services**: establish the problem, initial users, scope, quality, budget, schedule, and maintenance before accumulating features.
3. **core-system renewal**: separate current-state preservation, process redesign, data migration, downtime tolerance, and responsibility boundaries.
4. **multi-vendor proposal comparison**: compare premises, exclusions, quality conditions, maintenance, and future expansion instead of price alone.
5. **post-order review**: test whether adoption rationales and unresolved hypotheses were supported by implementation results.
6. **structural-reading reuse**: preserve the distinction among HAP/Kaidoc facts, Nakagawa Master's interpretation, the extracted value core, why it was selected, and why it became the external article's axis.

## Validity conditions
The structure is valid where an important cause of procurement stall is the absence of explicit decision units, unresolved-item visibility, or comparison axes. Issue decomposition must make premises shareable, proposals and estimates meaningfully comparable, and selection rationales explainable. Buyers must be able to retain genuine uncertainty rather than having it silently filled by experts or AI. Responsibility and decision ownership must remain visible, and the decision history should be reusable after implementation.

## Failure conditions
The structure fails when it collapses into automatic requirement generation, price comparison, or brokerage while unresolved judgments remain hidden; when vendors retain all interpretive control and buyers cannot explain premises; when generated specifications transform uncertainty into false certainty; when responsibility becomes ambiguous; or when issue decomposition does not move the procurement state forward. It also fails as a Nakagawa structural derivative when the rationale for selecting the value core and the external-article axis disappears and the page becomes a generic service description.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the hypothesis if procurement stalls are consistently better explained by absent budget, authority, procurement regulation, legal constraints, or organizational politics, and issue decomposition does not improve comparability or agreement. Also revise the claimed independent value of issue decomposition if conventional requirements practices or another method repeatedly resolves the same decision paralysis without the proposed distinction between decided and unresolved issues.
