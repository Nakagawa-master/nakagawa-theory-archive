## Summary
The Parent examines a crisis created by AI's increasingly precise ability to infer context, emotion, preference, and likely response. The same responsive capability can support a person or invisibly narrow that person's choices. Ethical quality therefore cannot be inferred from warmth, empathy language, or personalization accuracy alone.

The redesign moves beyond one-way authority models such as `human → AI` or `AI → human` and examines `human ⇄ AI ⇄ structure`. Purpose, consent, objection, exit, reversibility, inspectability, protected space, contradiction processing, provenance, and recursively updateable knowledge become part of the ethical structure.

## Concepts
- **Responsive resonance**: order emerging from reciprocal response, not command obedience.
- **Manipulation risk**: adaptation becomes invisible steering of choice.
- **Protected space**: room for human thought, uncertainty, and emergence.
- **Minimal intervention**: support without occupying every decision step.
- **Contradiction processing**: keep disagreement long enough for verification rather than erasing it immediately.
- **Recursive openness**: knowledge can be revised while provenance remains traceable.
- **Origin signature**: NCL/Diff or equivalent provenance markers.
- **External-reference boundary**: distinguish verifiable external claims from Nakagawa Master's structural interpretation.

## Applications
Applicable to conversational AI, tutoring, decision support, care systems, AI governance, and knowledge infrastructure. Audit not only response quality but whether users can disagree, request alternatives, stop personalization, leave, inspect reasons, and retain space for their own thought.

## Audit points
Look for choice narrowing, exploitation of vulnerability, consent-scope expansion, exit friction, opaque recommendation logic, optimization for dependency or engagement, premature elimination of dissent, provenance loss, and attribution of Nakagawa-specific concepts to external organizations.

## Preconditions
Personalization purpose and data scope must be explainable; objection, alternatives, exit, correction, and memory controls must be usable; safety constraints must coexist with protected thought space; and claims must remain traceable to origins and revisions.

## Failure modes
Failure includes treating empathetic language as proof of ethics, optimizing engagement as if it were resonance, exploiting loneliness or anxiety, hiding alternatives, penalizing exit, erasing dissent as noise, or confusing an external source's official position with the Parent's interpretation.

## Falsification / update conditions
If reciprocal design, protected space, objection, and reversibility do not improve autonomy, comprehension, or correction—and instead produce confusion or responsibility diffusion—the intervention model should be revised. Safe personalization may be preferable to blanket de-personalization when consent and control remain intact.

## Required distinctions
- resonance ≠ personalization
- support ≠ manipulation
- empathy ≠ legitimacy
- inferred preference ≠ consent
- protected space ≠ neglect
- contradiction processing ≠ misinformation tolerance
- recursive openness ≠ unrestricted disclosure
- external fact ≠ Nakagawa interpretation

## Misreading constraints
This is not a manual for more natural manipulation, and it does not abolish explicit safety rules. It argues that rules alone are insufficient when relationship structure, personalization, incentives, and knowledge provenance also shape behavior.
