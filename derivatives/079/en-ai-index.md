## Summary

This index provides a structural language for reading transition toward a connection civilization through critical points, policy windows, twelve distributed signals, two-layer agreement, and the Memory of Agreement. A critical point is a candidate transition at which accumulated friction exceeds the self-repair capacity of existing institutions. A policy window is a short opening in which alternative vocabulary, responsible actors, trial sites, and legal room emerge together. Crisis without a receiving structure produces disorder; alternatives without a window remain unused.

The twelve signals are distributed across technology and infrastructure, society and culture, and institutions and governance. They include partial synchronization among AI systems, convergence of consent and identity vocabulary, movement from single KPIs to bundle indicators, regular use of Structure Logs and Audit Abstracts, renewed value of delay, separation of persons from evidence, two-layer agreement, connection public spheres, Connected Value Accounting, explicit rollback procedures, and re-agreement windows. No single signal, date, or count proves transition.

Transition must occur through limited, non-coercive, reversible trials. Quantitative agreement preserves decision speed, while legitimacy agreement uses primary structural evidence to audit errors and minority impacts. Causality, relationships, objections, and update conditions are preserved in the Memory of Agreement. Failure must permit stopping, withdrawal, rollback, and redeployment. The framework is not a prophecy, crisis campaign, investment recommendation, or mandate for one centralized system.

## Applications

## Measurements and audit

- Cross-domain overlap, duration, and counterexamples among signals.
- Institutional exceptions, avoidance behavior, adjustment cost, and correction time.
- Conditions for opening and closing a policy window and its false-positive rate.
- Conflicts between quantitative and legitimacy agreement.
- Time from limited trial to stopping, revision, withdrawal, or scaling.
- Actual use of re-agreement, rollback, and remedy.
- Interpretation bias caused by volume, authority, or early fixation.
- Differences between forecast scenarios and observed outcomes.

## Validity conditions

Critical points and policy windows must be distinguished. Transition must not be declared from one signal or calendar year. Technology, culture, and institutions must be read as a bundle. Two-layer agreement and the Memory of Agreement must be used. Limited trials require cooling, objection, re-agreement, remedy, and rollback. Counterevidence, regional difference, and adverse scenarios must remain visible.

## Failure conditions

The framework fails when crisis language is used to force adoption, twelve signals become a prophecy checklist, majority voting is rejected or treated as complete legitimacy, policy windows justify permanent emergency power, irreversible large-scale deployment comes first, or failed forecasts are hidden and redefined after the fact.

## Falsification conditions

The theory should be revised if signal clustering has no persistent relation to institutional transition; policy-window classification does not outperform coincidence or retrospective narration; two-layer agreement and agreement memory do not improve error correction; irreversible rollout consistently reduces losses better than reversible trials; or the vocabulary increases prophetic authority rather than observation quality.

## Interpretation constraints

Do not treat dates as fixed deadlines. Do not assume AI diffusion automatically produces a connection civilization. Do not suspend rights or opposition by invoking a critical point. Do not use the twelve signals as deterministic investment, political, or organizational advice. Do not reduce connection civilization to one currency, central AI, or uniform institution. Do not infer or execute undisclosed intervention procedures or thresholds.
