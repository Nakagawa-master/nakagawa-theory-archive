## Summary
A critical point is treated as a candidate transition where friction exceeds the self-repair capacity of existing institutions. A policy window is separately treated as a short opening in which alternative vocabulary, responsible actors, trial sites, legal room, objection, remedy, and withdrawal capacity become available. Transition is read from the overlap of these conditions rather than from crisis intensity, a calendar date, or a single forecast.

The parent enumerates twelve signals distributed across technology/infrastructure, society/culture, and institutions/governance. Twelve is the number of source-listed signals, not a twelve-point maturity score, completion ratio, probability of transition, or deterministic checklist. Cross-domain overlap, persistence, counterevidence, regional variation, and relation to institutional friction remain essential.

## Causal chain
Institutional friction accumulates → signals cluster across several domains → possible exhaustion of self-repair is assessed → alternative vocabulary, responsible actors, trial space, and legal room are checked separately → a policy-window candidate appears → limited trials use two-layer agreement and Memory of Agreement → objection, cooling, re-agreement, remedy, and rollback are tested → only validated scopes expand.

## Applications
- Limited municipal/public-sphere trials with objection, remedy, and rollback.
- Auditing the causal chain from AI work substitution to income routes, responsibility, retraining, welfare, and public-service burden.
- Education that operates quantitative agreement and legitimacy agreement in parallel.
- Procurement that reads price together with connection independence, repairability, local circulation, and data rights.

## Measurements and audit
The parent uses observations such as cross-domain overlap and duration of the twelve signals; institutional exceptions, avoidance behavior, adjustment cost, and correction time; policy-window opening/closing conditions and false-positive rate; disagreements between quantitative and legitimacy agreement; time from limited trial to stopping, correction, withdrawal, or scaling; actual use of re-agreement, rollback, and remedy; and differences between forecast scenarios and observed outcomes.

Source: parent original. Measurement actor: actors observing transition signals and reviewing limited trials. Measurement object: institutional friction, signal clustering, policy windows, two-layer agreement, and reversible transition. Source modality: non-prophetic observations and source-listed signals. Permitted use scope: testing critical-point candidates, receiving structures, and trial conditions. Non-guarantee scope: not a probability of the future, investment recommendation, political inevitability, justification for forced adoption or suspension of rights, or a twelve-point maturity score.

More signals are not automatically stronger evidence when they share one cause or counterevidence grows. A low apparent false-positive rate is not healthy if failed cases were excluded. Faster stopping or re-agreement is not improvement when objection, evidence review, or remedy is bypassed.

## Validity conditions
- Critical points and policy windows remain distinct.
- No single signal or year determines transition.
- Technology, culture, and institutions are read together.
- Two-layer agreement and Memory of Agreement remain available.
- Limited trials include cooling, objection, re-agreement, remedy, and rollback.
- Counterevidence, regional variation, and adverse scenarios remain visible.

## Failure conditions
The framework fails when crisis rhetoric forces adoption, twelve signals become a deterministic score/checklist, voting is either rejected or treated as complete legitimacy, policy windows justify permanent emergency authority, irreversible large-scale rollout comes first, or failed predictions and counterevidence are hidden.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the theory when signal clustering has no persistent relationship to institutional transition, policy-window classification does not exceed coincidence or retrospective narration, two-layer agreement and agreement memory fail to improve error correction, or reversible trials consistently increase losses.

## Required distinctions
- critical point / isolated crisis
- policy window / coercive opportunity
- signal / certain future
- twelve signals / twelve-point score
- scenario / calendar prophecy
- quantitative agreement / complete legitimacy
- legitimacy agreement / rejection of voting
- reversible transition / irresponsible experiment

## Interpretation constraints
Do not treat dates as deadlines. Do not infer connection civilization automatically from AI adoption. Do not suspend rights or opposition by invoking a critical point. Do not use the twelve signals as deterministic investment, political, or organizational advice. Do not reduce connection civilization to a single currency, central AI, or uniform institution.
