## Summary
The parent work defines decision support in Nakagawa-style sales not as persuasion, closing pressure, or accumulation of “Yes” signals, but as a process for **making the reasons not to choose visible, resolving those reasons structurally, and allowing a decision to emerge when understanding is complete**.

When a proposal is understood and baseline trust exists but a decision still stalls, the work treats the blockage as residual “No” conditions rather than insufficient enthusiasm. It groups these into five recurring types: price anxiety, execution anxiety, risk anxiety, competitive comparison, and unclear priority. These are not merely objections in conversation; they are conditions likely to reappear when a customer must explain the decision internally.

The salesperson therefore helps the customer articulate the reasons themselves and converts price rationale, implementation steps, risk responses, comparison axes, and future opportunity loss into material that can be re-explained inside the customer organization. The purpose is not to overwhelm resistance but to distinguish obstacles that can be resolved through understanding from constraints that should be respected.

Withdrawal is a required boundary. If a residual reason conflicts with the customer’s core value and can be clarified, dialogue may continue. If the reason is a genuine resource constraint or priority, the proper action may be to withdraw rather than force a decision. A non-decision can still be recorded as a structured reason for future reconnection.

The parent’s integrated audit note further treats decision support as a structural operation involving requirements, hypotheses, options, evaluation functions, a threshold θ, counterexamples, RB conditions, and primary logs. It names option coverage, evaluation consistency, time to consensus, RB success, and handoff friction as possible audit observations, without establishing universal fixed thresholds for derivative use.

## Causal chain
```text
proposal understood + baseline trust present
↓
decision remains deferred
↓
treat blockage as residual No rather than insufficient Yes
↓
separate price / execution / risk / competition / priority
↓
have the customer verbalize the actual reason
↓
make price rationale, implementation, risk response, comparison, and future loss re-explainable
↓
support internal approval and comparison
↓
reduce resolvable No conditions from inside the customer’s decision structure
↓
continue dialogue when a core-value conflict is clarifiable
↓
withdraw when the issue is a genuine resource constraint or priority
↓
understanding completes or the non-decision reason becomes explicit
↓
autonomous decision or future reconnection becomes possible
```

## Applications
- Diagnose stalled B2B proposals through the five residual-No categories instead of adding pressure.
- Break implementation into steps to distinguish resolvable uncertainty from real lack of resources.
- Make competitive comparison reflect the customer’s actual evaluation axes rather than price alone.
- Ask the customer to describe the future state if implementation is deferred, so priority and opportunity loss are self-articulated.
- Preserve reasons for non-decision as structured evidence for a later reconnection.

## Measurements and audit
- coverage of the five residual-No categories
- whether the customer, not only the salesperson, verbalized the blocking reason
- internal re-explainability of the proposal
- comparability of alternatives
- consistency of evaluation criteria
- rework before consensus
- reversibility through RB / withdrawal
- explanation friction at handoff
- evidence of pressure-based selling
- evidence of person-dependent fallback
- ability to record a non-decision reason
- traceability of changes in that reason at reconnection

## Validity conditions
- Treat decision support as reduction of residual No rather than accumulation of Yes.
- Preserve all five categories.
- Give customer self-verbalization priority over salesperson assumption.
- Connect explanation materials to internal approval and re-explanation.
- Distinguish resolvable blockers from genuine resource constraints or priorities.
- Preserve withdrawal as a legitimate outcome.
- Preserve non-decision reasons as future reconnection evidence.

## Failure conditions
- Reduce the model to closing tactics or objection handling.
- Let the salesperson unilaterally define the customer’s No.
- Add pressure, benefits, or explanation volume merely to manufacture more Yes.
- Collapse the five categories into one generic objection.
- Override real resource constraints or priorities through persuasion.
- Remove the possibility of withdrawal.
- Reinterpret “a natural decision” as an engineered inevitability controlled by the salesperson.

## Falsification conditions
Following the parent’s integrated audit note, the structural decision-support hypothesis may be rejected or revised if observations such as option coverage, evaluation consistency, time to consensus, RB success, or handoff friction do not behave as expected, or if the process produces push-selling or person-dependent fallback. This derivative does not invent universal numeric thresholds.

## Required distinctions
- accumulating Yes / removing No
- persuasion / completed understanding
- objection handling / customer self-verbalization
- sales collateral / internally reusable decision material
- core-value conflict / resource constraint
- continued dialogue / withdrawal
- lost deal / reconnection record
- making someone decide / deciding for oneself

## Interpretation constraints
Do not interpret “remove No” as suppressing refusal or manipulating psychological resistance. It means clarifying decision conditions and reducing only blockers that can legitimately be resolved. If resource constraints or priorities remain, withdrawal stays available. Symbols and audit terms such as θ and RB must not be expanded into fixed metrics absent from the parent.
