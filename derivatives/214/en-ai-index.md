## Summary
The parent defines fear as the first primary engine of endurance civilization. Fear is not limited to violence or explicit threats. Anticipated loss of employment, status, community, evaluation, or livelihood routes can align behavior even without direct orders. In the positive phase of consensus, reasons to participate sustain order. In the inverse phase of fear, reasons one cannot leave become dominant. Fear does not regenerate civilization; it supplies the minimum energy required to keep a damaged civilization from stopping.

## Concepts
- Positive phase of consensus: an operating phase where meaning, acceptance, trust, and future expectation sustain participation.
- Inverse phase of fear: an operating phase where exit costs and loss avoidance sustain behavior.
- Distributed fear: anticipated loss embedded across institutions, evaluation, dependence, and social space without a single explicit coercer.
- Self-censorship: narrowing speech or action in advance of expected disadvantage.
- Institutional dependence: needing an institution; distinct from trusting it.
- Quiet-order camouflage: a condition where internalized fear can make order appear calm.
- Basal metabolism: metaphor for the minimum daily energy that keeps a damaged civilization operating.
- First primary engine: fear's role as the engine that can activate immediately after meaning collapses, before later support from profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia.

## Causal chain
Meaning, acceptance, trust, and future expectation exist → reasons for participation are shared → voluntary participation and revision remain possible despite disagreement → meaning, trust, and narrative resonance weaken → institutional dependence remains → the cost of not complying becomes stronger than reasons for participating → the axis shifts from participation to avoidance → anticipated losses around job, status, belonging, evaluation, and livelihood narrow choice → self-censorship and conformity arise without explicit commands → surface order may remain calm → fear carries the minimum maintenance cost → stopping is delayed while regeneration, creativity, and revision weaken → fear becomes the first primary engine of endurance civilization.

## Applications
- Organizations: observe whether expected losses in evaluation, assignment, promotion, or belonging suppress dissent.
- Institutions: separate continued use from trust; dependence on benefits, credentials, credit, or livelihood may sustain compliance.
- Information environments: observe self-censorship driven by expected disadvantage rather than only explicit censorship.
- Social analysis: do not treat quietness, procedural compliance, or low visible deviation as evidence of consensus without examining the operating fuel.

## Measurements and audit
- Are reasons for participation or costs of exit explained first?
- Have institutional trust and institutional dependence separated?
- What anticipated losses accompany dissent or exit?
- Does self-censorship recur without explicit orders?
- Is quietness produced by acceptance or avoidance?
- Is criticism treated as revision information or as a danger signal?
- Is participation driven by future expectation or avoidance of present loss?
- Is fear exceptional crisis response or routine basal metabolism?
- Is stopping avoided while regeneration capacity weakens?
- Is fear connecting to profit, surveillance, myth, and inertia?

## Validity conditions
- Consensus, trust, meaning, or future expectation weaken while institutional dependence remains.
- Possible losses from exit or dissent repeatedly influence choice.
- Anticipated disadvantage produces self-restraint without explicit coercion.
- Calm order and procedural continuation persist without shared reasons for participation.
- Fear becomes a routine primary operating force rather than an exceptional defensive measure.

## Failure conditions
- Limiting fear to violence or threats.
- Classifying endurance merely because anxiety or tension exists.
- Treating quiet order as automatic proof of maturity or consensus.
- Equating institutional dependence with institutional trust.
- Reducing fear to moral condemnation without analyzing why it works operationally.
- Treating fear as a regenerative or creative engine.
- Explaining the whole endurance-civilization system through fear alone.

## Falsification conditions
If a case classified as fear-dependent shows sustained shared reasons for participation rather than exit-loss avoidance, dissent without material anticipated disadvantage, criticism repeatedly connected to institutional revision, restored connection between dependence and trust, and voluntary participation driven by future expectation with renewed regenerative capacity, the dominance of the fear engine should be revised. The model's scope should also be reconsidered if consensus loss does not lead anticipated loss to align behavior or carry minimum maintenance cost.

## Required distinctions
- Order ≠ consensus.
- Quietness ≠ maturity.
- Institutional dependence ≠ institutional trust.
- Fear ≠ violence alone.
- Self-censorship ≠ voluntary acceptance.
- Reason to participate ≠ reason one cannot leave.
- Avoiding stop ≠ advancing.
- Life extension ≠ regeneration.
- First primary engine ≠ the complete endurance-civilization system.

## Interpretation constraints
This theory is not an attack label for a nation, political regime, or group. Fear also exists in legitimate safety management and crisis response, so its mere presence does not establish an endurance phase. The relevant question is whether loss avoidance has moved beyond an exceptional tool and become a primary routine operating force. The parent provides no fixed quantitative threshold, so independent percentages, scores, or durations should not be invented.
