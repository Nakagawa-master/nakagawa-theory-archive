## 15-Second Explanation
The principle of correspondence addresses a resonant structure in which people naturally align while feeling that they “chose for themselves,” through meaning, questions, options, silence, pauses, arrangement, and other means, without intensifying commands or persuasion. The original organizes its features into three qualities—nonverbality, autonomy, and propagation—and makes preserving free choice an ethical condition.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
Commands and numerical targets can move people in the short term, but they also produce resistance and superficial obedience. The Parent Original focuses on how people are moved not only by words and norms, but also by atmosphere, silence, and reciprocal response. It understands phenomena such as silence after a meeting determining the direction of the group, or one person’s small action spreading to those around them, not as coincidence but as correspondence.

Correspondence has three characteristics. The first is nonverbality: it works through silence and atmosphere without requiring commands. The second is autonomy: behavior becomes aligned not through coercion, but in a form where the other person feels they chose it themselves. The third is propagation: one person’s small behavior cascades to many others.

However, correspondence is not covert psychological manipulation. The Parent Original identifies “disguised correspondence,” which pretends coercion is resonance, and “excessive conformity pressure,” which eliminates diversity, as dangers. For correspondence to be ethical, there must be room that protects the other person’s options.

## Practical Process
**1. Place meaning first.** Present the meaning and background of the situation before ordering an action.

**2. Open the question.** Use questions with a high degree of freedom rather than leading or imposing.

**3. Show multiple good options.** Do not limit the choice to Yes/No; provide positive paths in both directions.

**4. Leave room.** Do not over-explain; make use of silence and pauses.

**5. Tune the nonverbal elements.** Arrange the setting, sequence, and atmosphere.

These five are the practical principles listed by the Parent Original. The “5” is the structure of the text’s principles; it does not mean a success rate or a fixed score.

## Examples of Application
**1. Meetings.** Instead of pressing for a conclusion with “Those who agree with this proposal, please raise your hands,” a hypothetical example is given in which the group first agrees on criteria by saying, “Let’s decide on three evaluation criteria for this proposal.” The “three” here is a concrete number used in the example.

**2. Hiring.** Rather than mechanically searching for people who meet conditions, the structure is to collectively imagine the person best suited to the role and let candidates emerge through consensus-building.

**3. Local consensus.** Instead of excluding opponents, redesign the concerns expressed by opposing views as resources and turn conflict into deeper consensus.

**4. Education and culture.** Rather than teaching through words alone, create the atmosphere and sense of unity in the setting, addressing learning that attracts people naturally rather than through coercion.

## Success Criteria
Natural alignment arises because meaning and the environment have been shared, even without commands.

The person who acted feels that they chose it themselves rather than being forced.

Even when one person’s behavior spreads, dissent and other options remain.

Opposing views are not excluded; they become material for improving consensus.

## Limitations
Because correspondence is powerful, misuse can turn it into invisible coercion. The Parent Original explicitly treats disguised correspondence and excessive conformity pressure as dangers.

An increase in conformity by itself is not proof of success. The integrated audit summary establishes a relationship in which resonance density, conformity rate, reenactment rate, phase stability S, and other measures are observed, and the hypothesis is rejected or revised when rigidity or excessive conformity appears. No specific fixed values are given in the article concerned.

## Preventing Misreadings
Correspondence is not a technique for manipulating someone without their noticing. If freedom and room for choice are lost, it falls outside the ethical conditions of the Parent Original.

It is not a theory that says “the higher the conformity rate, the better.” Excessive conformity is subject to falsification and revision.

Do not equate a person’s charisma or popularity with correspondence. The Original addresses structural resonance produced by meaning, questions, rhythm, and the environment.
