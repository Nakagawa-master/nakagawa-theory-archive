## Summary
The parent redefines real-estate market participants by transaction function rather than social title. Seller, self-use buyer, investor, business operator, and broker are separated by permissions, obligations, evaluation criteria, and logs. The self-use buyer is the protected subject; investors and business operators are market-driving actors; the broker is the coordinator of transaction safety. Multiple roles held by one person or company are allowed only when separated into distinct operating modes. Viewing rights and offer rights are separated, while investor demand is converted into market credit through transparency, proof of funds, execution history, explanation quality, and traceability. Negotiation is shifted from poker-like information advantage to consensus over shared assumptions, responsibility, and business structure.

## Concepts
- Role = Function: participant identity is based on transaction function, not title.
- Five Roles: seller, buyer, investor, business operator, broker.
- Protected Subject: self-use buyer.
- Market Driver: investor and business operator.
- Brokerage Purification: broker as mediation and transaction-safety coordinator.
- Separate-Mode Principle: multiple roles must operate as separate modes.
- Permission / Obligation / Evaluation / Log: role-specific control surfaces.
- Viewing Right: transparency access to property, numbers, risk, and comparisons.
- Offer Right: permission to express conditional demand.
- Pro Mode: isolated extension layer for advanced business functionality.
- Credit Conversion: convert transparency and execution evidence into market credit.
- Consensus: agreement based on shared numbers, responsibilities, and business structure.

## Applications
- Separate brokerage and acquisition/resale functions into different modes within one company.
- Give self-use buyers access to decision numbers, risks, conditional offers, and expert consultation.
- Attach proof of funds, execution history, cancellation rates, dispute rates, and documentation to investor offers.
- Require business operators to explain acquisition price through renovation, operations, exit, risk, and required margin.
- Evaluate brokers by execution, deadlines, disclosure quality, dispute prevention, and evidence management.
- Keep advanced planning, financing, and management tools inside a separate professional lane.

## Measurements and audit
- share of actors identified by function rather than title.
- rate of explicit mode separation for multi-role actors.
- separation of permissions, duties, evaluation criteria, and logs.
- explanation quality, execution rate, dispute rate, deadline adherence.
- detection rate of unjustified viewing restrictions or enclosure.
- detection rate of abusive or disguised lowball offers.
- proof-of-funds and execution-evidence completeness.
- buyer comprehension and expert-route utilization.
- leakage of pro functions into the protected buyer UI.

## Validity conditions
- Define roles by function.
- Preserve the five roles as distinct operating functions.
- Split multiple roles into explicit modes.
- Maintain role-specific permission, obligation, evaluation, and logs.
- Keep self-use buyers as protected subjects.
- Treat investors and business operators as market-driving responsibility-bearing actors.
- Keep brokerage centered on transaction safety.
- Separate viewing rights from offer rights.
- Pair stronger permissions with stronger evidence and accountability.
- Make expert routes available to all participant roles.

## Failure conditions
- Brokerage and inventory/business activity operate under one indistinguishable mode.
- The user's current role is not visible.
- Investors are treated as preferentially protected consumers.
- Self-use buyers are held to business-actor responsibility standards.
- Viewing rights are used for enclosure.
- Offer rights are used for coercion or disguised lowballing.
- Business operators do not explain the basis of profit or price.
- Professional functions contaminate the buyer protection UI.
- Priority is granted through money, private access, or relationships rather than transparency and execution evidence.

## Falsification conditions
The parent proposes monitoring explanation quality, execution rate, dispute rate, deadline adherence, enclosure detection, abusive-offer detection, and buyer comprehension. If these indicators persistently cross threshold θ in the adverse direction, or if over observation window δ phenomena M persist—such as normalized role mixing, repeated public backlash, frequent administrative intervention, hollowed-out buyer protection, or transparency turning into surveillance/exposure—the hypothesis must be rejected or revised. θ and δ are not universal fixed numbers. T/S/R is retained as parent notation without inventing an expansion.

## Required distinctions
- title vs function
- broker vs business operator
- investor vs self-use buyer
- market driver vs protected subject
- profit vs exploitation of information asymmetry
- viewing right vs offer right
- pro mode vs privilege
- multiple roles vs role mixing
- price battle vs consensus specification
- transparency-based credit vs relationship-based privilege

## Interpretation constraints
This is not an anti-investor or anti-business framework. It does not ban multi-business firms as such. It separates functions so responsibility does not disappear. Buyer protection does not mean exclusive expert access. Pro mode is not a higher social rank. Vol.5 wires roles and incentives; misuse, surveillance, exposure, and partial extraction risks are handled further in Vol.6.
