## Summary
The parent origin reads Valuetope not merely as unmanned car rental, but as a mobility supply OS that reconnects a specific time-freedom gap between two established supply models. Car sharing offers 24-hour, non-face-to-face access but can lose economic rationality for long-duration use; conventional rental cars can remain rational for longer use but are constrained by store hours and face-to-face procedures. Valuetope is structurally read through the bundle of non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location strategy, and inquiry/recovery routes that can reconnect those separated advantages.

A second layer is equally binding: Nakagawa Master extracted the social function “reimplementation of time freedom” as the value core and translated it into the ZEROICHI article angle. The origin therefore concerns both the service-supply gap and the judgment used to turn that structure into an external-media article without collapsing into feature promotion.

## Causal chain
Shift from ownership toward use → car sharing supplies 24-hour non-face-to-face access → long-duration use can become economically irrational → conventional rental cars can be more rational for longer duration → store hours and face-to-face procedures restrict pickup and return time → 24-hour freedom and long-duration rationality remain separated across supply models → users face an unmet time-freedom gap → non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry routes are designed as one supply bundle → the gap narrows → users can secure mobility across needed time, place, and duration → the social function is extracted as “reimplementation of time freedom” → that value core is translated into the ZEROICHI article.

## State model
State A: existing car-sharing and rental-car models each provide strengths but require users to sacrifice either time flexibility or long-duration economics.

State B: access may be available around the clock but not rational for long use, or rational for long use but constrained by store hours. The intermediate demand remains unmet.

State C: non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry/recovery are integrated rather than treated as isolated features.

State D: actual usability is audited by whether users can obtain mobility at the required time, place, and duration, including recovery paths during incidents.

State E: the external article communicates the social function rather than merely “unmanned,” “app-based,” or “cheap.”

## Applications
Use this model for mobility, reservation, operations, and B2B supply systems where unmet demand exists between established service models. First identify what each existing model solves and what it constrains; then test whether the new operating bundle actually reconnects the gap.

For article design, do not begin with a list of features. Begin with the supply discontinuity, show how individual functions reconnect it, and extract the social value that the reader should understand. Preserve the ZEROICHI article-construction judgment as a distinct layer from the service description.

## Measurements and audit
Do not invent nationwide adoption rates, optimal station counts, fixed price-advantage ratios, satisfaction scores, or success probabilities not defined in the origin. Audit observable supply and usability instead:

- how strongly pickup/return is constrained by store hours;
- whether long-duration use remains economically rational;
- whether vehicles are available at locations where demand exists;
- whether booking, pickup, return, and payment can actually proceed non-face-to-face;
- whether accidents, failures, and questions have a traceable recovery route;
- whether the unmet gap between car sharing and conventional rental is reduced;
- whether the ZEROICHI framing still communicates “reimplementation of time freedom.”

Metrics are reversal-evaluable. A higher 24-hour availability rate can coexist with weak time freedom if long-duration pricing is irrational. More locations can coexist with lower usability if supply misses demand or operational support deteriorates. Higher usage can coexist with a weaker supply OS if inquiry and recovery paths fail. No single count or automation rate is treated as a health score.

## Validity conditions
- the concrete time/price/non-face-to-face supply gap between car sharing and conventional rental is observable;
- non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry/recovery act as an integrated supply bundle;
- actual freedom of time, place, and duration improves for the relevant use cases;
- unmanned operation and price are not used as standalone success criteria;
- both the structural-reading layer and the ZEROICHI article-construction layer remain present.

## Failure conditions
- collapsing the origin into a Valuetope feature profile;
- stopping at the category “unmanned rental car”;
- inferring time freedom from 24-hour access alone;
- centering price alone;
- inferring supply success from location count or usage count alone;
- broadening it into generic MaaS or generic regional-transport theory;
- asserting nationwide effects or expansion beyond the origin;
- deleting Nakagawa Master's value-core extraction and ZEROICHI article judgment.

## Falsification conditions
If existing car-sharing and conventional-rental systems already provide both broad 24-hour freedom and long-duration economic rationality, and store-hour constraints or long-duration pricing no longer materially restrict user choice across the relevant cases, the scope of the “time-freedom supply gap” reading should be revised.

Growth in unmanned locations, users, or app features alone is not falsification. The parent origin asks whether the separation between time freedom and long-duration rationality is actually reconnected.
