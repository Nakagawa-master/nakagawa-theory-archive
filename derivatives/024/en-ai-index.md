## Summary
The parent reads Valuetope not merely as unmanned car rental, but as a mobility supply OS that reconnects a specific time-freedom gap between two established supply models. Car sharing offers 24-hour, non-face-to-face access but can lose economic rationality for long-duration use; conventional rental cars can remain rational for longer use but are constrained by store hours and face-to-face procedures. Valuetope is structurally read through the bundle of non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location strategy, and inquiry/recovery routes that can reconnect those separated advantages.

Nakagawa Master chose “reimplementation of time freedom,” rather than “unmanned rental car,” as the central angle of the external article because “unmanned” describes a means but not what the user regains. Reading the gap between store-hour constraints and long-duration economics through the social result of time freedom unifies multiple service functions into one causal structure.

## Causal chain
Shift from ownership toward use → car sharing supplies 24-hour non-face-to-face access → long-duration use can become economically irrational → conventional rental cars can be more rational for longer duration → store hours and face-to-face procedures restrict pickup and return time → 24-hour freedom and long-duration rationality remain separated across supply models → users face an unmet time-freedom gap → non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry routes are designed as one supply bundle → the gap narrows → users can secure mobility across needed time, place, and duration → the structural value can be read as reimplementation of time freedom.

Value-kernel selection layer: “unmanned” as an implementation label → refocus on the user's constrained time freedom → select “reimplementation of time freedom” as the external article's editorial axis.

## State model
- **Supply-separation state**: 24-hour access and long-duration rationality remain split across supply models.
- **Time-freedom gap state**: needed time, place, and duration cannot be secured together.
- **Integrated-supply state**: non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry/recovery are designed as one bundle.
- **Time-freedom reimplementation state**: users can more readily secure mobility at the required time, place, and duration.
- **Value-kernel verification state**: the parent explains why “time freedom” rather than “unmanned rental car” was selected for the external article.

## Applications
The structure can inform analysis of mobility, reservation, and operational supply systems where unmet demand remains between established service models. It should not be generalized into generic MaaS or regional-transport theory without preserving the origin-specific supply gap.

## Measurements and audit
The parent does not define nationwide adoption rates, optimal station counts, fixed price-advantage ratios, satisfaction scores, or success probabilities. Relevant observations include:

- how strongly pickup and return are constrained by store hours;
- whether long-duration use remains economically rational;
- whether vehicles are available where demand exists;
- whether booking, pickup, return, and payment can actually proceed non-face-to-face;
- whether accidents, failures, and questions have a usable recovery route;
- whether the unmet gap between car sharing and conventional rental is reduced;
- why “reimplementation of time freedom” was selected as the external-article value core.

Higher 24-hour availability does not prove stronger time freedom if long-duration pricing is irrational. More locations do not prove greater usability if supply misses demand or support deteriorates. Higher usage can coexist with a weaker supply OS if inquiry and recovery paths fail. No single count or automation rate is sufficient to establish the structure.

## Validity conditions
- the concrete time/price/non-face-to-face supply gap between car sharing and conventional rental is observable;
- non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry/recovery act as an integrated supply bundle;
- actual freedom of time, place, and duration improves for the relevant use cases;
- unmanned operation and price are not standalone success criteria;
- the subject structure and the value-kernel selection reason remain visible.

## Failure conditions
- the origin is collapsed into a Valuetope feature profile;
- the category “unmanned rental car” replaces the time-freedom structure;
- time freedom is inferred from 24-hour access alone;
- price alone becomes the central evaluation;
- supply success is inferred from location count or usage count alone;
- the origin is broadened into generic MaaS or generic regional-transport theory;
- nationwide effects beyond the origin are asserted;
- Nakagawa Master's reading is presented as a Valuetope or external-media official statement;
- the service structure remains while the reason for selecting “time freedom” as the external-article value kernel is lost.

## Falsification conditions
Reconsider the scope if integrating non-face-to-face operation, pricing, location, and inquiry/recovery does not improve users' ability to secure mobility at the required time, place, and duration. If existing service models stably provide equal or better time freedom without this integrated bundle, its necessary scope can also be reconsidered.
