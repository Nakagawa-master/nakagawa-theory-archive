## Summary
D-series Vol.2 treats friction with Legacy-OS as a protocol mismatch rather than simply a conflict of morality or personality. It turns Vol.1's non-hostility, non-assimilation, and asymmetric translation principles into an operational method. Before changing behavior, it redefines internal meaning for law and tax, employment, and family/community; then it inserts an Input → Translate → Output layer that converts vague or emotional pressure into function, number, and responsibility. Connection costs are accounted for, returns are compared against costs, and persistently loss-making connections can be closed through quiet exit codes.

## Applications
- Handle daily state/institution contact as terms, procedures, and operating conditions rather than personal warfare.
- Translate workplace moralizing or vague evaluation into measurable outputs, deadlines, and responsibility boundaries.
- Separate role evaluation from personal worth.
- Design etiquette, information sharing, contact frequency, and physical distance separately in kinship/community relations.
- Compare the costs of translation, explanation, contact, recovery, and L7 erosion against money, credibility, infrastructure, and learning returns.
- Use formal exit language when a connection remains structurally loss-making instead of escalating into argument.

## Measurements and audit
Observe emotional instant-reply frequency, separation between internal meaning and external wording, explanation growth driven by demands for mutual understanding, conflation of role evaluation with personal worth, boundary erosion in kinship, translation time, explanation count, contact time, recovery time, judgment quality, C-series output, L7 erosion, connection returns, and exit-code effectiveness. The parent does not establish one universal cost threshold or fixed numerical values for θ and δ.

## Validity conditions
- Preserve D-series Vol.1's non-hostility, non-assimilation, and asymmetric translation.
- Examine friction as protocol mismatch.
- Do not always make behavior change the first move.
- Treat translation as protocol conversion that preserves internal meaning.
- Keep differences among state, employment, and kinship connections.
- Observe both return and cost.
- Preserve withdrawal when L7 erosion continues.

## Failure conditions
- Directly transmitting righteousness while assuming the other OS must be rewritten.
- Requiring complete understanding or agreement and thereby multiplying explanation cost.
- Turning translation into self-negation, deception, or unconditional obedience.
- Equating employment evaluation with personal worth.
- Treating kinship as unlimited access rights.
- Forbidding withdrawal even when the connection remains structurally loss-making.
- Upgrading internal metaphors into literal legal identity claims.

## Falsification conditions
The parent audit uses Traceability, Stability, and Reversibility (T/S/R) as re-agreement conditions. If emotional cost continues to accumulate after translation, the connection remains persistently loss-making, L7 alignment falls below threshold θ, or phenomenon M such as degraded judgment or unrecoverable exhaustion is observed during window δ, the non-contaminating passage model based on meaning redefinition is subject to rejection or revision.

## Interpretation constraints
"Tax as subscription," "employment as B2B," and "family as diplomacy" are structural metaphors for internal meaning redefinition, not claims that taxation, employment, or kinship literally change legal category. Do not expand asymmetric translation into a license for manipulation, deception, or illegality. Do not reinterpret the parent as banning direct truth-telling in all cases or as promising that every connection can be preserved through translation.
