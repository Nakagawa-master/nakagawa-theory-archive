## Summary
Meaning Convergence Theory treats a feasible future definition as a terminal semantic constraint. Past and present facts are not physically rewritten; rather, their roles, priorities, and connections can be reassigned according to how they contribute to the defined future. Experiences previously labeled failures, detours, or surplus may become learning assets, constraints, or required components. The future definition remains revisable and must be tested against real constraints.

## Concepts
future definition; feasibility; terminal constraint; node; meaning vector; semantic rearrangement; reverse comparison; action alignment; meaning convergence; definition revision.

## Causal chain
local present evaluation → fixed meanings of past/present nodes → feasible future definition → constraint checking → reassessment of node roles → updated connections and priorities → current actions realign → meanings converge toward the future definition.

## State model
local evaluation / meaning fixation / future definition / feasibility audit / reverse comparison / semantic rearrangement / priority update / action alignment / convergence / definition revision.

## Applications
Business strategy, career design, research planning, institutional design, organizational restructuring, and resource allocation where existing facts must be reinterpreted without erasing evidence.

## Measurements and audit
Track specificity of the future definition, explicit constraints, reasons for node-role changes, connection changes, priority changes, observable action changes, retention of disconfirming evidence, and revisability of the future definition.

## Validity conditions
The framework applies when the future state is concrete enough for feasibility testing and when using it changes node roles and present actions in an explainable way.

## Failure conditions
Do not turn the theory into prophecy, physical rewriting of the past, wish fulfillment, fatalism, or retrospective success storytelling that deletes contrary evidence.

## Falsification conditions
Narrow or revise the claim if the defined future is infeasible, if semantic rearrangement creates no explanatory change in action, or if alternative future definitions repeatedly explain the same nodes better.

## Required distinctions
future definition / prediction; change of meaning / change of fact; feasibility / inevitability; reverse comparison / physical retrocausality; convergence / hindsight rationalization; terminal constraint / destiny.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not import quantum retrocausality, metaphysical certainty, numerical vector scores, probabilities, or implementation dates absent from the Parent.
