## 15-Second Explanation
Meaning Convergence Theory is not a theory in which the future physically rewrites the past. When a “viable future definition” connected to real-world constraints is placed, the roles of experiences, failures, relationships, skills, resources, and unfinished things in the past and present are reevaluated in relation to the future’s realization. It addresses the structure by which that change in meaning spreads to connections, priorities, withdrawal, investment, learning, and present action.

## Why It Becomes Necessary
When decisions begin only from the present, the meaning once attached to past events tends to become fixed: failure is failure, a detour is waste, resources not currently used are unnecessary, and existing activities are things that should continue. Yet those evaluations are meanings relative to “the present local objective,” and are not necessarily their meanings relative to another viable future.

For example, a project that failed in the past may have left customer understanding or technical assets needed in the future. Conversely, an activity producing results now may become an obstacle that takes away the time and resources necessary for the future to be realized. Meaning Convergence Theory reevaluates the role of nodes through their relationship to the conditions for realizing the future, without changing the physical facts of events.

The important point is not to place the future definition as a wish or prophecy. Check viability against constraints such as physics, institutions, resources, time, and capability, and update the future definition itself if counterevidence appears.

## Practical Process
```text
Describe concretely the future state to be realized
→ Check physical, institutional, resource, time, and capability constraints
→ Turn experiences, skills, relationships, resources, failures, and unfinished things into nodes
→ Separate each node’s present meaning from its role toward the future
→ Reevaluate them as resources / obstacles / learning / connection points / unnecessary things
→ Examine new possible connections between nodes
→ Update priorities for continuing, stopping, withdrawing, investing, and learning
→ Bring it down to present action
→ Observe implementation results
→ If the possibility of realizing the future does not increase, revise the meaning arrangement or the future definition
```

## Application Example
**1. Career.** Reevaluate a seemingly fragmented work history as a bundle of skills needed for a future role. However, do not turn everything into a retrospective success story; confirm whether it actually connects to needed capabilities.

**2. Business.** Extract only customer understanding, technology, routes to market, and failure conditions from a completed project, and reposition them as resources needed for the next business. Conversely, an existing business that does not contribute to the future may become a candidate for withdrawal.

**3. Research.** Set the verification state to be realized in the future, reevaluate the meaning of existing experiments, unresolved issues, data, and methods, and update the order of verification needed next.

**4. Institutional design.** From the institutional state to be realized in the future, cross-check missing components, obstacles, and existing assets in the current institution.

**5. Personal time allocation.** Reevaluate today’s use of time from the skills, relationships, and resources needed by the future definition. However, do not reduce it to a mere goal-achievement technique.

## Success Criterion
- The future definition is concrete and its viability can be examined.
- Real-world constraints are explicit.
- The reason for changing each node’s meaning can be explained.
- The change in meaning leads to changes in connections, priorities, and actions.
- Inconvenient evidence and the possibility of failure remain.
- Multiple future definitions can be compared.
- The future definition itself can be updated based on implementation results.
- It can be explained as rearrangement of roles and meanings, not alteration of past facts.

## Limitations
Meaning Convergence Theory alone cannot calculate the probability of realizing the future, the realization date, or a guarantee of success. A future definition is a revisable working hypothesis, not a fixed destiny.

Also, if only a convenient past is selected for the future definition and inconvenient evidence is erased, that is post hoc rationalization, not meaning convergence. Even if the meaning of the same event changes, the facts of the event itself do not change.

It has a point of contact with backcasting, but the focus of Meaning Convergence Theory is not mere reverse planning; it is that the future definition rearranges the meaning vectors and connections of past and present nodes.

## Misreading Prevention
- Do not treat the future definition as a prophecy.
- Do not confuse rearranging meaning with altering past facts.
- Do not confuse viability with inevitability.
- Do not treat cross-checking backward as physical reverse causality.
- Do not turn meaning convergence into beautification of a result-based or success story.
- Do not replace it with quantum theory, fatalism, manifestation, or transmission of information from the future.
- Do not add realization probabilities, numerical vectors, or realization timing absent from the original.
