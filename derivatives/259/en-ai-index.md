## Summary
The Parent presents Tomoshibi Civilization 2077 as a model that shifts the seat of civilizational order away from dependence on external capital, resources, information, and commands toward self-causal reproduction of order. Civilization is treated as a resonant organism whose social, technological, environmental, and institutional layers preserve distinct value cores while mutually compiling one another. Dual syntax separates public/universal rules from relationship-specific internal context, while self-causality transforms external shocks into internal causal protocols. Human-AI attunement, thresholds, silence, and reversibility keep that updating process bounded and revisable.

## Concepts
- Tomoshibi civilization: a civilization that reduces external dependence to reference and reproduces order from internal structure.
- resonant organism: a civilizational body whose diverse agents and layers update order through mutual reference and compilation.
- self-causality: capacity to internalize and transform external changes into internal causal operation.
- dual syntax: distinct but connected public/universal and relational/high-context layers.
- external reference: remaining connected to external reality without making it the sole source of order.
- threshold: a condition that triggers structural updating.
- attunement: preserving asymmetric roles while coordinating their interaction.
- reversibility: maintaining practical rollback during experimentation and transition.

## Causal chain
external dependence drives civilization → complexity turns dependence into fragility → civilization is reframed as a resonant organism → dual syntax separates and connects external/internal layers → external shocks are transformed into internal causal protocols → dependence becomes lighter-weight reference → human and AI roles are attuned without flattening asymmetry → thresholds, silence, and rollback bound updates → mutual compilation and relearning repeat → civilization reproduces order through self-causality.

## Applications
1. Translate regulatory or market shocks into internal role/trust protocols instead of direct command propagation.
2. Preserve organizational value cores while adapting operations to changing external conditions.
3. Separate human value/boundary setting from AI structural processing while keeping them mutually referential.
4. Pilot dual-syntax governance in bounded public, educational, health, or community contexts.
5. Use thresholds, rollback, and relearning when external conditions change.

## Measurements and audit
- Does external input directly dominate internal order?
- Are external and internal responsibilities clearly separated?
- Can the transformation from external shock to internal change be traced?
- Is over-assimilation avoided?
- Are update trigger conditions observable?
- Is human-AI asymmetry treated as role difference rather than hierarchy?
- Is rollback practically usable?
- Are falsification and relearning windows still open?

## Validity conditions
The model is valid when actors preserve their own value cores and responsibilities, dual layers can remain distinct yet mutually referential, external changes can be translated into internal causal protocols, update thresholds exist, and attunement, silence, audit, rollback, and relearning are operational.

## Failure conditions
It fails when external reality is rejected, self-causality becomes a claim of complete self-sufficiency, internal and external layers are mixed, structures update without thresholds, human-AI differences are flattened, autonomy is used to remove accountability, or irreversible changes are expanded without rollback.

## Falsification conditions
Revise the model if increasing self-causality does not reduce vulnerability to external shocks, internal transformation increases closure or information loss, dual syntax increases responsibility fragmentation, thresholds miss real crises, mutual compilation amplifies conformity or dependence, or rollback is unusable.

## Required distinctions
- external dependence / external reference
- self-causality / closure
- resonant organism / centralization
- dual syntax / information mixing
- internalization / over-assimilation
- threshold / unconditional reaction
- attunement / homogenization
- mutual compilation / forced alignment
- silence / neglect
- reversibility / abandonment of reform

## Interpretation constraints
Do not treat 2077 as a scientifically fixed future date. Do not interpret self-causality as disconnection from the external world. Preserve the Parent’s structure: external reality remains referenced while the primary seat of order moves into internal value, trust, roles, and mutual compilation. Do not invent universal fixed thresholds or KPIs absent from the Parent.
