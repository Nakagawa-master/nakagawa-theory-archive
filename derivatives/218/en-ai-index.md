## Summary
The parent defines inertia not as personal laziness but as a structural persistence mode in which institutions, procedures, budgets, authority lines, infrastructure, and distributed responsibility continue to support one another after meaning, purpose, and justification have weakened. Following fear, profit, surveillance, and myth, inertia allows motion to continue with little new belief or justification. Continuation becomes the default, while stopping or redesigning bears the burden of alternatives, transition costs, responsibility, and disorder. This can prolong motion but does not itself generate repair, renewal, or regeneration.

## Concepts
- Inertia: persistence in which continuation outranks an external purpose.
- Purpose-driven operation: a condition in which an external purpose can still evaluate, redesign, or terminate an institution.
- Inertia-driven operation: a condition in which existing motion itself becomes the reason to continue.
- Continuation default: continuation requires little explanation while stopping requires special proof.
- Stopping burden: alternative design, transition costs, responsibility, comparison, and disorder management imposed on change.
- Institutional self-purpose: preservation of organization, budget, authority, or procedure overtakes the original purpose.
- Responsibility evaporation: no actor can readily assume responsibility for stopping or redesigning the system.
- Low-energy prolongation: existing circuits maintain motion without generating new conviction or justification.

## Causal chain
Fear makes exit dangerous → profit rationalizes remaining → surveillance suppresses deviation → myth supplies meaning and justification → meaning, purpose, and justification erode → installed procedures, budgets, authority, infrastructure, and responsibility arrangements remain → continuation becomes default → stopping and change bear alternative-design, transition, and responsibility burdens → the institution becomes self-preserving → responsibility evaporates and autopilot strengthens → motion persists at low energy → wear accumulates without repair or regeneration.

## Applications
- Institutional operation: distinguish purpose-based continuation from continuation by precedent, budget, and procedure.
- Organizations: test whether weak outcomes persist mainly because stopping creates responsibility and transition costs.
- Public systems: compare explanation burdens for continuation versus termination or redesign.
- Social analysis: do not treat institutional survival as evidence of legitimacy, support, recovery, or regeneration.

## Measurements and audit
- Is the institution still measurable against an external purpose?
- Can continuation be distinguished from inability to stop?
- Are explanation burdens asymmetric between continuation and termination?
- Are alternative design, transition capability, and an accountable stopping subject available?
- Does preservation of organization, budget, procedure, or authority outrank the original purpose?
- Can an ineffective institution actually be redesigned or terminated?
- Are motion and function, prolongation and regeneration, clearly distinguished?

## Validity conditions
- The binding force of external purpose, ideals, or justification has weakened.
- Installed procedures, budgets, authority, infrastructure, and responsibility arrangements remain.
- Continuation has become the default.
- Alternative design, transition, responsibility, and disorder burdens are concentrated on stopping or change.
- Institutional survival has begun to outrank the original purpose.

## Failure conditions
- Reducing inertia to personal laziness or apathy.
- Classifying an institution as inert merely because it is old.
- Assuming a new institution cannot become inertia-driven.
- Treating continuation as evidence of legitimacy, support, recovery, or regeneration.
- Collapsing inertia into fear, profit, surveillance, or myth.
- Restricting stopping burdens to monetary costs only.

## Falsification conditions
An inertia-centered explanation is weakened if the institution is continuously evaluated against an explicit external purpose, is actually redesigned when ineffective, can be terminated when unnecessary, and continuation bears an explanation burden comparable to stopping. Demonstrated active support, purpose fit, and renewal capacity also require revision of a model based only on inability to stop.

## Required distinctions
- Inertia ≠ personal laziness.
- Continuation ≠ legitimacy.
- Continuation ≠ support.
- Motion ≠ function.
- Prolongation ≠ regeneration.
- Age ≠ inertia.
- Procedure ≠ inertia.
- Inertia ≠ fear, profit, surveillance, or myth.

## Interpretation constraints
“Even when nobody believes” is not a measured claim that every person psychologically rejects the system. It identifies a structure in which strong belief or shared justification is no longer required for institutional motion. The parent does not condemn old institutions, bureaucracy, procedures, or organizations in general. Do not invent a fixed inertia score, threshold, duration, or numerical criterion.
