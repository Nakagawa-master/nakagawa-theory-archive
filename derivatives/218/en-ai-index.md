## Retrieval summary
Inertia is not personal laziness or apathy. It is a structural persistence mode in which institutions, procedures, infrastructure, distributed responsibility, and established allocations continue to support one another after meaning, purpose, and justification have thinned. The institution no longer continues primarily because an external purpose directs it; continuation itself becomes the default, while stopping or changing it bears the burden of alternatives, transition costs, responsibility, and temporary disorder. This low-energy persistence can prolong motion but does not generate repair, renewal, or regeneration.

## Causal chain
```text
fear → exit becomes dangerous
profit → remaining becomes rationalized
surveillance → deviation is suppressed
myth → these mechanisms become meaningful / justifiable
meaning and purpose erode
→ installed procedures and arrangements remain
→ stopping costs and stopping responsibility rise
→ continuation becomes default
→ institutional self-purpose / autopilot / responsibility evaporation
→ low-energy prolongation
→ accumulated wear without regeneration
```

## Establishment conditions
- External purposes cease to govern institutional motion effectively.
- Records, budgets, authority lines, permits, evaluation items, maintenance routines, and responsibility-avoidance practices remain coupled.
- Capacity to design what follows a stop, an accountable stopping subject, comparative evaluation, or willingness to absorb transition disorder is weak.
- Explanation burdens are asymmetric: continuation is assumed while change or termination must prove itself.

## Failure / falsification boundaries
- If an institution remains continuously measurable against an external purpose, is redesigned when ineffective, and can actually be terminated when unnecessary, an inertia-centered explanation is weakened.
- Age, bureaucracy, procedure, or organizational size alone do not establish inertia.
- Demonstrated active consent or support cannot be replaced by the claim that persistence is merely stopping failure.

## Interpretation constraints
Do not reduce inertia to moral failure, equate continuation with legitimacy or regeneration, or collapse inertia into fear, profit, surveillance, or myth. Do not add claims about particular real-world institutions that the parent text does not make.
