## 15-Second Explanation

This Parent Original regards the wear of modern society not only as human malice, but as the limit of the old OS’s evaluation functions and reward structure, and presents a process for gradually migrating to a new OS of a separate lineage rather than repairing the inside. It runs B = individual activation, C = circulation, A = immune auditing, and D = the interface with the old OS as one system, and increases convergence after confirming implementation and reproducibility in local colonies.

## Why It Is Needed

Under the old OS, short-term results, externalized costs, diluted responsibility, conformity, and similar behaviors are readily rewarded as rational actions. The Parent Original diagnoses that even regulation, education, transparency, and consciousness reform can be collected back into the same structure through formalization, competition over loopholes, and so on as long as they operate inside the same evaluation function.

Therefore, rather than destroying the old OS all at once, it adopts Migration: locally activating a new evaluation function and circulation as a separate system, then gradually moving people and resources to areas where they have become established. At the destination of migration, “success = contribution” is treated not as goodwill, but as the condition for a circulation in which value, trust, resources, and meaning return to new output.

## Practical Process

1. **Observe the old OS’s reproducible patterns.** Look not at the good or evil of particular people, but at where short-term optimization, diluted responsibility, externalization, and conformity are rewarded.
2. **Read the difference between evaluation functions.** Do not confuse the old OS’s focus on advantage and enclosure with the new OS’s focus on contribution to circulation.
3. **Activate the B system.** Separate the survival layer from the expansion layer and protect intrinsic output that does not depend only on external evaluation.
4. **Create C-system connections.** Establish a state in which output is observed and used, while attribution to its starting point is retained.
5. **Close the circulation.** Check whether output → use → valorization → reward → new output turns.
6. **Identify blockages.** Treat the point where the circuit stops—observation, attribution, or transformation—as a plumbing accident.
7. **Implement the A system simultaneously.** Audit attribution, circuits, and contamination, and view C/E as effects on circulation rather than as personalities.
8. **Connect with the old OS through the D system.** Avoid frontal collision and assimilation; translate and transform only the necessary resources and let them flow.
9. **Test in a local colony.** First run the smallest closed circuit with the value function, attribution, and waste heat fixed.
10. **Expand after confirming reproducibility.** Do not seek majority approval first; increase the migration range as convergence onto an implementation that has become established.

## Examples of Application

**A limited internal project.** Rather than announcing a company-wide institutional renewal first, create a closed circuit in a specific project where the origin, use, and return of rewards for results can be traced. Even if the amount of communication increases, if the origin disappears and resources do not return to the output side, do not regard the connection as successful.

**An individual’s work design.** Separate the layer needed to sustain life from the layer that produces creation, exploration, and contribution. When worn down by the old OS’s evaluation competition, do not conclude that the will is weak; reconsider the arrangement that protects the internal combustion engine.

**Collaboration with an external organization.** Even if funding, credit, and institutions are used, do not bring evaluation functions based on titles, majorities, short-term results, and so on into the internal decision standard. Establish a D-system interface and transform only the necessary resources.

## Success Criteria

Success is not that the number of people who agree with the philosophy has increased. In a local implementation, observation, use, and attribution of output are established; rewards return to new output; deviations can be audited without condemning a person; and the internal evaluation function is maintained even when touching the old OS. It is important that this state be reproducible and that participation and departure be non-coercive and reversible.

## Limitations

- The Parent Original’s “only solution” does not mean that every option in the social sciences has been empirically and permanently eliminated. It is a claim derived from structural comparison within the text.
- This is not a process for replacing all society at once. The Parent Original places a gradual migration from local areas.
- “Colony” and “extraterritoriality” must be read as structural terms, not as legal immunity.
- The Parent Original does not present a fixed migration deadline, universal numerical threshold, or participation rate.
- “Contribution” is not identical with goodwill, self-sacrifice, or a large amount of service.

## Preventing Misreading

Do not turn criticism of the old OS into grounds for attacking particular people, companies, or political forces as evil. The Parent Original instead addresses how even behavior that is rational on average can converge toward destruction of the whole depending on the reward structure.

Also, do not read “migration” as revolution or forced conversion. The Parent Original begins with local implementation—read → try → introduce—and closes by explicitly stating that it is non-coercive, reversible, and verifiable, with freedom of education, research, and criticism.
