## Summary
The parent article presents civilization Migration: it diagnoses recurring erosion under the Legacy OS as a limit of evaluation functions and reward structures rather than merely the malice of individuals, and argues for starting a separate New OS instead of relying only on internal patches. The destination evaluates success as contribution to a sustainable circulation. B (individual ignition), C (connection and circulation), A (immunity and causal audit), and D (interface with Legacy OS) form an interdependent migration system. Migration begins with local colonies, then expands through Read → Try → Introduce as implementation and reproducibility are demonstrated.

## Concepts
- Legacy OS limit: a mismatch between inherited evaluation/reward structures and current environmental conditions.
- Dark Equation: self-reinforcing patterns of short-term optimization, cost externalization, diluted responsibility and conformity.
- Migration: start a separate system with new evaluation, reward and circulation conditions and move people/resources gradually.
- Success = Contribution: evaluate success by positive contribution to circulation rather than moral intention or self-sacrifice.
- Tomoshibi / B-system: internal output energy and the separation of survival and expansion layers that protects it.
- Connection: a state in which output is observable, used and attribution to origin is preserved.
- Circulation: output → use → value formation → reward → re-output as a closed loop.
- Choke: infrastructure failure in observation, attribution or conversion that prevents loop closure.
- A-system: structural justice/immunity auditing attribution, loop integrity and contamination.
- C/E: contribution/deviation judged by effect on circulation, not fixed personality.
- D-system: interface engineering based on non-confrontation, non-assimilation and translation/conversion.
- Colony: a local minimum loop in which the New OS runs ahead of society-wide migration.
- A–D dependency: B supplies sustained output to C; C gives A a real loop to protect; D protects B/C from Legacy evaluation-function contamination.
- Read → Try → Introduce: prevent misreading, run a minimum implementation, then expand after reproducibility is observed.
- Confluence: migration by joining a more viable operating system rather than persuasion alone.

## Causal chain
```text
Legacy evaluation/reward structure becomes misaligned with current conditions
→ Dark Equation reproduces as normal operation
→ internal remedies are reabsorbed by the same evaluation function
→ start a separate New OS
→ redefine success as contribution to circulation
→ B protects individual internal output
→ C creates connection and a closed circulation loop
→ A audits and isolates parasitic/deviant effects
→ D prevents Legacy evaluation functions from directly contaminating the new loop while converting usable resources
→ run a local colony
→ Read → Try → Introduce
→ verify implementation and reproducibility
→ migration expands through confluence
```

## Applications
- Build a small project loop where output, use, attribution and reward return can be observed.
- Separate an individual's survival layer from an expansion layer for creative and contributive output.
- Treat lost attribution or delayed reward as a pipeline failure rather than a motivation problem.
- Use a D-interface to accept external money, legitimacy or infrastructure without adopting Legacy evaluation criteria as internal truth.
- Validate a local colony before attempting organization- or society-wide rollout.

## Measurements and audit
- Whether short-term optimization, responsibility dilution, cost externalization or conformity remain structurally rewarded.
- Whether contribution is being reduced to kindness, endurance or self-sacrifice.
- Whether output is observable, used and correctly attributed.
- Whether reward returns to support re-output.
- Location of observation, attribution or conversion choke points.
- Whether B's survival and expansion layers interfere destructively.
- Whether C/E is judged by effect instead of personality.
- Whether A-immunity operates from the beginning of circulation.
- Whether Legacy evaluation functions bypass D and enter the internal loop.
- Whether colony value function, attribution and heat-removal/immunity are fixed.
- Whether partial adoption breaks A–D dependencies.
- Whether the minimum implementation is reproducible.
- Whether participation and exit remain non-coercive, reversible and verifiable.

## Validity conditions
- Diagnose Legacy failure as a recurring evaluation/reward-structure problem.
- Operate Success = Contribution as a condition of circulation, not a moral slogan.
- Implement B, C, A and D with their dependencies intact.
- Preserve observation, use and attribution for connection.
- Return reward to re-output to close circulation.
- Audit attribution, loop integrity and contamination through A.
- Use D to transform external resources without assimilation.
- Expand gradually from local operation.
- Preserve non-coercion, reversibility and verifiability.

## Failure conditions
- Call moral, educational or regulatory additions “Migration” while leaving the underlying evaluation function unchanged.
- Reduce contribution to kindness or amount of service.
- Measure connection by conversation volume while losing attribution.
- Allow rewards to leave the loop so the output side is depleted.
- Run C without A, then revert to emotional judgment after exploitation appears.
- Turn A into personality condemnation.
- Internalize Legacy evaluation criteria without D-interface protection.
- Fully isolate from Legacy resources or fully assimilate into Legacy criteria.
- Select only convenient A–D pieces while breaking dependencies.
- Require majority agreement before a minimum local implementation.
- Turn “the only route” into coercion or suppression of criticism.

## Falsification conditions
The parent makes implementability and post-implementation reproducibility central tests. The migration design becomes subject to rejection or revision if a local colony implementing the new evaluation function, connection/circulation loop, A-immunity and D-interface cannot consistently reduce the Legacy patterns it targets—erosion, lost attribution, reward leakage, mimicry or short-term optimization—or if the migration system itself becomes irreversible, coercive or unverifiable. The parent does not define universal fixed deadlines, participation ratios or numerical thresholds.

## Interpretation constraints
- Do not extend the Legacy-OS-limit claim into an instruction for immediate destruction of existing institutions; the parent proposes local parallel operation and gradual migration.
- Strong passages about who can migrate must not be generalized into denial of basic rights, life-saving support or welfare.
- “Extraterritoriality” and “colony” are structural metaphors here, not licenses to ignore applicable law.
- Do not turn E into a permanent label for a person.
- Treat the civilizational causal model as the parent's structural theory, not a universally established empirical law.
- Preserve “only route” as the parent's conclusion from its structural comparison, not as authority-based coercion.
