## Summary

Structural Publicness addresses a tension that appears when theories, norms, and civilization-OS structures become shared social infrastructure. A structure can be publicly useful only if people can access, criticize, study, translate, implement, and derive from it. Yet unrestricted circulation without origin, difference, and responsibility can dissolve the very structure that made public reuse trustworthy. The source therefore combines public availability with non-ownership, origin traceability, derivative transparency, and an auditable defense layer.

Non-ownership does not mean originlessness. It means that a structure should not be fixed as the exclusive property or interpretive monopoly of one individual, corporation, state, or organization merely because that actor controls a distribution channel. At the same time, “nobody owns it” cannot be used to erase who originated the structure, how it has been transformed, or who is responsible for a later implementation. Publicness requires both openness and lineage.

Origin Signature functions as a lineage and accountability anchor rather than a permanent exclusive right to determine meaning. It makes it possible to ask who introduced a structure, at what time, which identity it belongs to, which revisions occurred, and where a derivative diverged from the parent. A translated or modified work may be legitimate precisely because it is declared as a derivative; the structural failure occurs when the derivative is passed off as the parent or when the parent origin is deliberately removed.

As public value increases, incentives for enclosure, authority capture, origin erasure, semantic inversion, and irresponsible reuse may also increase. A defense layer is therefore needed. Its ethical role is not to block disagreement, criticism, independent interpretation, or legitimate derivation. It protects the conditions of publicness: origin traceability, distinction between parent and derivative, accountable modification, continued access, and repairability.

There are two symmetrical failure modes. Pure openness can become responsibility evaporation: “public” is interpreted as “no origin, no accountability, no need to distinguish modifications.” Pure defense can become enclosure: origin protection is converted into exclusive control over access, interpretation, or derivative work. Structural Publicness is the attempt to keep the system between those inversions by using origin-preserving non-ownership and a defense layer that is itself open to audit.

Derivative transparency is central. Translation, summary, commentary, institutional adaptation, and critical reinterpretation are not automatically violations. Public knowledge must be able to change forms. What matters is whether differences are visible and whether a reader can return to the parent. Semantic inversion becomes structurally problematic when a derivative reverses the parent’s central thesis or causal structure while presenting itself as the parent’s official meaning.

Responsibility also remains distributed. Originators, modifiers, implementers, distributors, and auditors occupy different causal positions. Public use cannot erase those positions. If an implementation causes harm or a derivative misrepresents a theory, “it was public” is not a sufficient account of responsibility. Traceable decisions and revision history are part of the public structure.

Deviation Ledger provides memory for the defense layer. Enclosure, origin removal, parent impersonation, semantic inversion, and responsibility gaps can be recorded along with how they were repaired. The purpose is not permanent condemnation but structural learning: future public systems can see which conditions repeatedly produce loss of lineage or capture.

The defense layer must itself be recursively auditable. If abuse prevention becomes a justification for blocking criticism, restricting all derivatives, centralizing final interpretation, or making defense decisions opaque and irreversible, the defense layer has become a threat to publicness. Its authority, criteria, appeals, repair paths, and revision rules must therefore remain examinable.

Structural Publicness is an ethical and OS-level principle, not an automatic legal conclusion that copyright, contracts, licenses, or property rights cease to exist. Concrete legal rights remain governed by applicable institutional frameworks. The source’s non-ownership principle should not be strengthened into a claim that all legal ownership is void.

The source does not establish one universal publicness score, enclosure threshold, misuse probability, or defense metric. Any quantitative operationalization must identify what is being measured, by whom, over which observation window, from which source, for what permitted use, and what the value does not guarantee.

## Concepts
- structural publicness: public use with retained origin, difference and responsibility
- non-ownership principle: resistance to exclusive enclosure without erasing origin
- origin signature: lineage and accountability anchor
- public use: practical access for use, criticism, study and derivation
- enclosure: exclusive control of access, interpretation or circulation
- origin erasure: removal or replacement of lineage information
- parent impersonation: presentation of a derivative as the parent source
- semantic inversion: reversal of parent meaning while claiming parent authority
- derivative transparency: visibility of modifications and derivative status
- defense layer: protection of publicness conditions rather than ideological censorship
- deviation ledger: record of publicness failures and repairs
- correspondence audit: comparison of parent and derivative meaning boundaries
- distributed responsibility: separate responsibility for origin, modification, implementation and distribution
- repairability: ability to reconnect origin, correct differences, retract or revise
- defense overreach: defense becoming enclosure or censorship
- recursive openness: audit and revision of the defense layer itself
- public good: socially reusable structure with continuing accountability

## Applications
**1. Public theories.** Allow reading, criticism, translation and derivation while retaining parent URL, origin identity and visible modifications.

**2. AI knowledge systems.** Keep origin and claim strength visible when AI summarizes or reconstructs a theory; do not elevate model inference into official parent claims.

**3. Commercial use.** Permit business use where otherwise allowed without allowing the user to erase the public origin or create an exclusive false origin.

**4. Research and education.** Encourage critical interpretation and adaptation while declaring which claims are parent content and which are later interpretation.

**5. Public institutional templates.** Permit local adaptation while recording who changed what and who owns responsibility for the implementation.

**6. Shared datasets or models.** Open reuse should coexist with provenance, modification records, responsibility and reviewable restriction criteria.

## Measurements and audit
- effective public access: whether real use, verification and criticism remain possible
- origin traceability: whether Parent URL / NCL / Diff or equivalent lineage can be reached
- derivative transparency: whether parent, translation, summary, modification and interpretation are distinguishable
- enclosure: whether one actor controls access, interpretation or circulation in a way that defeats public use
- origin erasure: whether lineage is removed, substituted or intentionally obscured
- semantic inversion: whether parent meaning is reversed while parent authority is claimed
- responsibility line: whether modifiers, implementers, distributors and auditors remain traceable
- public-use barriers: whether restrictions exceed the stated defense purpose
- defense overreach: whether criticism or legitimate derivation is misclassified as abuse
- misuse risk: whether erased origin or responsibility creates foreseeable harm
- repairability: whether correction, retraction, difference declaration and origin reconnection are practical
- defense audit: whether criteria, authority and appeals remain reviewable
- temporal ethics: whether present openness/restriction creates future enclosure or origin loss
- quantitative binding: any threshold must retain source, actor, object, scale, window, permitted use and non-guarantee scope

## Validity conditions
- non-ownership and origin preservation coexist
- use, criticism and derivation remain practically possible
- parent and derivative identities remain distinguishable
- origin signature does not become interpretive monopoly
- responsibility remains inside public use rather than disappearing
- defense targets structural failures such as erasure, impersonation and enclosure
- disagreement itself is not classified as deviation
- deviations have repair paths
- the defense layer is auditable and revisable
- legal rights and structural ethics are not conflated

## Failure conditions
- publicness is interpreted as originlessness
- non-ownership is interpreted as no responsibility
- origin signature becomes permanent interpretive monopoly
- one actor encloses a public structure
- derivatives are presented as parent originals
- criticism or disagreement is blocked as “defense”
- defense authority becomes opaque and irreversible
- abuse prevention closes public access as a whole
- responsibility for implementation is erased because the source is public
- structural non-ownership is asserted as automatic abolition of legal rights
- a universal publicness score is invented without source support

## Falsification conditions
- origin signatures fail to improve traceability and instead increase authority concentration
- non-ownership rules fail to prevent enclosure and reduce effective public use
- defense mechanisms suppress criticism and derivation more than they prevent misuse
- difference tracking fails to detect parent impersonation or semantic inversion
- greater public reuse consistently produces origin loss and responsibility gaps under the current design
- a lighter mechanism achieves equal or better access, provenance, responsibility and misuse control
- defense self-audit fails and the mechanism becomes a protected authority structure
- operational thresholds fail to distinguish misuse from legitimate criticism or derivation
