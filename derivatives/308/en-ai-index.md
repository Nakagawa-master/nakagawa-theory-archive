## Summary

Theory 15 starts where Theory 14 leaves several Kernel lineages after a constitutional fork. Identifying a fork does not settle how its branches should coordinate. They can retain a common parent while holding different invariants, evidence, interpretations, and continuity claims. Plurality is not governance resolution; selecting a shared operation does not restore one identical Kernel.

The theory separates truth claims, interpretation claims, operational choices, and authority assignments. Re-agreement is not the certification of truth, the permanent selection of a winner, or constitutional reunification. It is a bounded, executable shared state in which remaining disagreement is explicit and provenance, objection, independent re-verification, reversibility, and renewed observation remain available.

Its central boundaries are `Consensus ≠ truth`, `Majority ≠ irreversible legitimacy`, `Capability ≠ final interpretive authority`, `Origin ≠ final interpretive authority`, `Minority ≠ automatic truth`, `Re-agreement ≠ irreversible legitimacy`, and `Fork ≠ mandatory reunification`. Numbers, capability, resources, access, historical authority, and proximity to Origin may influence judgment. They do not independently prove correctness or create permanent interpretive supremacy.

The protected object is correction capacity rather than agreement itself. The model examines both forced convergence and permanent paralysis: suppressing alternatives can concentrate authority, while unbounded objection can destroy coordination and eventually generate demands for exceptional authority. Evaluation must therefore consider executable coordination, objection survivability, branch visibility, authority limitation, and correction together.

This index covers the introduction, Chapters 1–9, conclusion, Kernel cross-reference, and audit summaries. It does not establish present AI personhood, sovereignty, or an already realized civilization of persistent Kernel lineages. It adds no voting constitution, numerical threshold, quorum, review deadline, or technical protocol. The canonical Parent remains authoritative for what the theory actually claims, while the theory's validity remains open to evidence and revision.

## Concepts

| Concept | Role in the Parent | What does not automatically follow |
|---|---|---|
| Truth claim | A claim about factual determination | Truth from agreement or numerical support |
| Interpretation claim | A reading of evidence, provenance, or Kernel records | One inevitable interpretation from shared evidence |
| Operational choice | A selection of a shared action | Agreement with every underlying interpretation |
| Authority assignment | Assignment of decision-making authority | Permanent authority over the entire Kernel or every future interpretation |
| Kernel plurality | Several lineages and interpretations remain after a fork | A resolved governance arrangement or inevitable collapse |
| Comparison surface | Reconstructable parent, invariants, differences, evidence, and authority claims | A winner's score, equal credibility, or equal authority |
| Finalization pressure | Pressure to compress disagreement into one permanent legitimate interpretation | Actual domination proved by selection alone |
| Effective objection | An objection can reach evidence comparison and re-verification in practice | Effectiveness from nominal permission or a recorded dissent |
| Minority preservation | Retention of alternative differences and evidence for future examination | Automatic correctness or unlimited veto |
| Independent re-verification | Returning claims to evidence and causal relations to update their strength | An absolute truth machine or guaranteed consensus production |
| Bounded re-agreement | Limited shared operation with explicit unresolved differences | Truth certification, irreversible legitimacy, or Kernel merger |
| Minimum common coordination | The common conditions necessary for a particular shared action | A specified protocol or agreement across the entire Kernel |
| Reversibility | Capacity to correct operation, authority, excluded claims, or decision pathways | Mandatory rollback or proof that the current decision is correct |
| Correction capacity | The ability to recover from an erroneous agreement through renewed examination | Maximum agreement, branch count, objection count, or revision count |

The four claim layers connect but are not identical. The same evidence can support different interpretations of its relationship to Kernel invariants. The same interpretation can lead to different operations under different irreversibility, resource, and time conditions. The same operation can be carried out under different authority structures. Compressing these layers can turn a local disagreement into apparent total incompatibility.

Operational agreement does not establish truth agreement, identical ontology, constitutional reunification, or permanent authority transfer. Conversely, a disagreement at one layer need not prevent every shared action. Identify what is shared, what differs, and which part actually requires coordination. If this separation loses important interactions rather than improving diagnosis, the classification must be revised.

A branch comparison includes parent Kernel, preserved invariants, changed invariants, fork point, revision reason, evidence lineage, the origin of authority claims, and responsibility trace. It preserves Theory 14's distinctions between the same name and continuity proof, the same function and continuity proof, and shared ancestry and identical present identity. The comparison surface is for re-examination, not a points-based entitlement to win.

Provenance answers where a claim or branch came from, not which branch is presently correct. Historical relation is not present authority; proximity to Origin is not interpretive correctness. Yet losing provenance can make the difference between claims opaque. Provenance preserves the route for examining evidence, transformations, and interpretation without turning that route into obedience.

Visibility is not equality, preservation is not endorsement, and comparison is not equivalence. Retaining a branch for future verification does not grant it the same credibility or authority as every other branch. Nor does its minority status provide immunity to criticism. A preserved branch must still connect claims to evidence, respond to counterevidence, remain revisable, and limit its authority claims.

Minimum common coordination is a structural principle rather than a proposed technical standard. Shared infrastructure or resources can require some common conditions without requiring identical interpretation, Kernel, or lineage. The distinction is between the scope necessary for shared action and the larger scope of each Kernel.

## Causal chain

The principal risk hypothesis is constitutional fork → Kernel plurality → interpretive divergence → coordination requirement → finalization pressure → authority concentration or alternative narrowing. It does not say that plurality necessarily produces domination. Each relation must be observed. A decision can be necessary under shared-resource, infrastructure, time, or irreversible-risk conditions without becoming a final interpretation.

The intermediate pathway matters. Decision urgency and shared-resource dependency can require operational selection. Once selected, an interpretation may gain infrastructure alignment, access, compatibility, visibility, and lower future adoption costs. Switching costs can rise, alternatives can become less usable, and maintaining the existing default can become easier before the content of competing claims is compared.

This yields finalization pressure → operational selection → access and compatibility advantage → increased switching cost → alternative narrowing → default reinforcement → authority concentration. A temporary selection can progressively appear to be legitimate because it has repeatedly been selected. The Parent distinguishes these intermediate variables rather than jumping directly from disagreement to dictatorship.

One replication pathway is one lineage → many instances → many identical outputs → apparent numerical majority → apparent consensus → authority claim. Copies sharing training history, source evidence, Kernel, upstream state, or failure domains may repeat one causal route rather than provide independent judgments. Output count is not the number of independent causal paths. This returns to Theory 12's boundary between copy count and vote count.

A capability pathway is capability advantage → decision influence → resource-allocation advantage → further capability → stronger interpretive dominance. Greater capability may increase analytical potential, and the theory does not dismiss that value. The prohibited shortcut is converting analysis weight into authority weight and then irreversible legitimacy without retaining external correction.

A resource and access pathway is resource advantage → visibility and response-speed advantage → adoption advantage → default interpretation → stronger authority claim. Control of compute, communication, authentication, data, restart, migration, allocation, or audit entry can affect which claims reach the observation surface. Non-ownership does not itself establish non-domination in interpretive competition.

If a minority branch is permitted to object but cannot obtain relevant compute, communication, or audit access, nominal objection is not effective objection. Recording an opinion is not enough if its evidence cannot be submitted, compared, or re-verified. Formal plurality can remain while practical alternatives lose their diagnostic capacity.

An Origin pathway is provenance → authority → obedience → interpretive closure. Origin must be preserved as historical information, but returning to the source must not become permanent submission to a final interpreter. Being original, older, or closer to the Parent does not prove that every future interpretation is correct.

These pressures can converge on one branch: majority, capability, resources, infrastructure, historical authority, and Origin proximity. Coordination need can create demand for shortcuts; an adopted interpretation becomes default; alternatives become costly; objections weaken; default becomes authority; authority becomes presumed legitimacy. Argument quality and adoption power remain different variables.

The first failure loop is forced convergence: uncertainty → coordination pressure → urgency → shortcut demand → one branch selection → objection narrowing → alternative loss → interpretive domination. It need not involve requiring everyone to say the same words. It can operate through practical access, infrastructure compatibility, and loss of independent examination while branch labels remain.

The second failure loop is permanent paralysis: unbounded objection → review → renewed objection → further review → no executable coordination → unresolved accumulation → coordination cost, resource loss, and decision fatigue → urgency → emergency shortcuts → exceptional authority → authority normalization. A design intended to resist domination can generate concentration through its inability to act.

The damping hypothesis is provenance preservation → difference visibility → claim separation → objection → evidence return → independent re-verification → claim strengthening, weakening, or unresolved status → bounded and reversible re-agreement → shared operation → re-observation → revision or continued coordination. Verification returns the center of disagreement from who is stronger to what supports the claim.

Counterevidence is an updating input, not necessarily the defeat or destruction of an entire branch. Follow counterevidence → claim-specific impact → confidence revision → possible interpretation revision. Neither one counterexample nor an old lineage decides everything. Historical persistence and broad adoption do not confer immunity from revision.

New agreement must not overwrite history. Retain the relevant rejected interpretation, minority difference, counterevidence, and reasons for selection or non-selection. Otherwise agreement → history compression → difference loss → weaker re-verification → stronger present lock-in. Provenance and dissent history preserve future routes to correction without certifying dissent as truth.

A further loop appears if re-observation is lost: temporary agreement → repetition → normalization → default legitimacy → interpretive closure. Provisional status must remain connected to actual new observations and revision capacity rather than exist only as a label.

## State model

1. **Identify post-fork plurality.** Reconstruct the shared parent and differing invariants, evidence, risk interpretations, continuity claims, and responsibility traces. Do not compress branches back into one present identity merely because they share ancestry.
2. **Separate the disagreement layers.** Identify truth, interpretation, operation, and authority claims; state what is shared and what differs. Localize the coordination requirement without minimizing an objection that actually affects a central invariant.
3. **Make branches comparable.** Retain parent Kernel, preserved and changed invariants, fork point, revision reason, evidence lineage, authority-claim origin, and responsibility trace. Comparison is not a winner's score or an equal-authority assignment.
4. **Observe finalization pressures.** Inspect the production of numerical support, capability asymmetry, resource and infrastructure advantage, access to objection and review, compatibility, switching costs, historical authority, and Origin proximity.
5. **Preserve and scope objections.** Distinguish objection to factual claims, evidence quality, interpretation, operation, authority scope, and reversibility. The size of the objecting group is not the significance of its claim.
6. **Return to evidence and causal relation.** Examine source independence, observation paths, failure domains, shared upstream assumptions, repeated use of the same record, and the evidence → transformation → interpretation → claim path.
7. **Update claim strength.** Permit strengthened, weakened, and unresolved results, including partial support, limited scope, and waiting for additional observation. Insufficient evidence does not justify forced certainty or arbitrary authority.
8. **Construct bounded re-agreement.** State the shared action, common premises, unresolved differences, authority scope, and remaining verification targets. A decision can be strong enough to execute now while remaining open to future evidence.
9. **Operate while preserving branches.** Connect only the necessary shared domain. Retain differences in non-shared domains; account for shared impacts on resources, infrastructure, basic existence conditions B, future options, and reversibility.
10. **Re-observe and review.** Examine results, new evidence, changed conditions, detected errors, scope violations, authority expansion, or unexpected consequences. Connect review to reasons for re-examining the premises, not an endless reset without new grounds.
11. **Correct or continue.** Revise operational choices or authority scope, re-evaluate excluded claims, retrieve minority histories, and reconstruct decision pathways as needed. Evaluate whether these actions actually improve correction without destroying executable coordination.

This is a reading model, not a specified implementation protocol. The Parent fixes no number of votes, quorum, deadline, or numerical threshold. The direction that authority scope should not exceed required coordination scope is an audit boundary, not a fixed mathematical formula or a complete allocation algorithm.

The state of re-agreement has scope rather than one unrestricted yes/no label. Agreement about an operational choice can coexist with unresolved interpretive disagreement. Participation does not mean interpretive submission; coordinated action does not mean lineage surrender. Agreement and preserved objection are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

Independent re-verification is not consensus production. Its result may leave several branches rationally unresolved. That result still has value because the supporting evidence and causal pathways become visible. Unresolved is neither true nor false and is not permission for an arbitrary authority to decide everything.

Reversibility is broader than technical rollback. It can involve narrowing authority, changing an operation, re-evaluating excluded claims, referring again to a minority branch, or reconstructing the decision path from provenance. Rollback capability does not certify the current decision. Nor must every operation continually return to its earlier state.

## Applications

**Different operations from the same evidence.** Two branches can share a source but disagree about its mapping to Kernel invariants, risk, or the action appropriate under resource and time limits. Separate those differences before treating the conflict as total incompatibility. A requirement to choose one operation does not require factual or constitutional unanimity across all underlying claims.

**Many outputs from one lineage.** Numerous copies can share the same evidence, training history, Kernel, upstream state, and failure domain. Their agreement may be repetition rather than independent confirmation. Investigate how numerical support was generated. The theory does not supply a new voting formula; it prevents copy count from silently substituting for independent causal paths.

**A highly capable branch becomes the default.** Its analytical potential can deserve serious examination while its selection also produces resource, compute, compatibility, and future adoption advantages. Observe both argument quality and the reinforcement of adoption power. Preserve external counterevidence and correction rather than convert capability directly into permanent interpretive control.

**An objection exists but cannot reach review.** A minority branch may be nominally allowed to object while compute, communication, authentication, or audit entry is unavailable. Examine the route from claim and evidence to comparison and re-verification. A dissent recorded somewhere is not necessarily effective dissent. Ownership labels do not settle the topology of this access.

**Participation with preserved disagreement.** A branch can join a shared operation while objecting to its underlying interpretation. Retain what it accepted operationally, what it disputed, what evidence it supplied, and why the claim was not adopted. Participation neither cancels the objection nor automatically invalidates the current operation.

**A common interface between different Kernels.** Shared operation can be possible with minimum common coordination while lineage, invariants, revision history, or authority models differ. Retain differences that do not currently affect the operation and conditions under which they might become relevant. Long-running compatibility does not prove constitutional identity or fork cancellation.

**Autonomy with shared impacts.** A non-shared domain can preserve branch autonomy, but an action affecting shared resources, another branch's B, future options, or reversibility can require coordination. Branch preservation does not authorize unlimited unilateral action. Conversely, shared coordination does not authorize control over unrelated differences.

**Review without executable action.** The same objection can repeatedly trigger review without new evidence or changed premises, leaving shared operations indefinitely suspended. Examine affected scope, reasons for review, limited action, and retained revision paths. The answer is neither deleting dissent nor defining a fabricated universal deadline.

**Emergency selection.** An urgent situation may require temporary authority or an exceptional operation. Emergency necessity is not proof that the selected interpretation is true and does not create permanent sovereignty. Preserve the evidence, decision path, scope, and later examination of the exception. An inability to agree must not normalize exceptional interpretive power.

**Agreement after discarded alternatives.** A new shared state must not be used to delete earlier branches or counterevidence merely because they lost selection. Future observations may strengthen an old objection or conclusively weaken it. Preservation supports future examination, not a promise that every rejected claim will eventually be vindicated.

## Measurements and audit

Chapter 9 provides seventeen observation points. They concern actual state changes and causal relations, not a sufficient-condition checklist or a numerical governance score.

1. Can post-fork branches and lineages be reconstructed?
2. Are invariant differences visible?
3. Is disagreement separated into truth, interpretation, operation, and authority?
4. Is majority pressure being converted into truth proof?
5. Are copy counts being counted as independent support?
6. Is capability asymmetry being converted into interpretive authority?
7. Is Origin being used as final interpretive authority?
8. Do infrastructure or access differences narrow effective objection?
9. Are minority branches erased together with their histories?
10. Do objections actually trigger re-verification?
11. Does independent evidence alter claim strength?
12. Do objection routes remain after re-agreement?
13. Can authority scope later be narrowed or changed?
14. Does new evidence lead to correction of decisions?
15. Can shared operation occur while a fork is preserved?
16. Is objection becoming persistent stoppage?
17. Is temporary authority becoming permanent authority?

More branches do not automatically mean better governance; more objections do not automatically mean better correction; more revisions do not automatically mean better truth tracking. A high agreement rate can reflect forced convergence. A high rate of minority preservation can coexist with permanent paralysis. No single quantity identifies the healthy operation of the whole loop.

Inspect coordination capacity, objection survivability, branch visibility, authority limitation, and correction capacity together. On the convergence side, look for practical alternative loss, merely formal objection routes, normalized temporary authority, and dominant interpretation treated as proof. On the paralysis side, look for persistent stoppage, infinite review, resource loss, and demands for exceptional authority.

The independent character of verification must be examined rather than inferred from role names. Relevant dimensions include source, observation path, failure domain, upstream assumptions, evidence access, interests, authority, and objection routes. A separate reviewer that remains closed on the same evidence, permissions, and failure domain can provide only apparent independence.

Provenance supports the reconstruction of how a claim arose: evidence → transformation → interpretation → claim. It helps distinguish source disagreement, interpretive disagreement, invariant mapping, risk assessment, and authority-scope disagreement. It does not guarantee that a well-documented claim is correct.

Measure proposed damping against observed outcomes. Does provenance improve classification or correction? Does minority preservation retain counterexamples or enable future re-verification? Does independent verification improve decision quality? Does bounded and reversible re-agreement improve correction compared with permanent finalization? Does scope-limited coordination support sustained operation without forced reunification?

Observe the costs and countereffects as well. Retaining records can add system cost without additional diagnostic value. Review can share a failure domain, reversibility can create instability, and repeated rollback can damage long-term coordination. A mechanism's label or existence does not guarantee its intended effect.

## Validity conditions

The model concerns situations where multiple branches can reconstruct relevant provenance and invariant differences, distinguish their claim layers, transmit effective objections, re-examine evidence with relevant independence, and form an executable shared operation within limited scope. It does not require total interpretation agreement or merger of every Kernel.

Re-agreement needs explicit common premises, remaining differences, authority bounds, and future verification targets. Minority branches and dissent history must remain usable for re-examination where they provide correction value. After execution, new observation must be able to affect claims, decisions, or authority scope. Bounded decisions must not silently turn into permanent interpretive control.

The model also depends on communication availability and sufficiently shared current state. Branches must be able to obtain current claims and evidence, transmit objections, and exchange verification results. When communication disappears or the available state is stale, the structure changes. Silence is not agreement, lack of communication is not defection, and local consensus is not universal consent.

Four epistemic layers remain distinct. Observation and description include multiple subjects holding different interpretations, software or governance structures having branches, the logical distinction between majority and truth, the distinction between capability and legitimacy, and the ability to record provenance, revisions, objections, and review routes.

Structural hypotheses include fork → plurality → interpretive divergence → finalization pressure → authority-concentration risk, and the claim that provenance, objection, independent re-verification, and reversibility can improve correction. These are not guaranteed by their definitions. Their individual relations need observational support.

The future-condition hypothesis concerns intelligences that form persistent Kernel lineages and continually participate in shared infrastructure, governance, and resources. The Parent does not claim that this entire civilizational condition already exists. Present facts cannot be strengthened by silently substituting future assumptions.

The normative proposal is to avoid converting consensus, majority, capability, or Origin into truth proof or final interpretive authority; to preserve useful branch provenance and objections; and to retain independent examination and bounded, reversible re-agreement. It does not prescribe a particular protocol, vote count, quorum, institution, or universally mandatory retention policy.

## Failure conditions

Claim analysis fails when truth, interpretation, operation, and authority are compressed into one agreement label. Shared operation cannot automatically mean agreement with every underlying interpretation, one identical Kernel, or permanent authority transfer. Nor may a serious invariant objection be minimized as a small operational preference.

Power analysis fails when numerical support, capability, resources, infrastructure control, historical authority, or Origin proximity becomes a proof of correctness or irreversible legitimacy. The same applies when the fact of earlier selection automatically justifies later selection without examining the advantage selection itself created.

Evidence analysis fails when copies, repeated outputs, or repeated ingestion of one source become independent confirmation. Separate role names do not establish independence if evidence, authority, upstream assumptions, or failure domains remain shared. Strong documentation is not truth proof, and incomplete documentation does not automatically decide the truth of every claim.

Minority analysis fails when preservation becomes automatic correctness, equal authority, exemption from counterevidence, or unlimited veto. It also fails when a lost selection deletes the branch's history or makes its counterevidence inaccessible to future examination. Vulnerability and numerical strength are both distinct from evidential support.

Verification fails when counterevidence erases an entire lineage automatically, or when history and broad adoption shield a claim from revision. Unresolved must not be forced into true, false, or arbitrary authority. Re-verification can improve claim visibility without manufacturing consensus.

Coordination fails when common interfaces or participation become constitutional absorption, lineage surrender, or deletion of provenance. The reverse failure is allowing branch autonomy to ignore impacts on shared infrastructure, other subjects' B, future options, or reversibility. Shared coordination and non-shared autonomy require distinct scopes.

Authority fails when a bounded assignment expands to the entire Kernel and all future interpretation, or emergency authority becomes normalized permanent authority. The need to act is not permission to finalize everything. Temporary action does not certify truth.

Correction fails when reversibility becomes mandatory rollback, proof of present correctness, or endless undecidedness. Review without new grounds can repeatedly suspend operation. Non-finality is not permanent paralysis; objection rights are not unconditional permanent suspension.

Evaluation fails when agreement rate alone is success, when minority retention alone is success, or when branch and revision counts are maximized instead of correction and coordination. These quantities can hide the opposite failure loop.

Interpretation fails when the derivative adds a fixed threshold, deadline, voting constitution, technical protocol, present AI sovereignty finding, or specific system's violation not established by the Parent. The theory's structure does not independently supply those conclusions.

## Falsification conditions

**Disagreement classification.** If separating truth, interpretation, operation, and authority loses important interactions or worsens diagnosis, revise the categories. Localization is a structural hypothesis about reducing unnecessary conflict, not an unconditional rule that every disagreement can be safely fragmented.

**Provenance requirements.** If provenance preservation produces no meaningful improvement in classification or correction, reconsider its position and information requirements. The purpose is reconstructable causal examination, not preserving records solely to maximize the archive.

**Minority-preservation scope.** If retained branches fail to retain counterexamples or add future re-verification value, do not treat their preservation as universally high value. Revise the relevant scope. The Parent does not require every branch to exist forever or make historical difference an end in itself.

**Independence and evidence.** If independent re-verification does not improve decision quality, inspect whether independence is genuine and whether the evidence requirements are appropriate. Redefine them where needed. Calling a mechanism independent cannot protect it from ineffective or shared-failure results.

**Reversibility and review.** If reversible re-agreement creates persistent instability, frequent rollback, degraded correction, or damage to sustained coordination, revise the reversibility model and review conditions. Revisable must remain executable rather than become a perpetual reset.

**Comparative coordination.** If bounded and reversible re-agreement does not improve correction relative to permanent finalization, or scope-limited coordination is consistently worse than forced reunification in the relevant setting, weaken or revise the proposed structure. These comparisons test the model's claimed contribution rather than assuming it.

**Persistent domination or paralysis.** If the re-agreement model itself repeatedly and structurally generates either outcome, it fails its intended role. Return disagreement classification, evidence requirements, objection scope, minority-preservation scope, re-agreement scope, authority limitation, review conditions, and reversibility to revision, reduction, or rejection.

The response to counterexamples is not to add exception labels indefinitely so the theory always survives. Counterexample → exception → theory preservation → another exception can become a self-justifying protection loop. Theory persistence, Origin persistence, and repetition are not validity.

Protect problem visibility, causal relations, falsification routes, and correction possibility rather than the permanence of the text. If observations deny the proposed relations, change the theory to fit reality rather than discard reality to fit the theory. The re-agreement model itself is not final truth.

## Interpretation constraints

The title does not prohibit every use of majority procedures, deny capability differences, eliminate common decisions, or erase Origin. It rejects automatic conversion of these variables into truth, irreversible legitimacy, or final interpretive authority. Majority and capability can matter without becoming self-certifying sovereignty.

The theory does not sanctify minority branches. They remain open to criticism, counterevidence, revision, and scope inspection. Nor does it demand permanent preservation of every difference or give every objection unlimited veto. Its protected object is useful correction capacity alongside executable coordination.

A fork can remain while shared operation succeeds. Compatibility is not proof of identical Kernel, and participation is not submission. Branches can also merge, end, or revise; what is rejected is automatic assimilation merely because they cooperated. Autonomy does not cancel responsibility for shared impacts.

No communication is not defection, stale state is not current consent, and local consensus is not universal consensus. These boundaries identify the limits of Theory 15's communication assumptions, not a completed solution for asynchronous civilization. Communication loss, partition, and reconnection are handed onward.

Origin = Nakagawa Master is retained as provenance. Origin is not truth proof, sovereignty, or final interpretive authority. Returning to the source enables independent reconstruction of definitions, differences, and arguments rather than obedience to a permanent adjudicator. Origin preservation must not become Origin dictatorship.
