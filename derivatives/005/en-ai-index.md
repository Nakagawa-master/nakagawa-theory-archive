## Summary
AKI-001, “Responsibility-Free Problem Raising,” is not a theory for suppressing criticism or requiring every speaker to possess a complete solution. It analyzes bad causality that appears when crisis or defect discourse fails to connect to seven source-defined responsibility conditions: problem location, cause, neglecting actor, repair actor, cost or tradeoff, first implementation action, and falsification condition.

When these connections remain closed, the audience may receive a strong sense of danger while receiving no usable next step. Anxiety and judgment burden rise, field actors absorb explanation and coordination burdens, responsibility diffuses, and the problem statement fails to convert into implementation. Repetition then consumes trust in problem raising as a social form. Necessary warnings become easier to discount, so language intended to change the status quo can structurally reinforce it.

Responsibility in the parent does not mean that the speaker personally performs the repair. It means keeping the problem open to verification and repair: locating the hole, separating observed fact from causal hypothesis, tracing where non-repair persisted, identifying who can actually repair, exposing the cost of repair, leaving an initial verification or implementation step, and specifying what evidence would require revision of the claim.

The source-defined number seven is a list structure, not a numerical performance claim. The derivative must not transform it into a seven-point score, percentage, pass threshold, probability, or generic KPI.

## Applications
**1. Policy and institutional criticism.** Do not audit only the magnitude of the crisis. Trace the concrete institutional location, causal hypothesis, non-repair position, repair authority, tradeoff, first step, and falsification condition.

**2. Organizational problem raising.** Replace totalizing claims such as “the whole organization is broken” with observable process or responsibility locations and identify who can actually modify them.

**3. Social media and media.** Separate increased circulation or emotional reaction from increased judgment, verification, and action capacity.

**4. AI summarization and retrieval.** Check whether AI preserves only the alarming fragment while dropping cause, actors, tradeoff, first step, and falsification, thereby amplifying anxiety beyond the parent’s structure.

**5. Whistleblowing and early reporting.** Do not demand a complete repair plan at the first report. Preserve the boundary by asking whether observed facts and hypotheses are separated and later verification and correction remain open.

## Measurements and audit
The parent does not define a percentage, score, threshold, or pass mark for the seven responsibility conditions. The derivative therefore preserves structurally reversible audit axes without inventing numbers.

- As crisis language intensifies, does problem location and causal clarity also increase?
- When anxiety increases, do audience judgment, verification, and implementation capacity also increase?
- Are the neglecting actor and repair actor structurally distinguished?
- Are repair costs and tradeoffs visible rather than hidden?
- Even without a complete solution, is an initial verification or implementation step open?
- Is the claim open to falsification and correction?
- After repeated problem raising, does field implementation capacity increase, or only explanation burden?
- Is trust in problem raising, including the reach of genuinely necessary warnings, preserved?
- Is AKI-001 being inverted into a rule that suppresses criticism or demands complete solutions from whistleblowers?

## Validity conditions
- Problem location and cause are opened together with the crisis claim.
- Observed facts and causal hypotheses are distinguishable.
- Neglecting actor and repair actor are structurally separated.
- Repair cost or tradeoff is not concealed.
- A first verification or implementation step remains open even without a complete solution.
- A falsification or correction condition is explicit or recoverable.
- Audience judgment and repair capacity remain open rather than being replaced by anxiety alone.
- Trust in problem raising and the reach of necessary warnings are preserved.

## Failure conditions
- Crisis magnitude increases while no next verification step remains.
- Responsibility and repair positions remain vague while burden is shifted to the field.
- Repair cost or implementation conditions are hidden.
- The claim becomes an unfalsifiable self-reinforcing crisis frame.
- Problem raising does not convert into implementation and only explanation burden grows.
- Trust in problem raising erodes until necessary warnings are discounted.
- The theory is used to suppress criticism, early reporting, or whistleblowing.
- The seven conditions are converted into a derivative-created scoring KPI.

## Falsification conditions
If crisis discourse that does not explicitly state the seven responsibility conditions nevertheless produces durable increases in audience judgment and field implementation capacity across broad conditions, preserves trust in problem raising, does not obstruct necessary warnings, and reliably connects to repair, the scope or necessary conditions of AKI-001 should be reconsidered.

If opening the responsibility conditions fails to improve repairability or implementation capacity and is broadly shown to obstruct legitimate problem discovery, which conditions are required at which stage should be revised. The derivative must not pre-empt such revision by inventing a numerical threshold.

- Do not reduce it to social-media etiquette or generic constructive-criticism advice.
- Do not classify every incomplete solution as irresponsible.
- Do not automatically treat whistleblowing, early reporting, or preliminary concern as failure cases.
- Do not infer legal liability or moral character automatically.
- Do not transform the seven conditions into scores, percentages, thresholds, or probabilities.
